*ICGLR Defense Ministers’ Meeting: DRC Presses for Peace and Tests Regional Credibility
Livingstone, January 12, 2026 - The Defense Ministers of ICGLR member states met in an extraordinary session on January 10 in Livingstone, Zambia, to address the humanitarian and security crisis in eastern DRC and across the Great Lakes region, amid ongoing fighting between government forces and the AFC/M23 rebellion.
The meeting followed earlier sessions of the ICGLR Specialized Committee of Defense Experts and the Chiefs of Staff, the body responsible for overseeing the Non-Aggression Protocol.
A central focus was the performance of the Expanded Joint Verification Mechanism (EJVM), tasked with monitoring and investigating security incidents in the region. The mechanism has faced challenges, including irregular meetings and limited reporting, prompting chiefs of staff to draft recommendations for defense ministers.
The Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of National Defense, Guy Kabombo Muadiamvita, stressed that peace cannot be delayed or subordinated to political calculations, calling for regional mechanisms to become credible, operational, and deterrent to protect civilians and end cycles of violence.
He placed particular emphasis on the effective operationalization of the Expanded Joint Verification Mechanism Plus (EJVM+), describing it as a test of credibility for both the ICGLR and the region as a whole.
During the session, ministers officially installed the new commander and deputy commander of the EJVM, based in Goma, reinforcing the regional security framework.
The new roadmap prioritizes the implementation of the Washington and Doha processes, especially the ceasefire mechanism signed on October 14, 2025, between the Congolese government and the AFC/M23 under Qatari mediation. However, implementation on the ground remains difficult, largely because the verification and monitoring mechanism is not yet fully operational.
Revitalizing the EJVM is therefore seen as essential to ensuring effective ceasefire monitoring and restoring the credibility of ongoing peace efforts.

France has raised the issue of the reopening of Goma International Airport and confirmed that the entire €850 million in emergency humanitarian aid pledged for the Great Lakes region has already been fully disbursed.
France took part in a high-level meeting on the coherence and consolidation of the peace process in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and the Great Lakes region, held on January 17, 2026, in Lomé, Togo. It was convened at the initiative of Faure Gnassingbé, African Union mediator for the crisis in eastern DRC.

The Paris Conference aimed to mobilize international support to address the humanitarian emergency in the Great Lakes region while backing mediation efforts led by the United States, Qatar, and the African Union. In the same statement, the group maintained that the dispute between Kinshasa and Kigali does not concern it, asserting that the Congolese government alone bears responsibility for its relations with neighboring states and should resolve its disputes without involving the movement.

*Rwanda Warns Against “Uncontrollable Actors” and Reaffirms Commitment to Regional Stability
Washington Accords January 23, 2026 - In response to renewed clashes in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, Rwanda has reaffirmed its commitment to the Washington Accords signed with the DRC under U.S. auspices, while calling for the strict implementation of security measures to prevent escalation and protect civilians.
In an official statement issued on Thursday, January 22, Rwanda’s ambassador to the United States, Mathilde Mukantabana, outlined Kigali’s position on the implementation and implications of the accords.
Rwanda expressed its gratitude to the United States for prioritizing the peace process between Rwanda and the DRC, in coordination with Qatar’s mediation efforts, describing the Washington Accords as a major step toward lasting stability, security, and prosperity in the Great Lakes region.
However, the Rwandan diplomat warned of continued violence in eastern DRC, attributing it to “uncontrollable actors,” including mercenaries, militias supported by certain governments, and elements within the Congolese state. According to her, these actors attempted to undermine the Washington Accords by orchestrating violence in November and December 2025 and shifting blame onto Rwanda.
She also stated that the AFC/M23 rebels had unilaterally withdrawn from Uvira in South Kivu, a move Kigali described as a sign of commitment to de-escalation.
The statement recalls the historical insecurity in the region, particularly linked to the FDLR, which Rwanda accuses of responsibility for atrocities committed since 1994.
Rwanda emphasized that it continues to maintain defensive measures to protect its population against any resurgence of genocidal violence.
“The memory of the genocide survivors, the graves of a million victims, and the documented history of the Abacengezi insurgency compel us to maintain our defense capabilities until credible security guarantees are established and the threat disappears permanently,” the statement said.
On the political and economic front, Rwanda stressed that the Washington Accords are not intended to influence the internal affairs of the DRC, but rather to serve as a framework for regional development.
“The Regional Investment Framework for Peace (REIF) identifies key areas for economic cooperation between Rwanda and the DRC, demonstrating the tangible benefits of peace and creating investment and growth opportunities that directly benefit regional populations,” the statement added.
Rwanda reaffirmed its willingness to continue security coordination, contingent upon the disarmament and neutralization of the FDLR, while also supporting cross-border economic development.
Kigali concluded by reiterating its readiness to work with the DRC, the United States, Qatar, the African Union, and other partners to transform the Washington Accords into lasting peace and inclusive regional development.

*Ministry of Finance Defends New Secure License Plates, Denies Opacity and High Costs
Kinshasa DRC January 11, 2026 - The Ministry of Finance has rejected allegations surrounding the reform of the vehicle registration system and the introduction of new secure license plates in the Democratic Republic of Congo, insisting that the process is transparent, lawful, and financially justified.
In a statement, the ministry said it sought to “set the record straight” and counter what it described as confusion and unfounded accusations, reaffirming its commitment to good governance and transparency.
A Reform to Restore Order and Security
According to the ministry, the reform aims to correct serious security flaws and the absence of a clear contractual framework, which had enabled a parallel market to flourish. Authorities reported the circulation of duplicate license plate numbers and the sale of Congolese plates on foreign online platforms.
“Allowing such a situation to continue would have been a grave mistake,” the ministry said, stressing that the reform is intended to restore state authority, strengthen vehicle identification, and protect public finances.
A Relaunched Process After the 2022 Failure
The ministry acknowledged that an earlier project launched in 2022 failed due to technical and legal shortcomings, notably the absence of a required feasibility study and disagreements among potential partners.
The process was subsequently suspended and relaunched under a new legal framework, with approval from the Public Procurement Regulatory Authority (ARMP). Authorities insist this decision was made solely in the public interest.
No Irregular Technical Changes, Government Says
Responding to claims of last-minute technical changes, the ministry said the tender dossier received a no-objection opinion from the Directorate General for Public Procurement Control (DGCMP).
It added that the technical specifications are largely identical to those of the 2022 project and that all three shortlisted companies were invited to submit bids, ensuring equal treatment.
Contract Awarded in Line with Procurement Rules
The ministry denied any opacity or conflict of interest, emphasizing that the project was included in an approved procurement plan and validated by the Prime Minister.
Contrary to some reports, authorities said the contract was awarded through a restricted tender authorized by the DGCMP, not a negotiated procedure. They also noted that the winning bidders were selected before the current Minister of Finance took office.
Clarifying the Cost of License Plates
The ministry rejected claims that the plates cost only $30 to produce, stating that the official price of $65 includes manufacturing, logistics, transport, supplier margins, and system security services.
It also said no private intermediaries are involved in financing and that the share allocated to the public treasury has increased by 14.3%.
The price of license plates, unchanged for more than a decade, has been reduced from $120 to $115, with a further cut to $105 planned for 2027, a move the government says will increase state revenue.

The Ministry of Finance said it remains open to audits and dialogue and urged citizens to consult official documents. It called on the public to remain calm and rely on official sources, stating that “access to facts and documents is the best foundation for responsible public debate.”
*DRC Challenges $4 Billion Claim by US Company PayServices
Kinshasa/Washington January 14, 2026 - The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) is contesting a lawsuit filed by the American firm PayServices, which seeks $4 billion in damages in a US federal court.
According to the lawsuit, PayServices alleges it reached an agreement with Congolese public entities to digitize state payments, a project the company claims could have generated over $8 billion annually. The company asserts that the project was never implemented.
The complaint states that initial contacts occurred on December 6, 2023, at the DRC embassy in Washington, D.C., followed by an alleged exclusivity agreement in January 2024 with the Congolese General Savings Bank (CADECO). A payment of $20 million was reportedly expected before March 2024, but was allegedly never released. PayServices claims it attempted mediation before taking legal action.
The lawsuit further alleges breach of contract, pressure tactics, and attempted bribery, citing a supposed €5 million bribe. Several high-ranking officials are named, including the Minister of Portfolio, Julie Mbamubi Shiku; Minister of Finance, Doudou Fwamba Likunde; Chief of Staff to the President, Nkinzo Kamole; Governor of the Central Bank, Andrew Mwema; and CADECO CEO, Mukeba Muntuba.
Kinshasa Denies All Allegations
In a press release dated January 13, 2026, the Congolese Ministry of Portfolio refuted the claims, stating that PayServices never held legal banking status under U.S. law. The release cites a formal decision of the Idaho Department of Finance (October 18, 2024), which revoked all conditional accreditations and prohibited the company from using the term "bank" due to regulatory non-compliance.
The ministry emphasized that administrative checks under the Public Finance Act confirmed that PayServices was neither authorized nor qualified to receive or manage public funds. Consequently, no funds were transferred, and no financial loss occurred. Authorities also noted that no certified financial statements or independent audit were produced to substantiate the company’s claims.
The Ministry of Foreign Affairs stated that the alleged $72 million investment and the supposed contract between PCES Akiba and CADECO are baseless, lacking any legal, budgetary, or accounting foundation. The ministry framed the allegations of corruption and coercion as stemming from Kinshasa’s refusal to pay a company without legal banking status.
DRC Reserves Legal Rights
The statement confirmed that the DRC is cooperating with all competent jurisdictions and reserves the right to pursue legal remedies. The case continues in US courts, where the dispute over PayServices’ banking authority and the alleged damages is ongoing.

Strengthening the Court of Auditors and other oversight bodies.
Increasing transparency and public access to financial reports.
Enhancing local authorities’ capacity to mobilize and manage revenue.
Collaborating with the General Inspectorate of Finance (IGF) for better supervision.
Encouraging public accountability, including sanctions for mismanagement.

*Virunga National Park Welcomes New Mountain Gorilla in Mapuwa Family
Goma, January 20, 2026 - Virunga National Park announced the birth of a male mountain gorilla to Sebutimbiri, a female in the Mapuwa family. This marks the second birth recorded in the park this year.
With this addition, the Mapuwa family now numbers 28 individuals. The birth occurred in the Mikeno sector, one of the key habitats for mountain gorillas within the park.
The park highlighted the significance of the birth for both social dynamics within gorilla groups and broader conservation programs aimed at supporting this endangered species. International partners, including the European Union and UNESCO, continue to support efforts to protect these primates.
Earlier in January, Virunga National Park reported another milestone: the first twin birth of the year, which occurred on January 3, bringing attention to the park’s ongoing successes in conservation despite challenges.
Virunga National Park, established in 1925 on the border between Rwanda and Uganda, is Africa’s oldest national park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Covering 7,800 km² in North Kivu province, the park hosts exceptional biodiversity.

Conclusion
The reporting period from January 12 to 24, 2026, depicts a Democratic Republic of Congo at a critical crossroads, defined by a complex interplay of persistent conflict, intensive diplomacy, and strategic economic shifts. While high-level mediation through the Washington and Doha processes continues, the security situation in the east remains volatile, marked by shifting rebel positions, humanitarian distress for hundreds of thousands of displaced persons, and a growing reliance on drone technology in military operations. Concurrently, the government is asserting greater economic sovereignty, particularly in the mining sector, and pursuing ambitious internal infrastructure projects despite facing significant domestic political opposition and international legal challenges. Ultimately, the path toward lasting stability appears to hinge on the successful operationalization of regional monitoring mechanisms and the realization of a truly inclusive national dialogue
Overviews :
1. Diplomatic & Political Overview: A Region at a Crossroads
The DRC is currently navigating a period of intense but stalled diplomacy. While multiple peace frameworks exist-specifically the Washington and Doha processes-there is a palpable lack of trust between Kinshasa and Kigali.
The "Lomé Momentum": Togo has emerged as a key African mediator, hosting high-level sessions to harmonize these disparate peace initiatives.
Domestic Political Tension: Internally, the government faces a fractured landscape. The exile of figures like Seth Kikuni and the arrest of others like Aubin Minaku signal a deepening rift between the current administration and the opposition.
The Call for Dialogue: International partners, including Belgium’s King Philippe, are increasingly pushing for an "inclusive national dialogue" to address the root causes of instability.
2. Security Overview: Technological Shifts and Precarious Ceasefires
The security landscape in eastern DRC remains volatile, characterized by a "frozen conflict" that frequently thaws into violence.
Drone Warfare: A notable shift in FARDC tactics is the increased use of precision drone strikes to target AFC/M23 positions, as seen in Buleusa and Kikuku.
The Uvira Vacuum: The AFC/M23’s tactical withdrawal from Uvira has created a complex security environment, with the rebels officially placing the city under "international responsibility" while the government views the move as a strategy to deflect international pressure.
Reinforcements vs. Retreats: While rebels withdrew from some areas, they simultaneously reinforced positions in others, such as Masisi, indicating they are preparing for potential counter-offensives.
3. Economic & Technical Overview: Asserting Sovereignty
Kinshasa is actively seeking to reclaim control over its national resources and public systems, though this has led to significant legal and logistical friction.
Resource Nationalism: Gécamines’ decision to directly purchase 100,000 tons of copper from the Tenke Fungurume Mine for the US market is a landmark move to assert state sovereignty over metal pricing and marketing.
Infrastructure Struggles: Efforts to rehabilitate vital infrastructure, such as the Bandundu and Kikwit airports, have been plagued by technical failures and contract terminations, highlighting the challenges of internal development.
Legal Battles: The $4 billion lawsuit by PayServices underscores the risks and complexities the DRC faces as it attempts to modernize and digitize its state payment systems.
4. Humanitarian Overview: A Growing Regional Crisis
The human cost of the conflict has reached a "devastating human tragedy," as described by government officials.
Mass Displacement: Over 200,000 Congolese refugees are now in Burundi alone, with transit centers operating at 200% capacity.
Medical System Collapse: Frontline health facilities in zones like Fizi and Walikale are overwhelmed by gunshot victims and suffering from acute shortages of medicine and nutritional products.
Conservation at Risk: Even conservation efforts are caught in the crossfire; while Virunga National Park recorded a rare mountain gorilla birth, the park remains under threat from the ongoing presence of armed groups.
