*Ambassador Bazivamo Conveys President Kagame’s Message to Rwandans in Brazzaville
Brazzaville, Republic of Congo - Sunday, December 28, 2025
The Secretary General of the RPF Inkotanyi, Ambassador Christophe Bazivamo, met with Rwandans living in Brazzaville during a working visit to the Republic of Congo, where he conveyed a message from President Paul Kagame.
According to a statement released by the RPF Inkotanyi, Amb. Bazivamo delivered the greetings of President Kagame, wishing the Rwandan diaspora good health and continued progress, while outlining the development programs Rwanda has committed to. During that visit, eight new cooperation agreements were signed, adding to the 25 agreements concluded in April 2022 during President Kagame’s visit to Brazzaville.

Kinshasa, January 2, 2026
The AFC/M23 Alliance has sharply criticized President Félix Tshisekedi’s government for attributing the Democratic Republic of the Congo’s ongoing insecurity to foreign actors, calling it a tactic to hide domestic failures.

“The time for deception, lies, and false promises is over. From now on, the Congolese stand firm, determined and with great will. The year 2026 will be the year to strengthen our struggle for liberation, justice, peace, and the reconstruction of the State,” he said.
He also confirmed that Doha-facilitated talks between the DRC government and AFC/M23 have failed to yield a viable solution, and noted that the Tshisekedi administration has refused further negotiations, whether in person or virtually.
This statement highlights the deepening mistrust between the Congolese government and the AFC/M23 Alliance, raising concerns over the prospects for peace and stability in eastern DRC.

*DRC’s Size Used as a Pretext for Tshisekedi’s Failure to Protect Citizens
January 4, 2026- The sheer size of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) is increasingly being used as a pretext to justify President Félix Tshisekedi’s persistent inability to guarantee security for the Congolese people. As insecurity continues to worsen in eastern DRC, particularly in the Kivu region, senior officials have repeatedly argued that the country’s vast territory is responsible for decades of instability.
A recurring narrative among DRC leaders is that the Congo is simply too large to govern effectively. This argument has been frequently invoked by President Tshisekedi to explain the ongoing violence. In 2022, he publicly claimed that the country’s security challenges stem from its size, insisting that the national army could not be deployed everywhere to fight armed groups such as the ADF, an Islamic State-linked Ugandan militia, and other insurgents operating on Congolese soil.
Despite numbering around 130,000 troops, the FARDC is plagued by low morale, inadequate training, weak command structures, ethnic discrimination, corruption, and the integration of the FDLR, a Rwandan militia responsible for the 1994 genocide against the Tutsis, incorporated under Tshisekedi’s authority.
Tshisekedi’s reliance on Burundian troops, the Wazalendo militia, European mercenaries, and SADC forces has steadily declined, particularly after South Africa, Malawi, and Tanzania withdrew their contingents following military setbacks against the AFC/M23.
Some analysts now question whether Tshisekedi fully understood the vastness of the Congolese territory before assuming office, when he promised to restore security nationwide, or whether this reality only became apparent to him after taking power. Others openly question his true intentions regarding the country’s territorial integrity, wondering whether a reduction in size might better serve his political interests.
As the saying goes, a leader is one who knows the way, walks the way, and shows the way. The conduct of Tshisekedi and his inner circle suggests that the crisis in eastern DRC is of little concern, as long as Kinshasa remains untouched.
In a statement issued on November 26, government spokesperson Patrick Muyaya claimed that the Kivu regions affected by insecurity represent only a small portion of the DRC.
“What does 2,345,000 square kilometers really mean?” he asked, adding that foreigners often exaggerate the conflict by saying, “There is war in the DRC,” while visitors to Kinshasa are surprised to find calm in the capital.
Such remarks have reinforced the perception that the Tshisekedi administration has never prioritized the well-being of populations in eastern DRC.
In 2025, Brigadier General Eddy Kapend, commander of the 22nd Military Region, openly stated that the country was not concerned by the occupation of parts of eastern DRC by AFC/M23 fighters, stressing instead that the real issue would be their presence in Katanga province. He further noted that there were no major concerns as long as Katanga remained untouched.
That same year, Anthony Nkinzo Kamole, Chief of Staff to the Presidency, declared during an interview that the conflict in eastern DRC was happening 2,000 kilometers away, describing the eastern regions as “foreign zones”, while Kinshasa was considered their country.
These statements echo similar remarks made by Tshisekedi, Muyaya, General Kapend, Nkinzo, and others, including Jules Alingete, the Auditor General, who once said that the war was 2,000 kilometers from Kinshasa and that “we only see it on television”. Such views underscore a deep disconnect from the realities faced by eastern populations.
Beyond the argument of territorial size, another frequent strategy has been to blame Rwanda for all problems, allowing the Congolese government to avoid acknowledging its own failures.
Eastern DRC has become a battleground for more than 260 armed groups, resulting in thousands of civilian deaths, mass rapes, forced recruitment of children, and the displacement of millions, both internally and abroad.
Tshisekedi has further exacerbated the crisis by arming groups accused of serious human rights violations to support the FARDC against the AFC/M23, a movement that claims to be fighting against the genocide of Congolese Tutsis allegedly perpetrated by the Kinshasa government.
The FDLR, which invaded Rwanda in 1994, continues to be deeply entrenched within the Congolese army and is actively involved in arming the predominantly civilian Wazalendo movement.
To date, Tshisekedi has shown no clear political will to end the war or restore peace. He appears largely indifferent to the fate of Congolese refugees and civilians who continue to risk their lives daily amid the conflict.
Sheltered more than 2,000 kilometers away from the frontlines, the president has repeatedly minimized the crisis by arguing that the affected areas are too small to matter. Critics argue that he is now using the war in eastern DRC as a political tool, exploiting the ongoing instability to justify extending his hold on power.
*Togo Moves to Revive Peace Talks Between DRC Government and AFC/M23
January 5, 2026 - The government of Togo has initiated efforts to revive peace talks between the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and the AFC/M23 movement, amid renewed violence in eastern DRC.
According to diplomatic sources, Lomé was expected to host a high-level mediation meeting on January 17, 2026, aimed at restoring dialogue between the two sides. The initiative was intended to rebuild mutual trust and restart negotiations that have stalled in recent weeks. Other international mediators, including the United States and Qatar, were expected to take part in the meeting.
Relations between the DRC government and the AFC/M23 deteriorated sharply in early December 2025, following intense fighting in South Kivu province, particularly in the Rusizi Valley.
Tensions further escalated when AFC/M23 fighters entered the city of Uvira, claiming they were responding to continued attacks by Congolese government forces on their positions. On December 16, the AFC/M23 announced its decision to withdraw from Uvira, a move that was carried out two days later. However, the DRC government rejected the withdrawal, and its forces reportedly launched fresh attacks from the Fizi area.
In his end-of-year address, AFC/M23 leader Corneille Nangaa Yobeluo disclosed that Qatar had convened two meetings aimed at discussing the implementation of a ceasefire agreement, but said the DRC government declined to attend.
According to Nangaa, Kinshasa deliberately chose to sabotage the peace process, favoring a military option, as it had done in previous conflicts. He reaffirmed that the AFC/M23 remains committed to a genuine and lasting peace, and said the movement continues to place its trust in Qatar’s mediation efforts.
*DRC Agriculture Minister Criticizes FARDC and Intelligence Services Over War and Media Influence
The Minister of Agriculture and Food Security of the Democratic Republic of Congo, Muhindo Nzangi Butondo, has strongly criticized the FARDC and military intelligence services, accusing them of being intimidated by narratives circulating on social media amid the ongoing conflict.
Speaking in an interview on January 5, 2026, the minister argued that the DRC government is losing not only the war on the ground but also the information battle. He said the situation surrounding the fall of Uvira illustrates how misinformation and panic contributed to military failure.
“Take the example of Uvira. They announced its capture while M23 fighters were still in Luvungi, about 30 kilometers away. Once the news spread online, everyone believed Uvira had fallen, and the national army fled the city before the enemy even arrived,” he said.
On December 9, 2025, AFC/M23 fighters entered Uvira, before withdrawing about a week later following international pressure, particularly from the United States, which had assured that peace talks would continue.
According to Minister Muhindo, the AFC/M23 coalition maintains significant military strength, to the extent that, without external intervention, it could have advanced far beyond Uvira.
“I must be honest: if it had not been for a message from Marco Rubio saying, ‘We are not happy! We are not happy!’, they would likely be in Kalemie, in Tanganyika Province, because there was practically nothing stopping them. That message gave us time to prepare,” he stated.
The minister emphasized that the absence of further territorial advances by AFC/M23 should not be interpreted as a lack of military capability, but rather as the result of diplomatic intervention, mainly by the United States.
He concluded by asserting that the only real leverage the DRC government still retains is diplomatic, not military, underscoring the fragility of the country’s security apparatus in the face of organized armed groups.
*Tshisekedi Turns Away from Doha Mediation, Pushes for New Luanda Talks
President Félix Tshisekedi of the Democratic Republic of the Congo is seeking to relaunch peace talks in Luanda, while continuing to distance himself from the Qatari-led mediation process initiated in early 2025.
Tshisekedi last traveled to Luanda on December 14, 2025, where he held brief discussions with Angolan President João Lourenço on efforts to restore peace in eastern DRC. The Luanda framework, led by Lourenço, has been stalled since October 2024, when the Congolese government delegation refused to engage in direct dialogue with the AFC/M23 coalition.
The paralysis of the Luanda process coincided with President Lourenço assuming the chairmanship of the African Union (AU). During this period, mediation efforts shifted toward Washington and Doha, though with limited progress. Lourenço is expected to step down from the AU presidency during the AU General Assembly on February 15, 2026, to be replaced by Burundi’s President Évariste Ndayishimiye, potentially freeing him to re-engage fully in mediation efforts.
On January 5, 2026, President Tshisekedi again visited Angola to discuss security developments in eastern DRC. However, the agenda reportedly mirrored discussions already held under the Doha and Washington initiatives.
Although the Congolese presidency maintains that Tshisekedi still formally supports the Doha process, his actions suggest otherwise. The DRC recently declined to send representatives to two meetings organized by Qatar, which were aimed at assessing the implementation of the ceasefire agreement.
Meanwhile, regional tensions around mediation have intensified. Burundi’s foreign minister has publicly accused Qatar of using its diplomatic influence to block potential international sanctions against Rwanda-claims that have been repeatedly denied.
Sources in Kinshasa indicate that Tshisekedi has formally asked President Lourenço to initiate a new round of talks in Luanda, a request the Angolan leader has reportedly accepted. The proposed talks would involve a broad range of Congolese stakeholders, including members of the ruling coalition, political opposition figures, armed groups such as AFC/M23, religious leaders, and civil society representatives.
If launched, the new Luanda talks are expected to resemble the inclusive national dialogue formats previously held in Nairobi, as well as those advocated by religious leaders, particularly the Catholic and Anglican churches.
However, for the AFC/M23, the Luanda framework may lack the leverage of the Doha process. The group argues that broader national dialogues tend to overlook specific issues directly affecting them, such as the release of hundreds of their detained members, which they consider central to any lasting agreement.
As diplomatic maneuvering continues, uncertainty remains over which mediation track-Luanda, Doha, or another format-will ultimately shape the future of peace efforts in eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo.

The Minister of Finance of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Adolphe Muzito, stated on 8 January that former President Joseph Kabila Kabange should not be considered an enemy of the state, despite a death sentence handed down by the DRC’s High Military Court.

When asked if he believed Tshisekedi would carry out the death sentence if Kabila were arrested, Muzito responded that, to his knowledge, the President would not do so.
Muzito’s remarks come amid ongoing political tensions in the DRC over Kabila’s alleged role with rebel forces and reflect a cautious approach to a highly sensitive issue.

*Funding Secured for Major Expansion of Karenge Waterworks in Rwanda
Rwamagana, January 3, 2026
The Rwamagana District Administration has announced that funding has been fully secured for the expansion and distribution project of the Karenge Waterworks, with the first phase of construction already reaching a satisfactory level of completion.
The Karenge Waterworks is a strategic infrastructure project expected to significantly increase water supply to Rwamagana town, the district’s industrial zone, and parts of Kigali City. Under the expansion plan, the plant’s production capacity will rise from 12,000 cubic meters per day to 48,000 cubic meters, a fourfold increase.
The initial agreement for the expansion was signed between Rwanda and Hungary. More recently, on November 28, 2025, the Cabinet approved the final ratification of a financing agreement between the Government of Rwanda and the Abu Dhabi Fund for Economic Development, which is providing a loan of 34.7 billion Rwandan francs to support the expansion of the water transportation and distribution system linked to the Karenge plant.
Rwamagana District Mayor Mbonyumuvunyi Radjab confirmed that construction works are underway and progressing well. He explained that once operational, water from the Karenge plant will be shared between Kigali City and Rwamagana District, with part directed to the industrial area and the remainder distributed to households.
The Abu Dhabi-funded component of the project will focus on the construction of water reservoirs and the installation of large transmission pipelines, covering more than 40 kilometers, to ensure water reaches different sectors of the district. These pipelines will connect to secondary distribution networks serving residential areas.
According to the mayor, the project is planned over three years, with the first year nearing completion. Trial distribution of water to the population is expected to begin within the coming months.
Currently, 93% of Rwamagana District’s population has access to clean water. Local authorities say the ultimate goal of the Karenge Waterworks expansion is to achieve universal access to safe drinking water across the district.
*Rwanda’s Jet Fuel Storage Reaches 12 Million Liters
On January 5, 2026, the Rwanda Energy Corporation (REG) announced that Rwanda’s jet fuel storage capacity now stands at 12 million liters, a figure that reflects the country’s broader position in terms of petroleum storage capacity.
Overall, Rwanda has petroleum storage facilities totaling 118 million liters, combining both government-owned and private storage facilities across the country.
The government’s oil storage facilities in Gatsata have a capacity of 7,200,000 liters, while private oil storage facilities in Kabuye (Kigali City) can store 3,100,000 liters. In Rwabuye (Huye District), government tanks hold 1,900,000 liters, and in Bigogwe (Nyabihu District), government facilities store 3,000,000 liters.
Additional private fuel tanks in Jabana (Kigali City) have a capacity of 10,000,000 liters, while government-owned tanks in Rusororo can store up to 20,000,000 liters. Altogether, Rwanda’s fuel tanks have a combined storage capacity of 45,200,000 liters.
Rwanda’s fuel storage infrastructure includes government-owned tanks in Gatsata with a capacity of 6,700,000 liters and private tanks in Kabuye holding 2,700,000 liters. There are also government fuel oil tanks in Rwabuye with a capacity of 1,900,000 liters, fuel oil tanks in Bigogwe holding 2,000,000 liters, and private fuel oil tanks in Jabana with a capacity of 10,000,000 liters.
In addition, the government operates fuel oil tanks in Rusororo with a capacity of 36,000,000 liters, bringing the total fuel oil storage capacity to 59,300,000 liters. There are also government-owned kerosene tanks in Gatsata with a capacity of 1,500,000 liters.
Regarding aviation fuel, Rwanda operates government-owned jet fuel storage facilities in Rusororo with a capacity of 10,000,000 liters, as well as another facility in Kanombe holding 2,000,000 liters, for a total jet fuel storage capacity of 12 million liters.
By location, Gatsata has a total capacity of 15,400,000 liters of petroleum products, Kabuye holds 5,800,000 liters, Rwabuye stores 3,800,000 liters, and Bigogwe has 5,000,000 liters. Jabana accounts for 20,000,000 liters, Rusororo for 66,000,000 liters, and Kanombe for 2,000,000 liters, together reaching a national storage capacity of 118,000,000 liters.
In March 2025, the Minister of Trade and Industry, Prudence Sebahizi, stated that Rwanda’s petroleum storage capacity exceeded 110 million liters, with plans to expand it to 320 million liters within two years.

*Rwanda Launches Home-Based Cervical Cancer Screening Program

The initiative, announced by the Rwanda National Cancer Institute (RNCI), aims to expand access to screening and remove barriers that have long limited early detection.
Under the program, women use a self-sampling device at home. The sample is then collected and analyzed at a specialized laboratory to detect human papillomavirus (HPV), the leading cause of cervical cancer. Once results are available, women are informed by a health professional, and those who test positive for HPV are referred for further medical evaluation or treatment.
The screening is free of charge and targets women aged 30 and above, including those without symptoms. Health officials stress that cervical cancer often develops silently, making early screening essential for prevention and survival.
Although the program was piloted six months ago, initial uptake was limited. Authorities say the home-based approach is designed to overcome challenges such as shame, embarrassment, fear of examinations, and limited access to doctors, while remaining as reliable as conventional clinical screening.
Participating private health facilities include Legacy Clinic, DMC Carrefour, Good Life, Bien Naitre, Bwiza Medical, MBC Hospital, Polyclinic Medico-Social, and Saint Jean Polyclinique in Nyarugenge.
According to national cancer data, between 600 and 800 new cervical cancer cases are diagnosed each year in Rwanda, with around 600 deaths annually, highlighting the urgency of prevention efforts.
Rwanda aims to eliminate cervical cancer by 2027, in line with the WHO 90-70-90 strategy, which targets:
90% HPV vaccination coverage among girls aged 13-15,
70% screening of women aged 29-49, and
90% treatment for women diagnosed with early-stage disease.
Health authorities emphasize that early detection saves lives and that the new program empowers women to take control of their health while accelerating progress toward national elimination goals.

He said: "MRDP-Twirwaneho strongly condemns this evil plan of foreign forces, which only aims to attack Banyamulenge civilians in Bibokoboko and Minembwe and block their access, which will make their situation worse."
MRDP-Twirwaneho claimed that since March 2025, Burundian troops, collaborating with the DRC government, Wazalendo groups, and FDLR forces, have reportedly killed and tortured civilians, abducted individuals, committed sexual violence, and looted property.
Since October 2025, the Banyamulenge in Minembwe have faced worsening conditions, with blocked roads to markets and health centers, causing shortages of basic goods and a spike in deaths, particularly among children under five due to lack of medical care.
After the AFC/M23 coalition captured Uvira and much of the Ruzizi Valley in December 2025, many Burundian troops temporarily returned home. Recent reports indicate that they have now redeployed to Fizi, including the town of Baraka, to resume joint operations with DRC forces.
MRDP-Twirwaneho warned that it will not tolerate the presence of these troops while civilian security and property remain at risk.
This development highlights the ongoing regional complexities in eastern DRC, with foreign forces and local militias continuing to impact the safety and livelihood of vulnerable communities.
*Angolan President Lourenço Urges DRC Government and AFC/M23 to End Hostilities
Luanda, Angola - 8 January 2026: Angolan President and African Union Chairperson, João Lourenço, has called on the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) government and the AFC/M23 coalition to immediately cease hostilities and focus on a peaceful resolution to the ongoing conflict in eastern DRC.
President Lourenço made the statement following a meeting with DRC President Félix Tshisekedi, who traveled to Luanda to discuss strategies to restore peace and security in the region.
Expressing concern over the continued instability and displacement in eastern DRC, Lourenço warned that ongoing fighting is undermining international peace initiatives, including talks previously held in Washington and Doha.
He urged all parties to:
Cease hostilities immediately
Respect existing agreements
Prioritize the interests of the Congolese people Lourenço also called on the international community to support efforts aimed at stabilizing eastern DRC.
Tshisekedi has requested that new peace talks bring together representatives from all Congolese groups, including the AFC/M23, with discussions to be held in Kinshasa under the facilitation of the Angolan government.
According to reports, Lourenço has accepted Tshisekedi’s request, engaging with opposition figures in the DRC to prepare for the talks. Tshisekedi emphasized that the AFC/M23 must first observe a ceasefire, while the DRC government will verify compliance before formal negotiations begin.
This development signals renewed efforts to restart dialogue in the eastern DRC conflict, with Angola taking a central mediation role.
*Renewed Clashes in Masisi: AFC/M23 and Wazalendo Battle Amid Civilian Casualties
Masisi, North Kivu - 11 January 2026: Fighting has resumed in Masisi Territory between the AFC/M23 rebels and the Wazalendo coalition, aligned with the DRC government. According to local reports, clashes began at midnight in the Kazinga area, with residents hearing heavy and light weapons being fired.
Sources say the Wazalendo forces are attempting to prevent AFC/M23 from expanding control over additional areas, including the first and second Nyamaboko groups.
This flare-up follows a FARDC attack on 2 January 2026, during which six civilians were killed and 41 others injured in a populated area of Masisi. Both AFC/M23 and the United Nations peacekeeping mission (MONUSCO) condemned the January 2 attack.
The renewed clashes highlight the ongoing instability in North Kivu, where armed confrontations continue to threaten civilians and hamper peace efforts in the region.
Conclusion
Between late 2025 and early 2026, Rwanda and the surrounding region are characterized by a dual focus on internal development and complex regional diplomacy. Internally, Rwanda is experiencing significant growth in private investment and infrastructure, exemplified by the inauguration of the 7 billion Frw GLADS Apartment and the expansion of the Karenge Waterworks to meet increasing demand for clean water. The government is also modernizing economic services through initiatives like the Rwanda Development Board’s One Stop Center website to further streamline investment.
On the diplomatic and security front, the period is marked by several key developments:
Regional Influence and Cooperation: Rwanda continues to be viewed as a model for peacebuilding, with nations like Sudan actively seeking its expertise for national reconstruction. Furthermore, Rwandan peacekeepers continue to receive international recognition for their professionalism in missions like MINUSCA in the Central African Republic.
Ongoing DRC Tensions: Relations between Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo remain strained. The DRC government faces internal and external criticism for its handling of security in the East, with ongoing conflicts involving the AFC/M23 coalition. While there are shifting efforts to restart mediation-moving between Qatari-led and Angolan-led frameworks-the path to a lasting peace remains uncertain due to deep-seated mistrust and conflicting negotiation strategies.
Strategic Resource Management: Rwanda is proactively addressing future challenges, such as the need to expand its national fuel reserves to accommodate rising consumption driven by industrial growth and increased vehicle use.
In summary, while Rwanda is successfully advancing its domestic economic and developmental agenda, it remains deeply involved in-and affected by-the persistent security challenges and diplomatic maneuvering in the broader Great Lakes Region.
