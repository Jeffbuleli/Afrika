*DRC–U.S. Strategic Partnership: Government Rejects Claims of “Selling Off” National Interests
Kinshasa | December 31, 2025
The Government of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) has strongly denied allegations that the strategic partnership agreement signed with the United States constitutes a sell-off of the country’s economic or mineral interests.
Speaking during a joint press briefing on Tuesday, December 30, Deputy Prime Minister in charge of the National Economy, Daniel Mukoko Samba, alongside the Minister of Communication and Media, Patrick Muyaya, sought to clarify the economic component of the Washington Agreement, signed on December 4, 2025, under the auspices of U.S. President Donald Trump.
“This is neither a deal, nor a contract, nor a document intended to sell out the interests of the DRC,” Mukoko Samba stated, responding to widespread criticism circulating in political circles, civil society, and online media.
A Framework Agreement, Not a Transaction
According to the government, the agreement should be understood as a strategic and legal framework for cooperation, rather than a transactional document involving the transfer of assets or concessions.
“This is a technical framework agreement defining areas of cooperation—economy and trade, security, governance, education, science, and technology—without ceding a single mine, designating a single investor, or awarding any contract,” Mukoko emphasized.
He categorically rejected claims of a “fire sale” of Congolese mineral resources, stating that no mining concessions, no preferential pricing, and no derogation from Congolese laws are mentioned in the agreement.
Major Development Projects Highlighted
Mukoko Samba underlined that the partnership aims to support peace, security, and economic development, with the prospect of large-scale job creation. He cited flagship projects included in the cooperation framework, notably:
The Grand Inga hydropower project
The Lobito Corridor
Key national road infrastructure projects
Government officials stressed that the agreement goes beyond the mining sector, encompassing defense, security, and governance reforms, positioning it as a long-term development partnership.
Monitoring and Governance Mechanisms
To ensure transparency and implementation, the Washington Agreement provides for the establishment of a joint steering committee, composed of five representatives from each country. The committee is expected to convene within 90 days of the agreement’s signing, with evaluations scheduled every three years.
In the event of disputes, Mukoko Samba noted that Prime Minister Judith Suminwa would serve as the arbitration authority, providing an institutional mechanism for dialogue and resolution.
Government Pushback Against “Misinformation”
Minister Patrick Muyaya echoed these clarifications, accusing critics of spreading misinformation based on emotional or partial readings of a public and technical document. The executive branch insists that the agreement in no way compromises Congolese sovereignty.
As debates continue within political and civil society circles, the government says it remains committed to transparency and to defending national interests while pursuing strategic international partnerships.

The government of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) has confirmed the temporary suspension of the national military spokesman, Major General Sylvain Ekenge Bomusa, following remarks deemed discriminatory against Congolese Tutsi.
According to official sources, the suspension order was signed on December 28, 2025, by the Chief of Staff of the Armed Forces of the DRC (FARDC), Lieutenant General Jules Banza Mwilambwe.
The decision follows comments made by Maj Gen Ekenge on December 27 during a broadcast on state television (RTNC), in which he stated that marrying a Tutsi woman required caution, alleging that interethnic marriages would not result in children. The remarks sparked strong reactions within the country and beyond.
The incident occurred amid accusations that the DRC authorities tolerate or support hate speech targeting Tutsi communities, particularly in the eastern provinces, where atrocities have been reported.
In announcing the suspension, Lt Gen Mwilambwe stated that the comments were not aligned with the values of the national army or state policy. However, critics have questioned the sincerity of this position.
Fanny Kaj Kayemb, a senior finance official within the AFC/M23 alliance, argued that the punishment was symbolic and that the remarks reflected a broader command policy.“The military spokesman does not speak on his own,” Kayemb said. “He follows the line approved by the high command. In the DRC, the Commander-in-Chief is President Félix Tshisekedi.”
Kayemb further described the suspension as an attempt to mislead public opinion and the international community, asserting that the remarks were consistent with what he called a discriminatory political orientation.
Observers also point to past cases, notably statements by MP Justin Bitakwira, who publicly targeted Tutsi, especially Banyamulenge, without facing domestic sanctions, despite being sanctioned by the European Union in 2022.
Additionally, on December 22, 2025, President Félix Tshisekedi received two Congolese nationals living in the United States, including Jean-Claude Mubenga, who has previously made violent statements against Tutsi. The meeting reportedly focused on national unity, drawing further criticism from human rights observers.
According to several sources, verbal attacks against Congolese Tutsi have coincided with airstrikes and drone attacks targeting areas inhabited by Banyamulenge communities since early 2025, raising concerns about ethnic targeting and escalating tensions.

The four men are charged with distributing explicit videos allegedly showing Yampano engaged in sexual activity with his girlfriend.

Ishimwe François Xavier, who represented himself, also criticized the pretrial detention order, stating that he had demonstrated he was not responsible for distributing the videos. Similar arguments were presented by Ishimwe Patrick (Pazzo Man), who said his detention resulted from the court’s failure to adequately assess his defense.
All defendants, including Kwizera Nestor (Pappy Nesta), requested to be released and prosecuted without detention, stressing that the 30-day detention ordered by the lower court was unjustified.
The prosecution, present at the hearing, defended the decision of the Kicukiro Primary Court, describing it as transparent and lawful, and asked the Nyarugenge High Court to uphold the detention while investigations continue. Prosecutors added that other suspects linked to the alleged distribution of the videos have also been arrested.
After hearing both sides, the presiding judge announced that the court’s ruling on the appeal will be delivered on January 5, 2026.

*FARDC Accuses Close Associates of President Tshisekedi of Leading Mobondo Armed Group
Kinshasa / Kwilu, DRC — December 28, 2025
The Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC) have accused individuals close to President Félix Tshisekedi of leading the Mobondo armed group, which has destabilized western parts of the country for nearly three years.
The allegations were made by Captain Anthony Mwalushay, spokesman for military operations in provinces including Kwilu, during a press briefing on December 28, 2025, following weekend clashes in which five FARDC soldiers were killed.
According to Capt. Mwalushay, some individuals who publicly claim allegiance to President Tshisekedi are allegedly involved in commanding Mobondo fighters, while others are said to be collaborating with armed groups operating in eastern DRC.
“We have information about some who are working with armed groups in the east,” Mwalushay said. “For Mobondo, the problem is that many of those who support it are often seen behind the President of the Republic. During the day they appear harmless, but at night they lead Mobondo.”
The FARDC spokesperson stated that during the recent fighting, 15 Mobondo fighters were killed, and several others were arrested. He added that some detainees allegedly identified political and institutional figures as being linked to the group.
“Our institutions are investigating before releasing names,” Mwalushay said. “If Kinshasa supports those who are secretly behind Mobondo for political reasons, we soldiers will continue to do our job with weapons.”
The Mobondo movement, largely composed of members of the Yaka ethnic group, emerged in June 2022, initially targeting the FARDC and the Teke community, primarily over land disputes.
Since then, Mobondo attacks have severely affected security in rural areas surrounding Kinshasa and in neighboring provinces, including Mai-Ndombe, Kwilu, Kongo Central, and Kwango, resulting in thousands of deaths and widespread displacement.
In recent months, Mobondo fighters have reportedly attempted to expand their operations into peri-urban areas of Kinshasa, including Maluku and Kingakati. The government, however, says it has taken strong preventive measures to prevent further deterioration of security in the national capital.

Conclusion: A Nation at a Strategic and Security Crossroads
The period between late December 2025 and early January 2026 has been defined by a complex interplay of strategic diplomatic maneuvers, persistent security volatility, and ambitious domestic reforms. As the DRC enters 2026, it faces a critical "refoundation" phase aimed at balancing international partnerships with the urgent need for internal stability.
1. Strategic Diplomacy and Sovereignty A central theme of this period was the government’s defense of the Washington Agreement, a strategic partnership with the United States. Despite domestic criticism alleging a "sell-off" of national resources, the executive branch maintained that the agreement is a technical framework for long-term development in sectors like energy (Grand Inga) and infrastructure (Lobito Corridor) without compromising sovereignty. Simultaneously, the DRC’s entry into the UN Security Council as a non-permanent member on January 1, 2026, signals its intent to internationalize the eastern crisis and demand greater respect for international law.
2. Persistent Security and Humanitarian Challenges The security situation in the eastern provinces remains the nation's most pressing concern. The government continues to condemn what it describes as a "war imposed" by Rwandan forces and the expansion of territory held by AFC/M23. While joint FARDC-UPDF operations have enabled the return of some displaced persons in Irumu, other regions like Masisi and Ituri face ongoing violence, resulting in the displacement of thousands and the paralysis of essential services like education.
3. Internal Governance and Social Unrest Domestically, the government is grappling with issues of governance and human rights. The suspension of military spokesman Major General Sylvain Ekenge over discriminatory remarks highlights the sensitive nature of ethnic tensions and hate speech within the country. Furthermore, a series of criminal acts—including the assassination of a journalist in Bunia and a professor in Kinshasa—has fueled public outcry and demands for better protection of civilians.
4. Economic Modernization and Health Trends On a positive note, the DRC has made strides in digital reform, launching electronic systems to modernize foreign trade and secure vehicle registration to combat fraud and increase state revenue. In public health, there is an encouraging decline in monkeypox cases, though new outbreaks of measles in Kasai-Oriental present a fresh challenge to the health system.
Final Outlook The start of 2026 finds the DRC caught between the hope of economic transformation and the reality of chronic conflict. The success of the "year of refoundation" will likely depend on the government's ability to translate its new international diplomatic standing into tangible security gains on the ground and to foster national unity through inclusive dialogue and transparent governance.
