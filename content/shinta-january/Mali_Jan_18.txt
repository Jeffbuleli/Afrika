*Mali | Former OCLEI President Moumouni Guindo Under Investigation

This audit is presented as a step toward improving the ethical standards of public officials in the fight against illicit enrichment.
*Mali | Record Mining Revenues Exceed 1,000 Billion CFA Francs
Bamako, January 12, 2026 - Mali has recorded mining revenues of 1,022 billion CFA francs paid to the State, far exceeding initial projections of 400 billion CFA francs. The announcement was made by the President of the Transition, General Assimi Goïta, during the New Year’s greetings ceremony addressed to Malian civil society.
The results reflect negotiations that led to the recovery of 761 billion CFA francs in arrears from mining companies, an increase of approximately 580 billion CFA francs compared to previous years. These funds were disbursed following a sector audit launched in 2023 and the adoption of a new Mining Code.
The reforms include indexing revenues to world gold prices, replacing fixed payments, and depositing revenues from gold sales in local banks, generating an estimated annual flow of 6 trillion CFA francs for the Malian banking system.

*Mali | Kal Akal 2025 Annual Report Highlights Worsening Human Rights Situation in Azawad
Kidal, January 13, 2026 - The Kal Akal Association’s 2025 annual report paints an extremely worrying picture of the human rights situation in the Azawad region. Based on corroborated and verified information, the report highlights widespread and systematic violence perpetrated against civilian populations.
A total of 573 cases of summary executions and airstrikes were recorded, indiscriminately targeting women, children, and the elderly, particularly in markets and during community gatherings. Additionally, there were 276 cases of abduction, enforced disappearance, and torture, particularly affecting community leaders, which has severely undermined social cohesion and local governance.
The humanitarian consequences are considerable: more than 210 mass displacements, destruction of livelihoods and vital infrastructure, as well as 117 cases of looting and destruction of civilian property. Environmental degradation and restricted access to essential resources further exacerbate the population’s vulnerability. Despite their limited number, the documented cases of sexual violence have lasting consequences for victims and their communities.
Kal Akal concludes that 2025 will be among the darkest years in Azawad’s recent history, marked by the collapse of the social fabric, chronic insecurity, and profound collective trauma. In response, the organization reaffirms its commitment to documenting violations, strengthening the protection of vulnerable populations, and intensifying its actions in 2026 to promote resilience and provide support for victims.

*Mali | Sky Mali Cancels Flights Amid Fuel Shortages and Rising Insecurity
Bamako, January 6, 2026 - Sky Mali, the national airline, has announced the cancellation of multiple flights and schedule changes over the coming days due to severe fuel shortages in the capital.
According to airline management, the situation is beyond their control, stemming from logistical constraints in the fuel supply chain. Fuel scarcity in Bamako has been described as a critical issue, with panic buying, black market surges, and prolonged shortages affecting residents and businesses alike.
The crisis has been aggravated by the resurgence of attacks by JNIM jihadists, who control key access points to the capital and had previously disrupted fuel deliveries between September and October 2025. Although a temporary lull occurred following a military council agreement in November 2025, the end of the truce on December 5, 2025, led to renewed attacks on fuel convoys and public transportation.
The capital is currently facing power outages lasting from hours to over a day, limited fuel availability, and widespread insecurity in surrounding areas, including Fana, Kassela, Zantiguila, Kita, and Bougouni.
Sky Mali has pledged to reschedule affected flights and keep passengers informed, emphasizing its commitment to safety, service quality, and transparency. Residents, meanwhile, are struggling with daily disruptions, describing their situation as forced resilience amid energy shortages and rising insecurity.

Conclusion
The situation in Mali is characterized by a deepening divide between official state rhetoric of "recovered sovereignty" and a deteriorating reality of economic paralysis and widespread insecurity. While the transitional government celebrates diplomatic milestones and record mining revenues, the civilian population faces a critical energy crisis and escalating violence from multiple armed factions.
1. Geopolitical and Diplomatic Shift
The government has intensified its "paradigm shift" toward absolute national sovereignty, focusing on the defense of Malian interests and the strategic choice of partners like China. This period was marked by:
Regional Integration: Strong commitment to the Confederation of Sahel States (AES), with Mali handing over the rotating presidency to Burkina Faso.
Diplomatic Assertiveness: The AES issued a sharp condemnation of US military actions in Venezuela, signaling a move toward a more vocal, anti-interventionist foreign policy.
Guinean Relations: President Goïta attended the inauguration of Mamadi Doumbouya, reinforcing regional alliances with neighboring transitional authorities.
2. Economic and Infrastructure Crisis
Despite the announcement of record mining revenues exceeding 1,000 billion CFA francs, the domestic economy is struggling with severe logistical failures.
Fuel and Energy Paralysis: Bamako is facing a "critical situation" with fuel shortages that have caused flight cancellations for Sky Mali and Air Burkina and forced residents to wait up to seven hours for gasoline.
Supply Blockades: JNIM's "economic war" has led to a five-month blockade of Nioro du Sahel and the destruction of tanker convoys, driving up the price of basic necessities and causing the closure of businesses like hotels in Ségou.
3. Escalating Conflict and Human Rights Concerns
The security landscape remains volatile, with active combat on multiple fronts:
Insurgent Activity: JNIM and ISIS continue to clash with each other and target the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) and Russian mercenaries (Africa Corps) across the Ségou, Timbuktu, and Sikasso regions.
Military Response: FAMa has increased its use of aerial surveillance and drone strikes, targeting terrorist bases and gold processing facilities in Kidal, Kayes, and Mopti.
Civilian Impact: Human rights reports from the Azawad region describe 2025 as one of the "darkest years," citing 573 cases of summary executions and airstrikes. In Dogofry, a recent airstrike on a market resulted in 54 civilian casualties, the majority being women and children.
4. Domestic Policy and Social Control
The government is tightening control over the national narrative through both celebration and censorship:
Media Restrictions: The banning of Jeune Afrique for "subversion" and "glorifying terrorism" highlights a declining tolerance for external criticism.
Nationalism: The 4th National Day of Restored Sovereignty was used as a platform to promote civic education and patriotism among the youth.
Internal Scrutiny: The audit of the former OCLEI president suggests a push for higher ethical standards, or at least a reorganization of the anti-corruption apparatus.
