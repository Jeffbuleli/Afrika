*Rwanda and Oman Sign Four Strategic Cooperation Agreements
Kigali/Muscat, January 12, 2026 — Rwanda and the Sultanate of Oman on Monday signed four cooperation agreements aimed at strengthening bilateral ties across key sectors, including transport, technology, and innovation, the Omani government announced.
The agreements were concluded following an official visit by a high-level Rwandan delegation to Oman, led by the Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, Amb. Olivier Nduhungirehe. The delegation also included the Minister of Technology and Innovation, Paula Ingabire, the Minister of Finance and Economic Planning, Yusuf Murangwa, and the State Minister for Infrastructure at the Ministry of Infrastructure, Eng. Jean de Dieu Uwihanganye.
According to Omani authorities, the accords focus on enhancing cooperation in land ports and freight transport, with both countries set to benefit from joint initiatives aimed at developing logistics and trade infrastructure.
Additional agreements cover the establishment of a large data warehouse, the development of IT infrastructure, and collaboration on projects related to artificial intelligence. Under these frameworks, Rwanda and Oman will jointly build capacity, share expertise, and develop strategies and regulatory frameworks to advance technological innovation.
A separate agreement on air transport will see Oman Air launch direct flights to Kigali starting in June 2026. The airline will operate two non-stop flights per week, marking a significant step in improving connectivity between the two countries.
The fourth agreement, signed between the Rwanda Development Board (RDB) and Oman Airports Company, focuses on air transport development through joint investments, infrastructure development, and the exchange of technical expertise.
Speaking after the signing ceremony, Amb. Nduhungirehe described the agreements as a major milestone in Rwanda–Oman relations, emphasizing their tangible benefits for citizens of both nations.
“Rwanda and Oman share geographical and strategic links through the Indian Ocean, particularly as Rwanda is a member of the East African Community,” he said. “We are committed to implementing these agreements and to signing more in the coming days, all with the goal of delivering real benefits to our people.”
Oman’s Minister of Transport, Communications and Information Technology, Eng. Saeed bin Hamoud Al Maawali, said the agreements align with Oman’s broader strategy to expand cooperation with East Africa.
He added that the accords not only reinforce the strong bilateral relations between Rwanda and Oman but also contribute to shaping a wider framework for cooperation between the Middle East and Africa.

*Burundi Faces Power Struggle Between President Ndayishimiye and CNDD-FDD Leader Ndikuriyo
Bujumbura, January 14, 2026 — Burundi is witnessing a renewed struggle for political power between President Évariste Ndayishimiye and Révérien Ndikuriyo, Secretary General of the ruling CNDD-FDD party. Recent statements by Ndikuriyo have fueled speculation over who truly holds authority in the country.
At a press conference on January 2, Ndikuriyo suggested that his influence in Burundi rivals, or even surpasses, that of the president, signaling a shift in internal party dynamics. Observers note that Ndikuriyo has increasingly criticized senior government officials, refuted economic claims, and asserted control over key national decisions, including border management with the Democratic Republic of Congo and Rwanda.
He publicly challenged the Minister of Finance, Alain Ndikumana, over alleged security threats involving a fertilizer company, and contradicted government assurances on petroleum shortages, stating the problem would persist until at least 2029. These statements contrast with President Ndayishimiye’s assurances that mining and other resources would strengthen the economy.
Political analysts highlight that tension escalated over control of the National Assembly leadership. Ndayishimiye opposed Daniel Gélase Ndabirabe continuing as head of deputies, while Ndikuriyo and CNDD-FDD leaders insisted he remain. This disagreement extended to cabinet formation, with Ndayishimiye appointing ministers loyal to him, including in defense, security, justice, foreign affairs, and finance. Ndikuriyo publicly rejects the legitimacy of this government, further demonstrating the power divide.
The ongoing military challenges in eastern DRC have also weakened Ndayishimiye’s perceived authority, bolstering Ndikuriyo’s position within the party. Analysts note that while both leaders share ideological goals—opposing foreign exploitation and criticizing government institutions—their methods and influence are sharply contested.
Historically, the CNDD-FDD has experienced recurring internal conflicts, including power struggles under former President Pierre Nkurunziza between 2005 and 2010. Ndikuriyo’s assertiveness reflects a continuation of this pattern, where political authority is often enforced through loyalty, intimidation, or the marginalization of rivals.
Despite Ndayishimiye’s public warnings to government staff about dual allegiances, observers question his ultimate ability to enforce authority over CNDD-FDD’s powerful party structures. The current power struggle underscores the fragility of institutional governance in Burundi and the enduring influence of party leadership over state affairs.

“Tshisekedi went to Lomé, although no one knows exactly what he said there. That is when it was announced that the DRC issue was now in the hands of the AU and that President Faure was the mediator,” Tombola said. “He went and told him, ‘You have taken matters into your own hands.’”

“In all this, you wonder whether those who sit at the table truly ignore the problem,” he said. “Is it necessary to move from city to city when the issue is already well known and no decisions are being implemented?”
He argued that the DRC continues to disregard agreements reached in Nairobi, Luanda, and Washington, adding that had they been respected, the crisis would have been resolved. He therefore described the Lomé initiative as “another Tshisekedi maneuver.”
“Tshisekedi is not implementing what was agreed in Washington and Doha, but is instead launching another process to keep people busy,” he said. “It is not because he believes Lomé will bring a solution, but because he is delaying what needs to be implemented.”

*Rwanda Introduces Demerit Point System for Drivers; Cross-Border Drivers Exempt
Kigali, January 14, 2026 — Rwanda is set to implement a new electronic demerit point system to monitor drivers’ behavior on the roads, but drivers of cross-border vehicles will not be affected.
According to Alfred Byiringiro, Senior Technical Advisor in charge of transport at the Ministry of Infrastructure, the system will apply only to driving licenses issued in Rwanda and those issued abroad but held by permanent residents. Drivers temporarily transiting through Rwanda, such as those transporting goods across borders, will not be registered in the system.
“We have drivers who transit Rwanda temporarily, making multiple trips with cross-border vehicles. It is not possible to register these people. However, if someone chooses to stay in Rwanda, their license is registered and the points system is applied,” Byiringiro explained.
The new law, pending presidential approval, will be accompanied by a ministerial decree defining traffic offenses, penalties, and the number of points deducted for each violation.
Key provisions of the system include:
Every licensed driver starts with 15 points, which are deducted depending on the severity of offenses.
Minor offenses may result in 2 to 6 points being deducted, while serious offenses such as hit-and-run may incur an additional six-point penalty.
Points are reset annually, with deductions applied for offenses committed during the year.
If a driver cannot be immediately identified, points will be assigned to the vehicle number and applied to the driver once identified.
Drivers with suspended or revoked licenses are prohibited from driving in Rwanda, even with a foreign-issued license. Suspended licenses are reinstated after the period expires, while revocations are carried out by a court upon request from road safety authorities.
Byiringiro noted that the government hopes neighboring countries will adopt similar systems to harmonize cross-border road safety regulations.

*UN Court Rejects Alfred Musema’s Request for Early Release
The International Residual Mechanism for Criminal Tribunals (IRMCT) has rejected a request for early release filed by Alfred Musema, a former Rwandan tea factory director convicted for his role in the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi.
Musema, 76, was arrested in Switzerland on February 11, 1995, and later transferred to the custody of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR). He was found responsible for leading and participating in multiple massacres in Kibuye Prefecture between April and May 1994, including attacks on Gitwa Hill, Muyira, Rwirambo Hill, Mataba, and the Nyakavumu Cave, where more than 300 Tutsis were killed and burned.
On January 27, 2000, the ICTR convicted Musema of genocide, extermination, and rape, sentencing him to life imprisonment. Although the Appeals Chamber later overturned the rape conviction in November 2001, it upheld his life sentence for other crimes against humanity.
After serving part of his sentence in Mali and later in Benin, Musema has made several attempts to secure early release. His first request in August 2019 was rejected because he had not yet served two-thirds of his sentence, based on international standards that equate a life sentence with 45 years of imprisonment. A subsequent appeal was dismissed in January 2020.
In January 2025, Musema again petitioned for early release, citing more than 30 years of detention, good conduct in prison, positive relations with fellow inmates, and acknowledgment that the genocide occurred. He also indicated that he intended to live with his family in Switzerland if released.
In a decision dated January 12, 2026, IRMCT President Graciela Gatti Santana acknowledged Musema’s good behavior and recognition of the genocide but ruled that these factors were insufficient given the gravity of his crimes. The court noted that Musema has neither confessed nor expressed remorse, a key requirement for early release.
The judge further emphasized that Musema was a powerful figure in Kibuye who could have prevented the killings but instead stood by and, in many cases, actively participated. Medical reports also indicated that he is in good health, eliminating humanitarian grounds for release.
As a result, Musema will remain in prison.

*AFC/M23 Explains Withdrawal from Uvira Amid Security Concerns
Uvira, DRC – January 21, 2026 – The AFC/M23 coalition, which has been fighting the DRC government, explained the reasons behind its withdrawal from Uvira town before the arrival of neutral forces.
Spokesperson Lawrence Kanyuka told researcher Dr. Bojana Coulibaly that the coalition had planned to leave a monitoring group in the city to maintain security, but mediators refused, demanding a full withdrawal. “There was politics behind it. We explained that we were not leaving Uvira to abandon residents, but they did not allow our group to stay,” Kanyuka said.
The coalition emphasized that it complied with all requirements, taking serious measures to ensure safety, and dissociated itself from any violence that occurred after its departure. Kanyuka expressed regret over atrocities committed by Wazalendo groups and DRC forces, including looting, destruction of homes and churches, and torture since January 18, 2026.
The withdrawal plan had initially been announced on December 16, 2025, with the coalition leaving by January 17, 2026, after waiting for the neutral forces to arrive.

Attempts by some residents to reach the highlands connecting Uvira, Fizi, and Mwenga Territories were blocked by Wazalendo fighters. While those in Kamanyola are reportedly safe, many remain worried for relatives still in Uvira.

*AFC/M23 Leader Says Withdrawal from Uvira Aimed at Supporting Peace Talks

By January 17, 2026, AFC/M23 had partially left the city, awaiting the arrival of neutral forces, after which Congolese troops and Wazalendo militias re-entered Uvira.

*Security Measures to Remain Until FDLR Dismantled, Ambassador Tells US Lawmakers
Washington, January 22, 2026 – Rwanda has reaffirmed that its security measures will remain in effect until the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) dismantles the FDLR terrorist group, according to a statement by Rwandan Ambassador to the United States, Mathilde Mukantabana, before the US House of Representatives Subcommittee on Foreign Affairs.
Addressing lawmakers responsible for monitoring developments in Africa, Ambassador Mukantabana traced the origins of the FDLR, noting that its members have committed crimes for over 31 years. She highlighted that the US State Department designated the ALiR/FDLR as a terrorist organization in 2001 following attacks on tourists, including Americans, in Uganda's Bwindi Impenetrable National Park in May 1999.
“Today, the FDLR remains active in eastern DRC, integrated into the operations of the DRC military. UN experts continue to document its activities. This genocidal group, which has existed since 1994, has not been defeated, has been preserved, protected, and supported by successive DRC governments,” Ambassador Mukantabana said.
The ambassador stressed that the DRC’s cooperation with the FDLR, particularly in military operations against the AFC/M23, poses a direct threat to Rwanda. She cited a recent incident in late January 2025, when the group bombed the town of Rubavu, only to be repelled by Rwandan forces.
“Our operations are solely aimed at protecting the Rwandan population by preventing cross-border threats. These measures are not related to seeking political advantage in the DRC or supporting any specific group,” she explained.
Ambassador Mukantabana underlined that Rwanda’s security measures are temporary and will be lifted once the DRC complies with its obligations under the Washington Accords signed on June 27, 2025, and ratified on December 4, 2025. These accords require the DRC to dismantle the FDLR and repatriate its fighters.
“Rwanda’s security measures will be reviewed as the level of threat decreases. Once a review confirms that the DRC government has acted to dismantle the FDLR, these measures will be lifted,” the ambassador assured US lawmakers.
The statement reinforces Rwanda’s long-standing position that cross-border threats from armed groups in eastern DRC directly impact regional security and that responsibility lies with the DRC to neutralize such threats.
*US Urges DRC to Stop FDLR Operations in Eastern Congo
Washington, January 22, 2026 – Senior US State Department officials have called on the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) to prevent the FDLR terrorist group from operating in its eastern provinces, citing ongoing security risks for Rwanda and the region.
Sarah Troutman, a senior US official overseeing African affairs, told lawmakers that the FDLR, responsible for multiple attacks and linked to the 1994 Rwandan genocide, must not be allowed to operate in eastern DRC.
“The FDLR is a proven problem. It should not be allowed to operate in eastern DRC,” Troutman said during a session of US lawmakers monitoring developments in Africa.
The remarks came amid concerns that the DRC army continues to cooperate with the FDLR, in violation of the Washington-brokered peace agreement signed on June 27, 2025, which called for the group’s dismantling and for the removal of Rwandan security measures along the border.
US Representative Sarah Jacobs noted:
“On the part of the DRC army, they are said to continue working with the FDLR, a group founded by the organizers of the Genocide in Rwanda in 1994, in violation of the peace agreement.”
Rwandan Ambassador to the United States, Mathilde Mukantabana, reiterated that Rwanda’s security measures remain in place because the FDLR continues to receive support from elements within the DRC and poses a direct threat to Rwandan security.
The Washington Accords require the DRC to dismantle the FDLR and repatriate its fighters. However, recent reports indicate ongoing support to the group. On November 28, 2025, the DRC reportedly sent the FDLR and allied groups three tons of ammunition and three tons of food, followed by additional ammunition on January 14, 2026, allegedly to fight against the AFC/M23 rebel group.
Troutman emphasized that the dismantling of the FDLR is critical to regional stability and urged the DRC government to take “concrete steps” to comply with its commitments.
“We remind the DRC government that dismantling the FDLR as agreed is very important, and we want to see more concrete steps taken,” she said.
The continued presence and support of the FDLR in eastern DRC underscore the fragile security situation and highlight the challenges facing the implementation of the Washington peace agreement.
