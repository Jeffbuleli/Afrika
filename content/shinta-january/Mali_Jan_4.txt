Bamako, December 22–23, 2025 — Bamako hosted the second session of the College of Heads of State of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES) on December 22 and 23, 2025, marking a decisive stage in the consolidation of the political, security, and economic integration process between Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger.

Looking ahead, President Goïta outlined ambitious integration projects under consideration, such as the creation of a confederal airline, the construction of a trans-Sahelian highway, and the establishment of an inter-confederal railway network, all aimed at strengthening connectivity, economic integration, and regional solidarity.

At the conclusion of the opening ceremony, the three heads of state reaffirmed their shared commitment to building a sovereign, united, and prosperous Sahel, capable of responding collectively to security challenges and advancing endogenous development models rooted in cooperation and mutual support.

*AES College of Heads of State Adopts Motion of Support Amid Terrorism and Sovereignty Challenges
Bamako, December 23, 2025 — The College of Heads of State of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES) has adopted a motion of support reaffirming its unity and determination in the face of persistent security threats and what it describes as external attempts to undermine the sovereignty of the Sahelian bloc.
Meeting in Bamako on the margins of the second session of the College of Heads of State, the leaders of Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger expressed deep concern over the continuation of multifaceted terrorist acts targeting the Confederation since its establishment. According to the motion, these attacks are aimed at weakening a sovereign political project founded on solidarity, collective security, and endogenous development for the peoples of the Sahel.
Condemnation of Terrorism and External Support
In a strongly worded statement, the College of Heads of State denounced what it described as a dangerous escalation marked by the open involvement and support of foreign states accused of sponsoring destabilization efforts. The leaders stated that such actions have crossed an “unacceptable threshold of indignity,” threatening not only individual member states but the Confederation as a whole.
The AES leadership condemned in the strongest terms all acts of subversion directed against the Republic of Mali, emphasizing that these attacks constitute an assault on the collective security and political cohesion of the entire Confederation of Sahel States.
Support for Niger in Uranium Dispute
The motion also addressed economic and legal challenges facing member states, particularly Niger, which the College says is being subjected to judicial harassment by the French company ORANO. According to the statement, this legal action is linked to Niger’s sovereign management of its uranium resources.
The AES Heads of State described this dispute as an attempt to instrumentalize judicial mechanisms in order to maintain control over strategic mineral resources. They characterized such practices as outdated forms of economic domination, incompatible with the principles of sovereignty and self-determination defended by the Confederation.
Reaffirmation of Sovereignty and Collective Resolve
In response to these security, political, and economic pressures, the College of Heads of State reaffirmed its total and unwavering support for the peoples of the Sahel. The leaders praised their resilience, dignity, and courage in the face of terrorism, external pressure, and economic challenges.
The motion reiterated the Confederation’s firm determination to defeat terrorism, strengthen collective defense mechanisms, and fully exercise sovereign control over natural resources, ensuring that they are managed exclusively in the interest of Sahelian populations.
Call for Unity and International Solidarity
Concluding the motion, the College of Heads of State expressed gratitude to peoples and nations around the world that have shown solidarity with the Sahel. The leaders also congratulated the citizens of the AES member states for their steadfastness in the face of adversity.
Finally, the motion called on Sahelian populations to further strengthen their commitment, mobilization, and cohesion, stressing that unity remains essential to confronting any threat to the sovereignty, stability, and future of the Confederation of Sahel States.
The adoption of this motion underscores the AES’s intention to present a united political front, combining security, economic sovereignty, and popular resilience as pillars of its long-term vision for the Sahel.
*Second Ordinary Session of the AES College of Heads of State Concludes in Bamako with Major Decisions on Security, Sovereignty, and Integration
Bamako, December 23, 2025 — The Second Ordinary Session of the College of Heads of State of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES) concluded on Tuesday in Bamako, marking a decisive milestone in the consolidation of the confederal project uniting Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger. The session was held under the chairmanship of General Assimi Goïta, President of the Transition of Mali and outgoing President of the AES.
The meeting brought together Captain Ibrahim Traoré, President of Burkina Faso, and General Abdourahamane Tiani, President of the Republic of Niger. The Heads of State conducted an in-depth review of the political, security, economic, and institutional situation of the Confederation, as well as the level of implementation of decisions adopted since its creation.
Validation of Ministerial Work and Strategic Roadmap
The College welcomed the Summary of Conclusions of the First Confederal Council of Ministers, held on December 20–21, 2025, in Bamako. The opening ceremony of that session was chaired by General Assimi Goïta, while the substantive work was led by Major General Abdoulaye Maïga, Prime Minister of Mali. The Heads of State commended the quality of the document and approved its recommendations.
They also examined and endorsed:
The General Report on the implementation of the AES Year-One Roadmap;
The Synthesis Report on negotiations between AES and ECOWAS.
Strengthening the Institutional Foundations of the Confederation
The College praised the leadership of President Assimi Goïta for the effective implementation of the decisions adopted during the first AES summit held on July 6, 2024, in Niamey, and for advancing the Confederation’s first-year roadmap.
Significant progress was acknowledged in strengthening the identity, visibility, and international recognition of AES, notably through:
The adoption of a flag, logo, anthem, and motto;
The issuance of confederal identity and travel documents;
The launch of AES Television and Radio;
The establishment of the Confederal Investment and Development Bank (BCID-AES), which is set to become fully operational.
On telecommunications, the College urged ministers to accelerate measures facilitating intra-AES communications and connectivity with neighboring states.
Adoption of Key Legal Instruments
To consolidate the three strategic pillars of the Confederation—security, diplomacy, and development—the College adopted several Additional Protocols to the AES Treaty, covering:
Defense and security cooperation;
Coordination of diplomatic action;
Coordination of development policies;
Organization of confederal parliamentary sessions.
The Council of Ministers was instructed to ensure effective implementation of these instruments.
Security, Terrorism, and Geopolitical Challenges
The Heads of State strongly condemned terrorist attacks targeting AES member states, denouncing the involvement of foreign state sponsors, including actors within the region. They reaffirmed their unwavering unity, solidarity, and mutual assistance, emphasizing that any aggression against one member state constitutes an attack against the entire Confederation.
The College also condemned:
Economic and media warfare;
Disinformation campaigns;
Judicial manipulation aimed at undermining AES states and seizing their natural resources.
Ministers were instructed to counter these actions through coordinated communication strategies and the dissemination of reliable, official information.
The Heads of State commended the joint operations of the defense and security forces, noting a significant reduction in terrorist operational capacity. They also welcomed the official presentation of the flag to the Unified Forces of the AES (UF-AES), marking their formal entry into service.
In the legal domain, justice ministers were encouraged to strengthen judicial cooperation within the Confederation and with external partners, in line with AES sovereignty and regional realities.
Diplomatic Coordination and International Positioning
The College highlighted the emergence of AES as a major geopolitical actor in the sub-region. It welcomed the growing practice of pre-consultations before international meetings, enabling the Confederation to speak with one voice and support the candidacies of its member states within international organizations.
Foreign ministers were encouraged to deepen cooperation between diplomatic missions and consular services, while the AES diaspora was recognized as a strategic partner in development and international advocacy.
Development, Economic Sovereignty, and Integration
The Heads of State welcomed progress in economic integration, particularly:
The implementation of the AES Contribution Mechanism (PC-AES);
The creation of the BCID-AES as a tool for endogenous and sustainable financing.
They called for accelerated harmonization of economic policies, including customs, mining, transport, industry, and investment frameworks, as well as the free movement of people and goods.
The College reaffirmed strong support for member states exercising full sovereignty over natural resources, condemning legal harassment linked to such sovereign decisions. Youth and women were identified as central actors in building a resilient and inclusive economic model.
Relations with ECOWAS
The College took note of progress in consultations with ECOWAS and approved the conclusions of the May 22, 2025 meeting in Bamako between AES foreign ministers and the President of the ECOWAS Commission. The Heads of State reiterated their commitment to good-faith negotiations, prioritizing the interests of West African peoples bound by historic ties.
Leadership Transition and Future Outlook
In a key decision, the College appointed Captain Ibrahim Traoré, President of Burkina Faso, as President of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES) for a one-year term.
In his inaugural address, President Traoré paid tribute to General Assimi Goïta’s leadership and outlined the priority areas for the AES’s second-year roadmap, emphasizing continuity, security consolidation, economic sovereignty, and deeper integration.
General Assimi Goïta, in his closing remarks, thanked his peers for their trust and support and congratulated President Traoré on assuming the confederal presidency.
Closing and Acknowledgements
The Heads of State agreed that the next AES summit would be held on a date to be determined through diplomatic channels. Presidents Traoré and Tiani expressed their deep gratitude to the Government and people of Mali for their hospitality and for the successful organization of the session.
The Bamako summit reaffirmed the AES’s ambition to evolve toward a federative framework, anchored in sovereignty, solidarity, and shared prosperity for the peoples of the Sahel.
*Mali Applies Reciprocity Principle in Response to U.S. Entry Restrictions
Bamako, December 30, 2025
The Government of Mali has announced the application of the principle of reciprocity in response to new entry restrictions imposed by the United States on Malian nationals, according to a statement released by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation.
The Ministry said it had taken note of the U.S. decision, announced on December 16, 2025, to tighten conditions for Malian citizens seeking to enter the United States. Malian authorities expressed regret that the measure was adopted without prior consultation, describing it as a far-reaching decision taken unilaterally.
Bamako also rejected the security justification cited by Washington, arguing that it does not reflect the current realities on the ground nor the efforts undertaken by Mali to ensure national and regional security. According to the statement, the government considers the decision unjustified, unfounded, and lacking a factual basis, suggesting that its true motivations lie beyond the stated security concerns.
In response, the Ministry announced that, with immediate effect, Mali will apply the same entry requirements to U.S. citizens as those now imposed on Malian nationals by American authorities, in line with international diplomatic practice.
Despite the dispute, the Ministry reaffirmed Mali’s commitment to maintaining balanced, constructive, and mutually respectful relations with all its international partners, while insisting on respect for Mali’s sovereignty and equal treatment of its citizens.
The move marks a further escalation in diplomatic tensions between Bamako and Washington, amid broader shifts in Mali’s foreign policy posture in recent years.
*Mali Suspends Entry of U.S. Nationals, Announces Limited Exemptions
Koulouba, January 2, 2026
The Government of Mali has clarified the implementation of its recent decision concerning the entry of United States nationals into Malian territory, following its press release issued on December 30, 2025, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation announced.
In a new statement released from Koulouba, the Malian authorities confirmed that, in line with the principle of reciprocity, the entry of U.S. nationals into the Republic of Mali has been fully suspended, subject to a limited number of exceptions.
According to the Ministry, the suspension does not apply to the following categories:
U.S. citizens who are permanent residents of Mali;
U.S. nationals holding a valid Malian visa;
Specific categories of U.S. citizens, including diplomats, athletes, and any individual whose presence in Mali serves duly established national interests.
The government emphasized that this decision is part of a broader diplomatic response to recent restrictions imposed by the United States on Malian nationals, which Bamako considers unilateral and unjustified.
The Ministry further stated that the measure will be regularly and appropriately reviewed, depending on developments in bilateral relations and the broader diplomatic context. Any changes to the policy will be officially communicated through established government channels.
This clarification comes amid heightened diplomatic tensions between Mali and the United States, as Bamako increasingly asserts its sovereignty and demands equal treatment for its citizens in international relations.
*Mali–Japan Bilateral Cooperation: TOKTEN Program at the Center of Strategic Exchanges
Bamako, December 30, 2025
The Minister for Malians Living Abroad and African Integration, Mossa Ag Attaher, on Tuesday received His Excellency Yukuo Murata, Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of Japan to Mali, for high-level discussions focused on strengthening bilateral cooperation between Mali and Japan.
The Japanese Ambassador was accompanied by Dr. Oussouby Sacko, a Malian architect based in Japan and Executive Director of the Universities of Tokyo, whose presence highlighted the growing role of the Malian diaspora in fostering academic, technical, and development cooperation between the two countries.

Minister Mossa Ag Attaher praised Japan’s continued commitment to human capital development and emphasized the strategic importance of the Malian diaspora in national reconstruction and sustainable development. He reaffirmed the government’s determination to create favorable conditions that encourage skilled Malians abroad to actively participate in the country’s transformation.
Ambassador Murata, for his part, reiterated Japan’s willingness to deepen cooperation with Mali, particularly in areas related to capacity building, higher education, and technical expertise transfer. He highlighted the TOKTEN program as a practical and effective framework for strengthening people-to-people ties and promoting mutually beneficial cooperation.
Dr. Oussouby Sacko underscored the importance of academic collaboration and innovation, noting that partnerships between Malian institutions and Japanese universities could significantly contribute to training a new generation of skilled professionals capable of addressing Mali’s development challenges.
The meeting reflects the shared ambition of Bamako and Tokyo to reinforce their bilateral partnership through knowledge exchange, diaspora engagement, and sustainable development initiatives, positioning the TOKTEN program as a key instrument in this cooperation.

*Mali Secures Waiver of Port Penalties for Cargo at Dakar Port
Bamako, December 29, 2025
The President of the Malian Shippers’ Council (CMC) has announced that a waiver of port penalties—including demurrage, warehousing, and container detention fees—has been granted to Malian importers and freight forwarders using the Autonomous Port of Dakar (Senegal).
According to the CMC, the measure was obtained from Senegalese port authorities following a ministerial mission to Senegal held from November 18 to 21, 2025, and is formalized under Note No. 002492-PAD dated November 25, 2025. The decision is part of ongoing efforts to support Malian shippers facing logistical and financial constraints.
As of December 22, 2025, significant progress has been made in clearing Malian cargo stored at DP World Dakar:
Initial stock (November 26, 2025): 2,086 containers
Remaining stock (December 22, 2025): 939 containers
The Malian Shippers’ Council has urged owners of the remaining goods to immediately proceed with their removal, noting that the list of concerned cargo is available at the CMC. The Council has also reaffirmed its readiness to assist importers and freight forwarders throughout the clearance process.
However, the CMC issued a firm warning, reiterating that the three-month deadline for cargo release—set to expire on February 20, 2026—is final and non-negotiable. No extension will be granted beyond this date, the statement emphasized.
The announcement, signed in Bamako on December 24, 2025, was officially published on December 29, 2025, with the Chairman of the Malian Shippers’ Council thanking stakeholders for their cooperation and understanding.

*Mali Drives Economic Sovereignty with Fisheries Expansion and Judicial Reform
Mali, December 2025
Mali is accelerating initiatives to strengthen economic sovereignty and social development. At the 4th Fisheries Days, authorities unveiled a comprehensive plan aimed at increasing the fisheries sector’s contribution to the national economy to over 4% of GDP, despite ongoing climate challenges. Key measures include:
Modernization of the cold chain to reduce post-harvest losses.

Resolve protracted land disputes more efficiently.
Digitize the land registry for faster and more transparent access to property information.

Local procurement to promote regional value chains.
Securing logistics corridors to stabilize prices and enhance trade efficiency.
Through these efforts—economic recovery, judicial modernization, and youth development—Mali is charting a path toward sustainable, sovereign growth and greater regional integration.

Koulikoro — December 31, 2025 ; Amid ongoing security challenges, the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) are placing renewed emphasis on the Rapid Intervention Battalions (BIR) to enhance their operational capacity against terrorist threats. In Koulikoro, the 4th graduating class of this elite unit is undergoing intensive training to prepare for deployment in complex operational environments.

Obstacle crossing and alignment techniques
Two-wheeled vehicle control
Navigation through chicanes
Emergency braking procedures
While the precise operational deployment of these units has not yet been disclosed, their missions are guided by three core principles: mobility, specialization, and proximity. Conclusion
The period from late December 2025 to early January 2026 was marked by a decisive drive toward the institutional and strategic consolidation of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES). The second ordinary session of the AES College of Heads of State in Bamako served as a landmark event, resulting in several critical achievements:
Political Leadership and Unity: The appointment of Captain Ibrahim Traoré as the new President of the AES for a one-year term signals a commitment to continued leadership and the rotation of responsibility among member states.
Economic Sovereignty: The official inauguration of the Confederal Investment and Development Bank (BCID-AES) stands as a major step toward financial autonomy, designed to support strategic infrastructure and reduce dependence on external mechanisms.
Defense and Security Integration: The formal entry into service of the AES Unified Forces (UF-AES) and the adoption of new protocols on defense cooperation highlight the bloc’s unified stance against regional terrorist threats and external subversion.
Information and Cultural Sovereignty: The launch of AES Television and the successful hosting of the Arts and Culture Biennial in Timbuktu underscore efforts to promote shared values, national cohesion, and informational independence.
Diplomatic Assertiveness: Mali's application of the reciprocity principle regarding U.S. entry restrictions and its reaffirmed partnership with Russia reflect a foreign policy increasingly centered on sovereignty and the transition toward a multipolar international order.
In summary, this period represents a "new phase" for Mali and its Sahelian partners—transitioning from the conceptualization of the AES toward the operationalization of its core security, economic, and diplomatic pillars. The Bamako summit reaffirmed the ambition to evolve toward a federative framework anchored in solidarity and shared prosperity, even as the region continues to face complex asymmetric threats and external pressures.
