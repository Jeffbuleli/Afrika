Mali, October 25, 2025
REPORT ON THE SITUATION IN MALI FROM 13 to 25 October 2025
Politics and Diplomacy
*Auditors and Inspectors Convene in Mali to Strengthen Governance Standards
Mali - October 17, 2025 - Mali has hosted the third edition of the West African Internal Audit Days, a key regional gathering for professionals in control, inspection, and internal auditing. Held from October 16 to 17, the event brought together experts from ten countries, including the eight member states of the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA), along with Guinea and Mauritania.
The conference served as a platform for knowledge exchange and capacity building, focusing on promoting good governance, harmonizing regional audit practices, and aligning them with international standards set by the Global Institute of Internal Auditing.
Organizers emphasized the importance of equipping members with the necessary skills and certifications to conduct audits that meet evolving international norms. “This is a valuable opportunity for professionals to share experiences and strengthen their role in ensuring accountability across African institutions,” said one of the coordinators.
The Union reaffirmed its commitment to promoting sound and effective management in both public and private sectors across the continent. The two-day event concluded with an audience granted by Mali’s transitional president, General Assimi Goïta, who welcomed participants and commended their efforts to enhance transparency and good governance in the region.
*Foreign Minister Abdoulaye Diop Sparks Outrage Over Remarks on Mali’s First President
Bamako - October 15, 2025 - Mali’s Minister of Foreign Affairs, Abdoulaye Diop, has found himself at the center of a political storm after making controversial remarks about the country’s founding president, Modibo Keïta, during a panel discussion at the Lomé Forum in Togo on October 12.
Speaking on the theme “Africa Facing Complex Security Challenges,” Diop reflected on Africa’s early post-independence leaders, saying:
“When we look at our former President Modibo Keita, when he came to power at independence, he was barely 33 years old... He was a schoolteacher, he was not prepared to lead a country.”
The comments quickly sparked outrage in Mali, where Modibo Keïta remains a revered symbol of independence and national pride. Civil society organizations, historians, and social media users accused the minister of disrespecting the country’s founding father, calling on him to issue a public apology.
Faced with mounting criticism, Diop issued a statement on October 14, seeking to clarify his remarks.
“I would like to reiterate my deep respect for President Modibo Keïta, an iconic figure of our independence and a model of integrity, patriotic commitment, and foresight,” he said. “My intention was in no way to diminish his legacy, but to magnify the model of success that he represents in discussions on the contemporary challenges of African leadership.”
Despite the apology, the incident has reignited debate about the tone and responsibility of Mali’s political leaders when addressing the country’s historical figures, with many urging greater sensitivity toward national heroes who shaped Mali’s post-independence identity.
* Former Malian Prime Minister Zoumana Sacko Dies at 75
Bamako - October 18, 2025 - Former Prime Minister Zoumana Sacko, a prominent figure in Mali’s political and economic history, has died at the age of 75. His passing was confirmed on October 15, and he was laid to rest on Saturday, October 18, in Bamako.
Sacko, who most recently served as president of the CNAS party, held several key positions throughout his career. A trained economist, he served as Minister of Finance under the regime of Moussa Traoré, before resigning in 1987 following a gold trading scandal that implicated the first lady at the time. His resignation marked a historic precedent - the first voluntary ministerial resignation in Mali’s history.
After the March 1991 coup, Sacko was appointed Prime Minister in April 1991, leading the transitional government until the election of Mali’s first democratically elected president in June 1992.
Beyond his national service, Sacko held senior international roles, including as a senior economist with the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). Colleagues and observers described him as a man of rigor, integrity, and deep patriotism.
In 2013, he ran for president of Mali, continuing his lifelong commitment to public service. His death marks the loss of one of Mali’s most respected technocrats and statesmen.
*Ségou Regional Council President Appears in Video After Abduction by Terrorists
Ségou - October 15, 2025 - Siaka Dembélé, president of the Ségou Regional Council, who was kidnapped by armed terrorists on October 3 while traveling to Bamako, has appeared in a video released by his captors.
The footage, which surfaced on October 15, shows Dembélé addressing the Malian authorities, pleading for his release. In the video, he states that his abductors are not demanding a ransom but are seeking a prisoner exchange, calling for the release of several detained militants currently held in Malian prisons.
Dembélé appeared visibly tired but alive, seated alongside two other hostages, one of whom was dressed in military uniform.
The Malian government has not yet issued an official statement regarding the video or the reported demands. The abduction adds to a growing list of security incidents in central Mali, where terrorist groups continue to target local officials, soldiers, and civilians despite ongoing counterterrorism operations.
*U.S. Embassy Warns Citizens Against Travel to Mali Amid Worsening Security and Fuel Crisis
Bamako - October 24, 2025 - The U.S. Embassy in Mali has issued a security alert urging American citizens to avoid traveling to the country, citing a deteriorating security environment compounded by nationwide fuel shortages.
In a statement published on its website on October 23, the embassy expressed concern over “persistent and unpredictable disruptions to gasoline and diesel supplies” affecting several cities, including the capital, Bamako. The shortages have led to long queues at fuel stations, impacted electricity availability, and raised fears of further instability.
“The U.S. Embassy warns that U.S. citizens should continue to avoid travel to Mali,” the alert stated. “Those already in the country should limit unnecessary movement and ensure their vehicles are fueled up, if possible.”
The embassy also noted that it is rarely able to provide emergency services outside the capital due to the current security conditions. Americans in Mali are advised to avoid demonstrations, not attempt to cross roadblocks, and remain vigilant at all times.
Just 24 hours after the embassy’s notice, the U.S. Department of State followed up with a separate alert, authorizing the departure of non-emergency personnel and family members of U.S. government employees from Mali due to escalating security risks.
Mali has been grappling with fuel shortages, insurgent attacks, and political uncertainty in recent months, factors that have increasingly strained daily life and heightened concerns among foreign diplomatic missions.
*UK Appoints Former Intelligence Official as New Ambassador to Mali
Bamako - October 23, 2025 - The United Kingdom has appointed Angus Philip McKee, a former intelligence official and veteran diplomat, as its new ambassador to Mali. McKee presented his credentials on Thursday to Mali’s transitional president, marking the start of his mission in Bamako.
A senior figure within the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), McKee previously served as head of the intelligence policy department and held several high-level diplomatic postings in some of the world’s most volatile regions, including Tripoli, Damascus, Erbil, and Beirut, as well as in London.
His appointment signals London’s intention to strengthen engagement with Mali amid the country’s ongoing security and political challenges. McKee is widely regarded for his expertise in security diplomacy and crisis management, honed over years of work in conflict-affected areas of the Middle East.
During the same ceremony, seven other newly appointed ambassadors also presented their credentials to the Malian head of state, underscoring Bamako’s continued efforts to maintain diplomatic relations amid a shifting geopolitical landscape.
2. Economy
*Fuel Crisis Paralyzes Bamako as Shortages Deepen Nationwide
Bamako - October 28, 2025 - Mali’s capital has been brought to a standstill as a severe fuel shortage grips the country, halting transportation and slowing nearly all economic activity. Since October 22, daily life in Bamako and other major cities has been paralyzed, with residents struggling to find fuel and move around.
Long queues have formed at gas stations across the capital, where frustrated motorists wait for hours - often in vain. Although most stations have run dry, they remain crowded day and night as desperate citizens hope for new deliveries.
To prevent violence amid growing tensions, the government has deployed the national police to service stations. However, officers have struggled to maintain order as tempers flare. In several cases, station managers and attendants have been arrested for refusing to sell fuel or for illegally selling it to customers with jerry cans.
Earlier this week, authorities banned the sale of fuel to anyone unable to present their vehicle and required those purchasing fuel in containers to obtain special permits from local authorities.
Despite these measures, tensions continue to rise. “If the situation continues like this, there will surely be a popular uprising,” said one Bamako resident, reflecting widespread frustration. “The shortage has gone on too long, and people are at their wits’ end.”
For over a month, entire regions have been cut off, with officials blaming terrorist groups for disrupting fuel convoys entering the country. The government’s efforts to restore supply have so far had little visible impact, leaving millions of Malians facing an increasingly desperate situation.
*Malian Prime Minister Calls Fuel Shortage an ‘Attempt to Destabilize’ the Country
Bamako - October 22, 2025 - As Mali continues to grapple with a crippling fuel crisis, Prime Minister Major General Abdoulaye Maïga has described the situation as a deliberate “attempt to destabilize” the country.
Speaking on Tuesday, October 21, during a meeting with members of the Malian Group of Oil Professionals (GMPP), Maïga expressed deep concern over the persistent shortage that has paralyzed transportation and disrupted economic activity across the country.
“What is happening to us is a change in modus operandi and an attempt at destabilization,” the Prime Minister said, referring to the recent blockade of hydrocarbon supplies by terrorist groups. The attacks have resulted in the deaths of several tanker drivers and the destruction of hundreds of fuel trucks along key supply routes.
Discussions with oil operators centered on finding concrete solutions, including enhancing the security of fuel convoys, compensating damages, and ensuring business continuity. The president of the GMPP, Ibrahim Touré, urged companies to remain resilient and to stand in solidarity with national authorities.
Maïga reassured operators of the government’s commitment to resolving the crisis. “Let us share our grief. Our thoughts go out to the civilians and soldiers who have paid the price in blood in this crisis,” he said. “Rest assured that there is no point of divergence - your problems are our problems.”
The fuel blockade has worsened the humanitarian and economic situation across Mali, driving up the prices of rice, cooking oil, and other essentials. Farmers, too, have been hit hard - many unable to harvest their rice fields due to the lack of fuel for agricultural machinery.
Authorities say they are intensifying efforts to secure transport corridors and restore fuel supplies, but for now, the crisis continues to test the resilience of both citizens and the government.
3. Social
*Teachers’ Strike Ends as Malian Government Agrees to Pay Withheld Salaries
Bamako - October 21, 2025 - After nearly three weeks of disruption, public school teachers in Mali have ended their strike, allowing students to finally return to class on October 20. The strike, which began on October 2, had paralyzed the education sector since the start of the new school year.
The teachers stopped work after the government withheld salaries from several of them, labeling them as “fictitious civil servants” following a nationwide physical verification of state employees. The inspection report had identified more than 30,000 fake workers across public institutions - a figure that sparked widespread controversy.
Amid growing tension, the government later acknowledged errors in the report and entered negotiations with the teachers’ unions. Following talks between the Ministry of National Education and the Ministry of Economy and Finance, an agreement was reached to pay the affected teachers by the end of the month.
As part of the deal, authorities have dispatched a new inspection mission across all regions to identify legitimate teachers who were mistakenly omitted from the first audit.
The resolution of the strike brings relief to thousands of students and parents, though it also highlights ongoing challenges in public sector management and payroll transparency within Mali’s civil service.
*Fuel Shortage Forces Universities in Mali to Suspend Classes
Bamako - October 25, 2025 - The ongoing fuel crisis in Mali has begun to paralyze the country’s education system, with several universities announcing the suspension of classes due to transportation difficulties.
Between Wednesday and Friday, at least three private universities declared a one-month suspension of academic activities, citing the inability of both students and professors to commute amid severe fuel shortages. The measure, they say, is temporary but necessary as campuses struggle to operate without reliable transportation or electricity.
“The situation has become untenable,” said one university administrator. “Our staff and students simply cannot reach the university. We hope this pause will allow time for the crisis to be resolved.”
The shutdowns in the private sector may soon extend to public universities, as calls for a nationwide suspension of classes continue to gain traction on social media and in local media outlets.
According to sources close to the Ministry of National Education, officials are considering a similar move, with an announcement expected later this week if fuel supplies do not improve.
The decision underscores the widening impact of the fuel crisis, which has already crippled transportation, markets, and basic services across the country, threatening to bring Mali’s education calendar to a halt.
4. Security
*Terrorists Attack Police and Gendarmerie in San, Steal Military Equipment
San - October 15, 2025 - The town of San in central Mali was the scene of a terrorist attack on the evening of October 14, when heavily armed assailants targeted the local police station and gendarmerie.
The attack began around 7 p.m., with gunmen firing for several minutes before taking control of both security buildings. After seizing the premises, the assailants set them on fire and fled the scene.
According to the regional governor, the terrorists were able to evade military surveillance by arriving in two police vehicles stolen during a previous attack on the police station in Bla a month earlier.
The following day, the group released a video showing military equipment looted during the San attack, including four vehicles, a motorcycle, and a cache of weapons.
No police or gendarmes were reported killed or injured during the incident. Analysts note that security forces did not engage the attackers and appear to have abandoned their posts, as no hostages were taken during the assault.
The attack highlights ongoing security challenges in central Mali, where terrorist groups continue to strike government installations with relative impunity.
*Malian Army Kills 20 Terrorists in Central Mali Offensive
Niono - October 13, 2025 - The Malian Armed Forces announced the neutralization of around 20 terrorists during a military operation in central Mali.
According to the General Staff, soldiers stationed in Bewani conducted an offensive reconnaissance mission in two villages within the Niono district. The operation resulted in the recovery of weapons, military equipment, and motorcycles used by the militants.
Military authorities emphasized their continued commitment to combating terrorism across the country, reassuring local populations of the army’s determination to secure central Mali against armed groups.
The operation is part of ongoing efforts by Malian forces to strengthen security in regions increasingly affected by terrorist activity and insurgent attacks.
*Terrorists Burn 41 Fuel Tankers Near Mali-Côte d’Ivoire Border, Killing Four Drivers
Bamako - October 18, 2025 - Terrorist attacks on fuel convoys continue to cripple Mali’s energy supply, with 41 tanker trucks burned near the border with Côte d’Ivoire on Friday, October 17. The assault left four drivers dead, trapped and burned inside their vehicles.
Since early September, armed groups have imposed a hydrocarbon blockade, making it nearly impossible for tankers transporting fuel from neighboring ports to reach Malian cities without military escorts. Despite air and ground operations by the Malian Armed Forces, terrorist groups continue to disrupt deliveries, exacerbating an already critical fuel shortage nationwide.
The targeted convoy, carrying a full load of fuel, was split in two by the attackers, who then set the tankers ablaze. The attack comes amid widespread disruptions: interurban transport has slowed, while cities including Bamako, Mopti, and San are facing electricity outages due to the lack of diesel.
In Bamako, the few gas stations with remaining fuel are overrun by crowds, highlighting growing public frustration. Authorities warn that the fuel crisis, now entering its second month, poses serious risks to both security and daily life across Mali.
*Terrorist Group Forces Women to Wear Hijab on Malian Roads
Bamako - October 19, 2025 - A terrorist group operating in Mali has issued a new directive forcing all women traveling on public roads to wear the hijab, further highlighting the growing influence of armed groups in the country.
On Friday, October 17, Bina Diarra, spokesperson for the Support Group for Islam and Muslims, released a video statement targeting road transport companies. He instructed that women not wearing the hijab must not be transported, warning that companies failing to comply would face bans on operations. He also announced the lifting of the suspension of Diarra Transport, Mali’s largest transport company, which had been forced to cease operations since early September.
According to the terrorist spokesman, the company’s operations would resume only if it ceased involvement in disputes with the army and ensured that women without hijabs were not transported. The decree applies to all road users, not just commercial transport companies.
On October 18 and 19, buses were observed enforcing the directive, with all women passengers wearing the hijab under the watch of armed men deployed for checks.
Security analysts say the measure underscores the widening power of terrorists across Mali, as they increasingly dictate social and legal norms, while the transitional government appears unable to enforce its authority. Observers warn that this trend is contributing to a gradual Islamization of public life in the country.
* Transitional Authorities Dismiss Three Senior Military Leaders in Mali
Bamako - October 22, 2025 - The transitional government of Mali has dismissed three senior military officials in a major shake-up of the country’s armed forces hierarchy. The decision, announced on Wednesday, October 22, was made by the Council of Ministers.
The officials replaced include:
General Keba Sangaré, outgoing Deputy Chief of Staff of the Armed Forces, succeeded by Brigadier General Elisée Jean Dao, formerly Mali’s ambassador to China and a former Chief of Staff of the National Guard (2020-2022). Dao is considered a highly respected officer with close ties to the transitional president.
General Nouhoum Ouattara, outgoing Director of Military Intelligence, replaced by Brigadier General Sambou Minkoro Diakité, who previously led the National Gendarmerie.
Brigadier General Harouna Samaké, outgoing Chief of Staff of the Army, succeeded by Brigadier General Toumani Koné.
Observers say the reshuffle aims to boost morale among troops at the frontlines. For several months, militants have been able to carry out attacks and besiege military camps for extended periods, highlighting operational weaknesses.
The three new leaders are regarded as experienced and respected figures, and their appointment is expected to galvanize soldiers and reinforce the army’s efforts to secure Malian territory against ongoing terrorist threats.
*Terrorists Impose Blockade on Léré, Forcing Residents to Flee
Léré - October 25, 2025 - The town of Léré in Mali’s Timbuktu region has come under a terrorist-imposed blockade, according to a statement issued on October 24 by a spokesperson for the Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM). The town, located 70 kilometers from the Mauritanian border, has faced repeated attacks by armed groups since 2012.
The group warned residents that anyone wishing to leave must do so before midnight on October 25, raising fears of a humanitarian crisis. Léré is considered a strategic hub for controlling the border with Mauritania, where terrorists maintain hideouts, and serves as a key transit point for essential goods bound for regions such as Mopti and Timbuktu.
Eyewitnesses report that the blockade is fully enforced, with terrorists issuing death threats and imposing Sharia law, including restrictions on women who do not wear the hijab. Over the course of Saturday, vehicles carrying fleeing residents were seen leaving the town, while those unable to leave remain trapped under tense and dangerous conditions.
The blockade underscores the continuing security challenges in northern Mali, where armed groups increasingly control key towns and disrupt the movement of goods and people, heightening both humanitarian and economic risks in the region.
*Army Camp in Kidal Targeted by Kamikaze Drone Attack
Kidal - October 26, 2025 - The Azawad Liberation Front released a video on Saturday showing a kamikaze drone striking the former UN base in Kidal, now occupied by the Malian army and Russian mercenaries from Africa Corps. In a social media statement, the group claimed that a “strategic objective had been destroyed”, though the Malian Army General Staff has yet to comment on the incident.
Kidal, where the 2012 Tuareg rebellion began, was under the control of separatist rebels for over a decade. The Malian army and Wagner Group partners regained control of the city in November 2023, but it remains frequently targeted by insurgent attacks, with drones increasingly used in assaults.
The Malian transitional government has accused Algeria and Ukraine of arming the Tuareg rebels, claiming foreign support enabled attacks that killed both Malian soldiers and Russian mercenaries in Tizawatene in 2023. Analysts note that the continued use of drones by rebels poses a significant challenge to securing Kidal, highlighting the ongoing volatility in northern Mali.
The attack underscores the persistent instability in the region, where historical grievances, foreign involvement, and modern technology combine to make military control increasingly difficult.
Conclusion
Between October 13 and 25, 2025, Mali faced a deepening crisis marked by a severe fuel shortage that brought life in Bamako and other major cities to a near standstill. Described by Prime Minister Abdoulaye Maïga as an “attempt at destabilization,” the shortage stems from a sustained terrorist blockade of hydrocarbon supplies. Militant attacks, including the burning of 41 tanker trucks near the Côte d’Ivoire border, which claimed four drivers’ lives, have compounded the disruption.
The crisis has had immediate economic and social impacts:
Urban and interurban transport has slowed to a crawl.
Prices of rice, cooking oil, and other essentials have surged.
Farmers lack the fuel necessary to harvest crops, threatening food security.
At least three universities suspended classes, leaving students and professors stranded.
The economic paralysis is compounded by a deteriorating security environment. Terrorists have attacked police and gendarmerie posts in San, imposed a blockade on Léré, and enforced a directive requiring all women using road transport to wear the hijab-a rule that the population appears to be obeying. Observers note that the transitional government increasingly struggles to assert authority over these areas.
In response, the Malian government reshuffled its top military leadership, dismissing three senior officers, including the Deputy Chief of Staff of the Armed Forces and the Director of Military Intelligence, in an effort to restore control and morale.
The crisis has also drawn international attention, with the United States advising citizens to avoid travel to Mali and authorizing the departure of non-emergency personnel and families of U.S. government staff due to escalating security risks.
The combined fuel shortages, economic paralysis, and growing terrorist influence signal a period of heightened vulnerability for Mali, underscoring the urgent need for coordinated security, economic, and humanitarian responses.
