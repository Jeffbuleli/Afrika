*Rwanda, U.S. Deepen Cooperation on Nuclear Energy and Critical Minerals
Rwanda and the United States have strengthened their partnership in the fields of nuclear energy, mineral development, and energy innovation following high-level talks held in Washington, D.C.
Prime Minister Dr. Justin Nsengiyumva met with U.S. Secretary of Energy Chris Wright to discuss new areas of collaboration, including the peaceful use of nuclear energy, natural gas development, and the exploration and processing of key minerals essential to global clean energy transitions.
Dr. Nsengiyumva was in the United States to attend a dinner organized by the International Conservation Caucus Foundation (ICCF), where discussions also touched on sustainable energy policies and climate resilience.
The meeting builds on Rwanda’s growing engagement in the global nuclear energy community. In September 2025, Dr. Fidel Ndahayo, Director General of the Rwanda Atomic Energy Board (RAEB), announced that Rwanda is prioritizing several projects under the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) framework to expand the peaceful use of nuclear technology.
Among these initiatives is the “Rays of Hope” project, designed to enhance the use of nuclear energy in medicine-particularly in diagnosing and treating cancer and other serious diseases. The “Atoms4Water” project focuses on using nuclear science to monitor and improve water quality, as well as to locate underground water sources. Another program, “ZODIAC” (Zoonotic Disease Integrated Action), aims to strengthen early detection and response to zoonotic diseases that can spread from animals to humans.
To support these ambitions, Rwanda plans to establish the Center for Nuclear Science and Technology (CNST) in Bugesera District, near the Rwanda Institute for Conservation Agriculture (RICA). The facility will serve as a hub for nuclear research, training, and innovation.
The country also intends to adopt Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)-a new generation of compact, efficient nuclear power plants. According to Dr. Ndahayo, Rwanda has already committed to training 234 nuclear energy experts by 2028, paving the way for the installation of its first SMR plant by 2030.
In addition, Rwanda is preparing to deploy Positron Emission Tomography (PET) scanners to enhance nuclear medicine services in local hospitals. To ensure safety and compliance, the government continues to strengthen its independent nuclear regulatory authority, responsible for oversight and risk management in the sector.
Dr. Ndahayo emphasized that these initiatives are part of Rwanda’s long-term strategy to promote science-driven industrialization, encourage technology transfer, and position the country as a regional model in clean energy innovation.
For Rwanda to achieve its Vision 2050-becoming a high-income, developed nation-it will need to generate at least five gigawatts of electricity. Nuclear energy, he said, will play a key role in meeting that target by providing reliable and sustainable power.
Electricity generated from nuclear energy relies on the process of nuclear fission, where uranium atoms are split to release heat. This heat is then used to produce steam, which drives turbines to generate electricity inside a nuclear reactor-the heart of the power plant.
Through continued collaboration with international partners such as the United States and the IAEA, Rwanda aims to harness the potential of nuclear science to power its development, improve healthcare, and advance technological innovation across multiple sectors.
*Rwanda Reaffirms Link Between Peace, Development, and Environmental Protection
Rwanda’s Prime Minister, Dr. Justin Nsengiyumva, has emphasized that lasting peace and sustainable development are inseparable from environmental protection, urging nations to safeguard their natural ecosystems from terrorism and illegal activities.
Speaking during a high-level meeting hosted by the International Conservation Caucus Foundation (ICCF) in the United States, Dr. Nsengiyumva underscored the importance of placing environmental conservation at the heart of national development strategies.
“Peace and development cannot be sustained without continuing to protect the environment, on which our people depend for their livelihoods,” he said. “That is why Rwanda has placed environmental protection at the forefront of its development strategies for nearly three decades.”
The ICCF meeting gathered over 200 participants, including members of parliament, international leaders, conservationists, representatives of investment firms, NGOs, and public figures. Since its launch in 2006, the forum has hosted notable figures such as King Charles III, Jeff Bezos, Queen Noor of Jordan, Prince Albert II of Monaco, and former U.S. Presidents George H. W. Bush and Bill Clinton.
Rwanda’s Conservation Success
Dr. Nsengiyumva highlighted Rwanda’s post-Genocide recovery efforts, particularly in biodiversity restoration and wildlife conservation. He pointed to the country’s flagship annual event, Kwita Izina, which celebrates the naming of baby gorillas - a practice that has strengthened conservation awareness and boosted tourism.
“There was a time when mountain gorillas were so endangered that it seemed they might disappear completely. Today, their number has grown to over 1,000 - a fourfold increase - and they are no longer considered endangered,” he noted.
Rwanda’s partnership with African Parks to manage Akagera National Park has also yielded remarkable results. Since the collaboration began 15 years ago, the park’s animal population has grown from fewer than 5,000 to nearly 12,000. The country also successfully reintroduced lions in 2015 and later brought back black and white rhinos, a feat achieved with the support of the Howard G. Buffett Foundation.
Dr. Nsengiyumva stressed that such conservation successes would not be possible without the involvement of local communities, who serve as the first line of defense for protected areas.
Call for Regional Security and Cooperation
The Prime Minister cautioned African nations against allowing national parks to become safe havens for terrorist or criminal groups, warning that such activities undermine both peace and environmental sustainability.
“We cannot allow African parks to be used by terrorist groups and criminal networks to create insecurity,” he asserted.
He also expressed appreciation to the United States, acknowledging the role of former President Donald Trump in facilitating peace efforts between Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), saying that continued cooperation would help preserve regional ecosystems.
Dr. Nsengiyumva concluded by reaffirming Rwanda’s commitment to conservation and announcing plans to establish the African Conservation Academy in Akagera National Park, an initiative aimed at building continental expertise in biodiversity protection and sustainable development.

Ambassador Vincent Karega, Rwanda’s first resident envoy to Algeria, officially presented his credentials to President Abdelmadjid Tebboune, marking a significant step in strengthening bilateral ties between the two countries. The announcement was shared on October 28, 2025, via the X platform.

*Rwanda to Launch $70 Million Digital Identity Card Project
The Ministry of Technology and Innovation has announced that the Digital Identity Card Project will cost over $70 million (more than 101 billion Rwf) to complete. The initiative, which began in 2023, aims to modernize the national identity system and expand access to services for all residents.
Minister of Technology and Innovation, Ingabire Paula, stated that the World Bank has contributed over $48 million (69 billion Rwf) toward building the system but emphasized that additional funding is required to fully implement the project.
“There is a need to build a system and update services that were previously provided to citizens based on their identity cards, so they can start using the electronic identity card,” Minister Ingabire said in an interview with RBA.
The electronic ID card will be issued not only to Rwandans aged 16 and above, but also to foreign residents, refugees, asylum seekers, unaccompanied minors, temporary residents, migrants, and stateless people.
Unlike the traditional ID, the new card will have a QR code that can be carried digitally on a phone or computer, alongside a unique token number to access personal information. This will eliminate the need to carry a physical card when requesting services such as opening bank accounts.
The National Identity Agency (NIDA) has already registered over 3,300 people through a pre-enrollment platform, designed to verify identities and correct errors before issuing the electronic cards. The program officially launched on August 7, 2025, at the Kigali International Fair and will continue at the Indangamirwa Church, before expanding nationwide to districts including Huye, Gisagara, and Nyanza.
Two centers have been set up in each sector, staffed by trained personnel to assist residents with identity verification and documentation, including fingerprints and facial recognition. Currently, 1,144 workers have been trained, with plans to expand to 2,500 by May 2026.
The new ID card will reduce the data collected from 27 categories to nine essential data sets, including names, parents’ names, contact information, and place of birth, ensuring a complete and streamlined identity system.
Minister Ingabire added that by May 2026, Rwanda is expected to become the first African country to implement a fully operational digital ID card, ahead of other nations still in the development phase.
The project has been allocated 5.4 billion Rwf for 2024/2025 and 12.2 billion Rwf for 2025/2026, with additional donor support being sought to ensure nationwide rollout.
This digital ID system is expected to enhance access to public and private services, improve efficiency, and strengthen national identity management across Rwanda.
*Rwandan Heritage Abroad Still Predominantly in Belgium and Germany, Says Cultural Council Chair
KIGALI - Ambassador Masozera Robert, Chairperson of the Cultural Council, has revealed that over 90% of Rwandan cultural heritage remains in countries that colonized Rwanda, notably Belgium and Germany. He made the remarks on October 27, 2025, during Rwanda’s celebration of the International Day for the Protection of Audiovisual Heritage.
Celebrated for the fifth time in Rwanda, this year’s theme was “Heritage in audio and visual, a channel for the culture and history of Rwandans.” The day provides an opportunity to reflect on the significance of audiovisual materials in preserving and showcasing the diversity of cultural histories worldwide.
Amb. Masozera emphasized the importance of safeguarding Rwanda’s history, noting that audio and visual heritage requires careful preservation. “The history we have here contains a great wealth of documents including photographs, images and sounds that show how Rwanda has developed, but we still need to receive more because what we have is not enough,” he said.
He reiterated that a large portion of Rwanda’s cultural assets were removed during colonial times, stating, “The colonialists, especially Belgium, exploited us… up to 90% of Rwanda's cultural heritage.”
Recent efforts have seen some materials return to Rwanda. In 2021, Belgium, through a partnership with the Royal Museum for Central Africa, donated heritage items including audio recordings such as poems from 1950 to 2007. The previous year, Belgium also returned over 10,000 documents detailing research on Rwanda’s mineral locations dating back to 1923.
The celebration underscored the ongoing need to preserve and recover Rwanda’s rich audio and visual heritage, which reflects the nation’s history, culture, and identity.
*Habimana Kizito Among 67 EAC Observers Deployed for Tanzania Presidential Elections
DAR ES SALAAM - Rwanda’s Habimana Kizito, Vice-Chairperson of the National Electoral Commission, is among 67 observers deployed by the East African Community (EAC) to monitor the Tanzania presidential elections scheduled for October 29, 2025.
The deployment follows an invitation from the Tanzanian government and aligns with directives from the EAC Council of Ministers, which tasked the EAC Secretariat with observing elections across member states to promote democracy, peace, and regional stability.
Dr. Speciosa Wandira Kazibwe, former Vice President of Uganda (1994-2003) and Director of EAC election observation missions, emphasized the importance of fair elections, stating, “The conduct of fair and transparent elections is the key to democracy and regional unity.”
The observer team includes experts from all eight EAC member states, members of the EAC Legislative Assembly (EALA), representatives of electoral and human rights commissions, civil society organizations, and EAC offices.
Observers will monitor all stages of the electoral process, including campaigning, voting, vote counting, and post-election reporting. Their work is guided by Article 6(d) of the EAC Treaty, which obliges member states to uphold good governance, democracy, human rights, equality, proportionality, integrity, and respect for constitutional processes.
*RDC Begins Accepting FDLR Surrenders Amid Tensions with AFC/M23
KINSHASA, DRC - The Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC) have begun receiving surrenders from the Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda (FDLR), signaling a potential end to military operations against the group, which has initiated a process of disarmament.
FARDC spokesperson, Major General Sylvain Ekenge Bomusa, called on FDLR fighters on October 10, 2025, to surrender either to Congolese authorities or to the UN peacekeeping mission, MONUSCO. He warned that those refusing to comply would face military action, in accordance with the Washington Peace Agreement signed in June.
During the third meeting of the Joint Rwanda-DRC Security Council in Washington on October 21-22, officials from both countries, alongside U.S. and observer representatives, confirmed that while most FDLR fighters had yet to disarm, some had started the process, according to General Ekenge.
In an interview with Deutsche Welle journalist Wendy Bashi, General Ekenge stated that “the FDLR have followed the instructions of the Congolese army.” He added that the group is currently located in areas controlled by the AFC/M23 coalition in Rutshuru territory, North Kivu, which has hindered their ability to reach Congolese authorities or MONUSCO to formally surrender.
“Others have prevented them from laying down their arms,” General Ekenge said. “They want to surrender, but obstacles in AFC/M23-controlled zones have delayed this process.” He added that FARDC would continue its campaign to facilitate the group’s surrender.
The general acknowledged that the reasons behind AFC/M23’s obstruction remain unclear but reaffirmed the army’s commitment to encouraging FDLR fighters to disarm. “We are carrying out the tasks assigned to us. We have conducted a campaign within the FDLR to urge surrender, and we will continue,” he said.
Tensions persist over the implementation of the Washington Agreement, which includes provisions for information sharing on FDLR operations and affiliates. Rwanda and the DRC had agreed to exchange intelligence on potential threats, military capabilities, locations of units, and weaponry.
In July 2025, Rwanda’s Foreign Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe reported that some FDLR combatants had been recruited into the Congolese army and Wazalendo, with their positions known to Kigali. “We have all the information on the FDLR, including their locations and units. They cannot feign ignorance,” Nduhungirehe said.
Rwanda’s Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC), when listing 25 Rwandans as terrorists on October 25, indicated that top FDLR leaders remained in Walikale as of October 14. These include FDLR president Gaston Iyamuremye, alias Lt. Gen. Byiringiro Victor, and group leader Pacifique Ntawunga, alias Gen. Omega, both residing in the Buhaya neighborhood of Walikale.
The unfolding situation highlights the complex dynamics between the FDLR, FARDC, and AFC/M23, raising questions about the effective disarmament of the group and the security situation in North Kivu.
*Six New Senators Sworn In as Rwanda Welcomes Fresh Appointments and Returning Members
KIGALI, Rwanda - On October 24, 2025, the Rwandan Senate welcomed six new members, including four presidential appointees and two elected representatives, strengthening the upper chamber with a mix of experience and fresh perspectives.
Among those appointed by President Paul Kagame were Dr. Uwamariya Valentine and Alfred Gasana, while Prof. Dusingizemungu Jean Pierre and Uwizeyimana Evode began their second five-year Senate terms. The two senators elected by the Consultative Forum of Legal Political Parties were Dr. Frank Habineza and Alphonse Nkubana.
Dr. Uwamariya Valentine, born May 14, 1971, in Nyamasheke, holds degrees in Organic Chemistry from the former National University of Rwanda, a Master’s in Environmental Engineering and Water Technology from the Netherlands Institute of Technology, and another Master’s from the University of the Witwatersrand, South Africa. She has over 18 years of teaching and research experience, previously serving as Dean of the School of Science at the University of Rwanda and as Vice-Chancellor of Rwanda Polytechnic. Most recently, she held leadership positions in the Ministries of Education, Gender and Family Development, and Environment.
Alfred Gasana, former Member of Parliament and head of the Political Affairs Committee, has held key government roles, including Minister of Security and Rwanda’s Ambassador to the Netherlands. He previously led the Internal Security Department at the National Intelligence and Security Service (NISS).
Prof. Dusingizemungu Jean Pierre, who served as the head of IBUKA for a decade, has a long history in education and public service. Born in 1963, he joined the RPF Inkotanyi in 1992 and became Bourgmestre of Gahini in 1994. He first joined the Senate in 2020 and has now been reappointed for a second term.
Dr. Frank Habineza, born February 22, 1977, in Mityana, Uganda, served as a Member of Parliament from 2018 to 2024, including as Vice Chair of the Human Rights Committee. He has an extensive academic background, holding Master’s degrees in War Studies, Governance, and Democracy and Human Rights, and is currently studying law at the University of Kigali.
Alphonse Nkubana is an entrepreneur and politician with over 25 years of experience in business, governance, and institutional development. Founder of Positive Thinking Ltd, he has served as General Secretary of the PSP and as Spokesperson of the Federation of Legal Political Organizations in Rwanda. Nkubana holds a Master’s in International Relations from Mount Kenya University and a Bachelor’s in Accounting from Kigali Independent University.
Uwizeyimana Evode, born in 1970, began his government career in 2016 as Secretary of State for the Constitution. He participated in the 2015 constitutional review commission and first joined the Senate in 2020. He was reappointed for a second term and sworn in alongside his colleagues on October 24.
Senators in Rwanda are appointed or elected for five-year terms, renewable once, and this cohort is expected to bring a blend of expertise in governance, education, security, law, and entrepreneurship to the legislative body.
*Minister Nduhungirehe Commends France for Convicting Dr. Munyemana for Genocide Crimes
KIGALI, Rwanda - Rwanda’s Minister of Foreign Affairs and Cooperation, Ambassador Olivier Nduhungirehe, has expressed gratitude to the French judiciary for convicting Dr. Munyemana Sosthène of his role in the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi.
Dr. Munyemana, a former gynecologist at Butare Hospital affiliated with the former National University of Rwanda, was a respected figure in Tumba Sector and known as a friend of local leaders. However, he was implicated in crimes committed during the genocide, including imprisoning Tutsi victims before they were killed. He claimed his actions were intended to protect them from the Interahamwe militia.
On the night of October 23, 2025, the Appeals Chamber of the French People’s Court upheld his conviction for genocide, crimes against humanity, and complicity in genocide, sentencing him to 24 years in prison. The sentence confirmed the ruling from the trial chamber in December 2023, which Dr. Munyemana had appealed, seeking acquittal and a reduced sentence.
Minister Nduhungirehe praised the decision, stating, “The French judiciary acted with due diligence to provide justice to the victims of the Genocide against the Tutsi, as the Paris Court of Appeal confirmed yesterday when it sentenced Dr. Munyemana Sosthène to 24 years in prison for genocide, crimes against humanity, and complicity in genocide.”
Since 2014, several Rwandans residing in France have been convicted for their involvement in the 1994 genocide. Pascal Simbikangwa, former head of Rwanda’s intelligence service, was sentenced to 25 years in March 2014. In July 2016, Ngenzi Octavien and Tito Barahira, former commune leaders implicated in massacres in Kabarrondo, received life sentences.
In July 2022, Laurent Bucyibaruta, former Prefect of Gikongoro, was sentenced to 20 years for genocide and crimes against humanity. In June 2023, Hategekimana Philippe, a former gendarme involved in the Butare genocide, was sentenced to life imprisonment, with the sentence upheld on appeal in November 2024.
The conviction of Dr. Munyemana marks another step in holding perpetrators accountable and underscores France’s commitment to prosecuting international crimes.

*Rwanda and DRC to continue talks on FDLR dismantling and regional security
WASHINGTON, United States - Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) have agreed to reconvene next month to discuss joint security measures targeting the FDLR terrorist group, following the third meeting of the Joint Security Coordination Mechanism (JSCM) in Washington on October 21-22, 2025.
The meeting, which reviewed progress in implementing the June peace agreement, focused on strategies to dismantle the FDLR, exchange intelligence, and assess potential security challenges posed by the group. The United States Department of State reported that discussions included plans to encourage FDLR fighters to lay down their arms and be transferred to Rwanda before military operations commence to neutralize remaining threats.
The fourth JSCM meeting is scheduled for November 19-20, 2025, where representatives from both countries will evaluate progress and address emerging obstacles.
On October 10, the DRC military called on all FDLR fighters to surrender either to the Congolese government or to the United Nations peacekeeping mission (MONUSCO), a step meant to facilitate their repatriation to Rwanda. At the time of the third JSCM meeting, however, FDLR fighters had not yet complied, highlighting the ongoing challenges of implementing the peace accord.
Military operations against the FDLR are further complicated by the persistent presence of the AFC/M23 coalition in North and South Kivu provinces, where the DRC army has launched intensive strikes, including drone attacks using CH-4 unmanned aerial vehicles.
Both Kigali and Kinshasa reaffirmed their commitment to regional security, emphasizing that the dismantling of the FDLR remains a priority but will require careful coordination and sustained efforts in the coming months.

*Rwanda Targets Industrial Growth to Boost Exports and Strengthen Currency
The Minister of Trade and Industry, Prudence Sebahizi, has outlined new strategies aimed at boosting Rwanda’s industrial output and reducing the trade deficit.
Speaking to the Rwanda Broadcasting Agency (RBA), Minister Sebahizi emphasized that supporting investors and closely monitoring industrial projects will be key to achieving these goals. “If we increase the productivity of our products, it reduces the gap between what we export and what we import. When that gap narrows, our currency becomes more valuable,” he said.
The minister explained that the government is prioritizing the creation of favorable conditions for investors through the establishment of industrial zones and the development of essential infrastructure such as roads, water, and electricity. “We monitor investors on a daily basis to ensure progress,” he added.
According to the Ministry of Trade and Industry, Rwanda aims to increase exports from $4.2 billion in the 2024/25 fiscal year to $4.9 billion by 2026. The country also seeks to boost industrial growth from the current 3% to 10%.
Recent data shows that Rwanda’s industrial revenue has grown from $3.3 billion to $3.4 billion. Food processing leads the manufacturing sector, accounting for 24% of output, followed by beverages and tobacco (29%), machinery and hardware (8%), textiles and leather (8%), and furniture and office equipment (7%). Minerals represent another 8% of the country’s industrial production.
Minister Sebahizi reiterated that the trade and industrial development policy will continue to focus on promoting “Made in Rwanda” products as a cornerstone of sustainable economic growth.
*Rwanda Finalizes Master Plan to Protect Arable Land from Construction Encroachment
The Minister of Agriculture and Livestock, Dr. Mark Cyubahiro Bagabe, has announced that Rwanda is finalizing a comprehensive master plan to regulate the use of arable land and safeguard it from illegal construction activities.
Speaking during the World Food Day celebrations held on October 24, 2025, in Nyamagabe District, Dr. Cyubahiro said the Agricultural Land Use Master Plan aims to ensure that productive farmland is preserved for agricultural purposes, preventing land depletion caused by unplanned urban expansion.
“The beauty of this master plan is that it shows where the land is and what crops will be grown on it,” he explained. “Once published in the Official Gazette, no one will be allowed to build on this land - not even for schools or football fields - because every activity will have a designated place.”
Dr. Cyubahiro noted that the plan has already met all technical and policy requirements and is awaiting formal publication before implementation, expected in November 2025 following Cabinet approval.
He admitted that delays in finalizing the framework were partly due to the Ministry’s internal processes but reaffirmed that efforts are underway to ensure its timely rollout and enforcement.
The minister also urged Rwandans to focus on maximizing productivity on the available land rather than lamenting its scarcity. “To overcome the problem of limited land, we must increase yields by using all types of fertilizers and high-quality seeds,” he said. “Some say they are expensive, but not using them is even more costly - because you’ll end up with poor harvests and less income.”
Once enacted, the master plan is expected to clearly demarcate agricultural zones and crop types across the country, strengthening Rwanda’s long-term strategy for sustainable food security and land management.
*Rwanda Embraces 5G: A Glimpse Into the Future of Smart Technology
Five months after the official launch of 5G high-speed internet in Rwanda, the country is beginning to witness the potential of this transformative technology across various sectors. The milestone was first achieved by MTN Rwanda, which introduced the service at Kigali Heights and the Kigali Convention Centre (KCC)-marking Rwanda’s entry into the era of ultra-fast connectivity.
5G technology offers lightning-fast data transfer speeds-up to 1.0 gigabit per second (Gbps)-making it 10 to 100 times faster than current internet services, including fiber optic networks. This capability opens up opportunities for innovations such as remote surgery, self-driving vehicles, industrial automation, real-time gaming, and robotics.
The service, powered by Ericsson, connects multiple devices within a radius of about one kilometer, supporting a vast network of sensors, cameras, and smart machines that can communicate and operate seamlessly. Ericsson, which also partners with Airtel Rwanda, showcased the potential of 5G during the Mobile World Congress (MWC) held in Kigali, where it demonstrated how ultra-fast internet can transform industries and daily life.
One of the standout exhibits was a robotic dog, equipped with a camera, four flexible legs, and a 5G-enabled router using MTN’s SIM card. The robot was designed to perform industrial safety and monitoring tasks, including detecting fires, identifying broken fences, and ensuring workers wear proper safety gear.
“This robot helps ensure that employees are properly dressed for work. We no longer need someone walking around to monitor everything,” said Espoir Ndayishimiye, an Ericsson Rwanda engineer. “Artificial Intelligence and 5G allow it to operate continuously and respond instantly without delay.”
The robot communicates alerts through three methods-sending text messages, app notifications, or audible alarms-making it a useful tool for industries seeking efficiency and round-the-clock surveillance.
In addition to robotics, Ericsson showcased other 5G-powered innovations, such as multiplayer gaming platforms that connect users from different countries in real time, and the Ray-Ban Meta AI Smart Glasses, which integrate augmented reality (AR) and artificial intelligence (AI). These smart glasses can take photos and videos, play music, describe surroundings, report temperature, and even tell time-all through voice commands.
Globally, 5G technology is expected to be a major economic driver, projected to generate $13.2 trillion in revenue and create 22 million new jobs by 2035. So far, 5G networks have been deployed in 101 countries, including 15 across Africa.
With 5G already reaching over five million people in Rwanda, experts believe the technology will accelerate the nation’s transition toward a digital economy, fueling innovation in health care, manufacturing, education, and smart city development.
As Rwanda continues to expand 5G coverage, it positions itself among Africa’s leading digital pioneers-embracing the next wave of technological transformation that promises to reshape industries and everyday life.
*GasMeth Energy Boosts Investment in Lake Kivu Methane Gas Project to 810 Billion Rwf
GasMeth Energy Ltd, a company extracting methane gas from Lake Kivu, has announced an additional 230 billion Rwf investment into its gas extraction and processing operations, bringing its total investment to 810 billion Rwf. The first phase, valued at 522 billion Rwf, aims to supply methane gas for both domestic and industrial use, according to The New Times.
The project stems from a 2019 agreement between the Rwandan government and GasMeth Energy to harness methane gas for cooking, industry, and vehicles. Under the 25-year contract, GasMeth Energy is expected to extract up to 40 million cubic meters of methane per day, enough to supply 300,000 to 400,000 households based on current usage.
On August 18, 2022, the foundation stone for the processing plant was laid in Bwishyura Sector, Karongi District, marking the start of essential construction works. The project has faced challenges in securing investment and sourcing equipment internationally. CEO Stephen Tierney noted that delays were partly due to the need to secure buyers for the gas before raising sufficient capital, as well as slow delivery of key machinery from abroad.
“This time, the onshore and offshore works are almost complete. The underwater works have also begun,” Tierney said.
Supported by the Rwandan government and institutions such as Afreximbank, the plant has already employed 250 workers, with plans to expand the workforce to 800 by next year.
The project aligns with Rwanda’s push for environmentally friendly cooking fuels. According to the Seventh Household Income and Expenditure Survey (EICV7), households using clean fuels increased from 1% in 2017 to 5.4% in 2024. The survey highlights that 75% of households still rely on firewood, 18.8% use charcoal, and 5.4% use gas, biogas, or electricity. In Kigali, 23% of households use gas, compared to 59% using charcoal and 17% using wood.
