*Washington Hails Progress in DRC-Rwanda Peace Agreement Implementation
Washington, D.C. - The United States welcomed what it described as a significant step forward in the implementation of the peace agreement between the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Rwanda, following high-level talks in the U.S. capital.
Delegations from both countries convened on 21 and 22 October 2025 in Washington for the third session of the Joint Security Coordination Mechanism (JSCM). The meeting, held under the auspices of the United States, Qatar, and the African Union Commission, aimed to accelerate the execution of the peace accord signed on 27 June 2025, which had been technically initialed earlier on 18 June 2025.
Officials focused discussions on measures to neutralize the Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda (FDLR) and on the gradual lifting of Rwanda’s defensive measures, steps seen as crucial to stabilizing bilateral relations after years of tension.
“The dialogue reflects a shared commitment to peace and security in the region,” a U.S. State Department spokesperson said, emphasizing Washington’s support for continued international oversight.
The fourth session of the JSCM is already scheduled for 19 and 20 November 2025, signaling ongoing coordination between the two nations under the watchful eye of international partners. Analysts suggest that consistent engagement through the JSCM could pave the way for tangible progress in security and diplomatic cooperation along the DRC-Rwanda border.
*DRC National Assembly: Twelve Sacred Union MPs Vie for Presidency
Kinshasa, DRC - Twelve Members of Parliament (MPs) from the Sacred Union have officially submitted their candidacies for the presidency of the National Assembly, with no women among the contenders, coalition officials confirmed.
The announcement came on Friday, 24 October 2025, by André Mbata, permanent secretary of the Sacred Union. The candidacy files have since been forwarded to the High Political Authority, President Félix Tshisekedi, who is the sole authority empowered to designate the official candidate to be presented for a vote in the Assembly.
Among the declared contenders are three MPs from the ruling UDPS party-Doudou Dunia Mangu Mangu, Patrick Matata Makalamba, and Crispin Mbindule Mitono-and two from the UNC, namely Aimé Boji Sangara and Jean-Baudouin Mayo Mambeke. The remaining candidates come from a range of political groups, ensuring geopolitical representation across the east, west, and center of the country.
All twelve candidates have pledged to respect the final decision of the Head of State, signaling unity behind the presidential designation process within the ruling coalition. Analysts suggest that President Tshisekedi’s choice will be pivotal in shaping the legislative agenda and political alignment in the National Assembly for the coming term.
*Haut-Lomami Assembly President and Quaestor Dismissed Over Alleged Embezzlement
Kamina, DRC - The President and Quaestor of the Haut-Lomami Provincial Assembly, Basile Muleba and Séraphin Mukaya, were dismissed on Friday, 24 October 2025, following a plenary session dominated by a motion of no confidence. The motion was initiated by MP Jean Tayari Kiswabantu.
Of the 20 deputies present, 17 voted to remove the president and 18 voted to dismiss the quaestor, who stand accused of embezzling salaries of deputies and staff for March 2024 and May 2025, according to a memorandum submitted to the Deputy Prime Minister of the Interior.
In addition to the financial allegations, deputies criticized President Muleba for incompetence and behaviour deemed abusive and threatening, highlighting broader concerns about governance within the provincial assembly.
The session was conducted with the approval of the Ministry of the Interior, but notably, both dismissed officials were absent and did not respond to the vote. Observers suggest the decision reflects mounting pressure on provincial authorities to improve transparency and accountability.

Goma, DRC - Tensions remain high in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo as the government and the AFC/M23 rebel movement continue to accuse each other of violating the ongoing ceasefire.

*DRC’s Tshisekedi in Paris Calls for Global Action to End “Protracted Tragedy” in the Great Lakes Region
Paris, France - Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) President Félix Antoine Tshisekedi has denounced what he called a “protracted tragedy” caused by ongoing armed aggression in eastern Congo, urging the international community to take firm and coordinated action to restore peace.
Speaking Thursday, October 30, on the sidelines of the international conference on peace and prosperity in the Great Lakes region, held in Paris, Tshisekedi accused the M23 rebel group - backed by Rwanda - of being the primary driver of the worsening humanitarian crisis in his country.
The Congolese leader called for the full implementation of UN Security Council Resolution 2773, stressing the need for stronger humanitarian coordination and sustainable funding to support millions of displaced people.
“Behind every statistic lies a human tragedy,” Tshisekedi said, insisting that true peace in the Great Lakes must rest on justice, regional economic integration, and an end to the plundering of natural resources.
He concluded by urging the world to act decisively, saying prosperity and security are inseparable in the quest for lasting stability in the region.

*Reopening of Goma International Airport Sparks Debate Between Kigali and Kinshasa
Goma, DRC / Paris, France - The planned reopening of Goma International Airport in North Kivu has stirred diplomatic tensions between Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC). The announcement was made by French President Emmanuel Macron during the Paris Peace Conference on the Great Lakes Region on October 30, 2025.
The airport, closed for nearly a year due to clashes between the FARDC (Armed Forces of the DRC) and the M23 rebel group, is being presented as a humanitarian measure aimed at improving aid access to the region. Rwanda has expressed support for the initiative, emphasizing coordination with prior Doha discussions involving the M23.
Kinshasa, however, has reacted cautiously, underlining that the airport must remain under the sole management of Congolese authorities and stressing respect for national sovereignty. Government officials warned that any unilateral actions could undermine the diplomatic progress achieved during the Paris conference.
The announcement highlights the delicate balance in the Great Lakes region, where humanitarian needs intersect with ongoing security concerns and complex regional politics. Analysts note that the airport’s reopening will require careful coordination to ensure both the delivery of aid and the preservation of DRC’s authority over its territory.

*DRC Government Intensifies Fight Against Illicit Financial Flows in Mining Sector
Kinshasa, DRC - The Democratic Republic of Congo is stepping up efforts to combat illicit financial flows, particularly in its vital mining sector. Finance Minister Doudou Fwamba announced on Wednesday, October 29, 2025, at the opening of a high-level conference on Illicit Financial Flows of Commercial Origin (IFFC) in Kinshasa, that the Suminwa government is committed to enhancing transaction traceability and tackling tax evasion.
The conference, organized by the German development agency GIZ in collaboration with the General Directorate of Taxes (DGI), the General Directorate of Customs and Excise (DGDA), and the General Directorate of Administrative, Judicial, State Property, and Equity Revenue (DGRAD), aims to develop a comprehensive roadmap for structural reform of the national tax system.
Minister Fwamba highlighted the staggering losses caused by illicit financial flows, noting they amount to billions of dollars annually, depriving the country of critical funding for education, healthcare, and infrastructure projects.
To address these challenges, the government has recently implemented a series of measures designed to improve transparency and accountability. These include the creation of CONAFASIC (National Committee for Monitoring and Analyzing Illicit Commercial Financial Flows), the adoption of international tax agreements, and the establishment of an operational unit dedicated to the exchange of tax information.
“These initiatives are essential to ensuring that mineral wealth becomes a sustainable driver of development,” Fwamba said, emphasizing that a transparent and accountable mining sector is crucial for turning the country’s natural resources into long-term economic and social benefits.
The conference brought together key stakeholders from government, industry, and international partners to discuss strategies that will strengthen fiscal governance and curb illegal practices, signaling a determined push by Kinshasa to protect the nation’s economic interests and reinforce public trust in resource management.

*DRC Faces Severe Cholera Outbreak: Over 1,700 Deaths and 58,000 Suspected Cases
Kinshasa, DRC - The Ministry of Public Health of the Democratic Republic of Congo announced on Friday, 24 October 2025, that the country is experiencing one of its worst cholera epidemics in a decade, with more than 1,700 deaths and 58,000 suspected cases recorded since January 2025, representing a fatality rate of over 3%.
While 20 of the 26 provinces are non-endemic, the epidemic is being fueled by floods, conflicts, displacement, and inadequate water and sanitation systems. Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF), in collaboration with the Ministry of Health, has treated over 35,800 patients and vaccinated 22,000 people since January, but efforts are constrained by limited funding, insufficient staff, and coordination challenges.
Authorities and humanitarian partners are being urged to rapidly strengthen medical, logistical, and financial support to contain the outbreak, particularly ahead of the rainy season, which could worsen the crisis. Health experts warn that immediate action is crucial to prevent further loss of life.
*DRC: Prime Minister Calls Fight Against Female Cancers a National Imperative
Kinshasa, DRC - Speaking at the opening of a round table on women’s cancers in Kinshasa on Saturday, 25 October 2025, Prime Minister Judith Suminwa described the fight against female cancers as a “national imperative”, calling for an integrated, equitable, and sustainable strategy involving the public and private sectors, development partners, and local communities.
Minister of Public Health Roger Samuel Kamba highlighted that nearly 150,000 women are diagnosed annually with breast or cervical cancer, equating to two new cases per hour, often at an advanced stage.
The government is taking steps to strengthen the national cancer control centre, decentralize screening, modernize technical facilities, and distribute anti-cancer drugs for free in six provinces. Additionally, the cervical cancer vaccine is planned for introduction in 2026.
Despite these initiatives, officials note persistent challenges, including the absence of a national cancer registry, the centralization of infrastructure in Kinshasa, and the high cost of treatment, which continue to hinder nationwide access to care.

Overall Conclusion: DRC Report, October 20-31, 2025
The period between October 20 and 31, 2025, for the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) was characterized by a delicate balance between intensified diplomatic and economic engagement and persistent, severe security and humanitarian crises, particularly in the eastern provinces.
Political and Diplomatic Progress
On the political and diplomatic fronts, the DRC made notable strides in regional and international relations:
DRC-Rwanda Peace: The United States welcomed significant progress in implementing the peace agreement, following the third session of the Joint Security Coordination Mechanism (JSCM) in Washington, which focused on neutralizing the FDLR and lifting Rwanda’s defensive measures.
National Assembly Leadership: The ruling Sacred Union, under President Félix Tshisekedi's authority, designated Aimé Boji Sangara as its sole candidate for the National Assembly presidency, aiming to consolidate political unity.
International Diplomacy: President Tshisekedi engaged in high-level diplomacy in Paris, urging global action to end the "protracted tragedy" in the Great Lakes region and securing a €1.5 billion pledge for peace and humanitarian aid. He also strengthened bilateral cooperation with Egypt in military, economic, and diplomatic sectors.
Economic Stability and Reform
Efforts were focused on internal economic reform and external trade:
Currency and Prices: The government held a meeting to assess the appreciation of the Congolese franc (CDF), aiming to translate currency gains into lower market prices and launching a national campaign to combat price speculation.
Oil Sector Reform: The DRC and the IMF reached an agreement to resolve revenue shortfalls in the oil sector as part of a broader reform program to enhance budget transparency.
Regional Economic Ties: The 3rd DRC-Angola Economic Forum was scheduled for February 2026 to boost bilateral trade and cooperation. The construction of a 600-meter bridge linking Zongo to Bangui (CAR) was highlighted as a key infrastructure project for regional integration.
Security and Humanitarian Crisis
Despite diplomatic efforts, the security situation remains highly volatile, driving a catastrophic humanitarian crisis:
Escalating Violence: Intense clashes between the FARDC/Wazalendo and the AFC/M23 rebels were reported in North Kivu (Masisi, Walikale) and South Kivu (Nyangezi), prompting mass displacement and the U.S. to warn against ceasefire violations. The DRC government and the AFC/M23 continued to trade accusations over truce breaches.
ADF Attacks: Attacks attributed to the ADF rebels continued in Ituri (Kamagolo) and North Kivu (Lubero), resulting in civilian deaths, abductions, and mass displacement.
Humanitarian Scale: UN Secretary-General Guterres described the situation as one of the most severe globally, noting over 21 million people require assistance, 5.7 million are displaced, and 32,000 children remain in armed groups.
Health Crises: The country faced one of its worst cholera epidemics in a decade, with over 1,700 deaths and 58,000 suspected cases recorded since January 2025. The Prime Minister also declared the fight against female cancers a "national imperative".
In summary, the end of October 2025 demonstrated the DRC's determined push for a political solution to its conflicts and economic stability, notably through the JSCM and currency management. However, these positive steps are continuously undermined by rampant insecurity, inter-militia clashes, and the resulting, massive humanitarian and public health emergencies in the east.
