Novosibirsk, the third-largest city in Russia with a population of about 1.63 million, serves as the administrative capital of Siberia and a major cultural and economic hub - offering what officials describe as “significant potential” for this new cooperation framework.

*Burkina Faso Moves Forward with Nuclear Project: Lawmakers Approve Ratification of Agreement on the Peaceful Use of Nuclear Energy
Ouagadougou, November 21, 2025 - The Transitional Legislative Assembly (ALT) unanimously adopted, on Friday, the bill authorizing the ratification of the cooperation agreement between Burkina Faso and the Russian Federation on the peaceful use of nuclear energy. This marks a major step in the country’s ambition to develop electro-nuclear capacity and strengthen its energy sovereignty.
The plenary session of November 21 was highlighted by the presentation of the bill by the Minister of Foreign Affairs, Karamoko Jean Marie Traoré, who once again defended the initiative introduced by the government. The bill builds on the cooperation agreement signed with Russia on June 19, 2025, laying the foundation for a strategic partnership in the civilian nuclear sector.
Lawmakers Raise Key Concerns During Debates
During discussions, lawmakers expressed several important concerns, particularly regarding:
The overall cost of building and operating a nuclear facility;
Safety and security guarantees, especially in relation to technological risks;
The availability of domestic expertise to manage, operate, and maintain nuclear installations;
Modalities of regional cooperation, especially with other members of the AES Confederation, within a framework of integrated energy planning.
Several speakers stressed the need for a long-term vision, a strong regulatory framework, and significant capacity building for national technical personnel.
A Government Defense Focused on Energy Sovereignty
Minister Traoré responded in detail, emphasizing the strategic relevance of civilian nuclear energy for Burkina Faso.
According to him, nuclear technology represents one of the most reliable and effective solutions today to meet:
the growing demand for electricity,
the country’s industrial ambitions,
the need for economic competitiveness,
and the challenge of energy autonomy.
“If we want to live up to our industrial promises, enhance the competitiveness of our economy, and consolidate our energy sovereignty, this option becomes necessary,” the minister declared, stressing that nuclear energy will allow the country to reach a competitive level in the long term.
He also reaffirmed the unwavering determination of the President of Faso to see this initiative through, highlighting its structural and historic significance for the nation.
Unanimous Vote Gives Government the Green Light for the Nuclear Program
Convinced by the minister’s arguments, lawmakers cast a unanimous vote in favor of the bill, thereby granting the government the official mandate to begin the operational implementation of the national nuclear program.
Following the vote, the Minister of Energy, Mines, and Quarries, Yacouba Zabré Gouba, welcomed what he described as a “decisive milestone” for Burkina Faso. He stated that this adoption represents a major advancement, clearing the way for the development of a sovereign, diversified, and resilient energy capacity.
A Strategic Decision with Long-Term Implications
The adoption of this law marks the beginning of a structuring process for the future of Burkina Faso’s energy landscape. It opens the door to:
the technical planning of the nuclear project,
the training of a national specialized engineering corps,
the development of international partnerships,
and the establishment of a nuclear safety framework aligned with international standards.
This strategic decision, rooted in a desire for energy independence, positions Burkina Faso among the African nations turning to civilian nuclear power as a driver of economic transformation.
*Confederation of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES): Minister Pindgwendé Gilbert Ouedraogo Present at the Signing of the Memorandum of Understanding for the Creation of the AES Radio Station
Ouagadougou, November 26, 2025 - The Minister of Communication, Culture, Arts, and Tourism, Pindgwendé Gilbert Ouedraogo, took part in a major step toward strengthening communication within the Confederation of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES). On Wednesday, November 26, 2025, he attended the official signing of the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) that establishes the Confederation’s first joint radio broadcasting platform.
The MoU was signed by the Foreign Ministers of Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger, the three member states of the AES, marking a significant advance in the Confederation’s strategy to unify its information ecosystem, counter hostile media narratives, and enhance sovereign communication capacity.
The new station, named “Daandè Liptako”-Fulfulde for “The Voice of Liptako”-symbolizes the shared heritage, identity, and geopolitical importance of the Liptako-Gourma region, a territory historically linked to the three nations.
A Strategic Tool Against Disinformation
According to officials, the creation of Daandè Liptako is a direct response to the growing challenges posed by disinformation campaigns, perception warfare, and external attempts to influence public opinion in the Sahel. The station will function as a central pillar of the Confederation’s communication architecture, ensuring:
the production and dissemination of verified, credible, and timely information,
the countering of false narratives,
and the promotion of the Confederation’s vision, reforms, and achievements.
Clear Objectives Defined in the MoU
The memorandum outlines several core missions assigned to the radio station:
Strengthen communication mechanisms within the Confederation of Sahel States (CSA) in the face of security, political, and informational threats;
Raise awareness among populations and encourage positive behavioral change, particularly in matters of unity, security cooperation, and civic responsibility;
Serve as a centralized communication hub for the three member countries, enabling harmonized public messaging;
Promote dialogue, cultural exchange, and mutual understanding across the confederation territory.
Broadcast Structure and Regional Reach
The station’s headquarters will be based in Ouagadougou, reflecting Burkina Faso’s central role in the AES communication initiatives. To ensure broad coverage across the Sahel:
a relay antenna will be installed in Bamako,
another in Niamey,
allowing the station to broadcast continuously throughout the Confederation area and reach remote communities.
Programming is expected to include news bulletins, educational content, cultural programming, public service announcements, and special features promoting the values and goals of the AES.
Launch Expected at the Next Heads of State Summit
The radio station will be officially launched during the upcoming AES Heads of State Summit, scheduled for December 22-23, 2025, in Bamako, Mali. The launch will be one of the flagship achievements presented at the summit, alongside other initiatives aimed at strengthening the Confederation’s institutional framework and sovereign communication tools.
By attending this ceremony, Minister Pindgwendé Gilbert Ouedraogo reaffirmed Burkina Faso’s commitment to building a strong, coordinated, and resilient Sahel communication space, capable of defending the interests and image of its peoples on the regional and international scenes.

*Burkina Faso Advances Toward a Legal Framework for Occupational Health and Safety in Public Administration
Ouagadougou, November 18, 2025 - The Ministry of Public Service, Labor and Social Protection (MFPTPS), through the Directorate General of Social Protection (DGPS), has launched a two-day pre-validation workshop dedicated to the draft decree on occupational health and safety (OHS) within the Burkinabe public administration.
The opening session was presided over by the Ministry’s Secretary-General, Suanyaba Rodrigue Oboulbiga, who emphasized the government’s determination to strengthen the protection of workers across public institutions.
In his remarks, Mr. Oboulbiga recalled that the initiative reflects the State's commitment to preserving human capital, which he described as essential to productivity, institutional efficiency, and sustainable development. He noted that the future decree aims not only to clarify and unify the legal framework governing workplace safety but also to reinforce prevention mechanisms across all public bodies.
“Work can be a source of fulfillment, but it also exposes men and women to risks that can affect their physical and mental well-being,” he warned.
The Secretary-General pointed out a significant imbalance between the public and private sectors: while the private sector already operates under a clear legal framework ensuring safe working conditions, public administration remains far behind, with fragmented and insufficient legislative provisions in place.
To illustrate the urgency of reform, Mr. Oboulbiga cited data from the Autonomous Pension Fund for Civil Servants (CARFO). In 2023 alone, 608 workplace accidents and occupational illnesses were recorded among civil servants.
“While compensation mechanisms are well-established, prevention remains the weak link of our system,” he acknowledged, urging participants to contribute constructively and propose actionable improvements.
Throughout the workshop-held on November 18 and 19-participants will examine several key areas, including:
General hygiene standards within public institutions
Universal norms related to specific workplace risk factors
Accident prevention requirements tailored to public administration
Mandatory medical examinations for public employees
This pre-validation marks a decisive step toward establishing a comprehensive and coherent OHS framework, with the potential to significantly improve working conditions for thousands of civil servants nationwide.

The three-day session, held from November 19 to 21, 2025, will focus on reviewing four major draft regulatory texts aimed at strengthening the national labor framework.
Among the documents submitted for examination is a draft decree establishing working conditions for domestic employees-a sector often marked by informality and limited legal protection. Another key text concerns the procedures for filing, publishing, translating, adhering to, and withdrawing from collective bargaining agreements, a move intended to modernize social dialogue mechanisms.
The commission will also review two additional draft decrees:
one regulating the functioning of employment placement agencies and temporary work agencies, and
another defining and sanctioning violations of the Labor Code and its implementing provisions.
In his opening address, Secretary-General Oboulbiga praised the commission members for their unwavering dedication to promoting constructive social dialogue and maintaining stability in the world of work.
“I would like to emphasize the quality of the work you accomplished during the first session of this year. Your contribution to the review of the draft texts relating to staff representatives and the procedures for communicating internal regulations was remarkable,” he said.
Turning to the four draft decrees under review, Mr. Oboulbiga encouraged participants to pursue their deliberations in a climate of serenity, mutual respect, and consensus-building.
“I sincerely hope that your reflections and recommendations will lead to clear and operational texts that promote sustainable social progress,” he added before formally declaring the session open.
This second ordinary session marks another step toward reinforcing the legal and institutional environment governing labor relations in Burkina Faso, with outcomes expected to shape policy implementation in key segments of the country’s workforce.

*Implementation of the Universal Health Insurance Scheme: Benefits to Begin on February 1, 2026
Ouagadougou, November 21, 2025 - The Minister of Public Service, Labor, and Social Protection, Mathias Traoré, accompanied by his counterpart from the Ministry of Health, Robert Lucien Jean Claude Kargougou, held a press conference on Friday, November 21, 2025, in Ouagadougou to present the implementation of the Universal Health Insurance Scheme (RAMU). The session aimed to inform the public about the details of this major social protection mechanism for citizens of Burkina Faso.
Minister Traoré emphasized that less than 10% of the population currently benefits from social security coverage, while direct household health expenditures reach 34%, well above the maximum threshold of 20% set by the World Health Organization (WHO).
According to him, the RAMU (Universal Health Coverage Scheme) offers a structured solution enabling every citizen to access healthcare without financial hardship, while integrating with existing social security systems and allowing citizens the option to purchase supplemental private insurance.
RAMU services will include:
Consultations
Clinical and laboratory examinations
Surgical procedures
Maternity care
Hospitalizations and outpatient care
Functional assessments
Radiology and medical imaging
Rehabilitation
Medical transport
Consultations with licensed traditional medicine practitioners
These services will be provided in healthcare facilities that have signed agreements with the National Universal Health Insurance Fund (CNAMU), covering both public and private sectors.
Contributions will be as follows:
Public sector employees: 5% of gross salary
Self-employed workers in the formal sector: 15,000 CFA francs/month
Workers in the informal sector: 4,000 CFA francs/month
Retirees: 2% of their pension
Government contribution: 4,000 CFA francs/month for low-income individuals
Benefits will commence on February 1, 2026, initially targeting civil servants in the Kadiogo and Guiriko regions, after at least three months of contributions. Registration is open at CNAMU headquarters in Ouaga 2000 and at the regional offices of labor and social protection.

November 23, 2025 - The Eastern Region of Burkina Faso experienced a new surge of violence on Sunday, November 23, in a series of coordinated attacks attributed to Jama’at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM). These incidents, spread across three provinces, left several civilians and state agents dead, while causing significant material destruction.
Boudangou (Commune of Manni, Gnagna Province)
In Boudangou, armed individuals attacked the village, killing three civilians. Several shops were set ablaze during the assault, plunging the community into fear and deepening the displacement already affecting the area.
Komandougou (Commune of Fada N’Gourma)
The situation in Komandougou remains unclear, with local sources providing conflicting accounts. While some report no fatalities, others mention multiple victims.Despite the uncertainty regarding the human toll, the burning of shops and several homes has been confirmed, highlighting the scale and brutality of the attack.
Tandyari (Commune of Yamba, Gourma Province)
The deadliest of the attacks occurred in Tandyari, where gunmen targeted a forestry agent post. Approximately ten agents were reportedly killed. According to local sources, the attackers then seized weapons, ammunition, motorcycles, and various operational equipment before retreating.
*Burkina Faso - JNIM Claims Responsibility for New Series of Attacks
Ouagadougou, November 28, 2025 - The jihadist coalition JNIM has claimed responsibility for a new wave of coordinated attacks carried out on November 23 across multiple regions of Burkina Faso, according to a statement released on Friday.
In the eastern region, the group says it targeted the Komandougou military post, located in the commune of Fada N’Gourma. The assault reportedly left five Burkinabe soldiers dead and resulted in the destruction of a military vehicle. JNIM also claims to have seized a DJI drone, motorcycles, several AK-47 assault rifles with magazines, as well as additional ammunition.
In the central-eastern region, the group asserts it carried out another attack on a military position in the province of Koulpélogo, where seven soldiers were allegedly killed. According to its communiqué, the assailants recovered multiple assault rifles, magazines, a large quantity of ammunition, several heavy machine guns, and an RPG rocket launcher with its equipment.
These latest claims highlight JNIM’s capacity to operate simultaneously on several fronts and to seize significant quantities of military equipment. They also underline the mounting pressure on Burkina Faso’s armed forces in the eastern and east-central regions, where attacks continue to intensify despite ongoing security operations.
*Burkina Faso - Tapoa on the Verge of Security Collapse
November 30, 2025 - The security situation in the province of Tapoa has reached an alarming breaking point, with local sources reporting that more than 95% of the territory is now under the control of armed groups. The Burkinabe army maintains only a minimal presence in three localities - including Diapaga - and even there, its hold is described as extremely fragile.
The town of Diapaga has been attacked repeatedly in recent weeks. The military detachment has been overwhelmed, civilians and security personnel killed, and shops and warehouses looted. Videos filmed and circulated by the assailants themselves illustrate the extent of state erosion in the area. In Kantchari and Partiaga, the situation is equally dire: although military outposts remain, soldiers reportedly avoid venturing more than one kilometer from their bases due to the constant threat of ambushes.
Patrols have been reduced to cautious movements within city centers, with no real capacity for wider deployment. Meanwhile, armed groups are imposing - without resistance - a form of mandatory levy, described by residents as a “zakat” of 50,000 FCFA per inhabitant across the province. This practice underscores the extent of their administrative and coercive control.
The humanitarian situation is deteriorating just as rapidly. Only the primary healthcare centers (CSPS) in Diapaga and Kantchari remain functional, and even these operate with severely limited resources. All other health facilities in Tapoa are closed. Mortality rates are rising, particularly among pregnant women and children, due to the collapse of essential medical services.
The portrait emerging from Tapoa is that of an isolated, exhausted province on the brink of total breakdown. A massive and coordinated intervention is urgently needed to prevent the complete security and humanitarian collapse of the region.
*Burkina Faso - New Episode of Forced Displacement in Tapoa
Tapoa, November 30, 2025 - A new wave of forced displacement struck the province of Tapoa on Sunday, as residents from seven villages in the commune of Botou were expelled by fighters from JNIM, according to several local sources. Confronted with direct threats from armed groups, villagers fled en masse toward Botou, placing the town under intense strain as it struggles to absorb the sudden influx of displaced families.
Botou, the largest commune in Tapoa - home to more than forty-two villages - is now facing growing humanitarian pressure. These mass displacements underscore the rapidly worsening security situation in this border zone with Niger, where armed groups are steadily tightening their grip on rural communities.
The latest expulsions highlight how insurgents are expanding their territorial control, deepening an already severe humanitarian crisis across the province and leaving thousands increasingly vulnerable.
Conclusion: Strategic Sovereignty Amidst Security Paradoxes
The period from November 17 to 30, 2025, was defined by a dual dynamic in Burkina Faso: an aggressive pursuit of state sovereignty and diplomatic realignment on the international stage, contrasted sharply by persistent humanitarian and security crises on the ground.
1. Consolidation of the AES and Diplomatic Diversification Diplomatically, the government focused heavily on cementing the Confederation of Sahel States (AES). The preparatory meetings in Ouagadougou and the establishment of the "Daandè Liptako" joint radio station signal a rapid move toward operationalizing the confederation ahead of the December summit in Bamako. Simultaneously, Burkina Faso diversified its strategic partnerships, ratifying a nuclear energy agreement with Russia and strengthening economic and diplomatic ties with Belarus, Iran, and Slovakia.
2. Major Domestic Reforms Domestically, the government advanced significant structural reforms aimed at social protection and administrative efficiency. The final preparations for the Universal Health Insurance Scheme (RAMU), set to deliver benefits by February 2026, represent a major shift toward social welfare. Additionally, efforts to decentralize the civil service and harmonize labor inspections reflect a push to modernize public administration.
3. The Security and Humanitarian Challenge However, these institutional advances face the harsh reality of insecurity. While the state builds long-term frameworks, immediate stability remains fragile. The dire humanitarian situation in Arbinda, marked by blockade and famine , and continued attacks by armed terrorist groups in the North, highlight the disconnect between central governance achievements and the struggles of remote populations.
Outlook As Burkina Faso moves into December, the focus will shift to the second session of the AES College of Heads of State. The government’s ability to reconcile its ambitious geopolitical vision with the urgent need to secure territory and alleviate the suffering of besieged communities, such as those in Arbinda, will be the defining challenge of the coming weeks.
