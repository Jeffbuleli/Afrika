*Qatar and DRC Sign Six Bilateral Agreements to Strengthen Cooperation
Kinshasa, November 21, 2025 - During an official visit to Kinshasa, the Emir of Qatar, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, and the President of the Democratic Republic of Congo, Félix Tshisekedi, presided over the signing of six bilateral agreements aimed at deepening cooperation between the two nations.
The memoranda of understanding cover a range of strategic areas, including port management between Mwani Qatar and the Congolese National Transport Office (ONATRA), legal cooperation, visa exemptions for holders of diplomatic and special passports, and partnerships focused on youth and sports development.
The two governments also established a mechanism for regular political consultations and signed a memorandum between the Qatar Fund for Development and the Congolese Ministry of Social Affairs to support a multi-sectoral emergency response in South Kivu, addressing urgent humanitarian and development needs.
This series of agreements builds on previous commitments, notably the letters of intent signed in September 2025, which outlined an investment portfolio worth US$21 billion, signaling a significant boost in bilateral economic and strategic relations.
The agreements highlight both countries’ commitment to enhancing political, economic, and social collaboration, fostering sustainable development and regional stability.
*Crisis in Eastern DRC: Sama Lukonde Urges African Parliaments to Show Greater Solidarity
Kinshasa, November 21, 2025 - At the opening of the 47th Conference of Speakers of African Parliaments, President of the Congolese Senate Sama Lukonde called on legislators across Africa to demonstrate stronger solidarity in addressing the ongoing insecurity in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.
Describing the situation as a “silent genocide”, Lukonde highlighted the toll of millions of deaths and nearly 7 million displaced persons, stressing the urgent need for a coordinated continental response. He urged African parliamentary bodies to actively support ongoing peace initiatives, including those led in Washington and Doha, while advocating for parliaments to serve as “laboratories of solutions” tailored to African realities.
In response, the African Parliamentary Union (APU), represented by Vice-President Abdoul Esikiye, reaffirmed its solidarity with populations affected by humanitarian crises in both the DRC and Sudan.
The conference, held as part of the 84th session of the APU Executive Committee, concluded with a commitment to strengthen continental parliamentary cooperation and enhance unified action in addressing Africa’s security and humanitarian challenges.
*DRC and Rwanda Reaffirm Security Commitments at 4th Joint Mechanism Meeting in Washington
Washington, D.C., November 19-20, 2025 - The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Rwanda convened the fourth meeting of the Joint Security Coordination Mechanism in Washington, D.C., with participation from representatives of the United States, Qatar, and Togo, acting as the African Union mediator.
The talks aimed to accelerate the implementation of the Peace Agreement signed on June 27, 2025, with both parties reaffirming their commitment to the Order of Operations (OPORD) and the Concept of Operations (CONOPS) of the Harmonized Plan. This framework focuses on neutralizing the FDLR, disengaging Rwandan forces, and lifting Rwandan defensive measures.
The meeting assessed progress in the first phase of ongoing security operations, including intelligence sharing and awareness-raising activities led by the DRC, while addressing the challenges of the second phase, which emphasizes neutralization and disengagement.
Participants underscored the alignment between the Washington and Doha peace processes, welcoming the recent Doha Framework Agreement signed between Kinshasa and the AFC/M23 coalition. A high-level follow-up meeting is scheduled for January 17, 2026, in Togo, intended to consolidate progress in the Great Lakes peace process.
*Eastern DRC: AFC/M23 Claims Neutralization of Armed Groups under Doha Agreement
Goma, November 21, 2025 - The AFC/M23 movement announced that it had "neutralized" all local and foreign armed groups operating in eastern DRC, in accordance with the Doha Framework Agreement recently signed with the Congolese government.
According to Benjamin Mbonimpa, permanent secretary of AFC/M23, the agreement commits both parties to:
Cease all support for militias;
Disarm local groups, notably the Wazalendo;
Neutralize foreign forces such as the FDLR and the ADF;
Establish transitional security mechanisms to protect civilians.
The announcement comes amid ongoing fighting in the Walikale, Masisi, Rutshuru, and Lubero territories, despite the Doha ceasefire. The Congolese army claims to have regained control of several localities, including Katoyi, highlighting the persistent instability in the region.

*Kinshasa Officially Launches DRC-UK Trade Partnership
Kinshasa, DRC - On Tuesday, November 25, 2025, the Democratic Republic of Congo and the United Kingdom officially inaugurated their new trade partnership, aimed at strengthening bilateral commerce and diversifying the Congolese economy.
The agreement allows Congolese spices, coffee, and cocoa to access the British market duty-free, marking a major step in reducing reliance on the mining sector. The ceremony was presided over by the Minister of Foreign Trade, Julien Paluku, and UK Ambassador Alyson King, highlighting the strategic importance of this initiative for both nations.

The inauguration also launched a trade support program, backed by the International Trade Centre, TradeMark Africa, and the British Standards Institute (BSI), designed to assist Congolese businesses in meeting international standards and maximizing the benefits of the agreement.
Minister Paluku emphasized that this program will be implemented nationally to support export capacity building, market competitiveness, and the sustainable diversification of the Congolese economy.

*DRC: Senate Adopts Banking Reform, Joint Committee to Resolve Remaining Differences
Kinshasa, DRC - On November 27, 2025, the Senate adopted on second reading the bill reforming the activities and oversight of credit institutions, in a version differing from that passed by the National Assembly.
The Senate’s version notably reduces the compliance period stipulated in Article 190 from 60 months to 36 months, compared with the 120-month deadline originally proposed by MP Olivier Katuala. The reform, initially submitted on September 16, 2024, aims to modernize the banking framework, focusing on governance, competence of decision-making bodies, and external audits, while addressing constraints related to capital dilution, which were deemed incompatible with African standards.
After its adoption by the National Assembly on June 15, 2025 (354 votes in favor out of 363), and now by the Senate (73 votes for, 1 abstention), a joint committee will be convened to harmonize the two versions. Should an agreement not be reached, the Constitution stipulates that the Assembly’s text will take precedence before submission to the President of the Republic.

*DRC: Government Sets Strict Schedule for Resumption of Cobalt Exports
Kinshasa, DRC - The Congolese government has announced the imminent resumption of cobalt exports, following a suspension that began on February 22, 2025 and was extended until October 15, 2025. The temporary halt had been imposed to address a price collapse caused by overproduction in the global market.
Effective October 16, 2025, the government lifted the ban and implemented a quota system, allowing the export of 18,125 tons of cobalt by the end of the year. The allocation is 3,625 tons for October, and 7,250 tons each for November and December.
Minister of Mines, Louis Watum Kabamba, confirmed that exports will resume from November 27, 2025, emphasizing that the new policy-including an annual ceiling of 96,600 tons starting in 2026-is designed to protect national revenues, stabilize international prices, and strengthen the DRC’s sovereignty over this strategic mineral resource.
The government’s move underscores the DRC’s commitment to responsible resource management while supporting the stability of the global cobalt market, a critical input for electric vehicles and high-tech batteries.

Conclusion
The second half of November 2025 presents a stark dichotomy for the Democratic Republic of Congo: a nation making significant strides in international diplomacy and economic diversification, yet grappling with a deteriorating security crisis in its eastern provinces.
Security and Humanitarian Crisis Despite diplomatic efforts via the Washington and Doha processes, the security situation in eastern DRC remains critical. The ceasefire agreements have faced severe challenges, with the AFC/M23 coalition expanding its control in Masisi and South Kivu, establishing parallel administrations, and displacing thousands of civilians. The humanitarian toll is staggering, with over 19 million people in distress across the region and aid agencies facing financial constraints. While the FARDC has bolstered its capabilities through European Union funding and new military magistrate appointments, the persistent violence from armed groups like the ADF and M23 continues to be the deadliest threat to the population.
Diplomacy and Economic Diversification Conversely, Kinshasa has successfully pursued an aggressive strategy of economic diversification and international partnership. The period was marked by the signing of six major bilateral agreements with Qatar, including a $21 billion investment portfolio. Furthermore, the launch of a trade partnership with the United Kingdom and strengthened ties with Serbia signal a concerted effort to reduce reliance on raw mineral exports and boost local value chains. Domestically, the government’s 2026 draft budget reflects these priorities, allocating significant resources to security, infrastructure, and social protection.
Governance and Justice Internally, the DRC is navigating a complex political landscape. Tensions between provincial assemblies and executives have risen, evidenced by motions of censure in provinces like Mongala and Haut-Uele. However, the government has shown a firm commitment to justice and historical accountability, notably through the campaign for the recognition of the Congolese genocide, supported by religious denominations and African institutions. The swift prosecution in the Rawbank robbery case and the trial of armed groups in Walikale demonstrate a push to combat impunity and restore state authority.
Outlook As the DRC approaches the end of 2025, the nation stands at a pivotal crossroads. The immediate future will likely be defined by the government's ability to translate diplomatic and economic wins into tangible security improvements on the ground. All eyes will be on the upcoming high-level follow-up meeting scheduled for January 17, 2026, in Togo, which serves as a critical test for the consolidation of peace in the Great Lakes region.
