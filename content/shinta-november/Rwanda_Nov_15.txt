*Rwanda Confirms DRC Will Not Participate in Goma Airport Opening

The announcement follows a security meeting on the Great Lakes region held in Paris on October 30, 2025, where French President Emmanuel Macron revealed plans to open the airport in the coming weeks, with assistance from Qatar and the United States.
French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot explained that the Paris meeting aimed to accelerate discussions on Goma Airport’s reopening, with final decisions to be made in Doha, Qatar, between the AFC/M23 coalition and the DRC government. “This international conference was an opportunity to accelerate discussions on this issue, which will continue under the auspices of Qatar, with new information provided here in Paris,” Barrot said.

*Tshisekedi Repeats Claims That Rwanda Seeks to Annex Eastern Congo
KINSHASA, DRC - President Félix Antoine Tshisekedi has once again accused Rwanda of seeking to annex parts of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), repeating claims that have repeatedly been denied by Kigali. Speaking to members of the Congolese diaspora in Egypt on Saturday, November 1, 2025, the Congolese leader alleged that Rwanda and President Paul Kagame have a “grand plan” to divide his country and seize its mineral-rich eastern provinces.
“His goal is to divide our country and occupy it, and even annex the eastern part, which has land rich in minerals and agriculture,” Tshisekedi told the audience in Cairo.
Rwanda Dismisses Accusations
Rwanda has consistently rejected such allegations, insisting that its actions are driven solely by security concerns and not by any desire to claim Congolese territory or resources.
In a March 2025 interview, President Kagame reaffirmed that Rwanda’s primary focus is national security, not access to the DRC’s natural wealth. “If you look at the list of hundreds of companies from around the world - China, Europe, America, Canada, and others - that are interested in Congo’s minerals, Rwanda would be in 100th place, at the very end,” Kagame said in an interview with journalist Mario Nawfall.

Accusations Over Peace Talks

Uncertain Path to Dialogue
Tshisekedi’s renewed accusations come at a delicate time, as he and President Kagame are expected to meet soon in Washington, D.C., for talks hosted by U.S. President Donald Trump. The meeting aims to promote peace and strengthen economic cooperation between Kigali and Kinshasa.
Observers warn that Tshisekedi’s latest remarks risk undermining those efforts. His continued rhetoric against Rwanda, despite recent diplomatic progress, has raised questions about his commitment to reconciliation and regional stability.
*Rwanda Dismisses Tshisekedi’s Claims of Annexation Ambitions, Cites Corruption in Katanga
Kigali, Rwanda - Rwanda’s Minister of Foreign Affairs and Cooperation, Ambassador Olivier Nduhungirehe, has firmly rejected accusations by Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) President Félix Tshisekedi that Rwanda seeks to annex part of Congolese territory. The remarks come amid escalating rhetoric between the two neighboring nations, whose relations remain tense over ongoing insecurity in eastern DRC.
During a meeting with members of the Congolese diaspora in Egypt on November 2, 2025, President Tshisekedi alleged that Rwanda covets the mineral-rich eastern region of his country, claiming Kigali’s motives are driven by a desire to control its natural resources and fertile land.
In response, Minister Nduhungirehe dismissed the allegations as “baseless and ironic,” turning the accusation back on Tshisekedi himself. “If I have followed the news correctly,” he said on November 3, “it is the Tshisekedi family that wants to ‘keep’ the natural resources of Katanga in its coffers.”
Kigali Reaffirms Its Position
Rwandan officials have repeatedly maintained that their country has no territorial or economic ambitions in the DRC. President Paul Kagame has consistently emphasized that Rwanda’s primary concern is its national security - particularly the threat posed by armed groups operating near its borders.
In a March 2025 address, Kagame underscored that Rwanda’s interest in the DRC’s resources is minimal compared to that of global mining corporations. “If you look at the list of hundreds of companies from China, Europe, America, Canada and others that are involved in Congo’s minerals, Rwanda would come 100th - at the very end,” he said.
Allegations of Corruption in Katanga
The exchange of accusations comes as members of President Tshisekedi’s family face growing scrutiny over alleged involvement in mineral theft and corruption in the Katanga and Lualaba regions - areas that produce a significant share of the world’s copper and cobalt.
Local residents and industry sources have accused the Tshisekedi family, including the president’s brother Christian Tshisekedi, of turning Katanga into a personal “cash cow” by extracting vast mineral wealth without reinvesting in the province.
“Since 2019, Katanga has become the Tshisekedi family’s cash cow,” one Katanga resident told reporters. “They extract wealth from the province, especially Lualaba, but invest nothing back.”
Reports from mining groups indicate that security forces, including members of the Republican Guard, have been deployed to certain mining sites to facilitate illegal extraction and transport of minerals.
Legal Action Underway
In response to these allegations, Katanga’s regional authorities have retained Belgian lawyer Bernard Maingain to initiate legal proceedings against Christian Tshisekedi and his associates in both the DRC and Belgium, where they reportedly hold dual citizenship.
“We plan to file complaints in the DRC and in Belgium,” Maingain said, adding that the case involves “serious crimes of corruption, embezzlement, and the illegal use of funds.”
On October 28, DRC Minister of Justice Guillaume Ngefa announced that the National Commission for Combating Illegal Mining (CNLFM) had completed an investigation implicating several civilians, soldiers, police officers, and foreign nationals in large-scale mineral theft. Ngefa instructed the Prosecutor’s Office to pursue legal action against all those identified, regardless of their rank or nationality.
While the report’s findings have not been made public, it remains unclear whether members of the Tshisekedi family are among those named - a revelation that could have far-reaching political consequences.
A Widening Rift
The latest exchange between Kinshasa and Kigali reflects deepening mistrust between the two countries, whose strained relations have been marked by mutual accusations over security and resource exploitation.As both governments trade allegations of corruption and aggression, regional observers warn that the escalating war of words could further destabilize the Great Lakes region if diplomacy fails to prevail.
*Rwanda, Egypt Strengthen Cooperation During GEM Opening
CAIRO, EGYPT - Rwanda’s Minister of Foreign Affairs, Olivier Nduhungirehe, met with his Egyptian counterpart, Dr. Badr Abdelatty, to discuss ways to deepen cooperation between the two countries across multiple sectors. The meeting took place during the opening of the Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM) on November 1, 2025, which Minister Nduhungirehe attended as part of Rwanda’s diplomatic delegation.
Minister Nduhungirehe thanked Egypt for hosting the opening ceremony and reaffirmed Rwanda’s commitment to continue strengthening bilateral relations.
Grand Egyptian Museum: A Global Cultural Landmark
The GEM, the world’s largest museum, houses over 100,000 artifacts tracing Egypt’s 7,000-year history. Construction began in 2005 on a 50-hectare site near the Giza pyramids at a cost exceeding $1 billion. Visitors are greeted by an 11-meter-tall, eight-ton statue of Pharaoh Ramesses II, with staircases leading to statues of ancient kings and queens.
The museum is divided into three sections - social, royal, and religious life - showcasing historical changes over millennia. Among its highlights are King Khufu’s ship, temples known as ‘naos,’ statues of Egyptian gods like Osiris and Ptah, and a children’s education section with a library of historical documents. The museum also features a comprehensive display of Tutankhamun Nebkheperure’s artifacts, including 5,398 objects arranged in 7,500 square meters, presented together for the first time.
Strengthening Bilateral Relations
Rwanda and Egypt have steadily developed diplomatic and economic ties. On September 28, 2025, both countries signed a visa exchange agreement during the UN General Assembly in New York. Earlier in October, President Paul Kagame visited Egypt, meeting President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi and signing agreements on investment promotion, water management, land exchanges, and urban development.
Bilateral agreements signed in August 2024 cover health, transport, trade, and other sectors, complemented by ongoing collaboration in technology, industrial and commercial development, agriculture, education, youth, health, and military cooperation.
Diplomatic relations between the two countries date back decades. Egypt has maintained an embassy in Kigali since 1976, and Rwanda reopened its embassy in Cairo in March 2015. The 2017 visit of President Al-Sisi to Rwanda further strengthened the long-standing partnership.

*Ambassador Busingye Joins Britons in Honoring War Heroes
LONDON, United Kingdom - Rwanda’s High Commissioner to the United Kingdom, Ambassador Johnston Busingye, on Sunday, November 9, 2025, joined the people of Britain in commemorating those who lost their lives in the First and Second World Wars and subsequent conflicts.
The solemn ceremony, held at Whitehall in London, brought together national leaders, diplomats, and citizens to pay tribute to the fallen. Among those in attendance were King Charles III, Prime Minister Keir Starmer, and members of the diplomatic corps, including Ambassador Busingye.
Similar remembrance services were also held across the United Kingdom - in Edinburgh, Belfast, and Cardiff - as part of the country’s annual Remembrance Sunday tradition. The commemoration included the laying of wreaths at the Cenotaph monument and a two-minute silence to honor the memory of soldiers who perished in both world wars and other military operations.

The United Kingdom observes Remembrance Sunday each year on the second Sunday of November, in conjunction with Armistice Day on November 11 - the date marking the end of World War I in 1918. This year’s commemoration carried special significance, coinciding with the 80th anniversary of the end of World War II.
Prime Minister Keir Starmer paid tribute to the courage and sacrifice of the armed forces, emphasizing their enduring legacy. “Today we remember the extraordinary sacrifices made by our military in the war and the conflicts that followed,” he said. “Their actions have helped us achieve the freedom we enjoy today.”
During both World Wars, Britain fought on the victorious side but paid a heavy price. The country lost an estimated 886,000 soldiers in World War I and 384,000 in World War II.
Ambassador Busingye’s participation underscores Rwanda’s solidarity with Commonwealth nations in honoring shared histories of service, sacrifice, and resilience.

*Unity and Reconciliation Among Rwandans Reach 95.3% in 2025 - Minister Bizimana
Kigali, Rwanda - Rwanda’s unity and reconciliation rate has reached 95.3% in 2025, according to findings announced by Dr. Jean Damascène Bizimana, Minister of National Unity and Civic Engagement. The figures come from a national survey on unity, reconciliation, and sustainability conducted every five years by the National Unity and Reconciliation Commission (NURC).
In 2020, the national reconciliation rate stood at 94.7%, showing continued progress over the past five years. The 2025 survey covered 12,300 participants - including 8,100 households and 4,200 institutions - and consisted of 123 questions aimed at capturing a comprehensive picture of Rwanda’s social cohesion.
Of the respondents, 52% were women and 48% men. Youth under 35 represented 47%, while 53% were older. Educational backgrounds varied: 38% had completed primary education, 26% secondary, 14% university, 2% vocational, while 21% had no formal education.
In terms of faith, the majority identified as Catholics (41%), followed by ADEPR (24%), Adventists (12%), Anglicans (8%), Presbyterians (3%), Muslims (3%), Methodists (2%), Baptists (2%), Jehovah’s Witnesses (1%), and non-religious (1%).
Key Findings
The study revealed that:
Unity stands at 95.6%,
Reconciliation at 95%, and
Sustainability at 90.8%.
Among the most influential factors promoting national unity were courage and patriotism (98.7%), traditional values and beliefs (97.9%), sense of belonging (96%), and shared national values (95.9%). Respondents also cited lessons learned from the Genocide against the Tutsi (92.6%) and the failure of divisive political parties before 1994 (91.9%) as key motivators for unity.
For reconciliation, the strongest pillars included good interpersonal relations (98.5%), personal freedom and belonging (95.9%), shared historical understanding (90.6%), and the importance of remembrance of the Genocide against the Tutsi (99%).
Strong National Pride
An overwhelming 100% of respondents said they are proud to be Rwandan, with Kinyarwanda cited as the backbone of national identity.Other highlights include:
98% believe there is political will to build unity;
98% are proud of Rwanda’s national heroes;
98% consider Itorero and Ndi Umunyarwanda civic education programs effective;
99% agree that the Genocide was planned;
97% view Rwandanness as the most important identity;
97% believe the current leadership governs without ethnic discrimination;
96% support teaching history to the youth;
93% say all Rwandans have equal access to national opportunities.
Remaining Challenges
Despite the progress, challenges persist.
23% of respondents acknowledged that traces of the Genocide ideology still exist in their communities.
38% believe some Genocide perpetrators remain attached to divisive beliefs.
34% think certain ex-prisoners who served sentences still harbor hatred.
40% view foreign-based social media platforms as harmful to national unity.
32% admitted lacking awareness about civic responsibility.
45% of Genocide survivors reported that they still carry psychological wounds.
Regional Performance
The Eastern Province led the unity index at 97.2%, followed by:
Northern Province (95.5%),
Southern Province (95.4%),
Western Province (94.49%), and
City of Kigali (93.7%).
Top-performing districts included Kayonza, Ngoma, Ruhango, Nyanza, and Kirehe, while Gisagara, Rulindo, Huye, Nyarugenge, and Rusizi ranked lower.
Way Forward
Minister Dr. Bizimana emphasized the need to strengthen unity programs, particularly among the youth. He called for renewed efforts to:
Expand education on Rwandan cultural values, history, and the Kinyarwanda language;
Encourage youth participation in unity-building activities starting from the family level;
Combat Genocide ideology domestically and abroad; and
Promote national cohesion programs among the Rwandan diaspora.
He concluded by reaffirming the government’s commitment to ensuring that Rwandan unity remains strong, inclusive, and enduring for generations to come.

*DRC Government and AFC/M23 Sign Principles Framework Toward Lasting Peace
Doha, Qatar - The Government of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and the AFC/M23 coalition have taken a significant step toward ending years of conflict in eastern Congo, signing a set of eight guiding principles designed to address the root causes of the crisis and pave the way for a comprehensive peace agreement.
The document was signed on November 15, 2025, in Doha, where negotiations have been facilitated by Qatar with support from the United States.
Benjamin Mbonimpa, Permanent Secretary of the AFC/M23, said the agreement outlines a structured roadmap for the peace process, with each principle to be discussed in detail under a predetermined timetable. Only after the issues under all eight principles are resolved, he said, will both parties proceed to signing a final, holistic peace agreement.
“There will be no change in the site or operations until these principles are fully discussed and a final decision is reached on the general peace agreement,” Mbonimpa said. “Our people, please be patient-the journey is still long.”
Key Principles of the Agreement
The signed framework includes the following core commitments:
Restoration of State Authority in Eastern DRC: Government institutions and public services are to be progressively re-established in the east, implemented jointly by the DRC Government and the AFC/M23.
Interim Security Measures: Both sides agreed to adopt temporary security arrangements aimed at maintaining public order and protecting civilians during the transition period.
Creation of a National Truth and Reconciliation Commission: The parties will establish an independent body mandated to uncover the truth about past abuses, promote reconciliation, investigate crimes, and outline mechanisms for reparations.
The signing ceremony was attended by Dr. Mohammed bin Abdulaziz bin Saleh Al-Khulaifi, Qatar’s Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, and Massad Boulos, the U.S. Presidential Advisor for African Affairs.
Boulos welcomed the progress but cautioned that substantial work remains before a definitive peace agreement can be concluded.
“It will take time, but not too long,” he said, urging continued commitment from both sides.
The principles agreement marks one of the most significant diplomatic developments in the DRC conflict in years, though the ultimate success of the process will depend on sustained compliance, trust-building, and the ability of both parties to implement the roadmap on the ground.

*Rwanda Calls for Shift From Military Power to Political Dialogue in UN Peacekeeping
New York - Rwanda has reiterated its long-standing position that effective peacekeeping must prioritize political solutions over military strength. Speaking before the UN General Assembly’s Fourth Committee (Special Political and Decolonization), Colonel Deo Mutabazi, Head of Military Operations at the Rwandan Embassy to the United Nations, argued that durable peace cannot be achieved through force alone.
Col. Mutabazi said the international community has traditionally assumed that protecting civilians requires large and heavily equipped military missions, but this approach has repeatedly proven insufficient.
“We believe that no capacity, technology, or even training can better protect civilians than investing in finding ways to achieve peace through political dialogue,” he said. “This is where many UN peacekeeping missions remain weak.”
He stressed that addressing the root causes of conflict - including mismanagement, corruption, inequality, discrimination, and poverty - is essential for sustainable peace. Military force, he noted, cannot resolve problems that are fundamentally political.
Col. Mutabazi also criticized the mismatch between the mandates of some UN peacekeeping missions and the realities on the ground. At times, he said, mission responsibilities contradict what is actually needed, leading to tensions with host countries and even making peacekeepers targets for local populations.
He warned that such contradictions often emerge when missions attempt to resolve political crises through military means, despite the availability of peaceful alternatives.
“It is time to rethink how peacekeeping mandates are designed,” he added, arguing that decisions dominated by powerful countries have created missions “like Christmas trees” - overloaded with tasks that benefit external actors more than the host nation.

KINSHASA, DRC - Congolese political analyst Me Jean Baptiste Gasominari has argued that areas under government control in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) are in greater need of humanitarian assistance than those administered by the rebel group AFC/M23. Speaking on national television following a recent conference on the Great Lakes Region’s security, he stated that territories held by M23 in the east are currently safer and more stable than those managed by Kinshasa.
The remarks came after a high-level meeting in France on October 30, 2025, where French President Emmanuel Macron announced plans to reopen Goma Airport within weeks to facilitate humanitarian flights twice a week. The move is aimed at boosting relief operations in eastern Congo amid ongoing conflict.
According to Me Gasominari, the M23 movement has restored order in the territories it controls, enabling displaced residents to return to their homes. “When AFC/M23 took over Goma and Bukavu in January, the population that had been forced into camps by government actions returned home almost immediately,” he said. “Today, visitors to these cities can see fields once again full of crops and communities rebuilding their lives.”
He accused the Congolese government of spreading misinformation about conditions in rebel-held areas to mislead the international community. “The DRC government used lies to convince people to move into camps, just to show the world that opponents had disrupted civilian life,” he said.
Gasominari acknowledged isolated tensions in M23-controlled zones, attributing them to government drone attacks and blockades that restrict access to markets and basic goods. However, he maintained that large-scale humanitarian needs are concentrated in government-held regions such as Beni, Butembo, Ituri, and Tshopo - areas plagued by violence from the Allied Democratic Forces (ADF) and other armed groups.
“People there are being attacked, displaced, and killed,” he emphasized. “Those are the regions that truly need urgent humanitarian assistance.”
He concluded by criticizing the government’s narrative on aid distribution. “The claim that help is needed in M23-held areas is a deliberate lie,” Gasominari said. “Kinshasa no longer even has accurate information about those territories.”
*Kamonyi Police Arrest Six Suspects for Theft and Armed Attacks
KAMONYI, RWANDA - Rwandan police in Kamonyi District have arrested six individuals, including five men and one woman, for allegedly carrying traditional weapons and committing theft and other acts threatening public security. The arrests took place during the night of November 1-2, 2025, in Gihinga Cell, Gacurabwenge Sector, with the assistance of local residents and authorities.
CIP Kamanzi Hassan, National Police Spokesperson for the Southern Province, told IGIHE that the suspects were primarily involved in livestock theft and housebreaking, armed with knives, machetes, axes, and other implements.
“Those arrested were found with some of the animals they had stolen, including 15 chickens and six rabbits,” CIP Kamanzi said. “They are now all detained at the Gacurabwenge Police Station, and the Criminal Investigation Department has begun tracking down others involved.”
CIP Kamanzi also urged residents to abandon criminal activities, warning that the police will prosecute and punish anyone involved in acts that threaten security and public peace.
He praised the community members who continue to cooperate with law enforcement by providing timely information and called on others to follow their example, emphasizing that maintaining security is a shared responsibility.
*Kenyan Defence Chief Visits Rwanda to Strengthen Military Cooperation
KIGALI, Rwanda - The Chief of the Kenyan Defence Forces (KDF), General Charles Muriu Kahariri, is on a working visit to Rwanda from November 4 to 7, 2025, aimed at reinforcing bilateral military relations between the two East African nations.
On Wednesday, Gen. Kahariri met with Rwandan Minister of Defence Juvenal Marizamunda and Rwanda Defence Force (RDF) Chief of Defence Staff, Gen. Mubarakh Muganga, at the Ministry of Defence and RDF Headquarters in Kigali. Discussions focused on reviewing ongoing defence cooperation, identifying new areas of collaboration, and exchanging views on regional security challenges.
During his visit, the Kenyan military chief also toured the Kigali Genocide Memorial, where he received a briefing on Rwanda’s history and paid tribute to the victims of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi.
The visit underscores the continued commitment of Kenya and Rwanda to regional peace, security, and defence cooperation, building on their shared efforts within the East African Community (EAC) and broader continental security frameworks.
*Rwanda Hosts 19th African Heads of Military Colleges Conference, Calls for Innovation and Unity in Security
Kigali, Rwanda - Rwanda’s Minister of Defence, Juvenal Marizamunda, has called for greater innovation, collaboration, and unity among African nations to effectively address the continent’s evolving security challenges.
Speaking at the opening of the 19th African Heads of Military Colleges Conference (ACoC) on Monday, November 10, 2025, in Kigali, Minister Marizamunda emphasized that Africa’s strength in defense and security depends on its capacity to innovate and work together.
“In today’s technologically advanced world, facing many security challenges, the capacity of our schools to innovate and collaborate is what determines our security strength as Africans,” he said. “We face many challenges including terrorism, cyber warfare, illicit trade, climate change, and migration. To overcome them, we need soldiers who are highly skilled, technologically literate, ethical, and forward-thinking.”
The three-day conference, which gathers delegates from 24 countries, is being held under the theme: “The Future of Military Training and Education in Africa: Bridging the Technology Gap.” Established to enhance knowledge-sharing and professional development across the continent’s defense institutions, ACoC serves as a vital platform for advancing military education and cooperation.
The meeting brought together heads of military colleges, representatives of the African Union Peace and Security Service, and regional defense experts.
Minister Marizamunda urged participants to ensure that their deliberations translate into tangible progress. “I urge all participants to ensure that your discussions are open and constructive, and that the decisions you make are practical and actionable. The future of military education in Africa will depend on what we agree on today and how we implement it,” he said.
He also praised the Rwanda Defence Force (RDF) Military Academy for its leadership during Rwanda’s tenure as chair of ACoC, noting the institution’s role in fostering unity and advancing continental security education. Rwanda is expected to hand over the chairmanship to Tanzania at the close of the conference.
Brig. Gen. Andrew Nyamvumba, Commandant of the RDF Military Academy, highlighted that ACoC serves not only as a network of military training institutions but also as a hub for the exchange of knowledge and strategic insight. “This conference is a platform for pooling African military expertise and wisdom. It is where ideas are shared, traditions are refined, and partnerships are strengthened to build a secure, resilient, and self-reliant Africa,” he said.
Incoming chair Maj. Gen. Stephen Mnkande, Commandant of the Tanzanian Military Academy, stressed the importance of strengthening technology-driven institutions to respond to modern security threats.
Similarly, Air Commodore (Brig. Gen.) N. Maghidir, Commandant of the Libyan Military Academy, underscored that cooperation must remain the cornerstone of Africa’s collective security.
Delegates attending the conference represent countries including Cameroon, Egypt, Guinea, Libya, Mauritania, Morocco, Namibia, Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Malawi, Kenya, Ghana, Tanzania, Botswana, Burundi, Algeria, Zimbabwe, Togo, Uganda, Zambia, the Central African Republic, Somalia, and Rwanda.
The conference is expected to conclude with key resolutions on strengthening military education systems and enhancing regional defense collaboration to ensure a safer and more self-reliant Africa.
*DRC and Burundi Accused of Increasing Troop Deployments in Fizi Highlands to Target Banyamulenge
Fizi, South Kivu (DRC) - The Movement for the Defense of the People - Twirwaneho (MRDP-Twirwaneho) has accused the governments of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Burundi of deploying additional troops to the highlands of Fizi Territory with the alleged aim of targeting the Banyamulenge community.
In a statement released this week, the group claimed that more than 10 Burundian battalions are currently operating in the highlands of Fizi, Uvira, and Mwenga, and that these forces have launched “serious attacks” against Banyamulenge civilians since early last week.
According to MRDP-Twirwaneho, the deployment includes two Burundian battalions and one Congolese battalion (the 3303rd), all reportedly led by Gen. Chivire. The units are said to be stationed at “Point-Zéro,” a key military position at the entrance to Minembwe, a town predominantly inhabited by the Banyamulenge.
Twirwaneho further alleged that the Burundian troops had crossed into DRC territory via Lake Tanganyika, using a military vessel. The group claimed the troops came from several Burundian army divisions commanded by senior officers, including Brig. Gen. Hakizimana Pontien (1st Division), Brig. Gen. Ndenzako Michel (2nd Division), Brig. Gen. Habarugira Jean-Luc (3rd Division), and Brig. Gen. Manirakiza Désiré, known as Gacanga (4th Division).
“The increase in Burundian troops in Fizi shows that the military cooperation between the DRC and Burundi governments paves the way for the continued deployment of Burundian troops in the highlands in order to commit genocide against the Banyamulenge,” the statement read.
Minembwe and the surrounding highlands have been the scene of repeated violence targeting the Banyamulenge community - a Tutsi-speaking minority - since 2017. The attacks have reportedly displaced more than 328,000 people, destroyed over 550 villages, and led to widespread looting of livestock and the destruction of infrastructure including schools and health centers.
MRDP-Twirwaneho accuses the Congolese and Burundian armies, along with local Wazalendo militias and members of the FDLR (Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda), of sharing what it describes as “an ideology of hatred against the Tutsi” and coordinating assaults on Banyamulenge communities.
Neither the DRC nor Burundian authorities have issued an official response to the allegations.
The situation in Fizi remains tense, as reports of military movements and renewed clashes continue to raise fears of further humanitarian crises in South Kivu’s highlands.
*DRC Forces Accused of Launching Drone Strikes on Banyamulenge Communities in South Kivu
South Kivu, DRC - Armed forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC) have been accused of launching drone attacks on Mikenke, a village in Mwenga Territory, South Kivu Province, in an alleged attempt to target Banyamulenge civilians and leaders in displacement camps.
The strikes were reported at 8:00 a.m. on November 15, 2025, according to Congolese politician Moïse Nyarugabo, himself a member of the Banyamulenge community.
“Tshisekedi’s drones targeted the village of Mikenke, which is inhabited by civilians, and also hosts a camp for Banyamulenge whose villages were burned,” Nyarugabo said.“The numbers are not yet known, but there is no doubt that civilians must have been there.”
More Attacks Allegedly Planned
Nyarugabo further claimed to have “reliable information” indicating that FARDC forces, along with Burundian troops, Wazalendo militias, and the FDLR, are preparing new offensives in Minembwe and Mikenke, areas heavily populated by Banyamulenge communities.
He cited military movements in several locations - Point-Zéro, Mikalati, Kigazura, Marunde, Kipupu, Rugezi, and Mukoko - as evidence of imminent operations.
“Two generals, one Congolese and the other Burundian, are in that area preparing for the offensives. Unless the plan changes at the last minute, those who survived the Sukhoi and drone attacks may be bombed again,” he warned.
Years of Targeted Violence
The Banyamulenge, a Tutsi-speaking minority community in South Kivu, have faced sustained attacks since 2017 from a coalition of armed groups that local activists say operates with government support.
According to the MRDP Twirwaneho movement, which advocates for Banyamulenge rights:
328,000+ people have been displaced
550 villages destroyed
Nearly 140 schools burned
41 health centres demolished
126+ churches attacked
More than 500,000 cattle looted
Since October 16, residents of Minembwe have reportedly been cut off from market routes, accused by Burundian forces of “collaborating with enemies.”
Human rights observers warn that escalating military operations in South Kivu risk worsening an already dire humanitarian situation, while increasing the vulnerability of the Banyamulenge population, many of whom have been repeatedly displaced over the past decade.
*Rwanda Receives Seven FDLR Fighters Repatriated by MONUSCO
Rwanda has received seven former FDLR combatants and their family members repatriated from the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) by the UN peacekeeping mission, MONUSCO, on November 10, 2025.
The group had spent three days at a transit centre in Goma for ex-combatants wishing to lay down their arms before being returned to Rwanda.
According to MONUSCO, a total of 49 Rwandans were repatriated on the same day. Mission officials said the individuals expressed relief at returning home and urged others still in the bush to surrender.
From January to October 2025, MONUSCO repatriated 54 Rwandan fighters, mostly affiliated with the FDLR, along with 44 accompanying family members.
One of the returnees described life in the bush as “difficult,” appealing to remaining fighters to surrender so they too could return home safely.
Upon arrival in Rwanda, ex-combatants are transferred to the Rwanda Demobilisation and Reintegration Commission (RDRC) centre in Mutobo, Musanze District. There, they undergo several months of training to prepare them for reintegration into civilian life.
On October 30, the RDRC released 143 former FDLR members who had completed three to six months of reintegration training. Officials said efforts to support all who choose peaceful return will continue.
