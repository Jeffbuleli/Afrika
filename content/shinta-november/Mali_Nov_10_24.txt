*ECOWAS and the UN Warn of Existential Terrorist Threat in West AfricaDate: November 18, 2025
The rise of terrorism in West Africa dominated debates at the United Nations Security Council in New York, where UN Secretary-General António Guterres and the President of the ECOWAS Commission, Omar Alieu Touray, jointly sounded the alarm.
Their shared message: the threat is no longer confined to the Sahel or the Lake Chad Basin. It has spread across the entire West African region, posing an existential risk to millions of people.
Guterres emphasized that terrorism, once geographically localized, now strikes across a much wider arc, affecting both rural areas and strategic routes. In recent months, armed groups have repeatedly attacked escorted military convoys, killing and abducting soldiers and civilians. These actions are intended not only to spread terror but also to deliberately disrupt access to fuel, goods, and essential services, creating what he described as an “economic war”.
The consequences are severe: fuel shortages have forced some humanitarian operations, including those run by the UN, to scale back activities, threatening vulnerable populations. Guterres warned that the combination of insecurity, shortages, and the collapse of services could trigger a “catastrophic domino effect” on coastal countries such as Benin and Togo.
In response, ECOWAS Defense and Finance Ministers have considered establishing a regional intervention force, with an initial deployment of 1,650 troops and plans to scale rapidly to 5,000 soldiers. Guterres stressed that close cooperation between states is essential to strengthen the region’s security and political architecture.
Omar Alieu Touray reinforced this warning with sobering figures: between January and November 2025, the region experienced 450 terrorist attacks, resulting in over 1,900 deaths. He described terrorism as an existential threat to West Africa, highlighting the fragmentation of regional efforts, mistrust between states, and insufficient international funding.
Touray explained that extremist groups aim to isolate populations by blocking fuel access, disrupting trade, and cutting supply routes, thereby weakening communities and complicating security responses. He called for sustainable solutions that address root causes, including poverty, lack of public services, weak governance, and marginalization.
Both officials concluded that the region urgently needs consistent international support, particularly predictable humanitarian funding. Needs for 2025 amount to $4.9 billion, yet only one-quarter is currently covered. They urged strengthened cooperation, better coordination among regional forces, and renewed political commitment to prevent the crisis from spreading further across the continent.

*Confederation of Sahel States (AES) Calls for Sovereign-Led Regional Cooperation Against Terrorism
New York, United Nations Security Council - November 18, 2025
At a public briefing on “Peacebuilding in West Africa: Revitalizing Regional Cooperation in the Fight Against Terrorism in West Africa and the Sahel”, a representative of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES) delivered a statement on behalf of the foreign ministers of Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger, who were unable to attend due to scheduling conflicts.
The representative commended the leadership of Sierra Leone President Julius Maada Bio, chairing the Security Council, for convening the debate on revitalizing regional cooperation to combat terrorism in West Africa and the Sahel. Scope of the ThreatThe AES emphasized the escalating terrorist threat in the Sahel, highlighting that for over a decade, communities across the region have been subjected to violence imposed and supported by external actors. Women, children, and civilians have been killed, kidnapped, or forced into exile, while schools and health centers have come under attack. In response to the increasing inability of local Defense and Security Forces (FDS) to contain these threats, criminal networks have turned to economic terrorism, targeting civilian infrastructure and supply routes.
Formation of the AES : To confront these challenges, the leaders of Burkina Faso (Captain Ibrahim Traoré), Mali (General Assimi Goïta), and Niger (General Abdourahamane Tiani) have sovereignly established the Confederation of Sahel States (AES). This alliance aims to coordinate defense, diplomatic, and development efforts, creating a unified front against criminal and extremist organizations.
Defense and Security Efforts : The representative paid tribute to the AES Defense and Security Forces, noting their courage and sacrifices in safeguarding territories, people, and property. However, the statement emphasized that counter-terrorism must remain a regional and international effort, conducted with respect for the sovereignty of member states, strategic choices, and national interests.
Need for a New Approach to Regional Cooperation : The AES highlighted the necessity of restoring trust among West African actors and developing a common understanding of the terrorist threat. The representative called for a departure from approaches characterized by external interference, sanctions, and threats of military action, advocating instead for constructive dialogue, regional coordination, and intelligence sharing.
International Cooperation : The AES stressed that United Nations mechanisms, including the Security Council and Secretary-General, must adopt concrete, rapid, and tailored responses to terrorism. The representative criticized past UN missions, citing MINUSMA in Mali, whose mandates were unsuited to host-country security needs. The statement further underscored the need to combat terrorist financing, particularly ransom payments, and to mobilize humanitarian aid for affected populations, noting that only 14% of Sahel humanitarian needs are currently met.
Media and Information Warfare : The AES strongly denounced what it called “daily media terrorism”, accusing certain outlets of glorifying extremists and spreading propaganda to destabilize the region.
Conclusion : The representative concluded by honoring all victims of terrorism, both civilian and military, including Mariam Cissé, recently murdered by extremists. He reaffirmed the AES member states’ unwavering determination to combat terrorism and violent extremism and praised the resilience of the Sahelian people in the face of ongoing threats.

*Mali Government and Oil Operators Move to Resolve Fuel Supply BottlenecksBamako - November 20-21, 2025
As part of ongoing efforts to stabilize fuel distribution, the sixth weekly meeting with petroleum operators was held on November 20, 2025, under the chairmanship of the Minister of Industry and Culture, in the presence of the Director General in charge of Commerce, the Director of CECOGEC, and the Deputy Director General of the Petroleum Department.
The meeting focused on weekly monitoring of fuel supply measures and the evaluation of proposed solutions to address persistent logistical challenges. The Minister reported that after more than a month of stringent monitoring, a key indicator confirms a gradual normalization of the fuel supply: all but one tanker truck have successfully delivered fuel, indicating that petroleum is now entering the country regularly, with sufficient volumes available.
Despite this technical normalization, lines at gas stations remain long, highlighting an administrative bottleneck that slows operations and limits efficient distribution.
To address this, the government announced the upcoming conclusion of a memorandum of understanding with major oil companies, aimed at accelerating fuel supply management. The agreement will reduce processing times at the petroleum customs service from 72 hours to 24 hours, ensuring that fuel reaches consumers more quickly.
Officials emphasized that this structural reform will alleviate congestion at service stations, maintain a continuous supply via tanker trucks, and guarantee that all available fuel reaches users without delay. The memorandum of understanding is scheduled for signing on Friday, November 21, 2025, marking a decisive step in stabilizing Mali’s fuel distribution system amid ongoing security and logistical challenges.

*HRW Reports Civilian Casualties from Malian Drone StrikesMali, November 18, 2025
Human Rights Watch (HRW) has documented a deadly drone strike on a Tuareg family in the village of Tangatta, Timbuktu region, on the night of November 13. The attack killed seven civilians, including five children, leaving only a few survivors. The organization warns that these attacks could constitute war crimes, as international humanitarian law prohibits strikes targeting civilians or failing to take precautions to protect them. HRW calls on the Malian authorities to:
Launch independent investigations into the strikes;
Hold those responsible accountable;
Provide compensation to victims’ families;
Cease illegal drone operations against civilian areas.
This report underscores the humanitarian toll of escalating military operations in Mali and highlights the urgent need for compliance with international law to protect civilians.
*Mali: HRW Reports Army and Dozo Militias Kill Civilians in Ségou RegionNovember 18, 2025
Human Rights Watch (HRW) has documented two deadly operations by the Malian army and Dozo militias in the Ségou region, which killed at least 31 civilians and destroyed multiple homes. The attacks occurred on October 2 and October 13, 2025, targeting the villages of Kamona and Ballé.
The Dozos, traditionally hunters, have increasingly acted as auxiliaries to the Malian army in operations against JNIM. According to HRW, both attacks took place in areas where no jihadists were present, raising serious concerns about indiscriminate violence against civilians.
October 2 - Kamona: A large Malian military convoy, supported by Dozos on motorcycles, entered the village. October 13 - Ballé: A similar operation occurred, with soldiers and Dozos arriving in pickup trucks and on motorcycles. Residents described living under JNIM-imposed taxes, which the army reportedly interpreted as complicity with jihadists.
HRW contacted the Malian authorities for comment but received no response. The army claimed it had neutralized around 20 terrorists in Ballé, a version that contradicts witness accounts.
The NGO called for an independent investigation, stressing that the Malian armed forces must distinguish between civilians and combatants. Failure to do so, HRW warns, could constitute serious violations of international humanitarian law.

*Côte d’Ivoire Grants Refugee Status to Nearly 80,000 Burkinabe and Malian NationalsCôte d’Ivoire, November 21, 2025
In a significant humanitarian development, Côte d’Ivoire has officially granted refugee status to over 69,000 Burkinabe and 1,500 Malian nationals, bringing the total recognized refugee population in the country to nearly 80,000, up from just 2,500 previously. The decision, formalized through an interministerial decree on July 2, 2025, received minimal media attention and was only publicly noted on November 20 via a UNHCR technical dashboard.
This measure applies to asylum seekers registered from May 2021 onwards, primarily individuals who fled violence in southern Burkina Faso and certain regions of Mali to seek safety in northern Côte d’Ivoire. The newly recognized population is highly vulnerable, having left their homes due to escalating conflict and security threats in the Sahel.
According to the UNHCR, official refugee status grants access to identity documents and special passports, facilitating administrative procedures and legal travel. While Ivorian law from 2023 already guaranteed certain rights, including the right to work, this decree now provides clear and formal legal recognition of these protections.
The Daara-the Directorate of Aid and Assistance to Refugees and Stateless Persons-is tasked with implementing humanitarian programs for the newly recognized refugees. These initiatives include the distribution of aid, support with administrative and social procedures, and integration services. Refugee status also opens the possibility of a voluntary, assisted return to the country of origin, although current security conditions in the Sahel make such returns highly challenging.
By granting this legal recognition, Côte d’Ivoire provides the refugees with temporary protection, stability, and access to essential services, while reinforcing its role as a safe haven amid regional turmoil.

Conclusion: Escalating Crisis and Sovereign Resilience in Mali (November 10-23, 2025)
The period from November 10 to 23, 2025, in Mali was defined by a severe escalation of the security crisis and a dual effort by the transitional government to assert national sovereignty while addressing critical logistical and humanitarian challenges. The nation faced what multiple international actors described as an existential terrorist threat.
Major Findings
Intensified Security Crisis: The terrorist group JNIM (GSIM/Al-Qaeda affiliate) escalated its economic warfare, announcing the second phase of its fuel and supply blockade against Bamako and threatening to treat tanker truck drivers as combatants. This action coincided with multiple international warnings and evacuations of citizens by countries including Portugal, Norway, Denmark, and Sweden.
Civilian Harm and Human Rights Violations: Military operations, including those by the Malian army and Russian mercenaries, led to documented civilian casualties. Human Rights Watch (HRW) reported on deadly drone strikes that killed Tuareg civilians, including children, in the Timbuktu region , and lethal operations by the Malian army and Dozo militias in the Ségou region. JNIM also condemned the increased civilian targeting, particularly by Turkish drone strikes, as a "scorched-earth strategy".
Government Response and Asserting Sovereignty: Malian authorities consistently emphasized strategic autonomy and sovereign choice in defense and partnerships. Foreign Minister Abdoulaye Diop condemned "hostile media campaigns" and "destabilizing actions" while reaffirming the commitment of the Malian Defense and Security Forces (FDS) to securing the territory.
Logistical Breakthroughs Amid Shortages: Despite JNIM's blockade and persistent long lines at gas stations, the government reported a gradual normalization of the fuel supply due to successful deliveries. Officials are implementing a memorandum of understanding with oil companies to reduce customs processing time from 72 to 24 hours to alleviate distribution bottlenecks. Collaboration with Senegal was also initiated to unblock the critical Dakar-Bamako trade corridor.
Partnerships and Geopolitical Engagement:
- New Ties: Mali welcomed a Belarusian business delegation to explore investment in strategic sectors. Russia affirmed its commitment to supporting Mali against the fuel crisis, deploying the Army Corps for Africa to provide security and convoy escorts.
- Regional Cooperation: The Confederation of Sahel States (AES)-Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger-called for sovereign-led regional cooperation against terrorism and formally signed a radio frequency coordination agreement to enhance digital integration.
- International Dialogue: Minister Diop held constructive discussions with the UK, which praised Mali's efforts to restore normalcy , and reaffirmed Mali’s commitment to the UN, emphasizing operational autonomy of its armed forces.
Outlook
Mali is simultaneously battling a sophisticated terrorist insurgency and an international perception crisis. The government’s assertion of self-reliance and strategic autonomy is evident in its partnerships and its rejection of claims that Bamako is under threat. However, the economic strain from the blockade, the humanitarian toll from violence (including the displacement of thousands of refugees to Côte d'Ivoire ), and the allegations of severe human rights violations risk undermining this narrative of resilience.
The long-term success of Mali’s strategy will depend on its ability to translate technical improvements in fuel logistics into stable public services, ensure the protection of its civilian population from all actors, and achieve an inclusive political path forward, as urged by the UN and Algeria.
