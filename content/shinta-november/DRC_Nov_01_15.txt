Bukavu, 15 November 2025.
Report on the situation in the DRC from 01 to 15 November 2025
Diplomacy and politics
*DRC: European Union Urges Immediate Opening of Humanitarian Corridor in the East
Kinshasa, DRC - The European Union (EU) has called for the urgent establishment of a humanitarian corridor in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, to enable the delivery of aid and the resumption of humanitarian flights to conflict-affected areas.
The appeal was made on Wednesday, November 5, by Johan Borgstram, the EU’s Special Representative for the Great Lakes region, during a visit to Kinshasa. Borgstram voiced deep concern over the resurgence of violence linked to the M23 rebellion, which he said is supported by Rwanda and constitutes a violation of Congolese territorial integrity.
While commending the diplomatic efforts of Qatar and the United States to revive peace talks, Borgstram urged all parties to respect the ceasefire and adhere to the principles of international humanitarian law.
The EU envoy is expected to continue his mission in Kampala, where he will hold regional consultations aimed at restoring stability in the Great Lakes region.
*Kinshasa Hosts 31st ICGLR Meeting on Combating Illegal Exploitation of Natural Resources
Kinshasa, DRC - The capital hosted the 31st meeting of the Regional Committee of the International Conference on the Great Lakes Region (ICGLR) on November 7-8, 2025, bringing together government officials, civil society representatives, and technical partners to tackle the illegal exploitation of natural resources.
Organized under the auspices of the Congolese Minister of Mines, the forum focused on coordinating regional policies, strengthening security cooperation, and establishing a common legal framework. Participants also examined the links between illegal mining and armed conflict, aiming to ensure that strategic resources such as coltan, cobalt, and tin contribute to sustainable development.
The ICGLR meeting comes three weeks after the National Forum on socio-economic and mining issues, which addressed peace agreements and resource management in the DRC and the wider Great Lakes region.
*Doha: President Tshisekedi Meets Emir of Qatar to Discuss DRC Peace Process
Doha, Qatar - On November 4, 2025, on the sidelines of the second World Summit for Social Development, Congolese President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi met with the Emir of Qatar, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, to review progress in the Democratic Republic of Congo’s peace process, particularly in the conflict-affected eastern regions.
The meeting also focused on strengthening bilateral relations between Kinshasa and Doha ahead of the Emir’s planned visit to the DRC. Both leaders reaffirmed their commitment to promoting peace, social development, and mutually beneficial economic cooperation.
*Doha: President Tshisekedi Highlights Congolese Resilience and Calls for Equitable International Cooperation
Doha, Qatar - On the sidelines of the second World Summit on Social Development in Doha, Congolese President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi met with the Emir of Qatar, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, to review peace processes in the Democratic Republic of Congo and strengthen bilateral ties.
At the summit, President Tshisekedi lauded the resilience of the Congolese people after three decades of armed conflict and outlined reforms aimed at promoting social justice, including universal health coverage, free primary education, and a development program covering all 145 territories of the country.
He also called for international cooperation grounded in equity and reciprocity, advocating for a new global framework where social justice and lasting peace are recognized as fundamental rights for all peoples.
*DRC: Parliamentary Concerns Grow Over Delay in Reviewing 2026 Budget
Kinshasa, DRC - With less than 45 days remaining in the ordinary parliamentary session, which is primarily dedicated to budget matters, delays in reviewing the 2026 draft finance law are raising alarm among lawmakers and civil society.
The election of a new Speaker of the National Assembly, scheduled for November 13, has contributed to concerns over institutional paralysis. Christian Mwando of the opposition party Ensemble pour la République blames the delay on “political distractions” linked to internal petitions, while Gary Sakata of the ruling Sacred Union highlights that the law allows 60 days for both chambers to adopt the budget.
Parliamentary sources indicate that the review of the draft law is expected to begin only after the installation of the new leadership, seen as a prerequisite for the effective resumption of parliamentary work.
*Maniema: Vice-President of Provincial Assembly Re-elected Amid Impeachment Motion
Kindu, Maniema, DRC - Théophile Buleli Docta, Vice-President of the Maniema Provincial Assembly, retained his position after a motion of impeachment filed by three members of his own party was rejected.
Accused of holding multiple positions deemed incompatible with his parliamentary responsibilities, the Kabambare representative received broad support from provincial deputies during the plenary session on November 4: 17 votes against the motion, 3 in favor, and one abstention.
According to Assembly rapporteur Papy Omeonga Tchopa, the complaints appeared to stem more from internal party disputes than from Buleli’s institutional duties, underscoring the renewed confidence of the assembly in its vice-president.
*London: Prime Minister Suminwa Calls for Peace and Equitable Partnerships in Eastern DRC
London, UK - On the sidelines of the CNN Global Perspectives on Africa conference on November 3, Congolese Prime Minister Judith Suminwa Tuluka outlined her government’s vision centered on peace, good governance, and sustainable resource management.
Addressing ongoing insecurity in eastern DRC, she held Rwanda responsible for the capture of Goma and Bukavu by M23 rebels, while stressing that achieving lasting peace remains the top priority of her mandate after three decades of deadly conflict.
Prime Minister Suminwa also called for equitable economic partnerships, highlighting that the DRC’s mineral resources should benefit local development, support transparent supply chains, and create jobs for Congolese youth.
*DRC: National Assembly to Elect New Speaker on November 13
Kinshasa, DRC - Jacques Djoli, rapporteur of the National Assembly, announced on November 4 that the election of the new Speaker of the lower house, along with the deputy rapporteur, is scheduled for November 13, 2025.
The decision, made during a meeting of the bureau and the conference of presidents, sets the stage for the succession of Vital Kamerhe. MP Aimé Boji has been designated as the sole candidate of the Sacred Union of the Nation for the Speaker position.

*DRC: Interior Minister Shabani Calls for Suspension of No-Confidence Motions During Wartime
Kinshasa, DRC - On November 5, 2025, Interior Minister Jacquemain Shabani urged the suspension of no-confidence motions and petitions in provincial assemblies during wartime, emphasizing the need to preserve institutional stability amid recurring political crises.
Speaking at a meeting with governors, vice-governors, and members of provincial assembly bureaus, Shabani called on officials to ensure transparent and rigorous management of public resources and to maintain continuous dialogue with their assemblies. He also warned that the central government could invoke strong constitutional measures to prevent situations of ungovernability.
*Doha Hosts Framework Agreement Between Congolese Government and AFC/M23 to Restart Peace Talks
The Government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the AFC/M23 rebel movement have signed a framework agreement in Doha aimed at reviving negotiations toward a comprehensive peace deal.
The agreement, reached on Saturday, November 15, outlines both the methodology and the timeline for upcoming discussions. Although the full text is still being finalized, the document calls for the drafting-within two weeks-of several protocols that will guide talks on key issues including humanitarian access, security arrangements, disarmament and reintegration (DDR), the restoration of state authority, the return of displaced populations, economic recovery, and transitional justice.
It also highlights the need to address the root causes of the conflict, such as discrimination, national cohesion, and inclusive governance.
Despite this diplomatic breakthrough, the situation on the ground in North and South Kivu remains volatile. Clashes continue between the FARDC and AFC/M23, with both sides accusing each other of violating ceasefire commitments.
*National Assembly: Aimé Boji Elected President, Clotilde Mutita Becomes Deputy Rapporteur
The National Assembly of the Democratic Republic of the Congo has elected Aimé Boji Sangara as the new President of the Lower House, following a vote held on Thursday, November 13, at the People’s Palace in Kinshasa. Boji secured 413 votes out of 423, succeeding Vital Kamerhe.
The interim president of the chamber, Jean-Claude Tshilumbayi, officially confirmed Boji’s election in line with the Assembly’s rules of procedure.
During the same plenary session, opposition lawmaker Clotilde Mutita Kalunga was elected Deputy Rapporteur of the Bureau. She received 389 votes out of 424, taking over from Dominique Munongo.
*Kinshasa: Félix Tshisekedi and Isaac Herzog Strengthen DRC-Israel Cooperation
President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi on Tuesday, November 11, received his Israeli counterpart Isaac Herzog in Kinshasa for a working visit aimed at deepening bilateral relations and promoting a mutually beneficial partnership between the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Israel.
During a joint press briefing, Tshisekedi highlighted the investment opportunities available to Israeli companies in the mining, infrastructure, education, and health sectors. He also reaffirmed the DRC’s support for Israel and its security.
For his part, Isaac Herzog emphasized the historical and spiritual ties connecting the two nations and praised ongoing international efforts - particularly those of U.S. President Donald Trump - to advance peace in Central Africa, especially in the eastern DRC.
This meeting marks another step in the strengthening of diplomatic, economic, and security cooperation between Kinshasa and Tel-Aviv.
*Boma: The FCDHD Rejects Suspension of Motions in Provincial Assemblies
The Fédération congolaise des droits de l’homme et de développement (FCDHD) has voiced strong opposition to the Ministry of Interior and Security’s decision to suspend motions within provincial assemblies. The reaction was delivered on Tuesday, November 11, during a public statement in Boma.
According to Gabriel Makiese, a senior official within the FCDHD, the measure encourages poor governance and opaque management of resources in Kongo Central by restricting the provincial assembly’s essential oversight and accountability role over the executive.
Makiese called on Vice Prime Minister Jacquemain Shabani to reverse the decision and urged provincial lawmakers to prioritize the interests of the province over personal or political agendas. He also condemned what he described as interference from Kinshasa in local governance.
*DRC: Kinshasa and French Delegation Discuss MONUSCO Mandate Renewal

*Kinshasa: Judith Suminwa Opens 19th ICGLR Meeting, Calls for Regional Action on Peace
Congolese Prime Minister Judith Suminwa Tuluka opened the 19th meeting of the ICGLR Regional Inter-Ministerial Committee in Kinshasa on Thursday, November 13, ahead of the Summit of Heads of State. The gathering took place amid high regional tensions.
Held under the theme “Consolidating Peace and Security for Sustainable Development in the Great Lakes Region,” the meeting provided a platform for the Prime Minister and Minister of Regional Integration, Floribert Anzuluni, to reaffirm commitments under the 2006 Nairobi Pact and denounce alleged armed aggression against the DRC by a member state.
Both officials stressed the urgency of turning political commitments into concrete actions, urging member states to strengthen the regional monitoring mechanism to foster effective cooperation for peace, security, and development.
The session brought together high-level regional and international officials, setting the stage for the ICGLR Summit, scheduled for November 15.
*DRC: Joris Salden Warns Against Fraudulent Schengen Visa Agencies
During a visit to Kinshasa on Thursday, November 13, 2025, Joris Salden, Director General of Consular Affairs at the Belgian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, issued a warning to Schengen visa applicants about the fraudulent practices of some intermediary agencies.
Salden highlighted that certain agencies demand exorbitant fees for appointments or application processing, despite having no access to the official appointment booking platforms. He emphasized that the European Visa Center (EVC) cannot sell appointment slots, making such claims technically impossible.
He also denounced the use of forged documents by these agencies, which often results in visa refusals and penalties for applicants.
Salden urged applicants to follow official procedures through the CEV website and to report fraud when encountered. He reminded the public that the official cost of a Schengen visa is 90 euros, far below the inflated charges demanded by unscrupulous intermediaries.

*Ituri: Hundreds of Trucks Stranded as Bunia-Mahagi Road Crumbles
Bunia, Ituri, DRC - On Wednesday, November 5, 2025, hundreds of trucks carrying petroleum and essential goods were stranded on National Route 27 (RN27) between Bunia and Mahagi, as the deteriorating Pont Tchulu-Jina section, about 40 kilometers north of Bunia, became virtually impassable following heavy rains.
The quagmire threatens the timely supply of essential goods to the region, sparking concern among local communities and traders. *DRC: SEGUSE Digitizes Import-Export Numbers to Streamline Foreign Trade
Kinshasa, DRC - On November 5, 2025, the Integrated Single Window for Foreign Trade (SEGUSE) launched the digitization of procedures for obtaining and renewing import-export numbers in the Democratic Republic of Congo.
Under the new system, economic operators can now submit applications online through the SEGUSE digital platform, receive payment notifications from the General Directorate of Administrative and State Revenue (DGRAD), make payments via bank transfer, and obtain their numbers directly in their accounts.
Officials say the reform will reduce processing delays, minimize errors, and prevent the loss of documents. The initiative is part of broader efforts to modernize foreign trade, improve the business climate, and encourage compliance, with authorities warning that non-compliance may result in legal penalties.
*South Kivu: AFC-M23 Permanently Shuts Lomera Mining Site
Lomera, South Kivu, DRC - The Lomera mining site in the Luhihi group, north of Kabare territory, was permanently closed on November 4, 2025, by the armed group AFC-M23. Artisanal gold miners were ordered to dismantle equipment, including crushers and motorized pumps, effectively halting operations.
The reasons behind the closure have not been disclosed, but the decision has sparked concern among residents and local stakeholders. The citizen movement Pamoja kwa Amani highlighted that the site was a critical source of income for thousands of families.
Experts warn that the shutdown could worsen poverty and unemployment in the region, with potential social repercussions, including increased crime and violence. Many miners who had taken loans to invest in the operation are now left vulnerable and financially exposed.

*DRC Introduces $30 Tax on International Flights in Addition to Go-Pass
Kinshasa, DRC - The Congolese government has implemented a new $30 tax on all international flights, both arrivals and departures, in addition to the existing Go-Pass fee. The announcement follows a partnership signed on October 18, 2025, with U.S.-based Securiport LLC, a company specializing in securing transportation infrastructure.
Under the agreement, 15% of the revenue will go to the Congolese state, while 85% will fund the private partner’s implementation of modern security systems designed to prevent document fraud and counter transnational threats.
Officials, including Jacquemain Shabani and Jean-Pierre Bemba, said the initiative aims to strengthen border controls and bring the DRC’s aviation security in line with international standards. With the new tax, total airport fees for a round-trip international flight now reach $115.
*DRC: Mukoko Samba Assures Senate on Congolese Franc Stability and Protecting Purchasing Power
Kinshasa, DRC - On November 4, 2025, Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of National Economy Daniel Mukoko Samba reassured the Senate of the government’s commitment to maintaining the stability of the Congolese franc and safeguarding citizens’ purchasing power.

*DRC, IMF Conclude Second Review of Preliminary Agreement Amid Economic Resilience
Kinshasa, DRC - On November 5, 2025, the Congolese government and International Monetary Fund (IMF) staff concluded the second review of their Preliminary Agreement, following a mission led by Calixte Ahokpossi. Discussions were also held in Haut-Katanga province.
The review highlighted the resilience of the Congolese economy, with projected growth exceeding 5% for 2025 and 2026 and inflation reduced to 2.5% in October. Both parties praised fiscal discipline, coordinated economic policies, and accelerated structural reforms, while noting the continuing impact of conflict in the eastern regions on public finances and humanitarian needs.
A report summarizing the findings will be submitted to the IMF Executive Board for consideration under the Extended Credit Facility (ECF) and the Climate Resilience Facility (CRF).

Officials emphasized that ongoing efforts aim to strengthen economic resilience and sustain the downward trend in inflation.

*Kinshasa Floods Paralyze Neighborhoods After Torrential Rains
Kinshasa, DRC - Torrential rains on Tuesday, November 4, 2025, caused severe flooding across multiple neighborhoods of Kinshasa, disrupting traffic and daily life for residents.
In Lingwala, the districts of Lokole, Niangwe, and Kalembelembe were submerged, while in Kalamu, overflowing rivers and accumulated debris intensified the situation. Local authorities are reportedly assessing the damage and coordinating emergency responses to restore normalcy in the affected areas.

*DRC: English to Be Introduced as Oral Exam in Baccalaureate from 2026
Kinshasa, DRC - The Ministry of National Education and New Citizenship has announced that an oral English exam will be officially introduced in the baccalaureate starting in 2026. The reform will apply to all humanities tracks and aims to strengthen English proficiency among Congolese students, in line with Framework Law No. 14/004 of February 11, 2014, governing national education.
Exam boards for the oral English test will follow the same model as the oral French exam, with equivalent weighting, and results will be recorded using form “E80.” Authorities describe the move as a significant step toward modernizing the Congolese education system and better preparing young people for a globalized academic and professional environment.
*Kinshasa: Torrential Rains on November 12-14 Cause Deaths, Floods, and Major Damage
The Congolese capital, Kinshasa, was hit by torrential rains on the night of Tuesday, November 12, into Wednesday, November 13, and again on Friday, November 14, 2025. The storms resulted in at least two deaths, left many homeless, and caused significant material damage.
Neighborhoods situated below the city limits, including Limete, Selembao, Bumbu, Masina, N’djili, and Kintambo, were particularly affected. The flooding, worsened by the Ndjili, Nsanga, and Makelele rivers, inadequate drainage systems, and sand accumulation in riverbeds, damaged houses, plots of land, markets, and major infrastructure, including two bridges between Kauka and Matonge. Some roads became impassable, forcing residents of the Ndanu neighborhood to seek refuge on their rooftops.
These recurring floods highlight the population’s vulnerability due to poor drainage infrastructure and climate change, emphasizing the need for urgent action by authorities to protect exposed areas and mitigate humanitarian and material impacts.

Heavy rains that struck Kasai-Central earlier this week caused significant damage, notably cutting the railway line in Benaleka, Demba territory, paralyzing rail traffic between Kasai and Kasai-Central.

*DRC: Dollar Depreciation Fails to Lower Prices of Basic Necessities
Nearly a month after the US dollar fell against the Congolese franc, prices for basic necessities in Kinshasa’s markets remain largely unchanged, according to observations on Monday, November 10, 2025, in the Ngaliema district.
While some fresh products like fish and meat have seen slight decreases, other staples-including cassava flour, plantains, and vegetables-have not benefited, and certain items, such as soap, have even increased in price.
Traders attribute this to the lack of agricultural infrastructure and high transportation costs, which negate the effects of the dollar’s depreciation. Consumers in Kinshasa are awaiting concrete measures to ensure that exchange rate stability translates into real relief for household budgets.
*DRC: Teachers Denounce Payment Delays and Threaten Strike
Across the Democratic Republic of Congo, teachers are expressing discontent over recurring salary delays. They are criticizing the absence of salary reform and irregular recruitment practices, which complicate personnel management.
In South Ubangi province, the situation is particularly critical for teachers in Kungu territory, who have not received their September and October 2025 salaries. According to Emmanuel Bamolokola, spokesperson for the Espoir de Kungu teachers’ union, some educators are forced to take out loans at exorbitant interest rates, worsening their precarious situation.

*Ituri: Modernization of Morongo Airport (Bunia) to Boost Regional Economy
Modernization work at Morongo Airport (Bunia) in Ituri province is advancing rapidly, with a 90% completion rate, according to Paul Bunagana, provincial commander of the Civil Aviation Authority (RVA), on Friday, November 14, 2025.

Scheduled for completion in March 2026, this modern infrastructure will accommodate wide-body aircraft, improve connectivity within the province, and stimulate trade, contributing to economic recovery and opening up a region long affected by armed violence.

Conclusion
The first half of November 2025 for the Democratic Republic of Congo was marked by a stark contrast between high-level diplomatic and economic activities and a severe, deteriorating humanitarian reality, particularly in the east.
Internationally, the period saw a surge in diplomatic efforts focused on the eastern conflict, with the EU demanding a humanitarian corridor , President Tshisekedi engaging with Qatar on the peace process , and Prime Minister Suminwa calling for equitable partnerships in London. Macro-economically, the government projected stability, citing a resilient economy , a stable Congolese franc , and a significant drop in inflation , which was supported by the IMF's second review.
However, this high-level optimism was overshadowed by a deepening crisis on the ground. The humanitarian situation reached a critical point, with reports indicating nearly 25 million Congolese face severe food insecurity. The crisis is particularly acute in areas like Walikale, which is on the brink of famine with a 46% malnutrition rate. This situation is fueled by ongoing armed conflicts, which continue to injure civilians and displace populations , as well as by armed groups shutting down key economic sites.
Domestically, the government is navigating internal political and structural challenges. This includes parliamentary delays in reviewing the 2026 budget , calls by the Interior Minister to suspend no-confidence motions during wartime , and persistent issues of impunity and alleged large-scale corruption. Furthermore, critical infrastructure failures, such as major flooding in Kinshasa and South Kivu and the collapse of a key route in Ituri, continue to disrupt daily life and supply chains.
