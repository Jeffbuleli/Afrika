*Djibouti Parliament Sets Legislative Agenda for 2026 Session as Key Bills Head to Committees
Djibouti City, June 1, 2026 - The Conference of Presidents of the National Assembly has outlined the legislative roadmap for the first ordinary parliamentary session of 2026, setting in motion the review of a series of draft laws and establishing the timetable for upcoming plenary sittings.

Parliamentary officials say the structured calendar is intended to improve efficiency, ensure timely examination of government proposals, and strengthen coordination between committees and the executive branch.
The upcoming session is expected to focus heavily on financial governance and sectoral reforms, with several key policy areas set to be examined in committee hearings and subsequent plenary debates in the weeks ahead.

*Djibouti Launches Fisheries Value Chain Project to Strengthen Food Security and Economic Diversification
Djibouti, June 9, 2026 - The Government of Djibouti, in partnership with Japan and the United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO), has launched a new initiative aimed at strengthening the fisheries value chain as part of broader efforts to enhance food security and economic diversification.
The project, officially launched at the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries, Water and Marine Resources (MAEPERH), focuses on improving cold chain systems, reducing post-harvest losses, creating employment opportunities, and boosting the contribution of the fisheries sector to national development.
The ceremony brought together senior government officials and international partners, including MAEPERH Secretary General Ibrahim Elmi Mohamed, Japanese Ambassador to Djibouti Okochi Akihiro, UN Resident Coordinator José Barahona, and UNIDO Regional Representative Professor Patrick Jean Gilabert, alongside ministry experts and development partners.
Despite possessing a 370-kilometer coastline along the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden, Djibouti’s fisheries sector remains underdeveloped and contributes modestly to the national economy. Officials and partners described the sector as a largely untapped resource with significant potential for growth and transformation.
A key focus of the initiative is strengthening cold chain infrastructure, identified as one of the sector’s main weaknesses. Large quantities of fish are currently lost due to inadequate preservation systems, limiting both local supply and economic returns for fishermen.
The Government of Japan, through its development cooperation programs, is supporting the introduction of refrigeration technologies adapted to Djibouti’s harsh climatic conditions. Ambassador Okochi Akihiro emphasized that improving post-harvest preservation will reduce food waste and enhance access to nutritious seafood, while also increasing local value addition.
UNIDO’s representative, Professor Gilabert, highlighted that improved processing and marketing capacities would reduce losses, improve food availability, and generate new economic opportunities. He stressed that the initiative reflects the importance of international cooperation in advancing sustainable development goals.
UN Resident Coordinator José Barahona noted that the project goes beyond food security, positioning fisheries as a strategic sector for economic diversification and job creation. He underlined the importance of developing local processing industries to add value before products reach domestic and international markets.
Government officials also emphasized the expected benefits for coastal communities, particularly in terms of income generation and employment. The Ministry highlighted plans to strengthen technical training, including a “training of trainers” approach designed to ensure long-term national capacity building and sustainability.
The initiative aligns with Djibouti’s broader development strategies, including Vision 2035 and the National Development Plan, which prioritize economic diversification, industrialization, and resilience in the face of climate and food security challenges.
By leveraging its marine resources, Djibouti aims to transform the fisheries sector into a key pillar of sustainable growth, improved nutrition, and expanded economic opportunities for its population.
