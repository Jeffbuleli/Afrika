Despite these positive outcomes, health workers noted that cultural beliefs continue to discourage some families from seeking medical care. In different regions of Uganda, the condition is still commonly attributed to witchcraft, curses, ancestral spirits, or other traditional beliefs.
Health officials called for expanded public education campaigns to dispel these misconceptions and encourage early diagnosis and treatment, stressing that delayed medical care can reduce the effectiveness of treatment.
Luweero General Hospital reported receiving an average of eight new clubfoot cases each week from the districts of Luweero, Nakasongola, and Nakaseke, underscoring the continued need for specialized treatment services and community awareness.
Parents whose children successfully completed treatment shared their experiences, describing how early medical intervention enabled their children to walk normally after overcoming initial fears, social stigma, and misconceptions surrounding the condition.
Health authorities urged parents and caregivers to seek immediate medical attention whenever signs of clubfoot are identified, emphasizing that prompt treatment offers affected children the best opportunity for full recovery and healthy development.

*Uganda AIDS Commission Raises Concern Over High HIV Rates in Soroti City
Soroti, June 29, 2026 - The Uganda AIDS Commission has expressed concern over persistently high HIV prevalence in Soroti City, urging cultural, religious and community leaders to play a more active role in reducing stigma, promoting testing and encouraging treatment adherence as part of efforts to end AIDS as a public health threat by 2030.
The appeal was made during an engagement with leaders of the Kumam Cultural Institution under the Presidential Fast-Track Initiative on HIV prevention. Commission officials said community leadership remains critical in expanding awareness and improving uptake of HIV services, particularly in high-prevalence urban areas.
Commissioner Dr Stephen Awatiti, who has lived with HIV for nearly four decades, said early testing and consistent treatment can allow people living with HIV to lead long, healthy lives. He noted that stigma continues to prevent many from accessing care despite progress in expanding antiretroviral therapy across the country.
Soroti District HIV Focal Person William Oriokot said prevalence in Soroti City stands at 11.6 percent, significantly higher than the district average of 2.5 percent, warning that the city remains a key hotspot in the region. He attributed the high rate to factors including transactional sex, alcohol and drug abuse, early marriages, teenage pregnancies and increased mobility along transport corridors.
Community leaders from the Kumam Cultural Institution pledged to intensify sensitisation efforts through cultural gatherings, funerals and community meetings, with a focus on reducing stigma and encouraging behavioural change. They also committed to strengthening partnerships to support local HIV prevention programmes.
*Early Reporting and Stigma-Free Care Crucial in Ebola Response, Health Advocate Says
Kampala, June 30, 2026 - Ugandans have been urged to prioritise early reporting of symptoms, adhere to official health guidelines, and avoid stigma against Ebola survivors as efforts to contain the disease continue.
Shadia Nambasa, a representative of GOAL NextGen, said public cooperation and reliance on verified information remain critical in reducing transmission and improving recovery outcomes. She emphasised that Ebola can be managed effectively when cases are identified and treated early.
She noted that improved survival rates reflect stronger compliance with prevention measures and timely medical intervention, urging communities to follow recommended practices such as regular handwashing, observing physical distancing where necessary, and reporting suspected cases through official health channels.
Nambasa also cautioned against discrimination against survivors, stressing that individuals who recover from Ebola do not pose a risk to others and should be fully reintegrated into their communities. She encouraged schools and institutions to maintain hygiene measures, including handwashing facilities, to reduce the risk of infection.
She further urged parents to continue routine childhood immunisation programmes despite the Ebola situation and highlighted key warning signs of the disease, including high fever, severe headache, general body weakness, and unexplained bleeding, calling for immediate medical attention when symptoms appear.
She concluded by urging responsible information sharing, saying early reporting and factual communication remain essential tools in controlling outbreaks and saving lives.

*Three Arrested After Suspected Murder Allegedly Staged as Suicide in Kiboga
KIBOGA, June 17, 2026 - Three people have been arrested in Kiboga District after investigators concluded that the death of a 32-year-old man, initially believed to be a suicide, may have been a homicide staged to conceal the crime.
The victim, identified as Ciprian Kwitonda, a peasant from Ssesamu Village in Ntwetwe Sub-county, was found hanging from a coffee tree on June 16. While the scene initially suggested suicide, subsequent investigations led detectives to treat the case as a suspected murder.
Investigators determined that, prior to his death, Kwitonda had confronted his estranged wife, Esther Mukakamanzi, at a local market, where an argument reportedly escalated into a physical altercation. The incident was later reported to local authorities.
The investigation further established that Kwitonda subsequently followed his wife to the residence of the village chairperson, where another confrontation allegedly took place. During the incident, he was reportedly assaulted by his wife and another individual, Innocent Munyembanza.
Witness accounts indicate that Kwitonda appeared to be under the influence of alcohol during the altercation. Although his mother, who was present, reportedly urged him to return home, he never arrived.
His body was discovered several hours later hanging from a coffee tree in a nearby plantation.
Following a detailed examination of the scene and witness interviews, investigators concluded that the available evidence was inconsistent with suicide. Detectives believe Kwitonda may have been killed elsewhere before his body was transported to the coffee plantation and suspended to create the appearance of self-inflicted death.
Three suspects have since been taken into custody: Esther Mukakamanzi (36), Innocent Munyembanza (35), and Didas Muzarugamba, the village chairperson whose residence was reportedly the site of the final confrontation.
Investigators are reconstructing the sequence of events to determine the circumstances surrounding Kwitonda's death and the extent of each suspect's alleged involvement.
The case underscores the complexity of investigating suspicious deaths in which crime scenes may be deliberately manipulated to resemble suicide, highlighting the importance of forensic examination and witness testimony in establishing the true cause of death.
The suspects remain in custody as investigations continue.
*Kasese High Court Acquits Six Accused in Separate Murder Trials
KASESE, June 19, 2026 - The High Court in Kasese has acquitted six individuals facing murder charges in two separate criminal cases, ruling that prosecutors failed to present sufficient evidence to secure convictions.
In the first case, five men charged with murder and aggravated robbery over the killing of Gerald Bwambale, the LCII chairperson of Kyogha Parish, were found not guilty after the court concluded that the prosecution had failed to prove its case beyond reasonable doubt.
The acquitted men - Allan Bwambale, Timona Muhindo, Obed Muhindo, Noel Masereka, and Joackim Bwambale - had been arrested following the fatal shooting of Bwambale on the night of June 3, 2025. Prosecutors alleged that the local leader was shot while asleep at his home in Bwera Sub-county, Kasese District.
In its judgment, the court found that the prosecution's case relied primarily on circumstantial evidence and failed to directly connect any of the accused to the killing. The court also identified significant weaknesses in the handling of key exhibits, including an alleged firearm, spent cartridges, and projectiles recovered during the investigation.
The court determined that the inconsistencies surrounding the evidence created reasonable doubt, preventing a lawful conviction and resulting in the acquittal of all five defendants.
In a separate judgment delivered the same day, the court also acquitted Brighton Akandwanaho, 20, who had been charged with the murder of Eliphaz Kambale.
Akandwanaho was accused of fatally stabbing Kambale during an altercation at Vegas Bar and Lodge in Majengo Trading Centre on the night of December 24, 2024.
After reviewing the evidence, the court found that testimony presented by key prosecution witnesses contained material contradictions and failed to establish the accused's guilt beyond reasonable doubt.
The ruling emphasized the fundamental principle of criminal justice that convictions must be supported by credible and consistent evidence, with any unresolved doubt resolved in favor of the accused.
Following the acquittals, all six defendants were released after spending several months in detention awaiting the conclusion of their respective trials.
*Uganda Police Arrest More Than 3,600 Traffic Offenders in Nationwide Road Safety Crackdown
KAMPALA, June 22, 2026 - Uganda Police have arrested 3,691 motorists and motorcycle riders during a week-long nationwide traffic enforcement operation aimed at improving road discipline and reducing crashes caused by reckless driving and violations of traffic regulations.
The operation targeted a wide range of traffic offenses as authorities intensified enforcement efforts to improve compliance with road safety laws.
According to police records, 513 motorists were arrested for careless driving and dangerous overtaking, while 487 were cited for dangerous loading. Another 398 drivers were found operating vehicles in dangerous mechanical condition, and 118 were arrested for driving without valid licenses.
Motorcycle riders accounted for a significant proportion of those arrested. Police recorded 574 arrests for failure to wear crash helmets, 518 for riding without valid permits, and 198 for illegally carrying passengers.
The operation also resulted in 289 arrests for illegal vehicle modifications, 296 for disobeying traffic directions, 76 for violating approved route charts, and 211 for operating vehicles without the required reflectors and warning signs.
Additionally, 13 motorists were arrested for driving under the influence of alcohol.
The nationwide crackdown comes amid growing concern over road safety following a series of fatal road crashes reported across the country in recent weeks.
Authorities said enforcement operations will continue alongside public awareness campaigns designed to promote safer road use. The campaigns are focusing on issues such as speeding, helmet use, vehicle maintenance, road signage, and compliance with traffic regulations, particularly in areas identified as road crash hotspots.
Police believe that combining sustained law enforcement with road safety education will improve compliance with traffic laws, reduce accidents, and enhance the safety of all road users.

*Eight Arrested, Weapons Seized in Major Security Crackdown Across West Acholi
GULU, June 19, 2026 - A joint security operation in Uganda's West Acholi sub-region has resulted in the arrest of eight suspected members of an armed criminal network and the recovery of two sub-machine guns and 30 rounds of ammunition, marking a significant breakthrough in efforts to curb violent crime in northern Uganda.
The three-week intelligence-led operation was conducted across Gulu City and the districts of Nwoya, Omoro, and Gulu by a joint force targeting an organized group suspected of carrying out a series of violent crimes that have affected security and local communities.
The eight suspects, all residents of Nwoya District, are under investigation for multiple offenses, including murder, attempted murder, aggravated robbery, theft of firearms from security personnel and institutions, unlawful possession of military and police equipment, and wildlife-related crimes.
During the operation, another suspect, Daniel Komakech, died after allegedly resisting arrest and attempting to disarm security personnel during a nighttime operation on June 18-19. He sustained injuries during the confrontation and later died while receiving treatment at a military hospital.
Investigators believe the criminal network was responsible for several high-profile attacks across the region, including the May 5 killings of a private security guard and a businessman at a petrol station in Gulu City, the May 15 murder of a man at Awach Trading Centre, and the June 3 killing of a mobile money agent at Ongako Trading Centre.
The suspects are also linked to the attempted murder of a police officer during the theft of a firearm from Lalogi Police Station in Omoro District, as well as several armed robberies involving cash thefts in Gulu, Kitgum, and Nwoya districts.
Authorities further suspect the group of involvement in illegal wildlife activities after recovering wildlife trophies believed to have originated from Murchison Falls National Park.
Investigations remain ongoing as security agencies seek to identify and apprehend additional suspects believed to be connected to the network.
The operation is part of broader efforts to dismantle organized armed criminal groups operating in northern Uganda and restore security across the West Acholi sub-region.

The killing has shocked residents of Budhebela Village in Mayuge Town Council, sparking concerns over public safety and the presence of abandoned structures that residents say have become potential hideouts for criminals.
Community members have called for vacant buildings to be secured or rehabilitated, arguing that neglected properties pose a growing security risk, particularly to children.

The investigation remains ongoing.

The operation was conducted on June 21 at a residence in Kakunyu Village, Wakiso Town Council, after local authorities reported that an unusually large number of children were being housed at the premises.
During the raid, officers found 18 children-nine boys and nine girls-aged between three and 12 years living in two rooms under circumstances investigators considered suspicious.

Police arrested two suspects identified as George Mulindwa (33), a teacher, and Madinah Babirye (24), a casual worker. They remain in custody as detectives investigate possible human trafficking and related offenses.
The rescued children have been placed under protective care while authorities work to establish their identities and reunite them with their parents or guardians. The identification process is being conducted in collaboration with child protection and social welfare agencies.
Investigators are also seeking to determine whether the case is linked to a broader child trafficking network operating across multiple districts.
Authorities have urged parents and guardians whose children may have disappeared under suspicious circumstances to report the cases to the nearest police station as investigations continue. They also warned the public against participating in or facilitating child trafficking and called on communities to remain vigilant and report suspected cases promptly.
Conclusion
The period from June 17 to 30, 2026, reflects a dynamic phase in Uganda’s national trajectory, marked by political consolidation, accelerating infrastructure and economic transformation, and continued pressure points in health delivery and internal security.
Political and Institutional Dynamics
Politically, the National Resistance Movement (NRM) consolidated its dominance through a decisive victory in the Kikuube LC5 by-election and sustained mobilisation efforts in Kalangala, where development pledges and service delivery commitments shaped the campaign narrative. At the same time, Parliament became a focal point of institutional tension following proposals to amend the framework governing the Leader of the Opposition, raising concerns about the balance of power and the independence of oversight mechanisms.
In parallel, public health governance saw strengthened multi-stakeholder coordination, exemplified by the establishment of a joint disease surveillance committee between Buganda Kingdom, the Ministry of Health, and development partners, signalling deeper integration of cultural institutions into national response systems.
Economic Momentum and Structural Constraints
Uganda recorded notable progress on its long-term economic agenda, particularly in energy and infrastructure development. The Kingfisher oil project moved closer to production, with key facilities nearing completion, reinforcing expectations of first oil within the broader petroleum development framework. In infrastructure, a €110.5 million financing package for the Kitgum-Kidepo Road further strengthened prospects for improved connectivity, tourism growth, and regional trade integration.
However, these gains are tempered by persistent structural and regulatory challenges. Agricultural exporters face tightening UK food safety requirements affecting key commodities such as capsicum and sesame, while government continues efforts to accelerate implementation of World Bank-supported projects, which remain constrained by a low disbursement rate. At the macroeconomic level, renewed coordination between fiscal and monetary authorities underscores efforts to preserve stability amid external financial pressures and domestic financing needs.
Social, Health, and Security Pressures
The health sector presents a mixed picture of reform and strain. While authorities are advancing innovative immunisation strategies targeting younger urban parents, several systemic gaps persist, including rising bilharzia infections in Kayunga linked to delayed mass treatment campaigns, and the absence of Health Centre III facilities across Nebbi Municipality, which continues to limit access to basic healthcare services. Public health messaging also remains focused on addressing entrenched cultural misconceptions, particularly around conditions such as clubfoot.
Security and law enforcement agencies maintained heightened activity across multiple fronts. Joint operations disrupted criminal networks and trafficking activities, including the rescue of children in Wakiso and the exposure of counterfeit veterinary drug production in Kitagwenda. Nationwide traffic enforcement also led to the arrest of thousands of offenders, reflecting intensified efforts to improve road safety and regulatory compliance.
Overall Assessment
Taken together, this reporting period illustrates a country advancing significant economic and infrastructure milestones while simultaneously confronting enduring governance, service delivery, and public health challenges. The overall trajectory remains one of forward momentum, but sustained progress will depend on addressing institutional capacity gaps, strengthening local service provision, and ensuring that economic gains translate into tangible improvements in citizen welfare.
