Conclusion
The first half of June 2026 highlights a deepening and increasingly complex crisis in Sudan, where the conflict is no longer simply ongoing but evolving into an entrenched, multi-dimensional collapse spanning political, economic, security, and humanitarian spheres. Across these fifteen days, conditions have deteriorated further, with limited signs of meaningful progress toward resolution.
1. Political fragmentation and stalled diplomacy
International mediation efforts, including the Quintet framework in Addis Ababa, remain largely immobilized by internal divisions and competing political legitimacy claims. Disputes over representation, coupled with the categorical refusal by key blocs such as the Democratic Bloc to engage with the RSF-aligned “Tasis” coalition, have prevented even basic dialogue from taking shape. At the same time, the Tasis coalition maintains that negotiations are impossible without a prior humanitarian truce, reinforcing a broader pattern of mutually incompatible preconditions. The result is a diplomatic landscape defined by mistrust, fragmentation, and an absence of viable convergence.
2. Economic fragmentation and accelerating structural decline
Sudan’s economic crisis has intensified under the combined pressure of war-related destruction and emerging parallel governance structures. The establishment of a parallel monetary authority in RSF-controlled areas signals an emerging dual financial system, further undermining national cohesion and monetary stability. Meanwhile, government trade restrictions and import bans have contributed to market distortions, rising prices, and contested policy responses, while the continued collapse of the currency reflects deeper structural weaknesses tied to conflict, shrinking production capacity, and foreign exchange shortages. The fuel crisis, driven by refinery shutdowns and import dependence, further underscores the fragility of essential supply systems.
3. Intensifying drone warfare and systematic infrastructure targeting
The military landscape has shifted decisively toward expanded drone warfare, with both sides relying heavily on aerial strikes to target strategic and civilian-linked infrastructure. These operations have damaged or destroyed key assets, including bridges, fuel stations, transport routes, and water sources, further degrading civilian access to essential services. At the same time, the buildup of forces around strategic urban centres such as El Obeid signals a potential transition toward larger-scale ground offensives, increasing the risk of wider territorial escalation and mass displacement.
4. Deepening humanitarian catastrophe and social disintegration
The humanitarian situation has reached alarming levels, with tens of millions of people facing acute food insecurity and multiple regions at risk of famine as agricultural production declines and access to aid remains constrained. Civilians continue to bear the brunt of the conflict through displacement, infrastructure collapse, and exposure to repeated attacks on essential services. Beyond Sudan’s borders, displaced populations face growing vulnerability, including restrictive migration environments, deportations, and rising social hostility in host countries. The cumulative effect is a widening protection crisis affecting both internal and cross-border populations.
Final Synthesis
Taken together, these developments reflect a reinforcing cycle in which political deadlock sustains military escalation, military escalation accelerates economic breakdown, and economic breakdown deepens humanitarian catastrophe. Without a decisive shift toward de-escalation and credible political compromise, the trajectory suggests continued fragmentation of state capacity and a prolonged deterioration of civilian conditions, with consequences that will extend far beyond the current phase of the conflict.
