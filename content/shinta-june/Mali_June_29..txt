According to the military, the strikes were conducted on June 15 after aerial surveillance identified what it described as militant hideouts near the village of Kirnya. One of the locations was reportedly identified after a tricycle carrying fuel containers was observed traveling from the village toward a wooded area believed to be used as a refuge.
The military said additional strikes targeted another site northwest of Kirnya, where aerial reconnaissance detected a large tent concealed by vegetation and a pickup truck. The operation reportedly destroyed the encampment, the vehicle, and other logistical equipment.
In a separate operation, airstrikes were carried out about 32 kilometers northeast of Sévaré, where the army said four suspected militant assembly points were destroyed.
The Armed Forces stated that the operations resulted in the neutralization of around 10 suspected militants and the destruction of logistical assets. The claims have not been independently verified, and no information was immediately available regarding possible civilian casualties or responses from the armed groups.

Bamako, June 17, 2026 - Mali's Armed Forces (FAMa) said they conducted offensive aerial reconnaissance operations in the western sector of Léré, in the Timbuktu region, targeting what the military described as suspected militant logistical infrastructure.

A second strike targeted what the Armed Forces described as a militant supply depot containing numerous fuel drums and several motorcycles. The military said the site also functioned as a cross-border transit point for armed groups and was completely destroyed.
The military's account of the operation has not been independently verified, and no information was immediately available regarding potential civilian casualties or responses from the armed groups.
*June 14-15 Security Developments: Reported Military Defections and Claimed JNIM Attack in Western Mali
Bamako, June 14-15, 2026 - Mali witnessed a series of reported military defections and continued insurgent activity on June 14 and 15, underscoring the persistent security challenges facing the country.
June 14: Three Soldiers Reportedly Defect to the Azawad Liberation Front
On June 14, 2026, Col. Daffi Ag Mandoli and Chief Warrant Officer Inagni Ag Rhissa reportedly defected from the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) while stationed in Gao, joining the Azawad Liberation Front (FLA).
On the same day, Adjutant Sidi Ag Mohamed, a member of the Daoussahak community, also reportedly left the army to join the FLA in Ménaka. Sidi Ag Mohamed had previously belonged to the Movement for the Salvation of Azawad (MSA) before integrating into the Malian military under earlier reconciliation efforts.
The reported defections occurred amid ongoing tensions in northern Mali, where former signatories to the Algiers Peace Agreement and armed groups continue to oppose the central government.
June 15: Senior Officer Reportedly Leaves Army
On June 15, 2026, Commander Sidi Ag Daoud reportedly defected from the Malian army while serving in Nara, in the Ségou region, to join the Azawad Liberation Front.
Sidi Ag Daoud had entered the Malian Armed Forces through the Disarmament, Demobilization and Reintegration (DDR) process established under the Algiers Peace Agreement. His reported departure marked another high-profile defection involving former combatants who had been integrated into the national military as part of the peace process.
The reported departures over the two-day period suggest continued movement of personnel from the Malian military toward the FLA, although Malian authorities had not publicly confirmed the defections.
June 15: JNIM Claims IED Attack in Kayes Region
Also on June 15, 2026, the Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM) claimed responsibility for an improvised explosive device (IED) attack targeting an armored vehicle of the Malian Armed Forces.
According to the group's statement, the attack occurred on the Torodo-Goumara axis in the Kayes region of western Mali. JNIM said the armored vehicle struck the explosive device during military movements along the route.
The claim could not be independently verified, and the Malian military had not issued an official statement confirming the incident or reporting casualties at the time.
The alleged attack forms part of a broader pattern of insurgent operations targeting Malian security forces through roadside bombs and ambushes, particularly in central and western regions where militant groups remain active.
*Azawad Liberation Front Claims Drone Strike on Anafif Military Base
Kidal Region, June 15-16, 2026 - The Azawad Liberation Front (FLA) claimed to have carried out a drone attack on the Anafif military base in the Kidal region, targeting what it described as a command center used for military drone operations.
June 15: FLA Announces Kamikaze Drone Attack
On June 15, 2026, the FLA said it launched an attack using loitering munitions, commonly referred to as kamikaze drones, against the Anafif camp, where it claimed personnel from the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) and members of the Africa Corps (Russian military personnel) were stationed.
According to the group's statement, the strike formed part of its operation dubbed "Abikbik N Teghelt." The FLA claimed the attack destroyed a command station used to operate Malian military drones inside the base.
The group further alleged that the targeted facility played a key role in coordinating aerial operations in northern Mali.
June 16: Videos Released Claiming to Show Strike Aftermath
On June 16, 2026, the FLA released videos that it said showed the aftermath of the attack. The footage appeared to show large plumes of smoke rising from the military installation.
The group claimed the strike had targeted a control center for Turkish-made drones allegedly used by Malian forces during military operations in the Azawad region.
The FLA's claims, including the reported destruction of the drone control facility and the presence of Russian personnel at the site, have not been independently verified. Malian authorities had not publicly commented on the alleged attack or released information on possible casualties or damage at the time of reporting.
*June 15-18 Security Developments: Reported Disarmament, Civilian Casualties, and Insurgent Activity Across Mali
Bamako, June 15-18, 2026 - Reports from several regions of Mali indicated continued security developments between June 15 and 18, including the reported disarmament of community fighters, allegations of civilian casualties during military operations, renewed attacks by insurgents, and persistent transport disruptions linked to insecurity. Many of the claims remain unverified and have not been independently confirmed.
June 15: Reports of Dana Ambassagou Fighters Surrendering Weapons
On June 15, 2026, local sources reported that members of the Dana Ambassagou self-defence group operating in the Bankass, Diallassagou, and Koro areas of the Mopti region had begun handing over their weapons to Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM).
The reported disarmament follows similar incidents documented in previous weeks in Ségou region, where fighters from 14 Dogon communities were also said to have surrendered their weapons to the militant group.
The reports could not be independently verified, and no official statements were issued by Malian authorities or Dana Ambassagou regarding the alleged disarmament.
June 15: JNIM Alleges Civilian Casualties in Drone Strike Near Hombori
Also on June 15, a drone strike hit the village of Kénia, in the Hombori commune of the Douentza region.
According to figures released by JNIM, the strike resulted in 19 people killed or injured, the majority of whom the group claimed were women. JNIM alleged that the attack caused significant civilian casualties and accused government forces of targeting populated areas.
The figures and circumstances surrounding the strike have not been independently verified, and Malian authorities had not publicly commented on the incident at the time of reporting.
June 17: Allegations of Livestock Killings Near Aguelhok
On June 17, 2026, local sources alleged that a joint patrol involving the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) and personnel from the Africa Corps killed 145 sheep at the Atchou site near Aguelhok, in the Kidal region.
The same sources claimed that the shepherd responsible for the flock was taken away to an undisclosed location and that signs of violence were found at the scene.
These allegations have not been independently confirmed, and no official response had been issued by the Malian military or the Africa Corps.
Night of June 17-18: JNIM Claims Capture of Military Position in Niono
During the night of June 17-18, 2026, JNIM claimed responsibility for an attack on a Malian military checkpoint located at the main entrance to the town of Niono, in the Ségou region.
The group said its fighters overran the position after clashes with government troops and temporarily took control of the military post.
The claim has not been independently verified, and Malian authorities had not confirmed the reported attack or disclosed any casualties.
Transport Disruptions Continue Around Bamako
Throughout the reporting period, local sources indicated that road insecurity continued to disrupt regional transport links with Bamako.
According to the reports, passenger buses operating from Mauritania and Senegal had been unable to reach the Malian capital for several weeks because of insecurity and the closure of key road corridors.
The same sources also claimed that Russian personnel who had previously escorted some civilian convoys had suspended those operations, while Malian security forces and their allies shifted their focus toward reinforcing security in and around Bamako.
These reports could not be independently verified, and authorities had not issued official statements regarding the reported transport disruptions or changes to convoy escort operations.

*Malian Army Dismantles Alleged Fuel Trafficking Network in Sikasso Region

Conclusion
The reporting period from June 15 to 29, 2026, reflects Mali's continued efforts to consolidate state institutions, expand diplomatic engagement, and advance economic and social development amid a highly volatile security environment.
Diplomatic and Economic Engagement: The government intensified bilateral and multilateral diplomacy through engagements with partners including China, India, Japan, Spain, Canada, Senegal, Mauritania, Morocco, Russia, and the European Union, while deepening cooperation within the Confederation of Sahel States (AES). Economic initiatives focused on attracting investment, strengthening trade partnerships, improving the business environment, supporting women's entrepreneurship, and preparing major forums aimed at mobilizing domestic and international investment.
Governance and Institutional Development: Authorities pursued reforms across the justice, health, and public administration sectors. Key developments included the launch of judicial reform mechanisms, the opening of criminal court sessions, capacity-building programs for public institutions, improvements in pharmaceutical infrastructure, and initiatives to strengthen governance and public service delivery. The government also highlighted progress in agriculture, rural development, employment, and cultural heritage as part of its broader national development agenda.
Security Operations and Armed Conflict: The Malian Armed Forces continued intensive counterinsurgency operations across several regions, reporting numerous airstrikes against suspected militant positions, the destruction of logistical infrastructure, and operations targeting fuel trafficking networks believed to support armed groups. At the same time, JNIM claimed responsibility for multiple attacks on military positions, ambushes, improvised explosive device (IED) attacks, and the capture of several military checkpoints, underscoring the persistent operational capacity of insurgent groups despite sustained military pressure.
Humanitarian and Civilian Protection Concerns: The period was also marked by continued reports of civilian harm linked to the conflict, including allegations of civilian deaths during airstrikes, killings attributed to military operations and allied foreign personnel, and accusations of abuses that remain unverified. These incidents highlight the ongoing risks faced by civilian populations and the importance of independent verification and accountability in conflict-affected areas.
Shifting Local Dynamics: Several developments pointed to evolving local security arrangements. Reports indicated the return of displaced communities to parts of the Bankass area following local agreements with JNIM, while the reported reopening of the Mourdiah-Nara road after nearly two years of isolation suggested that locally negotiated arrangements continue to shape access and mobility in some regions. Although these developments may ease humanitarian and economic pressures for affected communities, they also illustrate the complex and fragmented security landscape across central and northern Mali.
Overall, the reporting period underscores the coexistence of active state-building efforts and persistent armed conflict. While the government continued to pursue institutional reforms, economic partnerships, and regional diplomacy, insecurity remained widespread, with competing military operations, insurgent activity, and localized negotiated arrangements continuing to define conditions across much of the country.
