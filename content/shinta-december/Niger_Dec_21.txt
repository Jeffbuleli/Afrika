*AES Condemns Nigerian Military Aircraft Incursion and Places Air Defenses on Maximum Alert
Niamey, December 8, 2025 - The Confederation of Sahel States (AES) issued a press release today condemning the unauthorized incursion of a Nigerian Air Force C-130 aircraft into Burkinabe airspace. The aircraft, carrying two crew members and nine passengers-all military personnel, was forced to land in Bobo-Dioulasso, Burkina Faso, following an in-flight emergency.
An investigation conducted by Burkinabe authorities confirmed that the aircraft lacked authorization to overfly Burkinabe territory, constituting a breach of both national sovereignty and international aviation law.
The AES denounced the act as hostile and in violation of international norms, emphasizing the importance of safeguarding confederal airspace, territorial integrity, and civilian safety. In response, and following instructions from the Heads of State, AES air and anti-aircraft defenses have been placed on maximum alert, consistent with the Declaration of the College of Heads of State of December 22, 2024. These defenses are now authorized to neutralize any aircraft that violates AES airspace.
The Confederation stressed that this measure is a demonstration of sovereign authority, aimed at ensuring that member states’ airspace is respected and that regional security is reinforced against unauthorized military operations.
Key points:
Aircraft: Nigerian Air Force C-130, 2 crew, 9 passengers.
Location of emergency landing: Bobo-Dioulasso, Burkina Faso.
AES response: Air defenses on maximum alert; authorized to neutralize violators.
Legal context: Violation of international civil and military aviation regulations.
Broader implications: Reinforces AES commitment to sovereignty, security, and territorial integrity.

*Niger: Abundant Fresh Produce Available in Niamey Markets

Cabbage from the southern Tahoua region, particularly Madaoua and Konni, is also plentiful. A full bag of 25 to 30 apples sells for 7,000 CFA francs, lower than in the past two years, reflecting a general trend of improved supply and accessibility of market garden products for consumers.

*Niger: Moderate Decline in Cereal Prices on Agricultural Markets
Niamey, December 12, 2025 - According to the Agricultural Market Information System (SIMA), cereal prices recorded a moderate decline during the week of December 3-9, 2025. Locally produced millet and sorghum each fell by 1%, while imported cereals remained largely stable, reflecting abundant supply and weaker commercial demand.
The average national price of a 100 kg bag of millet fell from 20,967 FCFA to 20,782 FCFA, remaining 26% lower than during the same period in 2024. Sorghum followed a similar pattern, decreasing by 1% to 18,068 FCFA, reflecting ample stocks and modest demand.
Maize prices remained virtually stable, with a national average of 18,518 FCFA per 100 kg bag, while imported rice in 25 kg bags stayed steady at 11,424 FCFA. These trends demonstrate the effectiveness of public regulation mechanisms and the structured supply chains.
Over the past year, significant declines were recorded: -26% for millet, -30% for sorghum, -34% for maize, and -28% for imported rice. Analysts attribute these decreases to abundant supply, favorable agricultural performance, rigorous stock management, and positive prospects for the 2025 agricultural season.

*Niamey: Inauguration of the PK7 + 250 “Sorey Bridge” on National Route 1 East
Niamey, December 17, 2025 - Prime Minister and Minister of Economy and Finance, Mr. Mahaman Lamine Zeine, presided over the official inauguration ceremony of the PK7 + 250 bridge, also known as the “Sorey Bridge”, on National Route 1 East, on Wednesday, December 17, 2025. He was accompanied by the Minister of Equipment and Infrastructure, Colonel-Major Salissou Mahaman Salissou.
The Sorey Bridge consists of two adjacent 100-meter spans, each with an 8-meter-wide roadway and two lanes in each direction, with a clearance under the beams of 8 meters.
Financed by the State of Niger through three loan agreements with the West African Development Bank (BOAD), the construction of the bridge is part of a broader road development and paving project in Niamey.
During the ceremony, the Minister of Equipment and Infrastructure highlighted that exceptional rainfall in 2024 had severely disrupted the national road network, submerging the previous 38-meter bridge at kilometer marker 7 + 250, causing embankment collapse and traffic interruptions. The project incorporated updated hydrological and hydraulic studies to ensure adaptation to climate change.
Upon completion, the Prime Minister praised the contribution of all involved in realizing this strategic infrastructure, particularly President General Abdourahamane Tiani, Head of State. The ceremony was attended by members of the National Committee for the Safeguarding of the Homeland (CNSP), government representatives, and numerous civilian and military dignitaries.

*Niamey Police Dismantle Four Criminal Networks Linked to Robberies, Kidnappings, and Murders
Niamey, December 9, 2025 - The Directorate of Judicial Police (DPJ) on Tuesday unveiled the results of investigations that led to the dismantling of four major criminal networks operating in Niamey. These groups were implicated in armed robberies, kidnappings, burglaries, and homicides, with one network including two former members of the Defense and Security Forces, dismissed for serious misconduct.
The first network, the most structured and violent, comprised about a dozen individuals aged 25 to 53. It specialized in targeting people carrying large sums of money, using intelligence-led operations and armed attacks in broad daylight. Investigators linked this group to multiple robberies, burglaries, and a homicide in October 2025, as well as cases of identity theft through individuals impersonating uniformed police officers.
The second network focused on high-value vehicle thefts, rapidly transporting stolen vehicles to Nigeria through established cross-border routes. Led by a Nigerian national with local accomplices, the group admitted to several thefts, highlighting the challenges posed by well-organized trafficking networks.
The third network, composed of young men aged 19 to 25 from Koira Tégui, engaged in kidnappings of women, including one case that resulted in murder. The fourth network is implicated in the murder of a woman discovered in the Christian cemetery of Koira Tégui, a case marked by extreme violence and currently before the courts.
At the ceremony, Public Prosecutor Ousmane Baydo reiterated the principle of presumption of innocence and called for heightened citizen vigilance, emphasizing that security is a collective responsibility requiring cooperation between the public and law enforcement.
The DPJ reaffirmed its commitment to combating crime and urged citizens to report suspicious activities, underlining ongoing efforts to maintain public safety in Niamey.
*Niger Citizens Demand Special Tribunal to Try Orano and Secure Reparations
Niamey, December 11, 2025 - A growing wave of citizen mobilization in Niger is challenging historical ties with the former French colonial power and foreign companies that have exploited the country’s natural resources. At the center of these protests is uranium mining, a longstanding source of political, social, and environmental tensions.
The Revolutionary Front for African Sovereignty (FRSA) has called for the creation of a special national tribunal to try the French company Orano and other French actors accused of crimes committed during and after the colonial period, describing these actions as “crimes against humanity”. The movement insists that such cases fall solely under Nigerien jurisdiction, invoking national sovereignty as the legal basis for the tribunal.
The organization specifically cites the illegal storage of radioactive materials on Nigerien soil as one of the most egregious violations. It urges the authorities to resist pressure from what it calls “colonial and imperialist jurisdictions”, stressing that a national court is the only framework capable of delivering fair justice and ending the alleged impunity of foreign actors.
This appeal follows the December 2 announcement by Nigerien authorities of their intention to initiate legal proceedings against Orano and seek reparations. The discovery of 400 barrels containing radioactive drill cores in the Arlit department has reignited public outrage and reinforced accusations of serious environmental negligence.
For many observers and residents, the case highlights decades of resource plundering at the expense of public health and environmental safety. Exposed radioactive waste poses a major threat to communities and leads to long-term soil and groundwater contamination.
Today, the main demand is clear: justice, reparations, and the return of strategic resources. The creation of a special tribunal is seen as a decisive step toward genuine national sovereignty in Niger.

The situation underscores the growing geopolitical and logistical challenges surrounding the transportation of strategic uranium resources from Niger.

*AES: President of the Confederation Opens First Confederal Session of the Council of Ministers in Bamako
Bamako, December 20, 2025 - The Koulouba Palace on Saturday hosted the opening of the first confederal session of the Council of Ministers of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES), a landmark moment in the institutional construction of the new regional bloc. The session was chaired by His Excellency General Assimi Goïta, President of the Transition of Mali, Head of State, and President of the AES Confederation, and brought together government ministers from Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger.
In his opening address, President Assimi Goïta described the meeting as an “inaugural and preparatory” session, laying the groundwork for the second session of the College of Heads of State, scheduled to take place in Bamako on December 22 and 23, 2025. He recalled that the Confederal Council of Ministers, established under Article 12 of the Constitutive Treaty signed in Niamey on July 6, 2024, represents one of the three pillars of confederal governance, alongside the College of Heads of State and the future Confederal Parliament.
This first confederal session is tasked with examining a series of strategic and foundational documents. Among the key items on the agenda are the progress report on the implementation of the first-year roadmap, the draft additional protocols in the areas of defense, diplomacy, and development, as well as the rules governing the functioning and sessions of the future Confederal Parliament. Once adopted, these instruments are expected to complete the institutional architecture of the AES and ensure its effective operationalization.
Addressing the broader vision of the Confederation, General Assimi Goïta reaffirmed that the AES is intended to serve as a framework for “emancipation, freedom, and true independence”, rooted in the resources, capacities, and ingenuity of the Sahelian peoples. He stressed that the three member states had made a sovereign and deliberate choice to unite their efforts in order to build a stable, secure, and peaceful regional space, capable of fostering development and the full flourishing of their populations.
“Our three states have made the sovereign choice to join forces to build a stable and peaceful space, conducive to the development and full flourishing of our people,” the President declared, while calling for pragmatic, coordinated, and strengthened cooperation in response to the security, economic, and geopolitical challenges confronting the region.
The President of the Confederation also strongly criticized what he described as “economic and media terrorism” allegedly exercised by certain international actors, which he said aim to undermine the popular support enjoyed by the AES. According to him, these pressures have instead reinforced the determination of the Confederation to continue along its sovereign path on the African and international stage.
Paying tribute to the defense and security forces of Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger, President Goïta praised their commitment and sacrifices in safeguarding national territories, often “at the risk of their lives.” He also commended the populations of the AES, whom he described as the true source of the Confederation’s legitimacy, for their resilience and steadfast support.
Looking beyond the immediate Sahelian context, the President broadened his message to West Africa as a whole, emphasizing the region’s shared history, culture, and geography. “Our peoples are naturally called upon to live together, in mutual respect, solidarity, and fraternity,” he concluded, reaffirming the AES’s stated ambition to promote regional cohesion, stability, and cooperation beyond its current membership.
Conclusion: Toward Sovereign Integration and Sustainable Resilience
The period from December 7 to 21, 2025, marks a decisive phase in Niger’s "Refounding of the Republic," characterized by a robust assertion of sovereign authority and a deepening of regional integration.
Regional Security and Sovereignty: The Confederation of Sahel States (AES) demonstrated a unified front in response to unauthorized airspace incursions, placing defenses on maximum alert to safeguard territorial integrity. This was further reinforced by high-level confederal meetings in Bamako aimed at operationalizing the AES and finalizing joint protocols for defense and diplomacy.
Economic Transformation: A clear shift toward economic sovereignty is evident through the launch of the first ESA Trade Fair and initiatives like the Faireniger brand, which promotes local consumption ("Buy Nigerien"). The mobilization of private investment through ANPIPS and the progress toward a confederal development bank (BCID-CSA) highlight a strategic drive to reduce external dependence and finance structural infrastructure.
Social and Institutional Progress: Significant strides were made in internal governance, from the renewal of traditional leadership structures (ACTN) to the implementation of community policing. Furthermore, international partnerships-ranging from health cooperation with China to strategic infrastructure loans from BOAD-continue to support national development goals.
Stability and Market Resilience: Despite regional challenges, domestic markets showed resilience with abundant fresh produce and a moderate decline in cereal prices, reflecting the effectiveness of public regulation and favorable agricultural prospects for the coming season.
In summary, the report reflects a nation and a region increasingly focused on strategic autonomy. By harmonizing judicial, economic, and security frameworks with its Sahelian partners, Niger is positioning itself to effectively address modern geopolitical challenges while fostering a "stable and peaceful space" conducive to the long-term flourishing of its people.
