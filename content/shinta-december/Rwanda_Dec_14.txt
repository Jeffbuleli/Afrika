*Burundi Signals Openness to Renewed Dialogue With Rwanda
Bujumbura, November 28, 2025 - Burundi’s Minister of Foreign Affairs and Regional Cooperation, Edouard Bizimana, has indicated that talks with Rwanda remain possible despite what he described as a “less than satisfactory” pace. He made the remarks during a press conference held on Friday.
Minister Bizimana emphasized the natural ties between the two nations, insisting that the relationship should not remain strained indefinitely.
“Burundians and Rwandans are like brothers. We share borders and a common culture,” he said. “I do not think there is a problem so big that it should lead families to remain closed. There are issues, but all will be resolved through dialogue - and that dialogue is ongoing.”
Bizimana acknowledged that meetings between the two countries are infrequent, but maintained that communication channels remain open.“Even countries in conflict always keep the path of dialogue open,” he noted. “Not all doors are closed. We continue to talk because we share a past, a region, and a future. Both sides are obliged to make efforts.”
The minister added that Burundi has made steps to improve bilateral relations, though some issues remain unresolved.
Background: Border Closure and Accusations
Burundi closed its border with Rwanda in January 2024, accusing Kigali of supporting the RED Tabara armed group - an allegation Rwanda has repeatedly rejected as baseless. Rwanda has argued that the accusations serve as a way for Burundi to deflect attention from its internal security challenges.
Since the closure, several attempts at reviving diplomatic ties have taken place, but without concrete results.
The decision to impose the border closure followed President Évariste Ndayishimiye’s 2023 state-of-the-nation address, during which he hinted at the move while accusing Rwanda of backing RED Tabara. Despite this, subsequent statements from Burundian officials have often contradicted one another, with accusations made one day and denied the next.
Rwanda’s Position
Speaking to journalists on November 27, 2025, Rwandan President Paul Kagame was asked about the current state of relations, days after a group of Burundian refugees voluntarily returned home and Burundian military officers participated in a meeting of regional land-forces commanders in Kigali.
Kagame reiterated that Rwanda has never closed its border with Burundi.
“The borders are still the same as they were,” he said. “But for us, even before the closure, Rwanda had no hand in it. It was the Burundians who chose to close the borders. Wherever they wish to open them, we have never closed them.”

*Nduhungirehe Challenges Claims That FDLR Is “Dead,” Questions DRC Commitment
Kigali / Washington - Rwanda’s Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, Ambassador Olivier Nduhungirehe, has pushed back against claims that the FDLR terrorist group no longer exists, arguing that such assertions contradict the Democratic Republic of Congo’s own commitments under a recently signed Rwanda-DRC peace agreement.
Nduhungirehe was responding to remarks by Gérard Araud, a former French Ambassador to the United Nations and the United States, who suggested that the FDLR had effectively ceased to exist. Araud, who previously served in France’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs on security matters during the presidency of François Mitterrand, made the comments amid renewed debate over security in eastern DRC.
On November 7, 2025, Araud posted on X accusing Rwanda of using the M23 movement as a proxy to plunder natural resources in eastern Congo. In response, Nduhungirehe rejected the claim, insisting that the AFC/M23 is not a Rwandan tool, but rather a Congolese movement defending Kinyarwanda-speaking Congolese, whom he said have faced marginalization and persecution since independence.
The minister recalled that the persecution of Kinyarwanda-speaking communities, particularly the Tutsi, intensified in July 1994, when members of the defeated Ex-FAR and Interahamwe militias fled into eastern Congo following the genocide against the Tutsi. He noted that these forces were evacuated under France’s Operation Turquoise and were not disarmed, laying the foundation for continued insecurity in the region.
Addressing allegations of resource plunder, Nduhungirehe said Rwanda has long accused the DRC of exploiting its own natural resources, including the so-called “3T” minerals - tin, tantalum, and tungsten. He argued that the two countries sit on the same geological formations, a reality documented in colonial-era maps, and stressed that the extraction sector in the DRC is largely dominated by Western and Asian companies.“You will not find a Rwandan company among the top 100 firms extracting precious minerals from the DRC, even during periods of war,” he said.
Araud has also alleged that Rwandan troops entered DRC territory, a claim Nduhungirehe denied, stating that Rwanda instead implemented border security measures to prevent attacks by the FDLR.“These measures are not intended to support the AFC/M23, which does not need them,” he said, “but to protect Rwanda from repeated attacks by FDLR genocidal groups, which are supported by Kinshasa.”
Despite these explanations, Araud maintained that the FDLR was no longer active, describing its members as either dead or retired. Nduhungirehe dismissed the argument, pointing to the June 27, 2025 Washington peace agreement, signed by Rwanda and the DRC with U.S. support.
Under the agreement, the DRC government committed to dismantling the FDLR, while Rwanda agreed to lift its security measures once the threat is neutralized. The accord also provides for cooperation on refugee repatriation between the two countries.
“If the ‘FDLR genocidaires are dead or retired,’” Nduhungirehe asked, “why did the DRC government agree to destroy them?” He further referenced CONOPS and OPORDs-operational plans overseen by the United States-which outline step-by-step stages for dismantling the group, from identifying bases to disarmament, capture, and reintegration.
The foreign minister concluded that the FDLR remains an active organization, rooted in genocidal ideology, and warned that 31 years after its fighters fled into the DRC, the group continues to recruit new members, posing an ongoing threat to regional stability.
*Kagame Accuses Burundi of Concealing Military Role in Eastern DRC Conflict
Kigali - President Paul Kagame has accused Burundian President Évariste Ndayishimiye of attempting to conceal Burundi’s military involvement in the conflict in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), revealing that Burundian soldiers were captured during fighting in North Kivu despite official denials from Bujumbura.
Burundi initially deployed troops to eastern DRC as part of the East African Community Regional Force (EACRF) in North Kivu province, where their mandate was to separate warring parties and facilitate peace negotiations.
In late 2023, however, President Félix Tshisekedi ordered the expulsion of the EACRF from Masisi territory in North Kivu. While the regional force was withdrawn, Burundian troops remained, but with a new mandate to fight the M23 movement, according to President Kagame.
Following the EACRF’s departure, the Burundian government increased its military presence in North Kivu under a bilateral agreement with the DRC, coinciding with the deployment of troops from the Southern African Development Community (SADC).
Speaking after the swearing-in of leaders on December 11, 2025, President Kagame said he personally telephoned President Ndayishimiye to question why Burundian forces were still operating in the DRC after the EACRF had been expelled.
According to Kagame, Ndayishimiye initially justified the deployment by citing security threats emanating from Congo, particularly in southern areas such as Uvira and Minembwe. Kagame said he then questioned why Burundian troops were operating in North Kivu, far from those locations.
“He said to me, ‘Who told you that we have troops there?’” Kagame recounted. “I told him that I had been shown evidence, but that I wanted to hear it from him directly. He replied, ‘Whoever told you lied. There are no troops in your north.’ I said, ‘That’s good. Since you say so, I believe you.’”
Kagame said that shortly after the conversation, numerous Burundian soldiers were captured during fighting in North Kivu. He added that during subsequent regional heads-of-state meetings, the Burundian president continued to deny that his troops had been captured.
“Many were captured in the fighting,” Kagame said. “When this was raised in meetings, some leaders denied it, while others confronted them, asking, ‘If you deny your soldiers, do you no longer want them?’”
By January 2025, Kagame said, M23 fighters had seized areas previously held by Burundian forces in North Kivu, and had also pushed them out of parts of South Kivu, including Minova, Kavumu, and Bukavu.
President Kagame further claimed that Burundi deployed more than 20,000 troops across eastern and central DRC, including South Kivu, Kalemie in Tanganyika province, Kindu in Maniema, Kisangani in Tshopo, and Walikale in North Kivu.
He alleged that Burundian forces have killed numerous Congolese civilians, particularly in the Minembwe area, which links the territories of Uvira, Fizi, and Mwenga, accusing them of using heavy long-range weapons and combat drones.
President Kagame criticized the international community for what he described as silence and inaction in the face of Burundi’s alleged military operations in the eastern DRC, warning that the situation continues to fuel instability and civilian suffering in the region.
*Rwanda Criticizes International Community Over Security Concerns and Killings of Banyamulenge
United Nations, New York - Rwanda has accused the international community of ignoring its security concerns and the ongoing killings of Banyamulenge civilians in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), warning that inaction is exacerbating instability in the Great Lakes region.
Speaking on December 12, 2025, during a meeting of the UN Security Council, Rwanda’s Ambassador to the United Nations, Martin Ngoga, addressed discussions on the recent clashes between the AFC/M23 and the Congolese Armed Forces in the Rusizi Valley, South Kivu province.
Ambassador Ngoga said the renewed fighting was triggered by the violation of a ceasefire by the DRC government, Burundian forces, Wazalendo militias, the FDLR terrorist group, and foreign mercenaries. He added that Kinshasa had openly signaled its intention to breach the ceasefire, despite commitments made during peace talks in Doha, Qatar.
Targeting of Banyamulenge Communities
Ngoga stated that since 2017, Congolese forces and Mai Mai armed groups have carried out sustained attacks against the Banyamulenge community in the Minembwe region, destroying approximately 85% of their villages.
He further accused DRC, Burundian, and Mai Mai forces of blocking trade routes used by the Banyamulenge to access markets, a tactic he described as a deliberate attempt to starve the population.
According to the ambassador, airstrikes and drone attacks on Banyamulenge villages and refugee camps, including in Mikenke, have intensified in 2025, yet have drawn little international response.
“Between March and December this year, there were 15 airstrikes in this area, and no one is talking about it,” Ngoga said. “I and other members of this Council have raised this issue here repeatedly, but nothing has been done.”
He questioned whether the UN Security Council had been unaware of the situation or had chosen to ignore it, urging members to examine conditions in South Kivu prior to the latest fighting.
FDLR Threat and Regional Security
Ambassador Ngoga reaffirmed Rwanda’s support for sovereignty and territorial integrity, but warned that the FDLR, which he said is backed by the DRC government, poses a direct threat to Rwanda’s security.
He said the alleged support for the FDLR by Kinshasa is backed by irrefutable evidence, including multiple UN Security Council resolutions calling for the group’s dismantling.
“The existence of the FDLR undermines all of this,” he said. “The evidence is irrefutable, the resolutions are irrefutable, and the truth is that the DRC government has supported the FDLR for 30 years. The FDLR does not only threaten Rwanda’s sovereignty, it threatens its existence and its people.”
Ngoga warned that while the international community may appear indifferent to the security of Rwanda or the Congolese Tutsi communities facing violence, it should not go so far as to accuse victims of violence when they act in self-defense.
Call for Political Dialogue
He welcomed the peace and economic development cooperation agreement signed by Rwanda and the DRC on December 4, 2025, noting that both sides committed to continue supporting political dialogue between the DRC government and the AFC/M23.
Ambassador Ngoga concluded that dialogue remains the only viable path to resolving regional conflicts, but stressed that genuine political will from all parties is essential to achieve lasting peace.

*Rwanda - Government Launches Major Overhaul of Kigali Public Transport System
Kigali, November 28, 2025 - Rwanda is set to introduce a new public transport system in Kigali City, following Cabinet approval of a major reform designed to improve efficiency, safety, and passenger experience. The reform will be implemented through a newly created government agency, Ecofleet Solutions, announced Eng. Uwihanganye Jean de Dieu, the State Secretary for Infrastructure.
Under the new model, buses will not stay longer than 10 minutes at terminals and will not exceed three minutes at bus stops, ensuring faster turnaround and reducing congestion. Ecofleet Solutions will manage and coordinate nearly all aspects of public transport in the capital.
Ecofleet Solutions to Oversee Operations
According to officials, Ecofleet Solutions will supervise the daily operation of public transport vehicles, while private companies will continue supplying buses, employing drivers, and monitoring day-to-day service delivery.
The agency’s responsibilities will include:
Ensuring enough buses are available and well distributed across routes.
Monitoring vehicle conditions and mechanical readiness.
Planning routes and tracking bus movements in real time.
Guaranteeing that buses are clean, safe, and properly maintained.
Managing infrastructure such as bus stops, terminals, and repair centers.
Overseeing all transport company contracts.
Importantly, companies will be paid based on service quality, not on the number of passengers transported - a shift intended to end the overcrowding that has long characterized Kigali’s buses.
Electronic Payment Becomes Mandatory
All passengers will pay electronically, with funds going directly to Ecofleet before being redistributed to operators. The current Tap & Go system will remain in use during the trial phase, but authorities are considering bank cards and Mobile Money as additional payment options.
Those caught paying cash will face strict penalties, as inspections are being intensified.
Driver Performance Tied to Contracts
Drivers will receive new contracts linking their pay to performance indicators, including:
Respecting the three-minute and 10-minute station limits.
Keeping buses clean.
Respectful treatment of passengers.
Safe and responsible driving.
“Ecofleet will buy, lease, operate, and manage the buses. Drivers will be paid based on meeting targets - profits are no longer their concern,” Eng. Uwihanganye explained.
Technology at the Heart of the System
The reform places heavy emphasis on technology, including:
Real-time tracking of buses.
Electronic passenger information boards at bus stops.
Mobile tools showing bus arrival times, routes, and nearby stops.
A modern system for detecting breakdowns and ensuring rapid repair.
The digital platform is expected to be fully operational within one month.
Electric Buses to Drive Sustainability
The government aims for all public transport vehicles in Kigali to be electric by the end of 2026. Ecofleet Solutions will roll out:
190 electric buses owned by the agency,
110 leased electric buses from private operators.
The program will launch on December 2, 2025, starting with the Downtown-Sonatubes-Remera corridor. Additional priority lanes will follow on Sonatubes-Gahanga and Nyabugogo-Gishushu routes.

Dr. Nsanzimana said, “While lenacapavir is a major step in the treatment of HIV, another 10th person has been asymptomatic for a long time and is not taking ARVs. A stem cell transplant involves replacing a patient’s damaged blood cells with healthy ones from a donor. This procedure helps strengthen the immune system and replace the cells in which the HIV virus hides, allowing patients to remain symptom-free without medication. However, it requires careful medical supervision due to risks such as infections, organ failure, or death.
The lenacapavir Yeztugo drug, developed by Gilead Sciences, is administered twice a year and has undergone multiple trials. The third phase trial, conducted in South Africa and Uganda with 5,300 participants aged 16-24, demonstrated no detectable HIV in recipients. Additional trials in Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, Peru, South Africa, and the United States confirmed high protection among gay men, transgender people, and other high-risk groups, showing 99.9% effectiveness in preventing HIV infection.
According to MINISANTE statistics, Rwanda sees about 3,200 new HIV infections and 2,600 AIDS-related deaths annually. Thanks to recent interventions, new infections have decreased by 82% and AIDS-related deaths have dropped by 86%, signaling major progress in the fight against HIV/AIDS in the country.

This development highlights the rapid territorial gains of AFC/M23 since the Rusizi Valley battle began last week, significantly altering the security dynamics in South Kivu province.
*Rwanda Welcomes Congolese Refugees Amid South Kivu Fighting
Rusizi District, 14 December 2025 - Mayor Sindayiheba Phanuel of Rusizi District emphasized that Rwanda is a peaceful country and continues to welcome Congolese refugees fleeing conflict in eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC).
The mayor explained that some refugees requested to live with family and friends rather than in temporary camps. 207 individuals were relocated from the Nyarushishi camp to stay with relatives and friends in areas including Bugarama Sector.
“207 requested to live with families of friends, especially in Bugarama. Those we helped to leave the camp where they were temporarily hosted went to live with friends and relatives. As we have said, Rwanda is a peaceful country, a country that welcomes and treats well anyone who comes to us,” Mayor Sindayiheba said.
The first batch of 199 Congolese refugees arrived in Rwanda on December 3, 2025, following intensified clashes between the DRC Army and its allies against the AFC/M23 coalition in South Kivu Province. Some refugees have since returned to the DRC through the Rusizi border crossing to Bukavu, while the majority remain with host families in Rwanda.
Since the Rusizi Valley battle began last week, AFC/M23 fighters have captured several strategic areas, including Katogota, Rurambo, Luberizi, Luvungi, Mutalule, Bwegera, Nyakabere, Sange, Runingo, and Kiliba. Reports indicate that even Uvira town experienced gunfire prior to the capture of Kiliba, reportedly from Wazalendo fighters, heightening the urgency for civilians to seek safety.
Rwanda’s response underscores its role as a safe haven for those fleeing conflict in neighboring regions.
*Burundi Deploys Military Veterans to South Kivu Amid AFC/M23 Advance
Uvira, 14 December 2025 - The Burundian government has deployed military veterans to the ongoing conflict in South Kivu Province, eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. On December 6, 2025, veterans were transported in four trucks across the Nyamoma Bridge toward the Sange center in Uvira territory to support operations alongside Burundian, DRC, Wazalendo, and FDLR forces.
The veterans were reportedly well-equipped with weapons and ammunition, tasked with preventing the AFC/M23 coalition from advancing toward Uvira city. Additional Burundian troops had entered the DRC on December 3, establishing positions in Lubarika and Luvungi.
Observers note that the South Kivu conflict has depleted internal Burundian military camps, leaving only units of the Security Forces of the President (BSPI) and the Military Police to guard the Chief of Staff of the Army, Gen Prime Niyongabo.
Despite these efforts, AFC/M23 fighters have captured key areas, including Sange, Lubarika, Luvungi, Kiliba, and eventually Uvira city, forcing a mass withdrawal of Burundian forces after heavy losses, numerous injuries, and significant equipment seizures.
The Burundian high command has since ordered a strategic retreat, as the coalition continues to reorganize following the setbacks in South Kivu.
*DRC: AFC/M23 Facilitates Return of Burundian Troops from South Kivu
Uvira, DRC - Burundian troops defeated in South Kivu were allowed to return home on the evening of December 10, 2025, crossing the Kavimvira border, now controlled by AFC/M23. Observers on both the Uvira and Bujumbura sides reported long lines of Burundian soldiers returning through the border, which has since been closed.
Since December 9, the Kavimvira border on the DRC side has been under the control of AFC/M23, which reportedly facilitated the soldiers’ repatriation.
These troops had been engaged in battles in the Ruzizi Valley and surrounding mountains, fighting alongside FARDC, Wazalendo, and FDLR forces against AFC/M23. Despite witnesses confirming their return, the Burundian Ambassador to the DRC denied the reports. Burundi had stationed more than 20,000 troops in South Kivu, and the conflict to capture Uvira resulted in heavy casualties and others fleeing to nearby mountains or back home. During these operations, Burundian forces were reportedly defeated in North and South Kivu.
In response to the ongoing conflict, Burundi has closed its border with the DRC on the South Kivu side to prevent infiltration by enemies.
*AFC/M23 Investigates Death of Communications Advisor in Goma
Goma, DRC - The AFC/M23 coalition, active in the Democratic Republic of Congo, announced the tragic death of its communications advisor, Magloire Paluku, on December 10, 2025. Paluku, a longtime journalist, was shot dead in the city of Goma, and the assailants remain unidentified.
The head of the AFC/M23 office, Corneille Nangaa, along with Alumba Lukamba Omokoko Jean Pierre, confirmed that Paluku died at Goma General Hospital.
“Following this criminal act, investigations are ongoing to identify those responsible,” said Alumba.
Born in Butembo, North Kivu Province, Paluku was the operator of Radio Kivu One. He joined AFC/M23 in July 2024, and since then has been a close advisor and friend to the group’s leader, Corneille Nangaa.
*AFC/M23 Delegates Return to Doha Peace Talks Following Uvira Capture
Doha, Qatar - The AFC/M23 coalition, active in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), announced that its delegates have returned to peace talks in Doha, Qatar. The announcement was made by AFC/M23 Deputy Spokesperson Dr. Oscar Balinda on December 11, 2025, following the coalition’s capture of Uvira in South Kivu province.
Dr. Balinda stated: “The invitation reached us, our people have taken the plane, and we are moving forward to complete the remaining six fundamental principles. We must also show a strong will to implement each principle to achieve lasting peace in the DRC and the region.”
The AFC/M23 delegation arrived in Doha on the morning of December 12, where they are scheduled to meet with the DRC government in talks facilitated by the Government of Qatar.
On November 15, AFC/M23 and the DRC government signed eight basic principles, including supporting humanitarian operations, restoring state power, establishing interim security measures, repatriating refugees, reviving the economy, and creating a truth, reconciliation, and justice commission. Prior agreements on a complete cessation of hostilities and the transfer of prisoners had not yet been implemented.
Dr. Balinda noted that if the DRC government begins to respect the ceasefire and releases some of the 700 prisoners requested by AFC/M23, there would be hope for progress on the other principles.
Earlier, on December 10, Bertrand Bisimwa, Deputy Director of AFC/M23 in charge of political, diplomatic, and governance activities, warned that the delegation in Doha would emphasize ongoing violations of the ceasefire agreement by the DRC government.
*South Kivu: DRC Forces Clash with Wazalendo Amid AFC/M23 Advance
Baraka, South Kivu, DRC - Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) forces taking refuge in Baraka, Fizi territory, clashed with Wazalendo armed groups on the morning of December 12, 2025. The Wazalendo attempted to disarm DRC forces after they fled Uvira, now controlled by the AFC/M23 coalition.
A local resident in the Mongemonge area described the chaos: “The bullets were above us. It was the Wazalendo who wanted to disarm the FARDC, but they failed.”
The fighting lasted about an hour, prompting authorities to suspend business and educational activities in Baraka while awaiting calm.
The DRC and Wazalendo forces had left Uvira on December 9, prior to the AFC/M23 entry. The DRC forces reached Baraka the following day, while others fled to Burundi. Sources confirm that DRC forces, previously in disarray, are still arriving in Baraka, joined by some Burundian troops who also fled.
Earlier, on December 7, AFC/M23 fighters attacked Sange, near Uvira, where Wazalendo clashed with DRC forces, leaving 36 dead, including civilians. Wazalendo reportedly attempted to recruit fleeing DRC forces to counter the AFC/M23 advance, but the DRC troops refused.
*DRC Forces Launch Airstrike in Minembwe Amid South Kivu Conflict
Minembwe, South Kivu, DRC - The Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC) launched an airstrike in the Minembwe region, which connects the territories of Uvira, Fizi, and Mwenga, on the morning of December 12, 2025. The attack targeted a camp in the Mikenke area as DRC and Burundian forces continue to clash in the region, according to politician Moïse Nyarugabo.
Nyarugabo condemned the strike, stating: “No army of this criminal alliance expects to gain military advantage from this attack. These are bombs being fired to kill and destroy. The planes and drones targeting civilian areas, villages, and people will not go unchallenged.”
He criticized the DRC government for accusing AFC/M23 of violating the ceasefire while conducting airstrikes and drone attacks on civilian populations.
DRC and Burundian forces are currently stationed in the highlands around Minembwe, where they have reportedly blocked access to markets and clinics, accusing local residents of collaboration with the enemy. The MRDP-Twirwaneho group, representing Minembwe residents, claimed these restrictions target civilians based on their Banyamulenge ethnicity.
In recent weeks, AFC/M23 has captured large parts of South Kivu in the Rusizi Valley, prompting many Burundian, DRC, Wazalendo, and FDLR forces to flee to areas including Bujumbura and Fizi. Nyarugabo noted that troops in the South Kivu highlands are the main source of security problems for civilians, but nearby, AFC/M23 and Twirwaneho fighters have pledged to end the threat.

*Capture of Uvira Seen as Key Opportunity for Regional Peace
Uvira, South Kivu, DRC - The capture of Uvira by AFC/M23 fighters has dominated headlines across Africa and globally. While some view it as a normal military operation against over 20,000 Burundian soldiers allied with FARDC, FDLR, Wazalendo, and mercenaries targeting Banyamulenge communities, others see a deeper political significance.
A delegate from the Great Lakes region likened the capture of Uvira to Moses striking the rock to bring forth water, signaling hope for the region. He noted that following the Washington Agreement signed on December 4, 2025, the takeover does not threaten regional security, calling it “one of the best developments in regional politics in recent years.”
The diplomat explained that the operation is the culmination of previous talks in Washington, Doha, Nairobi, and Luanda, some of which have yet to reach a conclusion. He emphasized that the capture of Uvira diminishes the will to fight among opposing forces, particularly affecting Burundi, Kinshasa’s main ally. With Uvira under M23 control, Burundian ground troops can no longer easily move in or out of the DRC, limiting military options.
Located just over 20 kilometers from Bujumbura, Uvira is strategically significant. The Burundian government has warned that threats to Uvira could extend to Bujumbura. Meanwhile, for President Tshisekedi, the loss of Uvira disrupts the flow of war materials, reducing Kinshasa’s logistical advantages.
M23 believes it can defend Uvira effectively, arguing that its control protects Congolese Tutsis and other victims of violence in the two Kivus. The group stresses that for lasting peace, all parties-Kinshasa, M23, Rwanda, and Burundi-must respect the Washington and Doha agreements.
While Rwanda has shown willingness to honor its commitments, the diplomat underscored that Burundi must also uphold its obligations. Meanwhile, M23 and the DRC government have resumed talks in Doha, as Burundian troops who had entered the DRC fled Uvira, some returning home while others sought refuge in forests.
*Burundian Warplanes and Drones Target Banyamulenge in South Kivu

According to AFC/M23 spokesman Lawrence Kanyuka, the strikes occurred in the early hours of December 13, 2025, with Burundian troops directly involved.
Kanyuka said: “Sukhoi-25 warplanes, combat helicopters, and military drones operating from Burundian territory indiscriminately attacked the Banyamulenge villages of Mikenke and Rwitsankuku, in blatant violation of international humanitarian law.”
The AFC/M23 coalition condemned the attacks, stating they targeted civilians, killed women and children, and amounted to war crimes and crimes against humanity.
On the morning of December 12, 2025, a Sukhoi-25 fighter jet also attacked a Banyamulenge refugee camp in the Mikenke area.
Politician Moïse Nyarugabo attributed the attacks on civilians to the continued battlefield defeats of DRC and Burundian forces, following their loss of large parts of the Rusizi Valley in the past two weeks.
*AFC/M23 Warns International Community Against Labeling Its Fighters as Rwandan Agents
South Kivu, DRC - The AFC/M23 coalition, active in the Democratic Republic of Congo, stated that its fighters should not be portrayed as foreigners or Rwandan agents, warning that such narratives could justify stripping Congolese of their citizenship. Spokesperson Lawrence Kanyuka made the statement on December 13, 2025, in response to accusations from members of the United Nations Security Council alleging ceasefire violations and support from Rwanda.
Kanyuka explained that recent heavy fighting in the Rusizi Valley, South Kivu province, was caused by DRC army forces, Burundian troops, FDLR militants, Wazalendo, and foreign mercenaries violating the ceasefire and attacking civilians.
He emphasized that AFC/M23 is a coalition of Congolese fighters addressing the root causes of insecurity, including poor leadership that backs armed groups such as the genocidal FDLR and targets Congolese Tutsi communities.
“The notion that AFC/M23 are ‘foreigners’ or agents of Rwanda distorts the truth dangerously, lending legitimacy to Kinshasa’s agenda to expel or marginalize Congolese citizens,” Kanyuka said.
While AFC/M23 and Rwanda share concerns over the FDLR threat, Kanyuka stressed that this does not indicate collaboration. The coalition is focused on protecting Congolese refugees, advocating for repatriation, and ensuring security for populations at risk from FDLR attacks, often in coordination with DRC and Burundian forces.
Kanyuka added: “We were forced to take up arms to defend ourselves, as the DRC government continues to target Congolese Tutsi and ignore signed agreements. Since the Doha talks began, DRC warplanes and drones have repeatedly attacked our positions and liberated areas, causing many innocent deaths.”
He also highlighted that DRC forces have blocked roads used by Banyamulenge residents in Minembwe, aiming to starve civilians, while attacks by drones continue, leaving them with no means of defense.
“The letters and announcements we have sent to the international community have not changed anything. If the DRC government can wage war on its own citizens, we have the right to defend our lives and those of our comrades under threat of extermination,” Kanyuka said.
AFC/M23 called for genuine dialogue and compliance by the DRC government with signed agreements to address the root causes of the conflict, urging the international community to press Kinshasa to honor its commitments.
*DRC Government Accused of Maintaining Ties with FDLR Despite Promises to Dismantle It
Kinshasa, DRC - Despite statements since October 10, 2025 claiming that the FDLR would be dismantled, evidence suggests the DRC government continues to support the group, raising concerns about ongoing regional insecurity.
The FDLR, accused of participating in the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi, was formed in 2000 by remnants of the Ex-FAR army, Interahamwe, politicians, and Rwandan refugees remaining in the DRC. Since May 2022, the FDLR has been directly involved in operations against M23, in coalition with the DRC army and groups such as Wazalendo.
The Rwandan government has repeatedly warned the international community that the FDLR receives support from the DRC government, including equipment, military training, and food, enabling the group to strengthen its operations.
On June 27, 2025, Rwanda and the DRC, with U.S. assistance, signed a peace agreement, which included the destruction of the FDLR, while Rwanda agreed to lift certain security measures. The DRC military statement of October 10 called on FDLR fighters to disarm and instructed Congolese military and civilians to stop collaborating with the group, in line with the Washington agreement.
However, a UN experts’ report released on December 8, 2025, revealed that the announcement strained relations between the DRC army and FDLR fighters, angering allied Wazalendo groups. The report stated that some DRC army officers and government representatives assured the FDLR that cooperation would continue due to the group’s significant role in ongoing operations, noting that the FARDC lacks the capacity to dismantle it.
Reliable sources indicate that on November 29, 2025, the DRC army delivered ammunition and food to the FDLR and allied groups, including NDC-R and Nyatura, in areas such as Bibwe and Hembe, following orders from Colonel Salomon Tokolonga, commander of the 3402nd Regiment of the DRC Army.
Col. Tokolonga, already on the U.S. sanctions list, has a history of supporting the FDLR and other armed groups responsible for atrocities against civilians.
Conclusion
The period from December 1 to 14, 2025, was marked by a striking paradox between major diplomatic progress and a rapid, violent escalation of regional conflict.
The central diplomatic development was the signing of the DRC-Rwanda Peace and Economic Cooperation Agreement in Washington, D.C., on December 4, 2025. The accord established a broad framework for regional stability, committing both parties to the dismantling of armed groups, notably the FDLR, and to joint economic initiatives such as the Rusizi III hydroelectric project.
Yet this newly established framework was immediately tested-and weakened-by a sharp resurgence of fighting in North and South Kivu, including the capture of strategic areas by the AFC/M23 coalition. The escalation triggered mutual accusations of ceasefire violations, undermining both the Washington agreement and the earlier Qatar-mediated truce. Kigali accused Burundi of deploying more than 20,000 troops and contributing to instability in South Kivu, while the renewed violence drove over 750 Congolese refugees into Rwanda. At the same time, reports alleging continued coordination between certain FARDC elements and the FDLR stood in direct contradiction to the commitments undertaken in Washington.
Ultimately, while a critical legal and political foundation for regional cooperation has been laid, the swift return to hostilities, persistent non-compliance, and continued proxy dynamics highlight how fragile the peace process remains. Lasting stability in the Great Lakes region will depend not on signed agreements alone, but on verifiable actions, sustained political will, and strict adherence to commitments by all parties involved.
