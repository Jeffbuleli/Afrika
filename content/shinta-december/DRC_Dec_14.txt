Politics and Diplomacy
*MONUSCO Welcomes DRC-Rwanda Peace Agreement, Calls for Rigorous Implementation
Kinshasa/Washington - On Thursday, December 4, 2025, MONUSCO welcomed the adoption in Washington of the peace agreement and the regional economic integration framework between the DRC and Rwanda, facilitated by the United States, praising the determination of Presidents Félix Tshisekedi and Paul Kagame.
The UN mission highlighted that this agreement - which includes a ceasefire, the disarmament of the FDLR, the withdrawal of Rwandan troops, and the return of refugees - provides a new opportunity to restore confidence, alleviate civilian suffering, and lay the foundations for lasting peace in eastern Congo.
In a statement, Bruno Lemarquis, acting head of MONUSCO, emphasized that the combination of this agreement with the one signed between the DRC and the AFC/M23 on November 15, as well as Security Council resolution 2773 (2025), offers hope for a shift from confrontation to political solutions, provided that implementation respects the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the DRC.
The mission also welcomed the involvement of Qatar, the African Union (AU), the East African Community (EAC), SADC, and mediator Faure Gnassingbé, while calling for continued support and the promotion of inclusive dialogue.
In line with its mandate, MONUSCO will continue to support these efforts, particularly in civilian protection, ceasefire monitoring, disarmament of armed groups, and the stabilization of affected areas.
*DRC Government Suspends "Save the DRC" Movement Linked to Joseph Kabila
Kinshasa - On Monday, December 1, 2025, the Congolese government suspended all activities of the "Save the DRC" movement, created in October in Nairobi at the instigation of former president Joseph Kabila.
Governors received instructions to strictly enforce this measure under penalty of sanctions, according to a telegram from Deputy Prime Minister of the Interior Jacquemain Shabani.
The ban, described as a "diversion" by Ferdinand Kambere, deputy permanent secretary of the PPRD, comes amid the platform’s opposition to the Kinshasa-led negotiations in Doha and Washington, which it considers a threat to national sovereignty.
This decision follows the suspension of the PPRD, confirmed on October 27, 2025, by the High Court of Kinshasa-Gombe, for "complicity with the AFC/M23 rebellion".
*Federalism Debate: CDER Warns Against a “Dangerous Drift” Amid Crisis
Kinshasa - The Current of Renewed Democrats (CDER), led by Jean-Lucien Bussa, has raised concerns over the resurgence of the federalism debate in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), recently reignited by former Speaker of the National Assembly Olivier Kamitatu.
In a statement published on December 1, 2025, the CDER described the debate as “inopportune” given the ongoing security crisis, identity-based tensions, and the looming risk of balkanization.
The movement stressed that national unity must remain the priority, citing the partial failure of decentralization, which has at times been exploited for ethnic or regional interests. Steering the country toward federalism, the CDER warned, would constitute a “historical and moral crime.”
The party called on the political class to act responsibly, urging leaders to focus on security, national cohesion, and the well-being of citizens rather than engaging in debates likely to fuel internal divisions.
*DRC-Belgium: Kinshasa and Brussels Strengthen Military Cooperation
Kinshasa - The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Belgium agreed to consolidate their military partnership during a meeting on Monday, December 1, 2025, in Kinshasa. The talks involved Congolese Deputy Prime Minister for Defense Guy Kabombo Muadiamvita and his Belgian counterpart Theo Francken, accompanied by their respective chiefs of staff.

*DRC-Rwanda: Peace Agreement Ratified in Washington to Turn the Page on Three Decades of Tension
Washington - Presidents Félix Tshisekedi and Paul Kagame ratified the DRC-Rwanda Peace Agreement on Thursday, December 4, 2025, in Washington, marking a major step toward the pacification of eastern DRC after nearly three decades of violence.
Based on the Declaration of Principles of April 25, 2025, the agreement commits both countries to mutual respect for territorial integrity, cessation of support for armed groups, and the creation of a joint security coordination mechanism. Under the accord, Kinshasa pledges to neutralize the FDLR, while Kigali must withdraw its troops from Congolese territory.
The agreement also provides for the voluntary return of refugees, guaranteed humanitarian access to vulnerable populations, and the establishment, within three months, of a regional economic integration framework.
President Tshisekedi reaffirmed his commitment to implementing the agreements "in all sincerity," calling for the same rigor from Rwanda." President Kagame welcomed an agreement “that contains everything necessary” to end the conflict, cautioning that its implementation may face ups and downs, while reaffirming Rwanda’s commitment to a stable and prosperous future.
*Tshisekedi Assures U.S. and Rwanda of DRC Commitment to Dismantle Armed Groups
Washington, D.C. - President Félix Tshisekedi has assured the United States and Rwanda that the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) will fully implement the provisions of the peace and economic cooperation agreement signed with Rwanda, including the dismantling of armed groups operating on its territory.
Speaking after the signing ceremony held in the United States on December 4, 2025, where he and Rwandan President Paul Kagame formalized the agreement, Tshisekedi reaffirmed his government’s commitment to peace and security.
“The DRC is committed to respecting, with full integrity, all the requirements of this agreement,” Tshisekedi said. “We will do so continuously, in the pursuit of peace and security for our people, and for the unity and sovereignty of our country.”
The Congolese leader expressed confidence that Rwanda would also honor the agreement, stressing that the two neighbors would work together in a spirit of mutual respect.
“We are moving toward a peaceful partnership based on mutual respect, non-interference, and a shared determination to combat armed groups, end all support for them, and build a true partnership,” he added.
Tshisekedi said the agreement places both countries in exceptional circumstances that could pave the way for lasting peace, while emphasizing that the DRC government would remain vigilant as it works with regional and international partners to fulfill its obligations.
“This Washington peace and economic agreement must serve our people,” he said, “and stand as a clear sign of our determination to close the chapter of confrontation and open a new era of cooperation and lasting peace in the region.”
Key Provisions of the Agreement
The Rwanda-DRC peace agreement предусматривает the neutralization and dismantling of the FDLR and other affiliated armed groups, while Rwanda is expected to lift security measures it had previously put in place in response to regional threats.
The accord also outlines joint initiatives between Rwanda and the DRC, including:
Construction of the Rusizi III hydroelectric dam
Shared management of resources in Lake Kivu
Cooperation in trade and mining
Protection of national parks and biodiversity
The United States, which facilitated the agreement, said that full implementation would lead to sustainable peace and development in the Great Lakes region, delivering tangible benefits to citizens on both sides of the border.

The two partners welcomed the progress in discussions between the Congolese private sector and the government, and expressed their willingness to explore financing options to synchronize the work with ongoing projects in Angola, maximizing the economic and geopolitical impact of the corridor, which they described as an essential catalyst for regional development.

*United States Accuses Paul Kagame of Orchestrating War in Eastern DRC
New York - During a UN Security Council meeting on December 12, 2025, US Permanent Representative Michael Waltz accused Rwandan President Paul Kagame of having “planned and executed the war in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC).”
The US representative claimed that Kigali has controlled the M23 rebel movement and its political wing, the AFC, since 2021, providing logistical support, equipment, training, and troops-estimated at 5,000 to 7,000 soldiers-to carry out armed operations in eastern Congo.
According to Washington, Rwanda has deployed surface-to-air missiles and sophisticated weaponry on multiple fronts, fueling the offensive that led to the AFC/M23 capture of the strategic city of Uvira. The US also reported increased use of suicide drones and ground artillery.
The United States called for the immediate withdrawal of Rwandan forces and accountability measures against those responsible for these acts, emphasizing the urgent need to restore peace and stability in the eastern DRC.

Kinshasa - Congolese opposition leader Martin Fayulu described the peace agreement signed on December 4, 2025, in Washington between the DRC and Rwanda as a “trap” orchestrated by Rwandan President Paul Kagame, during an interview on France 24 on December 11.

*Over 1,100 Taxpayers Adopt Standardized Invoicing
Kinshasa - The Ministry of Finance announced on Tuesday, December 2, 2025, that more than 1,100 taxpayers are now using standardized invoicing through the e-UF electronic invoicing system, provided free of charge by the government.
In a press release, Minister Doudou Fwamba reaffirmed his support for economic operators following the implementation of the invoicing requirement. He specified that the e-UF system can be used temporarily or permanently, including by large companies with their own invoicing systems.

*AFC/M23 Announces Major Tax Reform in Kivu
Goma - On December 3, 2025, the AFC/M23 coalition unveiled a historic tax reform in North and South Kivu, aimed at simplifying the taxation system and reducing administrative burdens.
The plan calls for the elimination of 225 taxes and duties, representing nearly 60% of the current system, according to Fanny Kaj, Deputy Head of the Finance Department. The reform seeks to end the fragmentation of the 536 existing taxes by merging levies, removing various operating licenses, and consolidating sector-specific taxes.
The reform also includes the complete digitization of tax collection, the creation of a permanent tax reform commission, the opening of local collection centers, and the establishment of a single-window system operational from January 2026.
Developed in consultation with the Federal Executive Council (CEF), civil society, and religious actors, this initiative is expected to improve transparency, reduce the burden on businesses, and strengthen the economic attractiveness of North and South Kivu.
*Standardized Invoicing Adopted to Modernize Tax Management
Kinshasa - As of December 1, 2025, the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) has made the use of standardized invoicing mandatory, marking a major reform in the country’s tax system.
Led by the General Directorate of Taxes (DGI), this reform aims to strengthen transparency and combat tax fraud. The secure electronic document standardizes the identification of the seller and buyer, the unique invoice number, the goods or services invoiced, and the applicable taxes.
The measure applies to public and private companies, NGOs, public institutions, traders, and various service providers, requiring an adaptation of invoicing systems while providing consumers with reliable and traceable proof of purchase.
Backed by a broad awareness campaign, the reform is designed to increase public revenue collection and improve the country’s economic governance, aligning with the government’s modernization agenda.

*DRC: MSF Warns of 15,000 New HIV Infections Among Youth in 2024
Kinshasa, DRC - Doctors Without Borders (MSF) revealed on Wednesday, December 3, 2025, that nearly 15,000 young Congolese under the age of 25 contracted HIV in 2024, according to data from the National AIDS Control Program (PNMLS) presented during a press conference in Kinshasa on the sidelines of World AIDS Day.
Gisèle Mucinya, medical coordinator of MSF's HIV project, described the situation as alarming, citing insufficient prevention of mother-to-child transmission, limited access to information, testing, and treatment, and persistent stigma.
She highlighted key obstacles, including the limited availability of tests, the cost of voluntary testing, and restrictions on minors being tested without parental consent.
Since 2019, MSF has been running youth clubs providing medical and psychological support, and the organization is calling on the government to increase funding amid the disengagement of partners such as USAID and the World Bank. This support is aimed at intensifying awareness campaigns and expanding HIV prevention initiatives nationwide.

*Brig Gen Byamungu Returns to Uvira Amid M23 Takeover
Uvira, DRC - The remarkable story of Brig Gen Bernard Byamungu unfolded in the city where he once served as head of operations for the Congolese government army. Originally a CNDP soldier, Byamungu was integrated into government forces in 2009, but his journey would take a dramatic turn.
In May 2012, Brig Gen Byamungu returned to Uvira, his last place of residence, facing life imprisonment and the stripping of his military medals. Between 2009 and 2012, he had commanded operations for the government army across Uvira, earning notoriety in the region.
Later in 2012, Byamungu joined a new rebel group composed of former CNDP soldiers. Those who defected from the government army in Fizi and Uvira were few, and many were quickly arrested. Some attempted to flee to North Kivu, where they would later establish the M23 rebel group. Byamungu, however, opposed this move.

They were then transferred to Bukavu and onward to Ndolo military prison in Kinshasa.
After eight years, an agreement between President Félix Tshisekedi and M23 resulted in Byamungu’s release. By December 9, 2025, the city of Uvira had fallen under the control of AFC/M23, the same group with which Byamungu aligned. Upon returning to the courthouse where he had been sentenced, he was arrested by small soldiers, who confiscated his rings and necklace following the verdict.
Meanwhile, the AFC/M23 solidified its presence in Uvira, as well as other key cities including Goma and Bukavu, asserting control over large parts of eastern DRC.

Faced with this resurgence of attacks as the end-of-year holidays approach, civil society is calling on the army to strengthen security, while the FARDC have not yet responded.
*North Kivu: Civil Society Raises Alarm Over Deteriorating Humanitarian Situation in M23-Occupied Areas
North Kivu - Civil society is raising the alarm about the critical humanitarian situation in the territories of Masisi and Rutshuru, where people returning to areas controlled by the AFC-M23 are living in extremely precarious conditions.
According to Léopold Mwisha, a local civil society activist, families, mainly composed of farmers and herders, are suffering from malnutrition, lack of medical care, and insufficient shelter, while their agricultural and pastoral activities are severely disrupted.
He is calling for urgent intervention from authorities and humanitarian partners to assist these vulnerable communities and mitigate the consequences of the rebel occupation.
Conclusion
The period from December 1 to 14, 2025, was marked by a stark and troubling contradiction between diplomatic ambition and security reality in the Great Lakes region.
The signing of the DRC-Rwanda Peace Agreement in Washington on December 4 raised hopes for a lasting de-escalation, outlining commitments to a ceasefire, the neutralization of the FDLR, and the launch of joint economic initiatives such as the Rusizi III hydroelectric project.
Yet this diplomatic breakthrough was almost immediately undermined by a sharp deterioration of security in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, particularly in South Kivu. The capture of Uvira by the AFC/M23, accompanied by renewed fighting and mass displacement, exposed the fragility of the agreement. Kinshasa and Washington openly accused Rwanda of violating the accord, alleging continued military support for the rebellion and an escalation of cross-border operations.
At the same time, both countries pressed ahead with internal reforms: Rwanda advanced key infrastructure projects, while the DRC emphasized economic stabilization and state resilience, citing nearly $7.4 billion in foreign exchange reserves and the adoption of a security-centered 2026 national budget.
Ultimately, this two-week period illustrates a central paradox: while political frameworks for peace and development are taking shape, their success remains hostage to realities on the ground. The rapid collapse of the ceasefire underscores that the implementation of agreements, the cessation of proxy warfare, and the protection of civilians remain the most urgent-and most fragile-tests for peace in the Great Lakes region.
