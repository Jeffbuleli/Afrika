Verify compliance with safety measures, including the immediate cessation of all gold panning activities
Prefect Mohamed Ag Alwali addressed the local population, emphasizing that these measures aim to:
Guarantee residents’ safety
Prevent conflicts related to site exploitation
Maintain control pending sustainable management solutions
Although the deposit could provide significant local economic opportunities, the current security context prohibits exploitation. The prefect urged strict adherence to authorities’ directives, warning that non-compliance could lead to sanctions.

*Mali: Radio Stations Suspended and Journalists Arrested - Junta Tightens Press Freedom
On November 23, 2025, the High Authority for Communication announced the three-month suspension of radio stations Aadar Koïma in Gao and Aadar Koukia in Ansongo. This decision followed their coverage of the creation of a movement demanding the departure of Assimi Goïta.
Key developments:
Journalist arrests: On November 5, 2025, journalists Malick Aliou Maïga and Youssoufa Moussa were arrested in Gao and Ansongo, respectively, for reporting on the same topic.
State control: These measures illustrate the total control exercised by the State over communication, where any critical speech or reporting can result in imprisonment.
Threat to journalists: Media professionals face constant risk of violent arrest or prolonged detention, even for a misinterpreted phrase or sensitive report.
Impact on independent media: Local radio stations, community television channels, and news platforms are regularly targeted, shut down, or intimidated, depriving communities in Gao and Ansongo of reliable sources of information.
Broader implications: This demonstrates the military dictatorship’s grip on freedom of expression, reinforcing a climate of fear and attempting to impose a single narrative, at the expense of debate, transparency, and public trust.
*Mali: Kal-Akal Denounces New Ethnic Cleansing in Timbuktu Region (November 25-27, 2025)
Following the violence between November 25 and 27, 2025, the Kidal-based human rights organization Kal-Akal issued a damning statement denouncing a new "ethnic cleansing" in the Timbuktu region.
According to the organization, a joint patrol of the Malian army and Africa Corps conducted a series of deadly operations in localities including Goundam, Gargando, and Bintagoungou, resulting in numerous casualties, abductions, and significant material damage. Kal-Akal asserts that the victims are primarily civilians targeted because of their ethnicity.
Atrocities documented include:
Summary executions and enforced disappearances
Systematic looting and burning of markets
Villages such as Amarnane, Nijhaltate, and Taroma saw mutilated bodies, wounded women and children, and people abducted to unknown locations
Destruction of water points, tents, and pastures, worsening the humanitarian situation in an already insecure region
Kal-Akal’s demands and actions:
Immediate protection of civilians and safe humanitarian access
End to impunity for forces involved
Continued documentation of abuses to hold perpetrators accountable
For Kal-Akal, the stability of the region depends on justice, transparency, and protection of vulnerable communities.
*Mali: Panel of Democrats Condemns Massacres by Malian Army and Afrika Korps in Timbuktu Region
The Panel of Democrats, composed of members of the Malian opposition, expressed profound outrage at the massacres of November 25 and 26, 2025, committed by a joint patrol of the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) and Russian elements of the Afrika Korps.
These attacks resulted in 14 civilian deaths and several disappearances in the Timbuktu region, notably in Razelma, Amaranane, Tissikoreye, Émimalane, Toroma, Takara, Zouéra, and Nijhaltate.
The Panel calls on the Attorney General of the Transition and the United Nations to open a formal investigation.
The Panel’s key demands:
Unequivocal condemnation of summary executions and destruction of civilian property.
Independent international investigation and prosecution for war crimes.
Condemnation of the scorched-earth policy pursued by Africa Corps since 2024.
Immediate protection of civilians, particularly the nomads of Lake Faguibine.
Solidarity with the families of the victims.

Conclusion
The period from November 24 to December 7, 2025, presents a stark duality in Mali’s trajectory, characterized by significant diplomatic and macroeconomic strides contrasted against a deteriorating security and human rights landscape.
1. Diplomatic and Economic Fortification The transitional government has successfully reinforced its international standing and regional integration. The Confederation of Sahel States (AES/CSA) is rapidly operationalizing, with intense preparations for the upcoming College of Heads of State session in Bamako. Simultaneously, Mali is diversifying its strategic partnerships, strengthening ties with Iran, Moldova, and the Philippines. Economically, the government received strong validation from the IMF and WAEMU for its fiscal discipline and a projected growth rate of 6.1%. The launch of the first National Export Day further signals a push toward economic autonomy and industrialization.
2. The Security Paradox and Energy Crisis Despite these institutional successes, the security situation remains precarious. While the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) continue to destroy terrorist bases and acquire new assets, insurgent groups like JNIM have intensified their blockade strategies. The encirclement of Bamako and the resumption of attacks on fuel convoys have triggered a severe hydrocarbon crisis, directly impacting the daily lives of citizens and threatening economic stability. The collapse of the temporary fuel agreement with JNIM highlights the fragility of the country's supply lines.
3. Human Rights and Social Strain The cost of the conflict on the civilian population is escalating. Human rights organizations and opposition panels have documented alarming allegations of "ethnic cleansing," summary executions, and scorched-earth tactics in the Timbuktu and Azawad regions attributed to FAMa and the Africa Corps. Concurrently, the civic space is shrinking, evidenced by the suspension of radio stations and the arrest of journalists, signaling a tightening grip on freedom of expression.
Final Outlook As Mali prepares to host the AES Heads of State summit in late December, the government faces the critical challenge of reconciling its diplomatic ambitions with urgent domestic realities. The coming weeks will be pivotal in determining whether the state can leverage its regional alliances to secure its territory and energy supply, or if the worsening humanitarian and security crises will undermine the macroeconomic and political gains achieved thus far.
