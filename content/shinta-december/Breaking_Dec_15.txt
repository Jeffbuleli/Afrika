*Burundi Faces an Unwelcome New Neighbor After Fall of Uvira, Senator Says
Bujumbura, December 15, 2025 - Burundian Senator Evode Uwizeyimana has warned that Burundi must come to terms with the AFC/M23, following the capture of Uvira in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, or risk sliding into a strategic and economic dead end.
Speaking on Burundi National Television, Uwizeyimana said the takeover of Uvira places Burundi in a difficult position, urging authorities to seek an understanding with the group now controlling the city. “Not doing so would expose Burundi to problems without solutions,” he cautioned.
The senator stressed Uvira’s strategic and economic importance, describing it as both a former hub for anti-M23 operations and a key center of trade for Burundi. He noted that defeated forces from Goma and Bukavu, including Wazalendo fighters, had regrouped there, making the city a prime target.
Uwizeyimana warned that continued M23 advances could isolate Burundi, leaving it with limited access routes and threatening cross-border commerce vital to Bujumbura’s economy. He also raised concerns over Burundian troops still stranded in the highlands of South Kivu, arguing that their withdrawal would require negotiations with the M23.
According to the senator, the capture of Uvira was unrelated to the Washington peace agreement between Rwanda and the DRC and followed months of military preparation by the AFC/M23 amid stalled negotiations.
In response to the takeover, Burundi closed the Kavimvira and Vugizo border posts with the DRC, adding to existing border closures with Rwanda, further underscoring the growing regional strain.
*Nduhungirehe Accuses International Community of Ignoring FARDC Airstrikes
December 15, 2025 - Rwanda’s Minister of Foreign Affairs and Cooperation, Amb. Olivier Nduhungirehe, has criticized the international community for what he described as silence over airstrikes carried out by the Congolese army in areas controlled by the AFC/M23.
Reacting on his X (formerly Twitter) account, Nduhungirehe said Sukhoi-25 aircraft and FARDC drones bombed the Rwitsankuku area on the morning of December 15, killing civilians, notably in Banyamulenge-inhabited villages in South Kivu.
He accused Western countries of failing to condemn what he called repeated and deliberate attacks on densely populated areas, while reacting only when the AFC/M23 retaliates and later withdraws.
Air and drone strikes in Banyamulenge areas have intensified in recent days following the defeat of Burundian, FARDC, Wazalendo and FDLR forces in the Rusizi Valley.
*Cardinal Ambongo Calls for Inclusive Dialogue After M23 Seizure of Uvira
Kinshasa December 14, 2025 - The Archbishop of Kinshasa, Cardinal Fridolin Ambongo, has renewed calls for an inclusive national dialogue following the capture of Uvira by the AFC/M23. Speaking at the close of the 15th plenary assembly of the Association of Episcopal Conferences of Central Africa (ACEC), he urged Congolese leaders to address the root causes of the conflict in eastern DRC.
Cardinal Ambongo questioned the effectiveness of the Washington Agreement signed on December 4 between Presidents Félix Tshisekedi and Paul Kagame, noting that Uvira fell under occupation less than a week later. He warned against peace initiatives that exclude Congolese voices and legitimize the exploitation of the country’s resources.
Emphasizing that “peace remains possible,” he endorsed the CENCO-ECC social pact as a credible path toward lasting peace and coexistence in the DRC and the Great Lakes region.
*US Considers Sanctions as Violence Undermines Washington Peace Deal
The United States is considering sanctions and other diplomatic measures to enforce the Washington peace agreement between the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Rwanda, US Ambassador Lucy Tamlyn said on Monday, December 15, in Kinshasa.
Speaking at a press conference, Tamlyn reaffirmed Washington’s commitment to the accord, warning that all available tools-including bilateral and multilateral actions-are under review to ensure compliance. She expressed US disappointment over the renewed violence in eastern DRC, which erupted a day after the agreement was signed, and pointed to Rwanda’s responsibility.
Tamlyn said the fall of Uvira, South Kivu’s provisional capital, does not reflect a failure of the US-sponsored initiative, stressing that President Donald Trump remains personally engaged in efforts to restore peace.
Meanwhile, the UN Secretary-General has strongly condemned the latest AFC/M23 offensive in several parts of South Kivu, including Uvira, and called for an immediate and unconditional ceasefire, warning of the risk of wider regional escalation.
*Tshisekedi in Luanda Amid Renewed Violence in Eastern DRC
President Félix Tshisekedi of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) arrived in Luanda, Angola, on Sunday, December 14, for a working visit, according to several sources.
The visit is reportedly aimed at reviving the Luanda process, a regional diplomatic initiative to restore peace in eastern DRC, amid continued violence in the Kivu region linked to the M23 armed group. According to Le Potentiel, the talks seek to reinforce regional mediation efforts.
Other sources say Tshisekedi met with Angolan President João Lourenço to discuss bilateral cooperation and regional peace and security in Central Africa.
The visit comes days after the DRC-Rwanda peace agreement, signed on December 4, 2025, in Washington, under US mediation. Despite the accord, fighting has intensified, with the AFC/M23, allegedly backed by Kigali, seizing several towns in South Kivu, including the strategic city of Uvira.

*AFC/M23 Facilitates Return of Over 500 Burundians from Uvira
Uvira, December 14, 2025 - The AFC/M23 coalition has assisted more than 500 Burundian nationals to return home from Uvira, days after they were stranded by fighting and the closure of the Kavimvira and Vugizo border crossings by Burundi.
The returnees, many of them traders who had been working in South Kivu, said they were trapped in Uvira after the rebels entered the city on December 9. “I was stuck because of the fighting. Sometimes we had to hide. I am happy to return safely,” one Burundian said at the Kavimvira crossing.
Another returnee, identified as Mugenzi, said dozens of Burundian businesspeople faced similar difficulties and stressed that the move was a temporary return, pending security developments.
AFC/M23 spokesperson Lawrence Kanyuka said the operation followed discussions with Burundian authorities, arguing that the group had not closed the border. “These are our fellow Africans. Our border is open, but Burundi’s is closed. We asked them to open it for humanitarian reasons,” he said.
Meanwhile, more than 20,000 Congolese civilians who fled to Burundi following the fall of Uvira remain unable to return, as Burundian authorities have kept the border closed.
*Burundi Deploys Troops and Heavy Weapons Near Rwandan Border as Tensions Rise
Bujumbura, December 15, 2025 - Burundi has intensified its military presence along the Rwandan border, deploying troops, heavy weapons, and combat equipment amid escalating regional tensions linked to the conflict in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.
According to security sources, Burundian forces have reinforced positions in Busoni commune, Butanyerera province, near the Gasenyi-Nemba border, with senior military officials confirming the deployment of heavy artillery to counter potential threats from Rwanda. Additional troops have been transported to military camps, including the 411th camp in Mutwenzi, while night patrols by police and Imbonerakure youth have increased in border areas.
Security measures have also tightened in Bujumbura Province (former Cibitoke), where Burundian forces reportedly fired shells toward Kamanyola, in South Kivu. These strikes preceded AFC/M23 advances across the Rusizi Valley, reaching Uvira on Lake Tanganyika.
Before the UN Security Council, Burundi accused Rwanda of backing the AFC/M23, while residents near the border report growing fear of a possible interstate conflict. “We sleep dressed, ready to flee,” one local resident said.
Multiple sources further report a significant influx of weapons, including combat drones, with around 30 trucks delivering arms to the Mukoni military base in recent weeks-fueling concerns that Burundi is preparing for a wider confrontation.
*South Kivu: Sukhoi-25 Jets and Drones Strike Banyamulenge Areas
South Kivu, December 15, 2025 - Warplanes and drones have carried out a series of airstrikes on areas inhabited by the Banyamulenge community in South Kivu province, causing civilian deaths and injuries, according to opposition politician Moïse Nyarugabo.
Nyarugabo said the first drone strike hit the Rugezi area in Fizi territory at around 5:00 p.m. on December 15. Shortly afterward, a Sukhoi-25 fighter jet bombed Rwitsankuku, before continuing its attacks on Gahwera and Mikenke.
“These strikes killed many civilians and left others wounded,” Nyarugabo said, adding that a similar Sukhoi-25 attack had already targeted Rwitsankuku on the morning of December 14.
According to him, the aircraft and drones involved are believed to belong to Burundi. He called for accountability, urging that those who ordered the strikes - including pilots and airbase controllers - be held responsible.
The air and drone attacks have intensified since last week, following the defeat of Burundian, FARDC, Wazalendo and FDLR forces by the AFC/M23 in the Rusizi Valley, further deepening fears of a widening regional conflict.
*Walikale: FDLR Burns Village, Four Killed
Walikale, December 15, 2025 - The FDLR armed group burned down an entire village in the Tuonane area of Waloa Loanda, in Walikale territory, North Kivu, killing four people and forcing residents to flee to nearby villages.
Local sources say an FDLR fighter known as “General Mudayongwa” ordered residents to leave land he claimed to have purchased from local authorities-an allegation the community denies. After villagers refused, FDLR fighters torched homes and attacked the area.
Two members of the Mai-Mai Kifuafua group were among those killed during clashes involving small arms and heavy weapons.
The attack comes despite commitments in the June 27 DRC-Rwanda peace agreement to dismantle the FDLR. A UN experts’ report released December 8 said the group continues to operate, with elements of the Congolese army still cooperating with it.
*More Than 30 Dead in Boat Capsizing on the Kwango River
At least 30 people have died after a wooden boat (baleinière) capsized on Saturday, December 13, on the Kwango River, near Bolo village, in Bagata territory, Kwango province.
According to National Assembly member Gary Sakata, who spoke on Monday to Radio Okapi, most of the victims were teachers traveling to Bandundu to collect their salaries. Several passengers are still reported missing, and the exact cause of the accident has not yet been established.
The boat was carrying more than 100 passengers along with around 700 sacks of maize and cassava, coming from the villages of Kingukukapayi, Kusu, and Fambondo. It reportedly hit an obstacle, causing it to capsize and resulting in the loss of all luggage.
Some passengers were also traveling to Bandundu to prepare for end-of-year celebrations, Sakata added.
The tragedy follows a similar incident on the Kwango River in September 2024, when another boat capsized, killing 12 people and leaving several others injured or missing.
