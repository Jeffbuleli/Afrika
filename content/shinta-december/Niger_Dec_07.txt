*ESA Deepens Institutional Integration at High-Level Ministerial Session
OUAGADOUGOU, Burkina Faso - November 26, 2025 - The Alliance of Sahel States (ESA) marked a major step forward in its regional integration project during an extensive ministerial coordination session that brought together the foreign ministers of Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger. The meeting was chaired by Captain Ibrahim Traoré, President of Burkina Faso and current Chair of the ESA, and focused on accelerating the implementation of the confederation’s emerging institutional framework.

Niger's Minister of Foreign Affairs, Cooperation, and Nigeriens Abroad, Bakary Yaou Sangaré, said the project represents “a sovereign information space enabling the Sahelian peoples to express their own aspirations for development without external mediation.”
On the sidelines of the ministerial session, Minister Sangaré held in-depth bilateral talks with Dr. Khosravinejad Akba, Special Envoy of the Iranian Foreign Minister. Their two-hour meeting focused on technical cooperation in areas such as energy infrastructure, agricultural technology transfer, and pharmaceutical production capacity. Discussions also explored Iranian investment opportunities in Niger’s renewable energy sector, signaling growing interest in diversified partnerships.
*Niger Reactivates Decentralization Framework to Reinforce National Governance
NIAMEY, Niger - November 27, 2025 - After a three-year hiatus, Niger has formally reactivated the Consultation Framework on Decentralization (CCD), reaffirming its central role in guiding local governance reforms. The re-launch took place during a workshop at the Mahatma Gandhi International Conference Center, presided over by Major General Mohamed Toumba, Minister of State for the Interior, Public Security, and Territorial Administration. He was joined by Niamey Regional Governor Assoumane Abdou Harouna and Claudio Tognola, Head of Swiss Cooperation, representing international development partners.
Minister Toumba underscored the government’s strategic vision, noting that decentralization is “not simply an administrative reorganization, but a fundamental societal choice - a governance model that places communities at the heart of public action and national development.”
Technical sessions examined the status of the transfer of competences from central to local authorities, revealing that municipalities currently receive less than 2% of the national budget. Participants recommended a progressive increase to 5% by 2029, aligning the reform with WAEMU’s regional integration targets.
The revitalized reform agenda is organized around four priority pillars:
Clarifying the powers transferred between the central government and local entities.
Establishing sustainable financing mechanisms for local governance.
Reorganizing local planning and development processes.
Significantly strengthening the capacities of local authorities.
Sectoral ministers from Education, Health, Water Resources, and Environment presented detailed progress reports, highlighting advances made to date while acknowledging persistent structural challenges that continue to impede full implementation of the decentralization policy.

*Niger: Uranium Convoys Continue Amid High Tension
NIAMEY, Niger - December 3, 2025 - The uranium sector in Niger remains at the center of escalating tensions following the departure of the first convoy from the SOMAÏR site in Arlit on November 27. Despite warnings from Orano and prior ICSID rulings, multiple shipments have left northern Niger under heavy military escort, reflecting both the political sensitivity and strategic importance of the material.
Local sources reported that the first convoy traveled the Arlit-Agadez road before continuing to Tahoua, arriving in Abalak on December 1. The convoy was protected by Nigerien security units and accompanied by 31 Russian mercenaries, underscoring the high-risk nature of the transport.
In Niamey, President General Abdourahamane Tiani reaffirmed that the uranium belongs “to the Nigerien people” and that its export does not depend “on anyone’s authorization.” Government sources indicate that Niger is seeking a credible buyer to avoid involvement in nuclear proliferation.
Meanwhile, French company Orano denounced the convoy as “illegal,” claiming it had no knowledge of the cargo’s final destination and emphasizing that the ICSID ruling explicitly prohibited any transfer of uranium stockpiles from Arlit. Orano has reserved the right to pursue legal action, including prosecution.
On the night of December 3, 53 trucks belonging to MB TRANS, a company owned by Baye Mohamed, arrived in Niamey carrying uranium, while an additional 20 trucks owned by gold trader Saleh Boss were reportedly en route. These developments mark a decisive phase in the ongoing standoff over Niger’s uranium exports.
*President Tiani Visits Saguia Amont Hydro-Agricultural Site
NIAMEY, Niger - December 3, 2025 - President General Abdourahamane Tiani inspected the Saguia Amont hydro-agricultural development site near Niamey on Wednesday, highlighting ongoing efforts to boost local food production and support farmers.
Welcomed by the Minister of Agriculture and Livestock, the Governor of the Niamey Region, and the Director General of the National Office for Water and Sanitation (ONAH), the President met with 410 farmers to review the rehabilitation of the 105.29-hectare site.
The rehabilitation aims to increase rice yields from 6 to 9 tons per hectare. Works focused on upgrading the pumping system, which includes three pumps with a combined capacity of 540 m³ of water per hour, as well as reinforcing two sections of dikes, the pumping station shelter, 12 km of irrigation and drainage networks, access roads, and housing facilities.
Funded under the national Large-Scale Irrigation Program at a cost of 902,634,016 CFA francs (excluding taxes), the site is now fully operational. Initial estimates post-rehabilitation indicate an expected production of approximately 790 tons of paddy rice, up from 578 tons before the works, while reducing production costs per hectare from 190,000 to 135,558 CFA francs.
President Tiani also toured the rice silo and cooperative headquarters, engaging with producers about the varieties cultivated and the challenges faced. Established in 1973 with Libyan support, the Saguia Amont irrigation scheme remains a critical component of Niamey’s food security and agricultural development strategy, particularly for the city’s 5th district.

*Niger Plans Legal Action Against Orano After Discovery of Radioactive Barrels
Niamey, December 2, 2025 - The Nigerien government announced its intention to sue the French mining group Orano following the discovery of 400 barrels containing radioactive core samples in Madaouela, northern Niger.
Speaking at a press conference, Minister of Justice Alio Daouda condemned Orano, describing the company as a “predator that has lost its prey” and criticizing its historical handling of Niger’s uranium sector. “Several million tons of radioactive waste were left exposed to the open air,” the Minister said.
Measurements of the barrels revealed radiation levels between 7 and 10 microsieverts per hour-far exceeding the 0.5 microsievert safety standard. The samples reportedly contain bismuth-207 and chromium X, highly toxic substances capable of contaminating anyone within a 10-meter radius, prompting serious environmental, health, and social concerns.
The Minister emphasized that Niger’s nationalization of its uranium sector in December 2024 and the discovery of the barrels represent a clear breach of responsibility by Orano. The government plans to seek full compensation for damages.
Orano, which has denied involvement in the controversial convoy of uranium, stated it is prepared to take legal measures, including criminal proceedings, to defend its rights. The incident also reportedly violates an ICSID arbitration ruling from September 2025, which prohibited Niger from selling or transferring uranium produced by SOMAIR.

*A Year of Injustice: Moussa Tchangari, Symbol of a Silenced Civil Society
Niamey, December 3, 2025 - One year ago today, Moussa Tchangari was arrested in Niamey by armed men without a warrant.
Since then, the Secretary General of Alternative Espaces Citoyens, a respected and steadfast figure in Nigerien civil society, remains imprisoned in Filingué Prison. Accused of “apology for terrorism” and “undermining national defense,” he has only been questioned twice in ten months, while international organizations such as Amnesty International, the FIDH, and several UN experts denounce his arbitrary and politically motivated detention.
Moussa Tchangari is not unknown. For decades, he has defended human rights, social justice, and the dignity of Nigeriens under all regimes, never yielding to fear or pressure. His career demonstrates remarkable consistency: denouncing abuses, protecting the most vulnerable, and reminding the authorities of their obligations to the people. Today, this commitment has earned him imprisonment, even though he has simply been exercising his role as a vigilant citizen.
Under the transition led by General Abdourahamane Tiani, critical voices appear to have become the primary targets. The government relentlessly attacks men and women recognized for their integrity and civic engagement, while those who plunge the country into insecurity or anarchy benefit from astonishing leniency. This reversal of priorities creates a disturbing climate of arbitrariness, where loyalty to the nation is paradoxically rewarded with prison.
The prolonged imprisonment of Moussa Tchangari illustrates the excesses of a regime that conflates dissent with threats, criticism with treason. By targeting human rights activists rather than actual criminals, the authorities are undermining not only the rule of law but also the trust of the Nigerien people. The immediate and unconditional release of Moussa Tchangari remains, now more than ever, a demand for justice and a democratic imperative.

Conclusion
The period from November 24 to December 7, 2025, highlights a critical juncture for Niger as the transition government attempts to balance an aggressive pursuit of strategic sovereignty against a backdrop of deteriorating security and internal socio-political tension.
1. Strategic Realignment and Institutional Sovereignty
The government has accelerated its pivot away from traditional Western influence, cementing the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) as its primary geopolitical anchor. This is evidenced by the creation of "ESA Confederation Radio" to counter external narratives and the deepening of ties with non-traditional partners like Iran, China, and Italy for cooperation in energy, security, and education. Concurrently, the reactivation of the Consultation Framework on Decentralization signals an attempt to strengthen local governance and reduce reliance on central bureaucracy.
2. Resource Nationalism and Economic Resilience
Economic policy is increasingly defined by resource nationalism, particularly regarding uranium. The government has asserted total control over exports, defying international arbitration and warnings from the French company Orano. The movement of uranium convoys under Russian mercenary escort and the initiation of legal action against Orano for environmental damage mark a decisive break from past mining partnerships. Furthermore, the government is prioritizing food security through large-scale irrigation rehabilitation and strictly regulating domestic energy markets to curb informal practices.
3. The Security Paradox
Despite the government's assertive posture, the security situation remains highly volatile and is arguably worsening in specific sectors:
Expansion of Terror: Jihadist groups (JNIM and ISIS) are encroaching on the capital, with attacks occurring as close as 30 kilometers from Niamey. Targeted assassinations of school officials and village leaders in Tillabéri undermine state presence.
Infrastructure Threats: The emergence of the MPLJ and its claimed destruction of the Agadem-Dibella oil site introduces a new threat to the state's economic lifeline.
Lawlessness in the North: In Agadez, the intersection of gold mining, ethnic tension, and criminality has led to kidnappings and retaliatory violence, exposing gaps in state control.
4. Human Rights and Civil Liberties
The consolidation of power has coincided with a constriction of civil liberties. While the state advances social welfare through universal birth registration and gender-based violence campaigns, political space is shrinking. The prolonged detention of former President Bazoum , the imprisonment of civil society figures like Moussa Tchangari , and the sentencing of journalists under national security laws reflect a governance model that increasingly conflates dissent with treason.
In summary, Niger is aggressively carving out a new path defined by sovereignty and self-reliance, but this transition is fraught with peril. The government's ability to maintain public support will likely depend on whether its diplomatic and economic pivots can deliver tangible security improvements before the expanding violence destabilizes the capital or cripples the economy.
