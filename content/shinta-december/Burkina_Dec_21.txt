*Burkina Faso, EU Review Partnership Actions and Priorities
Ouagadougou, December 7, 2025 - Senior members of the Burkinabe government on Friday met with a delegation from the European Union and its Member States to review progress under their multilateral partnership and align future cooperation with Burkina Faso’s national priorities.

Karamoko Jean Marie Traoré, alongside the Minister of Security, Divisional Police Commissioner Mahamadou Sana; the Minister of Economy and Finance, Dr. Aboubacar Nacanabo; and the Minister Delegate for Regional Cooperation, Ms. Stella Eldine Kabre/Kaboré. *AES Condemns Unauthorized Nigerian Military Aircraft Overflight
Ouagadougou, December 8, 2025 - The Confederation of Sahel States (AES) has strongly condemned what it described as a violation of its confederal airspace after a Nigerian Air Force C-130 aircraft was forced to land in Bobo-Dioulasso, western Burkina Faso, on Monday following an in-flight emergency.
In a press release issued the same day, the AES said the military aircraft, which was carrying two crew members and nine passengers, all military personnel, had been operating in Burkinabe airspace without authorization.
According to the statement, an investigation was immediately opened by the competent Burkinabe authorities after the aircraft’s landing. Preliminary findings established that the plane did not have clearance to overfly Burkina Faso, prompting the Confederation to denounce the incident as a breach of sovereignty.
“The Confederation of Sahel States condemns in the strongest possible terms this violation of its airspace and the sovereignty of its member states,” the statement said.
The AES further described the incident as a hostile act carried out in violation of international law and international civil and military aviation regulations. It added that measures are being taken to ensure the security of confederal airspace, safeguard the sovereignty and territorial integrity of member states, and protect civilian populations.
Acting on instructions from the Heads of State, the AES announced that its air and anti-aircraft defense forces have been placed on maximum alert, in line with the Declaration of the College of Heads of State of December 22, 2024.
Under these directives, AES defense forces are now authorized to neutralize any aircraft found violating the Confederation’s airspace, the statement warned.

*Burkina Faso, Morocco Sign 12 Agreements to Deepen Bilateral Cooperation
Ouagadougou, December 10, 2025 - Burkina Faso and the Kingdom of Morocco have signed 12 new cooperation agreements aimed at strengthening long-standing bilateral relations, following the fifth session of their Joint Commission on Bilateral Cooperation held in Ouagadougou on Wednesday.
The ministerial meeting was co-chaired by Burkina Faso’s Minister of Foreign Affairs, H.E. Karamoko Jean Marie Traoré, and Morocco’s Minister of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation and Moroccans Abroad, H.E. Nasser Bourita. It concluded with the adoption of the session’s minutes, a joint communiqué, and the signing of multiple cooperation instruments.
Among the agreements is a deal on the reciprocal recognition of national driver’s licenses, as well as three cooperation agreements covering labor, security, and civil protection. Six memoranda of understanding were also signed in the fields of agriculture, trade, youth, housing and urban planning, and the exchange of expertise in security and road infrastructure.
In addition, the two sides initialed a framework agreement on scholarships, internships, and the sharing of expertise, along with another framework agreement focused on vocational training.
The holding of the joint commission reflects the shared commitment of President Ibrahim Traoré of Burkina Faso and King Mohammed VI of Morocco to consolidating six decades of friendship and fraternity between the two nations.
“This meeting underscores the strength and depth of the ties that unite our two countries, founded on fraternity, solidarity, mutual respect, and fruitful cooperation,” Minister Bourita said, reaffirming Morocco’s solidarity with Burkina Faso and its support for efforts to preserve territorial integrity, sovereignty, and national security.
For his part, Minister Traoré described the meeting as a concrete expression of South-South cooperation centered on shared development and the affirmation of sovereignty. He praised Morocco’s role in promoting peace and stability, particularly in the Sahel, and highlighted Rabat’s support for the Sahelian Confederation.
The Burkinabe foreign minister expressed hope that the Ouagadougou meeting would accelerate the implementation of commitments made by both parties. The fifth session follows expert-level talks held last October in Ouagadougou. The sixth session of the Joint Commission is scheduled for 2027 in Morocco.

Didougou, December 17, 2025 - The Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM) announced two deadly attacks on military posts in Didougou province.
In Nokouy, three Burkinabe militiamen were killed, and military equipment was reportedly seized.
In a separate attack last Wednesday in Lanfila, three additional militiamen lost their lives, with several small arms captured by the group.
These incidents highlight the continuing security threats facing Burkinabe forces in the region.
*JNIM Releases Military Training Video from Camp Abou Adam in Burkina Faso
Burkina Faso, December 20, 2025 - The Jama’at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM/GSIM) group released a 5-minute video from Camp Abou Adam, addressing the nation in Moorish and Fulani.
In the footage, the speaker claims that JNIM includes members from all ethnic groups in the Sahel and emphasizes that the group does not discriminate. The video also declares the group’s current objective is to overthrow President Ibrahim Traoré, whom it blames for the hardships faced by the Burkinabe population.
This release forms part of JNIM’s ongoing media and propaganda campaign amid persistent security challenges in Burkina Faso.
Conclusion: A Period of Strategic Transformation and Resilience
The reporting period from December 07 to 21, 2025, underscores a pivotal moment in Burkina Faso’s journey toward national sovereignty and regional integration. Under the leadership of President Ibrahim Traoré, the government has intensified its "radical transformation" of diplomatic relations, shifting toward partnerships based on mutual respect and shared benefits
Key developments during this period include:
Regional Integration and the AES: The inaugural confederal session of the Council of Ministers in Bamako and the launch of the Confederal Bank for Investment and Development (BCID-AES) represent major milestones in consolidating the Confederation of Sahel States as a platform for "emancipation, freedom, and true independence".
Diplomatic Diversification: Burkina Faso has significantly broadened its international engagement, presenting credentials in countries as varied as the Philippines, Greece, Cuba, and Gabon. The signing of 12 new agreements with Morocco further illustrates a commitment to South-South cooperation centered on shared development.
Sovereignty and Security: The government took a firm stance on territorial integrity, notably in its response to the Nigerian military aircraft incident, while also engaging in high-level diplomacy to resolve the matter through an official apology from Nigeria. Despite persistent security challenges from groups like JNIM, the state continues to prioritize national territorial restoration.
Governance and Internal Reforms: Significant strides were made in public administration, including the modernization of civil service roles and the review of the National Development Plan for 2026-2030. Initiatives like the "Faso Mêbo" presidential project have successfully mobilized support from both the diaspora and international organizations.
Transparency and Human Rights: The presentation of the 2024 EITI Report and various human rights training sessions highlight an ongoing effort to strengthen accountability and the protection of fundamental rights within the national framework.
In summary, this fortnight reflects a nation actively redefining its role on the global stage while simultaneously reinforcing its internal governance and regional alliances to build a more "peaceful, resilient, and prosperous" future
