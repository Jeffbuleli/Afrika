*Sudan Power Sector Collapses as War Inflicts $3 Billion Damage, Solar Adoption Surges
Khartoum, May 18, 2026 - Sudan’s electricity sector has suffered damages estimated at $3 billion since the outbreak of the ongoing conflict, triggering a nationwide shift toward solar energy as households, businesses, and telecommunications operators struggle to cope with a collapsing national grid and soaring diesel costs, according to a United Nations report released Monday.
The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in Sudan said widespread destruction of power generation facilities and transmission infrastructure has forced a rapid, largely unplanned transition to solar energy, despite persistent cost pressures and disrupted supply chains. The country, which benefits from some of the world’s highest solar irradiance levels, has an estimated technical potential of 15 gigawatts (GW) of solar power and 1.5 GW of wind energy. Yet before the war, grid electricity reached only 45% to 60% of the population, leaving rural and remote communities heavily dependent on off-grid solutions.
The report estimated Sudan’s installed solar capacity at about 190 megawatts (MW) by 2025-far below pre-war government ambitions to generate 3,300 MW of renewable energy by 2033, including 2,190 MW from solar sources.
The conflict also derailed state-led programs such as plans to distribute 2.5 million solar home systems to off-grid communities. In their place, private imports of solar equipment surged between 2024 and 2025 as grid failures intensified and fuel prices became increasingly prohibitive.
However, the UNDP warned that the sector remains highly fragile. Because all components-including panels, batteries, and inverters-are imported, the market is heavily exposed to currency depreciation, foreign exchange shortages, and high domestic logistics costs. The report detailed that importation costs through Port Sudan were significantly inflated by internal transport fees, port storage charges, demurrage, and documentation expenses, all of which collectively eroded affordability for end users.
In retail markets across cities such as Atbara, Port Sudan, Dongola, and Khartoum, solar panel prices reached between 218,000 and 255,000 Sudanese pounds for a 590-watt unit in late 2025, while 10-kilowatt lithium batteries were priced above 5 million Sudanese pounds.
Despite rising adoption, regional disparities remain pronounced. Khartoum leads with solar penetration above 7%, followed by Kordofan at around 6% and Darfur at 5%, while eastern and central regions lag at roughly 3%.
The economic consequences of the energy crisis have been severe, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises and agriculture. In Gedaref, farmers reported significant yield declines linked to unreliable power supply. In Blue Nile, diesel scarcity pushed fuel prices to extreme levels, while irrigation systems in White Nile were left idle for extended periods due to fuel shortages.
The telecommunications sector, serving nearly 24 million subscribers nationwide, has adapted more effectively by adopting hybrid energy systems combining grid electricity, diesel generators, solar power, and battery storage.
The UNDP called for urgent interventions to stabilize solar supply chains, expand access to microfinance, strengthen maintenance networks, and train local technicians. It also highlighted ongoing initiatives, including the installation of hundreds of solar-powered water systems and health facility electrification projects, which have reduced operational costs, cut emissions, and improved access to essential services in conflict-affected regions.

The project, initiated in 2020 and completed in 2023 by the China Harbour Engineering Company, forms part of broader efforts by South Sudan to strengthen national infrastructure and reduce dependence on external systems.
Authorities also noted early steps toward developing a domestic aviation industry, including the launch of South Supreme Airlines, aimed at improving internal and regional connectivity.

Most of Sudan’s gold mining activities are concentrated in the River Nile, Northern, and Red Sea states. Gold remains Sudan’s leading export commodity, representing more than 58 percent of the country’s total exports.
Despite the sector’s strategic importance, Sudan continues to face major challenges related to gold smuggling. Technical estimates indicate that between 48 and 60 percent of domestically produced gold leaves the country through unofficial border crossings, unmonitored ports, and illegal export channels, resulting in substantial losses of public revenue.

*MSF Flags 80 Suspected Mpox Cases in Darfur’s Jebel Marra Amid Humanitarian Strain
Khartoum, May 18, 2026 - Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) has reported 80 suspected cases of mpox in the East Jebel Marra locality of Sudan’s South Darfur State, raising concerns over a potential outbreak in one of the country’s most vulnerable displacement-affected regions.
According to MSF, its medical teams have been managing suspected infections since May 2 in the Java and Souni areas, where densely populated displacement camps and frequent population movements have created conditions conducive to rapid disease transmission. The organization is currently working with the World Health Organization (WHO) to collect and analyze biological samples, with laboratory confirmation still pending.
While MSF emphasized that no outbreak has been officially confirmed, it said it is closely monitoring suspected cases across primary healthcare facilities in the area, where medical resources remain extremely limited.
Jebel Marra and neighboring Tawila host an estimated 700,000 displaced people, many living in overcrowded camps with little access to clean water, sanitation, or preventive healthcare. Humanitarian workers warn that these conditions significantly increase the risk of infectious disease spread.

The Fourth Infantry Division, which oversees operations in Blue Nile, announced that the 13th Infantry Brigade carried out coordinated clearing operations in Abdaqla, Adi, Washimbu, Um Shanqar, and Kinshinkaru in Qaysan locality. Military officials said the areas were fully secured following what they described as sweeping offensives against RSF positions.
According to the army, its forces consolidated control over the recaptured zones before continuing pursuit operations toward the international boundary with Ethiopia, where remaining armed elements were reported to have withdrawn.
The advance follows the recapture of the Barka area earlier in the week, part of a broader push to reverse territorial gains made by the RSF and its ally, the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N) led by Abdelaziz al-Hilu, which had seized several localities in March including Karamak.
Since the beginning of the year, the RSF-SPLM-N alliance has conducted repeated attacks across Blue Nile, targeting towns such as Karamak, Qaysan, and Bau, with significant damage to infrastructure and disruption of agricultural and trade activity.
Sudanese authorities have accused Ethiopia of providing logistical support to the RSF-SPLM-N alliance operating near the border-allegations that Ethiopia has denied.
The Fourth Division said additional troop deployments have been established across the recaptured areas to prevent renewed infiltration or regrouping by armed elements, describing the operation as part of a wider effort to stabilize border zones.
The ongoing conflict in Blue Nile has displaced an estimated 50,000 people in recent months, with humanitarian conditions worsening as communities face insecurity, limited access to services, and fears of further escalation ahead of the rainy season.
*Sudanese Army Advances Toward Kurmuk After Seizing Key Rebel Positions in Blue Nile
Kurmuk, May 28, 2026 - The Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) announced Wednesday that they are nearing the strategic town of Kurmuk in the Blue Nile region after capturing what the military described as the last remaining rebel strongholds surrounding the area.
In a statement, the army’s Fourth Infantry Division said government forces and allied units had “demolished the final rebel positions” around Kurmuk and were preparing to move toward the city itself. The military also reported repelling a Rapid Support Forces (RSF) assault on the Al-Barka station south of Kurmuk, claiming to have inflicted heavy casualties while capturing combat vehicles and military equipment.
According to the army, operations are advancing from two directions - north and south - in an apparent effort to encircle rebel forces stationed inside Kurmuk.
The latest advance follows a series of recent territorial gains by the army in Blue Nile. Earlier this week, the SAF announced it had retaken Al-Barka on the southern outskirts of Kurmuk, before later claiming control over several areas in Qaisan district, including Adi and Abu Daqla. Military officials said pursuing troops pushed armed groups toward the Ethiopian border.
Kurmuk, located near the frontier with Ethiopia, was captured in March by a joint alliance of the RSF and the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N) led by Abdelaziz al-Hilu, following the escalation of fighting in southern Blue Nile earlier this year.
Sudanese authorities have repeatedly accused Ethiopia of providing logistical support to the RSF-SPLM-N alliance operating in the region, though Addis Ababa has not publicly responded to the allegations.
In a separate statement issued Wednesday, the SPLM-N disputed the army’s account, accusing government forces of attacking civilian areas in Adi and Abu Daqla using more than 20 combat vehicles. The movement alleged that civilians were killed and injured, homes burned, and gold mining equipment looted during the assault.
The SPLM-N also claimed the violence displaced residents from both areas and said more than 100 children remain missing after families fled into surrounding forests and valleys.
*Dozens Killed in RSF Assault on Villages in North Kordofan
Bara, May 29 - At least 30 civilians were killed on Thursday following a deadly assault by Rapid Support Forces (RSF) fighters on several villages near the town of Bara in Sudan’s North Kordofan state, amid escalating violence in the region.
The attack targeted the villages of Al-Murra, Um Saadoun al-Sharif, and Al-Radha in the West Bara locality. Armed units traveling in approximately 20 combat vehicles reportedly stormed the area, causing heavy civilian casualties and triggering panic among residents.
Local tribal fighters launched a counter-response shortly after the assault in an attempt to repel the attackers, while tensions remained high across the affected communities amid fears of further escalation.
Community leaders called for urgent mobilization to protect vulnerable villages and appealed for military and security support to reinforce local defenses against continued attacks.
Bara, the second-largest city in North Kordofan, has witnessed repeated clashes since the outbreak of the conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the RSF. Control of the city has shifted multiple times during the war and it currently remains under RSF control.
The RSF has intensified operations across several towns and localities in North Kordofan in recent months, while the Sudanese army and allied forces continue to maintain control over El Obeid, the state capital and one of the region’s main strategic strongholds.
*Sudanese Army Repels Assault on Amora Station in Blue Nile Region
Ad-Damazin, May 29 - The Sudanese Armed Forces announced on Thursday that they had repelled a new attack launched by an alliance of the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and the SPLM-N on the Amora station in Qaisan district, Blue Nile region, preventing an attempt to cut off strategic supply routes to the border town of Qaisan.
According to military officials, the assault targeted the Amora station northeast of Qaisan and was carried out by RSF fighters alongside SPLM-N forces commanded by Joseph Tuka. Sudanese army units from the 13th Infantry Brigade stationed in Kuri reportedly engaged the attackers and succeeded in forcing them to retreat after intense fighting.
The army stated that several combat vehicles belonging to the attacking forces were destroyed, while others were captured intact during the confrontation.
Military sources indicated that the operation aimed to sever logistical and military supply lines linking the 13th Infantry Brigade in Kuri to the town of Qaisan near the Ethiopian border. The strategy, according to the sources, was intended to weaken army positions and pave the way for rebel forces to advance toward and seize Qaisan.
Sudanese authorities have repeatedly accused Ethiopia of providing logistical backing to armed groups operating in the Blue Nile region, which borders both Ethiopia and South Sudan.
The latest clashes come as the Sudanese army intensifies operations across Qaisan district. In recent days, government forces reportedly repelled another joint RSF-SPLM-N attack on Al-Barka station south of Kurmuk and regained control of several areas north of the strategic town.
The conflict in Blue Nile escalated earlier this year after RSF and SPLM-N forces captured Kurmuk in March, following the opening of a new southern front in February. Since then, fighting has continued across the Kurmuk and Qaisan districts, both of which hold strategic importance due to their proximity to international borders.
Kurmuk and Qaisan have historically been major flashpoints in Sudan’s armed conflicts. Prior to the 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement, the two towns witnessed more than two decades of warfare and repeatedly changed hands between government forces and the Sudan People’s Liberation Army during Sudan’s long civil war.
*Sudanese Army Downs RSF Drone Carrying Missiles Over White Nile State
Kenana, May 30 - The Sudanese Armed Forces announced on Saturday that air defence units had shot down a long-range Rapid Support Forces (RSF) drone over White Nile State following a wave of aerial attacks targeting towns and strategic facilities in the region.
According to military authorities, the drone was intercepted over the Al-Hudaib area near the city of Kenana after air defence systems tracked its movements and successfully neutralized it before it could reach its target.
Officials stated that the aircraft was carrying approximately eight missiles, raising concerns over the growing use of long-range drones in the conflict.
White Nile Governor Qamar al-Din Mohamed Fadlallah praised the armed forces for the operation, describing it as evidence of the military’s high level of preparedness and the effectiveness of Sudan’s air defence capabilities in responding to aerial threats.
The interception came after a series of RSF drone strikes targeted several towns across White Nile State, including Rabak, Kosti, and Kenana.
The attacks caused significant damage to critical infrastructure and public facilities. Among the sites hit were a hospital in Al-Jabalain city, Kenana’s sugar factory and airport, as well as fuel and medicine warehouses in Kosti and Rabak.
Authorities reported major material losses and disruptions to essential services following the strikes, amid growing fears over the increasing impact of drone warfare on civilian infrastructure and public services in Sudan’s ongoing conflict.
*Drone Strike on Civilian Vehicles in West Kordofan Kills 10, Including Eight Children
Khartoum, May 31 - Ten civilians, including eight children, were killed and several others injured on Saturday when a drone struck two civilian vehicles along the Abu Zabad-Al-Fula road in Sudan’s West Kordofan State, according to political and local sources.
The attack reportedly hit two passenger vehicles traveling on the highway, leaving scenes of severe destruction and multiple casualties. Video footage circulated by activists showed a burned-out vehicle with charred remains inside, highlighting the scale of devastation at the site.
The National Umma Party attributed the strike to a military drone and condemned what it described as a deliberate attack on civilians. In its statement, the party said the incident resulted in the deaths of ten people, including eight children, with several others suffering injuries of varying severity.
The party further warned that repeated drone strikes on civilian areas and transportation routes represent serious violations of international humanitarian law, calling for immediate measures to protect civilians and prevent further attacks.
West Kordofan has witnessed a growing number of drone strikes in recent months, with civilian infrastructure, markets, and supply routes increasingly affected by the escalation of aerial warfare.
The latest incident follows a series of deadly attacks across the region, including a major strike on Ghabesh market on 19 May that reportedly killed dozens of civilians.
Human rights monitors have raised concerns over the rising use of drones in the conflict, noting that both sides have intensified aerial operations across Kordofan and Darfur.
Reports indicate dozens of drone attacks on markets, healthcare facilities, fuel depots, and transport corridors since the start of the year, contributing to a sharp rise in civilian casualties. Observers have also warned that drone strikes now account for a significant proportion of conflict-related deaths in Sudan, underscoring the increasing impact of aerial warfare on civilian populations.
*Tribal Clashes in South Darfur Leave Dead and Displace Thousands Amid Renewed Violence
Kubum, May 31 - Fighting between the Salamat and Beni Halba tribes has erupted again in Sudan’s South Darfur State, triggering deadly clashes, widespread displacement, and the burning of multiple villages, eyewitnesses reported on Sunday.
The violence reignited on Saturday in and around the town of Kubum before spreading to nearby areas including Wastani, Mirkindi, and parts of Central Darfur where both communities are present. Armed fighters from both sides reportedly deployed Rapid Support Forces (RSF) vehicles during the confrontations, intensifying the scale and mobility of the clashes.
Eyewitness accounts indicate that several fighters were killed, though the exact number remains unconfirmed. In addition to the loss of life, entire villages were set ablaze, forcing thousands of civilians to flee toward Ad al-Fursan and surrounding safer areas.
Disturbing footage circulating on social media shows burning structures in Kubum, with other videos reportedly depicting bodies and scenes of brutality, including allegations of mutilation and abuse of captives.
Local sources said that some bodies remained unburied due to fears of renewed attacks and ambushes, further deepening the humanitarian crisis in the area.
The Salamat and Beni Halba communities had previously reached a reconciliation agreement in December 2025, brokered in an effort to end earlier rounds of intercommunal fighting. However, the latest violence is believed to have been triggered by the killing of a herder last week, which reignited longstanding tensions between the two groups.
Observers note that the RSF’s reliance on tribal recruitment networks continues to play a role in local conflicts, with fighters often reverting to tribal loyalties during intercommunal disputes. The reported use of RSF-linked vehicles in the fighting underscores the blurred lines between formal paramilitary structures and tribal militias operating across Darfur’s conflict zones.
CONCLUSION
The second half of May 2026 underscores a complex and highly fragmented landscape in Sudan, characterized by aggressive military maneuvers on the ground, a devastating humanitarian toll, and parallel, yet fragmented, diplomatic tracks.
1. Geopolitics and Fragmented Diplomacy
The period saw a distinct contrast between the state's official diplomatic engagements and grassroots anti-war efforts:
Bilateral Alignments: The Sudanese government aggressively pursued long-term international and regional alliances, highlighted by Prime Minister Kamil Idris’s high-level reconstruction and economic talks in Turkey and the institutionalization of the upcoming Strategic Cooperation Council with Saudi Arabia.
Civilian Mobilization: Simultaneously, external peace initiatives led by civilian and opposition leaders advanced independently. The convergence of a broad anti-war civilian coalition in Nairobi aimed to establish a unified democratic front, standing in stark contrast to Sovereignty Council Chairman General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan’s rejection of externally sponsored talks and his push for an internal, state-controlled dialogue.
2. Escalating Drone Warfare and Security Fractures
The security theater is increasingly defined by technological escalation and shifting internal dynamics:
The Toll of Aerial Warfare: The proliferation of long-range drone strikes by both the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has severely impacted civilian infrastructure, resulting in tragic mass casualties at markets, transport corridors, and healthcare hubs-such as the devastating strikes in Ghabesh, North Darfur, and West Kordofan.
Territorial Shifts: While the SAF registered notable territorial gains-breaking the months-long siege of Dilling and reclaiming key border sectors in the Blue Nile region-the operational landscape remains volatile.
Internal RSF Cracks: The RSF faces deepening structural vulnerabilities, characterized by high-profile command defections and the resurgence of deadly tribal fractures, such as the renewed clashes between the Salamat and Beni Halba communities in South Darfur.
3. Economic Resilience vs. Systemic Collapse
Sudan’s economic indicators reflect a dual reality of localized resilience and severe national infrastructure decay:
Critical Logistics & Trade: The reopening of key transit routes instantly stabilized hyper-inflated food and medicine prices in previously blockaded cities like Kadugli and Dilling. Furthermore, a surge in official gold exports in April and May provided vital revenue streams, though the sector remains crippled by a massive global market drop, localized liquidity shortages, and rampant smuggling.
Energy Transition: The near-total collapse of the national power grid, causing over $3 billion in damages, has triggered widespread public protests over blackouts. Paradoxically, this has accelerated a rapid, private-sector-led transition to solar energy adoption across households and telecommunications providers.
4. Deepening Humanitarian and Institutional Crises
The societal fabric within Sudan continues to deteriorate under the weight of financial neglect and health hazards:
Institutional Neglect: Public services are teetering on absolute collapse, marked by spreading nationwide protests from educators suffering from severe wage arrears, and the forced closure of over 50 vital nutrition centers in West Darfur due to severe international funding shortages.
Public Health Resilience: Despite these immense hurdles, the Ministry of Health achieved a remarkable public health milestone by cutting the number of unvaccinated, "zero-dose" children in half through aggressive mobile campaigns. However, this progress remains highly precarious as local health networks simultaneously battle suspected mpox outbreaks in Darfur and scale up emergency cross-border screenings to prevent the regional importation of Ebola.
Summary Outlook
Ultimately, the events of late May 2026 reveal a nation locked in a war of attrition. While the formal government focuses on post-war digital and economic reconstruction blueprints with foreign allies, the daily reality on the ground remains dictated by volatile frontline shifts, cross-border tensions, severe institutional underfunding, and an unyielding humanitarian crisis that continues to demand urgent global intervention.
