*Government Promises to Accelerate Labour and Social Protection Reforms
Djibouti, April 30, 2026 - The Djiboutian government has reaffirmed its commitment to accelerating labour market reforms and strengthening social protection systems as the country marked International Workers’ Day.
In a statement, the Minister of Labour, Formalisation and Social Protection, Omar Abdi Said, welcomed the re-election of President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh, describing it as a strong endorsement of a governance agenda centred on stability, sovereignty, and social justice.
He said the new mandate presents an opportunity to translate policy commitments into tangible improvements for workers across the country.
Positioning Djibouti as an open economy and strategic trade hub linking Africa, Asia, and Europe, the minister noted that rapid labour market changes are placing increasing pressure on existing social policies, requiring urgent reform.
A key milestone in the reform agenda is the ongoing revision of the Labour Code, which is expected to modernise employment regulations, accommodate emerging forms of work, and strengthen protections for employees while improving clarity for employers.
The government is also reinforcing the Labour Inspectorate, expanding its field presence and introducing stronger mechanisms for conflict prevention and resolution. Officials emphasised that compliance with occupational safety, health, and working conditions standards remains a non-negotiable priority.
In parallel, a national occupational health and safety policy is in its final stages of validation, aimed at reducing workplace accidents and occupational diseases.
Authorities are also advancing efforts to formalise the informal economy, a structural priority intended to broaden access to social protection and improve economic regulation.
Civil service reform remains another central pillar, with a new statute designed to enhance career management, strengthen transparency, and improve administrative efficiency.
To address persistent youth unemployment, the government is implementing a national employment strategy targeting 2035, with a focus on vocational training and skills alignment in sectors such as construction, logistics, digital technologies, and renewable energy.
The minister also highlighted entrepreneurship as a key driver of job creation, stating that small business development plays a critical role in expanding employment opportunities, particularly for young people.
On social protection, the government pointed to recent advances including the monthly payment of pensions, direct bank integration of retirement benefits, and the digitalisation of family allowances to improve access and transparency.
A major reform underway is the development of Djibouti’s first Social Security Code, intended to harmonise existing systems and gradually extend coverage to informal workers and the self-employed.
The minister called on the private sector to increase its contribution to job creation, particularly for youth and women, stressing that employment generation is a shared national responsibility.
International Workers’ Day remains a key moment in Djibouti for dialogue on labour rights, employment policy, and social justice between government, employers, and workers’ representatives.
*Djibouti Selected to Host Next CONFEMEN Session in 2028
Djibouti, May 2, 2026 - The Republic of Djibouti has been selected to host the next session of the Conference of Ministers of Education of the States and Governments of La Francophonie (CONFEMEN), scheduled for 2028.
The decision was made during the 61st CONFEMEN session held from April 27 to May 1 in Yaoundé, Cameroon, where member states chose Djibouti following a competitive selection process involving Romania and Gabon.
Officials said the selection reflects renewed confidence in Djibouti’s role within the Francophone education space and recognition of its ongoing commitment to strengthening education systems and promoting Francophone cooperation.
A Djiboutian delegation led by Ayan Osman Abrar, technical advisor to the Minister of Education and national correspondent for CONFEMEN, took part in the deliberations of the session, which brought together education authorities from across member states.
Discussions among participating countries focused on strengthening cooperation in education policy, improving quality standards, and reinforcing solidarity within the Francophone community.
Founded in 1960, CONFEMEN brings together education ministers from 41 Francophone states and governments, meeting every two years to coordinate policies and advance shared educational priorities.
*Adjusted Working Hours Introduced in Public Administration for Summer Period
Djibouti, May 3, 2026 - President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh has approved an adjustment of working hours across the public administration, effective May 5, as part of measures aimed at improving work conditions during the hot summer season.
According to a presidential circular, government administrative services will now operate from 8:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., Sunday through Thursday.
Authorities indicated that the measure is intended to address high temperatures typically experienced during this period, while maintaining administrative efficiency and ensuring continuity of public service delivery.
The directive specifies that the new schedule does not apply to essential sectors such as schools, universities, and hospitals, which will continue to operate under their existing timetables.
Government members have been instructed to ensure strict implementation of the decision, while allowing flexibility where necessary to accommodate essential duties and service requirements.
Officials said the seasonal adjustment is also expected to contribute to improved energy management, alongside efforts to adapt working conditions to climatic realities.
*Guelleh Sworn In for New Term Amid Intense Diplomatic Activity
DJIBOUTI, May 9, 2026 - President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh was officially sworn in for a new five-year term on Saturday following his re-election in the April 10 presidential election, where he secured more than 97% of the vote. The inauguration ceremony gathered several African leaders, foreign delegations, and representatives of partner nations in Djibouti City.
In his inaugural address, President Guelleh pledged to continue economic and social reforms aimed at modernizing the country while preserving national stability. He stressed the importance of education, healthcare, youth employment, women’s empowerment, and technological innovation as central pillars of Djibouti’s future development.
“Education is the key to our future,” the president said, while also emphasizing the need to adapt the country’s education system to labor market demands and strengthen opportunities for young people.
The Djiboutian leader further announced plans to expand investments in digital technology and artificial intelligence, while calling for balanced regulation to preserve social cohesion and national values.
On regional and international affairs, President Guelleh reaffirmed Djibouti’s commitment to peace, cooperation, and African integration, stating that climate, health, and economic challenges require collective responses from African nations.
Following the inauguration ceremony, President Guelleh held a series of bilateral meetings with regional leaders and foreign representatives. He participated in a trilateral discussion alongside Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed and Sudanese Sovereign Council Vice-President Malik Agar, where talks focused on peace, security, and economic integration in the Horn of Africa.
Separate meetings were also held with South Sudanese President Salva Kiir and Yemeni Presidential Council President Rashad al-Alimi to discuss bilateral cooperation and regional stability.
Additional diplomatic talks brought together President Guelleh with Egyptian Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly, Algerian Senate Speaker Nasri Azzouz, French Minister Catherine Vautrin, China’s Special Envoy Luosung Jiangeu, and United Nations Special Envoy for the Horn of Africa Guang Cong.
The United States delegation was led by Stanley L. Brown of the Bureau of Political-Military Affairs, accompanied by Ambassador Cynthia Kierscht.
In the days leading up to the inauguration, Djibouti witnessed the arrival of numerous heads of state, special envoys, and senior officials from Africa, the Middle East, Asia, and international organizations. Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud arrived on Friday with a high-level delegation, underscoring the close ties between Djibouti and Somalia in the fields of security, politics, and economic cooperation.
South Sudanese President Salva Kiir Mayardit, African Union Commission Chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf, and representatives from Côte d’Ivoire, Uganda, Japan, and the Arab League were also among the officials attending the ceremonies.
Security and logistical preparations intensified across the capital ahead of the event, with authorities coordinating official ceremonies, diplomatic meetings, and protocol arrangements as Djibouti positioned itself at the center of regional diplomatic engagement.

Djibouti, May 10, 2026 - Speaker of the National Assembly Dileita Mohamed Dileita has received Japan’s Parliamentary Vice Minister for Foreign Affairs, Onishi Yohei, during the latter’s visit to Djibouti for the inauguration ceremony of President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh.

Japan maintains its only permanent overseas military base in Djibouti, supporting international maritime security operations and anti-piracy missions in the region.

*Djibouti Advances Digital Transformation Agenda Through Stakeholder Engagement Workshop
Djibouti, April 23, 2026 - The People’s Palace in Djibouti hosted a stakeholder information and mobilisation workshop focused on two major digital development initiatives: the Djibouti Foundations of Digital Technology (DFN) project and the East Africa Regional Digital Integration Project (EARDIP).
The workshop brought together technical partners and institutional representatives to review the scope, objectives, and implementation challenges of both projects, while also providing a platform for stakeholder input aimed at strengthening inclusive and sustainable execution.
Officials said the session was also designed to explain the functioning of the complaints management mechanism, which will be used to collect feedback, address concerns, and improve project delivery in line with international standards.
Opening the workshop, Project Manager and Director of ICT Development Faycal Khaireh Chirdon highlighted the importance of digital transformation in building a more inclusive and connected society.
He stressed that the government’s approach goes beyond infrastructure development, focusing instead on creating an ecosystem that enables citizens to acquire essential digital skills and participate meaningfully in the digital economy.
Chirdon reaffirmed the government’s commitment to reducing the digital divide in Djibouti, noting that both DFN and EARDIP are central to this objective.
The two initiatives are supported through funding from the World Bank’s International Development Association (IDA), with $10 million allocated to the DFN project and $20 million directed toward EARDIP.
According to officials, the investments are intended to strengthen ICT sector competitiveness, attract private sector participation, and enhance regional digital integration across East Africa.
The project manager also underscored the importance of stakeholder participation, describing local participants as key actors in ensuring that project implementation reflects ground realities.
He encouraged active engagement from attendees, noting that feedback and contributions would be integrated into ongoing project improvements.
In line with the World Bank’s Environmental and Social Standards, particularly ESS10, a formal grievance redress mechanism has been established to ensure transparency and accountability throughout implementation.
Authorities said the mechanism will help capture concerns from communities and stakeholders, enabling timely resolution of issues related to project activities.
The workshop reflects Djibouti’s broader strategy of leveraging digital infrastructure investments to drive economic modernisation, improve service delivery, and strengthen regional integration in the ICT sector.
*Economic Task Force Engages Private Sector Amid Middle East-Driven Trade Pressures
Djibouti, April 12, 2026 - The National Economic Task Force has held discussions with private sector representatives to assess emerging economic pressures linked to ongoing tensions in the Middle East, which continue to disrupt global trade routes and supply chains.
The meeting, chaired by Minister of Economy and Finance Ilyas Moussa Dawaleh, brought together senior government officials and leading business figures, including the President of the Chamber of Commerce, Youssouf Moussa Dawaleh.
The session focused on gathering direct feedback from economic operators on the impact of regional instability on Djibouti’s economy, particularly in relation to logistics disruptions, shipping delays, and rising transport and supply chain costs.
Officials noted that the Middle East remains a critical corridor for global energy flows and essential goods, making the region’s instability a key risk factor for import-dependent economies.
Despite these pressures, the Minister reassured stakeholders that national stocks of essential commodities remain sufficient for several months, with hydrocarbon reserves secured to meet demand through at least June.
He commended wholesale distributors for maintaining relative price stability under difficult conditions, while issuing a strong warning against retail-level price manipulation.
The minister condemned what he described as opportunistic price increases by some retailers, stating that such practices were unacceptable and would be met with strict regulatory enforcement if necessary.
The meeting forms part of the broader work of the National Task Force, a platform designed to maintain continuous dialogue between government and economic actors in order to anticipate external shocks and strengthen national economic resilience.
Authorities said the mechanism is intended to improve coordination, ensure market stability, and support timely policy responses in periods of global uncertainty.
*IMF-World Bank Spring Meetings: Djibouti Warns of “Asymmetric” Global Shock Impact on Africa
Washington, April 20, 2026 - Djibouti has warned of far-reaching and “exceptional” consequences of the current global crisis on African economies, during the IMF-World Bank Spring Meetings held from April 13 to 18 in Washington.
Representing Djibouti, Central Bank Governor Ahmed Osman Ali described the global environment as “unprecedented,” citing the ongoing Middle East tensions and disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz, a critical maritime corridor through which an estimated 20% of global oil flows.
He noted that these disruptions have triggered sharp increases in energy prices, higher maritime transport costs, and widespread supply chain instability, with direct implications for international trade and food systems.
Despite these external pressures, Djibouti reported continued macroeconomic resilience, with growth estimated at 6.5% in 2025 and projected at around 6% in 2026, supported by strong port activity and infrastructure-driven economic performance. Authorities also indicated that inflation remains contained and the banking sector stable.
However, officials cautioned that this resilience remains fragile due to structural vulnerabilities, particularly the country’s heavy reliance on imported food and energy. Rising freight and energy costs are increasingly affecting both businesses and household purchasing power.
Djibouti has responded with a series of emergency measures, including price controls, strategic stock management, prioritisation of essential imports, and targeted support interventions, particularly in the energy sector. A national task force has also been activated to coordinate responses between public and private actors.
During discussions with the IMF Managing Director, the Central Bank Governor described the situation as a “tipping point,” stressing that the intensity of current global shocks is placing significant strain on fiscal stability, particularly through rising fuel import costs and price stabilisation policies that have increased pressure on public finances.
He also highlighted the social dimension of the crisis, noting that food inflation remains particularly severe in a country where households spend a large share of their income on basic consumption needs.
On a broader scale, he warned that Africa is disproportionately affected by global economic disruptions, describing the situation as a “profound asymmetry” in which the continent bears the impact of crises it has contributed little to creating.
In response, Djibouti called for urgent and coordinated international financial support to help stabilise vulnerable economies, strengthen resilience, and safeguard long-term development prospects across the continent.
*Galamo-Yoboki Section of National Route 1 Nears Completion
Dikhil, April 29, 2026 - The Djiboutian government has announced the imminent completion of a key road segment on National Route 1 (RN1), linking Galamo to Yoboki in the Dikhil region.

Local authorities also took part in the visit, including the Prefect of Dikhil and the President of the Regional Council, alongside senior officials from the Djibouti Roads Agency (ADR) and the Directorate of Roads and Infrastructure (DPCR).
Officials confirmed that construction works on the section are now largely complete, with only final technical and safety verifications remaining before the road is opened to traffic.
According to the Director General of the ADR, the project has entered its commissioning phase, with final checks underway to ensure operational readiness.
The Galamo-Yoboki segment forms part of broader national efforts to upgrade transport infrastructure and improve regional connectivity, particularly along corridors critical for the movement of goods and services.
Authorities say the new road is expected to significantly improve mobility within the Dikhil region, strengthen links to border areas, and support local economic activity by reducing transport times and costs.

*Around 100 Social Housing Units Ready for Relocation of Djaga-Bouldhouq Families
Djibouti, May 5, 2026 - Approximately 100 newly completed social housing units are ready to receive the first families from Djaga-Bouldhouq (District 7), according to the Urban Rehabilitation and Social Housing Agency (ARULoS).
The Director General of ARULoS, Abdourahman Ali Ahmed, confirmed the readiness of the site during an inspection visit to the Nassib area, located south of the capital, where the relocation project is being implemented.
The housing development includes a range of unit types, from one-bedroom (F1) to five-bedroom (F5) homes, with living spaces varying between 96 and 192 square metres. Authorities describe the project as a key component of broader efforts to restructure informal settlements and improve urban living conditions.
ARULoS rejected claims circulating on social media suggesting that the relocation site is situated near a landfill in Shebelley, stating instead that the area is part of a rapidly expanding urban zone with nearby public infrastructure, including a military compound, the Nitobé primary school, and a hospital currently under construction.
The agency also indicated that the Nassib development is expected to host several thousand additional social housing units in the coming years, as part of a national urban development and housing access strategy.
Officials added that affected families have reportedly agreed to the relocation process voluntarily, having signed memoranda of understanding with the authorities. The distribution of keys to the first beneficiary households is expected to begin shortly.

Conclusion
The period from April 12 to May 10, 2026, marks a pivotal phase in Djibouti’s national trajectory, defined by the formal beginning of President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh’s new five-year mandate. Re-elected with more than 97% of the vote, the administration enters this term with a renewed emphasis on modernization, stability, regional leadership, and social justice.
Several major themes emerged during this period that are likely to shape Djibouti’s political and economic direction in the years ahead.
Socio-Economic Reform and Modernization
The government continues to pursue an ambitious dual-track strategy focused on digital transformation and structural economic reform. Major infrastructure and financing initiatives-including the near-completion of the Galamo-Yoboki section of National Route 1 and the $35 million trade finance agreement secured through the International Islamic Trade Finance Corporation (ITFC) for Red Sea Bunkering-reinforce Djibouti’s ambition to consolidate its position as a leading logistics, maritime, and commercial hub linking Africa, the Middle East, and global markets.
At the same time, authorities are accelerating labor market reforms, expanding social protection mechanisms, and modernizing public administration. Investments in digital integration projects, supported by the World Bank, signal a long-term commitment to reducing the digital divide, improving governance, and strengthening economic competitiveness.
Diplomatic Influence and Regional Positioning
Djibouti further strengthened its diplomatic visibility and regional influence throughout the period. President Guelleh’s inauguration became a major platform for bilateral and multilateral engagement, bringing together heads of state, regional leaders, and international partners to discuss security, economic integration, and cooperation in the Horn of Africa.
The country also reinforced its standing within the Francophone and African diplomatic space, notably through its selection to host the 2028 session of the Conference of Ministers of Education of La Francophonie (CONFEMEN). Simultaneously, Djibouti maintained active engagement with strategic partners such as France, Japan, China, and neighboring Horn of Africa states, while continuing to position itself as a center for dialogue, maritime security, and regional stability.
Security, Governance, and Economic Resilience
Authorities demonstrated a strong commitment to public security and institutional control through intensified operations against organized crime, trafficking networks, and illegal migration. Large-scale security interventions in District 7 and anti-drug operations in Balho highlighted the government’s determination to preserve internal stability and reinforce law enforcement capacity.
At the economic level, Djibouti faced growing pressure from external geopolitical shocks, particularly the impact of Middle East tensions and disruptions affecting maritime trade routes and energy markets. Government officials repeatedly warned of the “asymmetric” effects of these global crises on African economies, emphasizing rising logistics costs, inflationary risks, and pressure on household purchasing power.
In response, authorities implemented measures aimed at stabilizing essential commodity supplies, maintaining fuel reserves, and controlling price speculation in order to protect consumers and preserve macroeconomic stability.
Social Development and Human Capital
Social development remained a central component of government policy during the period. Programs such as the Nassib social housing project, vocational training initiatives led by the Djiboutian Agency for Social Development (ADDS), and expanded employment and entrepreneurship strategies reflect broader efforts to address unemployment, improve living conditions, and strengthen social inclusion.
The government also emphasized education, youth empowerment, and workforce development as essential pillars of long-term national transformation, particularly in sectors such as logistics, digital technology, renewable energy, and infrastructure.
Overall Assessment
Overall, Djibouti enters this new political cycle with a broad and ambitious agenda that seeks to balance economic modernization with social stability and institutional resilience. While external geopolitical tensions and global economic uncertainty continue to pose risks to trade flows, energy costs, and fiscal sustainability, the country’s projected economic growth of approximately 6% in 2026, combined with its proactive diplomatic engagement and infrastructure-driven strategy, suggests a relatively resilient outlook.
Ultimately, the success of Djibouti’s new phase of governance will depend on its ability to sustain investor confidence, manage external shocks, deepen social reforms, and translate its strategic geographic advantages into long-term, inclusive economic growth.
