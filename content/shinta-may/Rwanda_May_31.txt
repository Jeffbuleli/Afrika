*RSSB Promotes Digital Savings Tool to Boost EjoHeza Participation Among Youth
HUYE, Rwanda, May 26, 2026 - The Rwanda Social Security Board (RSSB) has intensified efforts to encourage long-term savings among young people by promoting a new digital feature that automatically transfers money from mobile wallets into EjoHeza retirement accounts.
The campaign was conducted during the three-day “Memorial Rutsindura” volleyball tournament held from May 22 to 24 in Huye and Gisagara districts. The event commemorates former volleyball player, teacher and coach Alphonse Rutsindura, who was killed during the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi.
RSSB officials used the gathering to raise awareness about EjoHeza, Rwanda’s voluntary long-term savings scheme, while introducing participants to the “Iremere na EjoHeza” digital platform, designed to help members save consistently through automated mobile money deductions.
Speaking during the event, EjoHeza coordinator for Huye and Nyamagabe districts, Marie Louise Uwimbabazi, said the technology was created to address one of the biggest challenges faced by savers: forgetting to make regular contributions.
The system allows EjoHeza members with an MTN Mobile Money account to link their mobile wallet to their savings account and schedule automatic contributions on a daily, weekly or monthly basis.
According to RSSB, once the service is activated, savings are transferred automatically whenever funds are available in the user's mobile wallet, helping members maintain regular contributions without having to remember each payment.
Officials emphasized that if there are insufficient funds in a subscriber’s account, no penalties or debts are incurred, with the system simply waiting until funds become available.
The initiative particularly targets young people, including motorcycle taxi operators, informal workers and others in the private sector who may not have access to conventional pension schemes but earn income regularly.
Several participants who have already adopted the platform said the automated system makes long-term saving easier and more reliable.
Residents attending the event described the technology as a practical solution that reduces the risk of missing contributions and helps build retirement savings gradually over time.
The campaign also highlighted the broader benefits of EjoHeza, which aims to expand retirement coverage beyond formally employed workers. Members can register, monitor their accounts and make contributions using mobile phone services or through dedicated online platforms.
Huye District Vice Mayor for Economic Development, André Kamana, encouraged residents to embrace the program, describing retirement planning as an essential component of long-term financial security.
He said the initiative helps citizens prepare for old age and reduce the financial burden that retirement can place on families and communities.
According to district figures, residents of Huye have accumulated more than 2 billion Rwandan francs in EjoHeza savings since the program's launch. The district currently has more than 136,000 registered members, representing approximately 34 percent of its population.
Women account for more than 74,000 members, while over 62,000 men are enrolled in the scheme. Local authorities say they hope to increase participation further, particularly among young people.
Under the EjoHeza program, members become eligible for retirement benefits at the age of 55. Authorities have also indicated that reforms are being considered to allow participants to access part of their savings before retirement under specific conditions.
Officials further noted that EjoHeza savings continue to generate annual returns exceeding 10 percent, making the scheme one of the country's most attractive long-term savings options for citizens seeking financial security in retirement.

Health experts estimate that contaminated food results in the loss of approximately 33 million healthy life years annually, underscoring the significant social and economic burden associated with food-related illnesses.
To reduce the risk of food contamination, health authorities recommend following basic food safety practices, including maintaining good hygiene during food preparation, separating raw and cooked foods, cooking food thoroughly and storing perishable items at appropriate temperatures.

Medical experts warn that persistent vomiting and diarrhea should be treated as serious warning signs, particularly in children, as they can rapidly lead to dehydration and other potentially life-threatening complications.
Parents are advised to seek medical attention immediately if a child develops symptoms such as unusual behavior, excessive thirst, reduced urination, low energy levels, bloody stools, severe abdominal pain or a high fever.
Among adults, severe foodborne illness may present through persistent fatigue, dehydration, frequent vomiting, repeated episodes of diarrhea, dry mouth, reduced urination and high fever.
Public health officials stress that proper food handling, preparation and storage remain among the most effective measures for preventing foodborne diseases and reducing the global burden of illness linked to contaminated food.
