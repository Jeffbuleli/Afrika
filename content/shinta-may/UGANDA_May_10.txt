*BoU Warns Sovereignty Bill Could Trigger Shilling Slide, Fuel Inflation Pressures
Kampala, April 28, 2026 - Uganda’s central bank has cautioned that the proposed Protection of Sovereignty Bill, 2026 could destabilise the country’s macroeconomic environment by weakening the shilling, reducing foreign investor inflows, and reigniting inflationary pressures if passed in its current form.
Appearing before Parliament’s Joint Committee on Defence and Internal Affairs and Legal and Parliamentary Affairs, the Governor of the Bank of Uganda warned that the legislation risks undermining decades of financial liberalisation that have supported economic growth and stability.
He stressed that while the intention behind strengthening national sovereignty is legitimate, any disruption to capital inflows could have far-reaching consequences on Uganda’s balance of payments position and foreign exchange reserves. He added that reduced inflows would likely lead to currency depreciation, which in turn would increase the cost of imported goods and push inflation above the central bank’s 5% target.
The central bank further cautioned that Uganda’s foreign reserves, estimated at around USD 6 billion, could come under pressure if investor confidence weakens. It noted that recent stability in the shilling has been supported by a balance of payments surplus of approximately $1.5 billion in the last financial year.
According to the bank, any shock to these inflows could force reserve drawdowns, increasing economic vulnerability.
In recent months, the shilling has shown relative resilience, trading in a range of approximately Shs3,650-3,850 per US dollar. It has also remained broadly stable against regional currencies, supported by export earnings, remittances, and development financing.
However, economists warn that this stability remains fragile and highly dependent on continued foreign exchange inflows. A sustained depreciation, they caution, would raise the cost of imports such as fuel, machinery, and pharmaceuticals, feeding into broader inflation across key sectors including manufacturing and transport.
Over time, a weaker currency could also reduce household purchasing power, increase the burden of foreign-denominated debt, and dampen investor appetite for Ugandan assets.
The central bank emphasised that maintaining investor confidence is essential to sustaining Uganda’s long-term economic transformation agenda and its ambition of achieving a $500 billion economy.
The ongoing debate now places Parliament in a position to balance sovereignty-focused legislative ambitions with the need to preserve macroeconomic stability. While supporters of the Bill advocate for stronger national control mechanisms, monetary authorities are urging careful technical revisions to avoid unintended financial disruption.
As deliberations continue, financial markets are expected to closely monitor policy signals, particularly those affecting foreign inflows and exchange rate stability. The central bank’s message remains firm: protecting economic stability is central to safeguarding sovereignty itself.

*Regulator Directs Insurers on Mandatory Sensitization on Workers’ Compensation
Kampala, April 29, 2026 - Uganda’s insurance regulator has directed general insurers to roll out mandatory public sensitisation campaigns on workers’ compensation insurance, in a move aimed at improving awareness of claims procedures and reducing disputes linked to workplace injury compensation.
The directive, contained in a circular issued on March 16, 2026, was signed by Insurance Regulatory Authority of Uganda Chief Executive Officer Kaddunabbi Ibrahim Lubega.
It requires all non-life insurance companies to implement structured awareness programmes targeting both employers and employees covered under workers’ compensation and group personal accident policies.
Insurers have been instructed to educate policyholders on key aspects of the system, including procedures for reporting workplace injuries, required documentation for claims, and the process for submitting and tracking compensation claims.
The circular further emphasises the need for improved understanding of the role of medical practitioners in assessing disability and incapacity, as well as clearer guidance on policy issuance, renewal processes, and broader policyholder engagement.
The regulator said enhanced sensitisation is expected to strengthen transparency, improve compliance, and support safer workplace practices, while also increasing efficiency in claims management across the sector.
The directive follows consultations between the Insurance Regulatory Authority and the Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development, which identified significant gaps in claims handling and policy awareness.
Among the concerns raised were premature assessments of permanent incapacity before full medical recovery, delays in resolving compensation claims, and an increase in disputes escalated to the Medical Arbitration Board.
The Medical Arbitration Board serves as an independent mechanism for resolving disagreements related to workplace injuries and occupational disease compensation, particularly in cases involving conflicting medical evaluations.
According to the regulator, many of these challenges stem from limited awareness among employers and policyholders regarding procedures, documentation requirements, and statutory timelines under the workers’ compensation framework.
The new directive is intended to address these gaps, streamline claims processes, and improve the overall functioning of Uganda’s workers’ compensation system.

*Uganda Reaffirms Commitment to Liberalizing Aviation Sector
Kampala, May 6, 2026 - Uganda has reiterated its commitment to liberalizing its aviation industry and strengthening competition frameworks as part of wider efforts to integrate into Africa’s single air transport market.
Speaking at the opening of a continental capacity-building workshop on competition regulation in air transport held in Munyonyo, the Director General of the Uganda Civil Aviation Authority (UCAA), Fred Bamwesigye, said aviation plays a central role in driving economic growth, trade, tourism, and regional integration across the continent.
He noted that while improved air connectivity is important, it must be supported by strong regulatory systems to ensure fairness and sustainability in the market.
According to him, initiatives such as the Single African Air Transport Market (SAATM) and the African Continental Free Trade Area present major opportunities for growth, but their success depends on the establishment of effective competition frameworks.
Bamwesigye cautioned that without proper regulation, liberalization could result in market distortions, unfair competition, and reduced consumer confidence in the aviation sector.
Uganda, although not yet a full member of SAATM, has taken steps toward alignment with continental aviation standards. The country, as a signatory to the Yamoussoukro Decision, has already introduced measures including multiple airline designation, unrestricted flight frequencies, and fifth freedom traffic rights on selected regional routes.
Officials said Uganda is progressively strengthening its legal and regulatory framework to support full liberalization. Key reforms include the enactment of the Competition Act in 2024 and the establishment of a Consumer Protection Unit within the aviation regulator.
The country has also developed draft Civil Aviation Competition and Consumer Protection Regulations, which are expected to be finalised and enacted by June 2026.
Bamwesigye added that internal consultations regarding Uganda’s formal accession to SAATM are nearing completion, after which a cabinet memorandum will be submitted for approval.
He acknowledged that inconsistent implementation of competition rules across African countries remains a major challenge, making regional technical engagements such as the Munyonyo workshop critical for harmonisation.

Kampala, May 8, 2026 - Uganda’s National Lotteries and Gaming Regulatory Board (NLGRB) has called for the creation of an East African Gaming Regulators Forum to harmonise responsible gaming standards and strengthen cross-border enforcement against illegal gambling operators.

The panel also noted a growing shift toward embedding responsible gaming into core regulatory requirements rather than treating it as a voluntary compliance measure.
In Uganda, the Responsible Gaming Directives 2025 already require operators to implement protective measures such as self-exclusion tools and spending limits as part of their operational licensing conditions.
Regulators further emphasised the importance of stronger consumer protection rules, particularly in advertising, to prevent misleading claims that promote gambling as a guaranteed income source.
Participants agreed that as the sector expands, sustainable growth will depend not only on revenue generation but also on maintaining regulatory integrity, protecting consumers, and strengthening public trust.
The proposed East African Gaming Regulators Forum is expected to provide a structured platform for coordination, information sharing, and joint enforcement, with regulators expressing optimism that such cooperation could significantly enhance oversight of the rapidly evolving digital gaming industry.
