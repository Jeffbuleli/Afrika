Humanitarian and community actors are calling for urgent intervention, urging relief organizations to conduct rapid assessments and organize emergency assistance for affected households.
The situation adds to an already fragile humanitarian landscape in eastern DRC, where ongoing armed conflict continues to drive repeated waves of displacement across North Kivu and South Kivu provinces.
*Army Upgrades Communication Systems to Secure Military Operations
Kinshasa - May 5, 2026
The government of the Democratic Republic of Congo is strengthening the communication capabilities of the armed forces through the acquisition of advanced systems aimed at securing military operations in conflict zones in the east of the country.
The announcement was made during a briefing before the National Assembly’s Defense and Security Committee by Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of National Defense and Veterans Affairs Guy Kabombo Mwadiamvita.
According to the report adopted by lawmakers, Congolese forces deployed in eastern provinces face major communication challenges, particularly in areas where the Congo River Alliance and the March 23 Movement are active. Authorities say these groups occupy significant portions of North Kivu and South Kivu.
Military officials warned that intercepted communications have increasingly exposed troop movements and operational plans, complicating coordination on the battlefield. They noted that secure communication is now considered essential for operational success.
The minister explained that military communication systems are central to command and control, enabling units to receive and transmit orders across strategic, operational, and tactical levels. He stressed that without secure technologies, frontline units remain vulnerable to interception and disruption by opposing forces.
He further stated that adversary groups have developed relatively advanced communication infrastructures capable of intercepting or decoding military messages, creating additional operational risks for government forces.
To address these gaps, the government has entered into partnerships with allied countries to acquire modern communication systems designed to ensure confidentiality and secure transmission of military orders.
However, authorities acknowledge that long-term strategic autonomy will require the development of national capabilities, including the potential deployment of satellite-based communication systems.
The initiative comes as eastern DRC continues to face persistent insecurity, with ongoing clashes involving multiple armed groups, including the AFC/M23 coalition and other actors such as the Allied Democratic Forces and CODECO militias. The conflict has led to widespread displacement, casualties, and destruction across North Kivu, South Kivu, and Ituri provinces.
Lawmakers described the report as a key strategic framework intended to strengthen military planning and improve national security decision-making in response to the evolving conflict landscape.

Clashes continued through Sunday before Wazalendo fighters withdrew from the area, allowing the AFC/M23 coalition to retake control of the town.

The recapture of Tushunguti marks a renewed shift in control over a key strategic location that has changed hands multiple times in recent months due to ongoing fighting in the region.
Situated along a sensitive border zone, the area remains a frequent flashpoint between armed groups and has significant strategic importance due to its position linking two conflict-affected provinces.
The latest developments are expected to further worsen an already fragile humanitarian situation, with civilians once again forced to flee their homes amid renewed violence. Uganda Arrests Over 230 Foreign Nationals in Crackdown on Illegal Activities
April 28, 2026 - Authorities in Uganda have arrested more than 230 foreign nationals during coordinated operations targeting alleged illegal activities, including cybercrime, unauthorized religious activities, and gambling-related offenses.
According to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, officers from the Immigration and Naturalization Service detained a total of 231 individuals in separate operations carried out between April 27 and 28.
In the first operation, 62 Nigerian nationals were arrested in Adjumani District after being found conducting religious evangelistic activities without the required authorization, authorities said.
A second and larger operation took place in the Bukoto-Ntinda area of Kampala, where 169 foreigners were arrested inside a building allegedly used for various illegal activities.
Those detained in Kampala include nationals from Ethiopia, Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Ghana, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Cambodia, Nepal, Indonesia, and Malaysia.
Preliminary investigations suggest that some individuals claimed they had been deceived with false job offers before entering the country. Authorities also reported that a portion of those arrested were linked to cybercrime activities, while others were allegedly found with equipment used for illicit operations.
Officials said the building where the suspects were found was fully equipped with facilities, including a restaurant, allowing occupants to remain inside for extended periods while carrying out the alleged activities.
The government stated that individuals found in violation of immigration laws will face prosecution, while those linked to criminal activity will be handed over to relevant investigative agencies for further action.
