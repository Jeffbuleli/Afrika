She added that early screening and consistent access to treatment, particularly Hydroxyurea, significantly improves survival rates and reduces complications, allowing affected children to live more stable and productive lives.
“When a child is screened at birth and linked to care, especially Hydroxyurea treatment, and is consistently followed up, there is a higher chance of survival,” she said.
The Ministry of Health also highlighted the broader economic burden of sickle cell disease, noting that families often face high treatment costs and prolonged hospital stays that reduce household productivity.
Officials said the introduction of locally manufactured Hydroxyurea is expected to lower treatment costs, improve drug availability, and reduce reliance on imported medicines.
Dr Atwine praised Quality Chemical Industries for investing in local production capacity, saying it aligns with Uganda’s broader strategy to integrate sickle cell management into primary healthcare services and expand community-level interventions, including engagement with religious leaders in awareness and counselling efforts.
Quality Chemical Industries CEO Ajay Kumar Pal said the company is committed to addressing major public health challenges in Uganda and across Africa by improving access to affordable and high-quality medicines.
“At QCIL, we are committed to improving access to essential medicines for diseases that pose the greatest public health burden on the continent,” he said.
Co-founder and Chairman Emmanuel Katongole noted that Africa bears the highest global burden of sickle cell disease and emphasized the importance of local manufacturing in reducing treatment gaps.
“We ventured into the manufacture of sickle cell drugs after realizing that 80% of the world’s 4.4 million sickle cell disease cases are in Africa,” he said, adding that local production could significantly improve affordability and access.
Officials say the initiative represents a major milestone in Uganda’s efforts to strengthen pharmaceutical self-reliance and improve long-term health outcomes for patients with chronic diseases.

*Uganda Reassures Fuel Stability Despite Global Oil Market Volatility
Kampala, May 22, 2026 - The Government of Uganda has assured the public of stable fuel supply across the country despite ongoing disruptions in global oil markets triggered by instability in the Middle East.
The Ministry of Energy and Mineral Development said Uganda continues to maintain sufficient petroleum reserves and that supply chains remain secure, even as international fuel prices and logistics costs rise.
Speaking at a press briefing at the Uganda Media Centre, Permanent Secretary Eng. Irene Bateebe said tensions affecting global shipping routes, including the Strait of Hormuz, have disrupted oil flows and increased freight, insurance, and importation costs worldwide.
She noted that East African countries, including Uganda, have been affected due to their dependence on imported petroleum products, particularly from the Gulf region. However, she emphasized that national fuel stocks remain adequate.
“The Government continues to closely monitor developments in the international oil market and remains committed to ensuring stable petroleum product supply for the country,” she said.
Officials said Uganda National Oil Company, in partnership with international suppliers such as Vitol, has diversified import sources to reduce vulnerability, with supplies now coming from West Africa, Europe, India, and the Americas.
Authorities acknowledged recent fluctuations in pump prices, attributing them to global supply constraints, exchange rate movements, rising freight costs, and increased regional demand. Temporary shortages experienced in some border areas were linked to cross-border fuel purchases driven by price differences with neighbouring countries.
The ministry urged the public to avoid panic buying and dismissed misinformation circulating on social media regarding fuel shortages.
“We urge the public to remain calm, avoid panic buying, and disregard misinformation circulating on social media because the country continues to receive regular fuel deliveries through both the Kenya and Tanzania supply routes,” Bateebe said.
Uganda’s fuel market remains liberalised, with prices set by oil marketing companies under government oversight to prevent smuggling and unfair pricing practices.
On long-term energy security, officials highlighted ongoing investments in petroleum storage and distribution infrastructure aimed at strengthening national resilience against external shocks.
The Jinja Storage Terminal is being expanded from 30 million litres to 40 million litres, while the Mahathi Infra Terminal on Lake Victoria, with a capacity of 70 million litres, continues to support regional fuel logistics.
Work is also progressing on the Kampala Storage Terminal in Mpigi District, projected to hold up to 320 million litres and integrate into the national refined products distribution network linked to the planned Hoima refinery pipeline system.
Government confirmed continued development of the Uganda Refinery Project in Kabaale, a 60,000-barrels-per-day facility valued at approximately USD 4 billion. The project includes a refinery complex, a 211-kilometre multi-products pipeline, and associated storage infrastructure.
Officials said the refinery is expected to reduce dependence on imported fuels while boosting industrial growth, petrochemical production, fertiliser manufacturing, LPG development, employment, and regional trade.
The ministry also announced progress in upstream exploration, including preparations for a new petroleum licensing round scheduled for the 2026/2027 financial year and ongoing seismic surveys in Kasurubani.
On policy reform, government has finalised the National Petroleum Policy 2025, replacing the 2008 framework to align with evolving energy priorities, including commercialisation, regional integration, and the global energy transition.
New regulations on liquefied petroleum gas operations are also expected to be gazetted to improve safety standards, quality control, and regulatory oversight.
Officials reiterated government’s commitment to strengthening energy security through expanded infrastructure, increased local capacity, and improved management of Uganda’s petroleum resources.
*URSB Introduces Mandatory Entity Registration Numbers for Organisations in Push to Formalise Economy
Mbarara, May 24, 2026 - The Uganda Registration Services Bureau (URSB) has announced plans to roll out a nationwide system of Entity Registration Numbers (ERNs), requiring all organisations to obtain unique identifiers to transact business with government.
Officials say the programme, which will begin in the 2026/27 financial year, is aimed at strengthening formalisation, improving compliance, and integrating entities into government digital systems.
According to Arthur Kwesiga, Commissioner for ICT and Innovation at URSB, the ERN system will be mandatory for any entity seeking access to government services, licenses, or official authorisations.
“We are starting this program in the coming financial year and entities that will not acquire the registration numbers will face challenges in accessing government services, obtaining or renewing licenses, securing lawful authorizations among others,” he said during a stakeholders’ engagement in Mbarara.
Under the new framework, organisations without ERNs may be unable to operate legally or engage in government-related transactions, as the system becomes a core requirement for regulatory compliance.
The initiative is intended to function similarly to National Identification Numbers used for individuals, providing a unified digital identity for institutions such as companies, NGOs, cooperatives, churches, trusts, trade unions, government agencies, and investment schemes.
“You either acquire it or remain informal in doing business,” Kwesiga added.
He noted that organisations already registered with URSB will have their ERNs automatically linked to existing Tax Identification Numbers or official contact emails.
However, the proposal has raised concern among some stakeholders, particularly faith-based and non-profit organisations, who questioned whether the system could indirectly expand the tax base.
Edith Kyamazima of Mbarara Full Gospel Church argued that churches and similar entities should not be subjected to additional administrative requirements, noting their non-profit status.
In response, URSB dismissed claims that the system is linked to taxation, insisting instead that it is designed to enhance transparency, accountability, and data integration across the formal sector.
Officials also clarified that obtaining an Entity Registration Number will be free of charge.
URSB said the rollout is part of broader efforts to modernise business registration systems, improve regulatory oversight, and streamline interactions between organisations and government institutions.
*Uganda Gaming Regulator Pushes Digital Reforms as Online Betting Surges
Kampala, May 26, 2026 - The National Lotteries and Gaming Regulatory Board (NLGRB) has announced a new wave of technology-driven reforms aimed at modernising Uganda’s gaming sector as online betting and digital transactions continue to reshape the industry.
Speaking during a media roundtable in Kampala, NLGRB Chief Executive Officer Denis Mudene Ngabirano said traditional regulatory systems are no longer adequate to supervise an industry increasingly powered by digital platforms.
“The future of gaming is technology-led and increasingly sophisticated,” he said. “As regulators, we must equally automate our systems and strengthen monitoring capabilities to respond effectively to emerging trends.”
Central to the reforms is the expansion of the National Central Electronic Monitoring System (NCEMS), which allows real-time tracking of gaming activity and operator compliance across the country.
The system has already been linked to the National Identification and Registration Authority (NIRA) to strengthen age verification and prevent underage gambling. Uganda maintains a legal gaming age of 25 years, which regulators say is intended to protect vulnerable groups from gambling-related harm.
Officials also confirmed that amendments to existing gaming laws are being prepared to better regulate online gambling, strengthen player verification systems, and align legislation with rapidly evolving digital practices.
According to the regulator, nearly 74 percent of gaming activity shifted online after the Covid-19 pandemic, significantly altering both consumer behaviour and enforcement challenges.
Proposed reforms include clearer separation between online and land-based gaming licences, as well as updated technical standards for operators.
Uganda has also developed nine national gaming standards in collaboration with the Uganda National Bureau of Standards (UNBS), which the regulator says places the country among Africa’s early leaders in structured gaming oversight.
“We want regulation that supports innovation while also ensuring accountability and player protection,” Mudene said.
The Board is also exploring policies to support local innovation by introducing differentiated licence fees to encourage Ugandan-owned gaming technology companies and digital platform development.
“We have many talented Ugandan IT professionals,” he said. “The intention is to create an environment where local innovation can participate competitively within the gaming ecosystem.”
Beyond enforcement and licensing, the regulator emphasized responsible gaming, including mental health support services for individuals affected by gambling addiction. Treatment is offered free of charge through partnerships with health providers, and confidential support is available via a toll-free helpline.
Authorities also noted that illegal gaming remains a significant challenge, with thousands of illegal machines destroyed and operators blocked through cooperation with mobile money providers and payment processors.
The gaming sector generated shs323 billion in tax revenue in the last financial year, with hundreds of billions already collected toward the current fiscal target.
Uganda currently has 63 licensed operators, over 2,000 approved gaming premises, and an estimated 23,000 workers in the sector, the majority of whom are women.
Officials say the long-term goal is to anticipate industry shifts rather than react to them, ensuring regulation keeps pace with rapid technological change while safeguarding consumers and sustaining sector growth.
*Dr. Musoke Urges Africa to End Raw Material Exports, Push Industrialization Agenda
Nairobi, May 29, 2026 - Senior Presidential Advisor on Agribusiness and Value Addition, Dr. Hillary Emmanuel Musoke, has called on African governments to urgently scale up industrialization and value addition, warning that continued export of raw materials is weakening the continent’s economic independence.
Speaking during the 2026 African Union Day commemorations at the Edge Convention Centre in Nairobi, Dr. Musoke said Africa’s long-term prosperity depends on its ability to process and manufacture goods locally rather than exporting unprocessed commodities.
Addressing delegates in a high-level panel discussion themed “Agenda 2063: The Africa We Want,” he described Africa’s reliance on raw material exports as a structural economic loss that deprives the continent of jobs and wealth creation opportunities.
“Africa is not poor in land, labour, or water. We are poor in value,” he said. “In that gap, we are exporting both jobs and wealth in containers. We are exporting our sovereignty.”
Dr. Musoke argued that despite holding a significant share of the world’s uncultivated arable land and freshwater resources, Africa continues to spend more than $50 billion annually on food imports, reflecting underdeveloped agro-processing capacity.
He highlighted Uganda’s coffee sector as an example, noting that while African countries export raw beans, most of the value is captured abroad through roasting, branding, packaging, and retailing.
He estimated that Uganda alone loses over $1.5 billion each year due to limited domestic processing of agricultural commodities.
“The most painful symptom of this hemorrhage is migration,” he said, linking youth unemployment and outward migration to weak industrial development across the continent.
Dr. Musoke connected Uganda’s development strategy to the African Union’s Agenda 2063 framework, noting that government programs such as the Parish Development Model, agro-processing zones, and youth-focused agricultural technologies are aimed at strengthening value addition and commercialization.
He also emphasized the importance of the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) in creating larger regional markets for processed African goods and reducing dependence on external economies.
Other speakers at the summit echoed similar concerns, calling for stronger regional integration, industrial policy reforms, and increased investment in skills development to support manufacturing growth.
Pan-African scholar PLO Lumumba warned that Africa’s rapidly growing youth population could become either a major economic asset or a source of instability if industrial job creation remains insufficient.
Dr. Musoke proposed several reforms, including harmonized trade policies across African states, increased financing for agro-industrial enterprises, education systems focused on technical skills, and the development of African-owned certification and branding systems.

“You are there because of Uganda’s national interests. You are modern instruments of national power. Continue serving Uganda with loyalty and utmost discipline,” he added.
Lt Gen Okiding commended the attachés for what he described as professionalism and effective representation of Uganda abroad, saying their performance had been satisfactory.
At the opening of the retreat, Chief of Joint Staff Lt Gen Jack Bakasumba highlighted the importance of defence attachés in promoting Uganda’s image and strengthening international partnerships.
“Your major role is to present and protect Uganda’s strategic interests by working closely with ambassadors and heads of diplomatic missions,” he said.
He also warned against indiscipline and corruption within the forces, stressing that integrity remains central to military service.
Col Allan Matsiko, speaking on behalf of the Chief of Defence Intelligence and Security, said the retreat aimed to deepen officers’ understanding of Uganda’s foreign policy priorities and improve coordination with international partners.
He noted that defence attachés play a critical role in military diplomacy, intelligence cooperation, and the promotion of Uganda’s trade, investment, and tourism interests abroad.
The retreat also included engagements with officials from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Uganda Tourism Board as part of efforts to align defence diplomacy with broader national development objectives.
Conclusion
The month of May 2026 has been a period of profound transition and heightened urgency for Uganda, marked by structural overhauls in governance, severe public health mandates, and a concerted push toward economic self-reliance. The intersection of these political, economic, and social dynamics underscores a nation navigating critical internal friction while aggressively asserting its sovereignty and regional leadership.
1. Political Consolidation and Institutional Reform
The political landscape was defined by President Yoweri Museveni’s aggressive restructuring of national leadership for the 2026-2031 term. The unveiling of the new Cabinet-notably retaining key figures like First Lady Janet Museveni while dropping long-serving stalwarts such as Matia Kasaija and Gen. Moses Ali-signals a strategic effort to balance regime continuity with generational and regional renewal, exemplified by expanded ministerial representation for the West Nile sub-region.
Concurrently, the election of Jacob Oboth-Oboth as the new Speaker of Parliament has set a benchmark for institutional reform. While the legislature faces deep-seated skepticism from political analysts regarding its autonomy, the administration is under intense public and opposition pressure to match its anti-corruption rhetoric with transparent, non-selective enforcement. This public scrutiny was further amplified by high-profile disputes, such as the President's public rebuke of veteran journalist Andrew Mwenda, highlighting the ongoing tension surrounding state economic strategies and governance.
2. Economic Stewardship, Industrialization, and Formalization
Economically, Uganda is aggressively pursuing value-addition and economic formalization to insulate itself from global volatility. The government's unwavering defense of its industrialization agenda-championed by both the Presidency and senior advisors like Dr. Hillary Emmanuel Musoke-reflects a critical policy pivot aimed at ending raw material exports (particularly in the coffee and gold sectors) to retain jobs and wealth domestically
To streamline economic management and broaden oversight, the Uganda Registration Services Bureau (URSB) introduced mandatory Entity Registration Numbers (ERNs) to digitize and formalize businesses and organizations. Despite localized fuel price fluctuations driven by Middle East instability, the state successfully stabilized its energy supply chains by diversifying import sources through partnerships like the Uganda National Oil Company and Vitol. Simultaneously, sectors ranging from agribusiness to digital gaming are modernizing, though they continue to battle systemic bottlenecks like counterfeit inputs and regulatory lag.
3. Emergency Public Health and Bio-Medical Innovation
The re-emergence of the Ebola threat triggered by imported cases from the Democratic Republic of Congo served as a severe test of Uganda's crisis-response framework. The Ministry of Health’s immediate imposition of sweeping containment measures-including a 40-week suspension of border markets, flight cancellations, and the postponement of major cultural milestones like Martyrs Day-demonstrated a zero-tolerance approach learned from the 2022-2023 outbreak.
While these strict border and transport restrictions cause inevitable short-term socioeconomic strain, they have successfully prevented local transmission. Furthermore, Uganda is positioning itself at the forefront of global health innovation, highlighted by Dei BioPharma's US patent application for a next-generation mRNA Ebola vaccine platform and the historic local manufacturing of the sickle cell drug Sikurea. These milestones signal a clear trajectory toward pharmaceutical self-reliance.
Summary Outlook
Ultimately, the report for May 2026 portrays a resilient Uganda in the midst of a structural reset. Whether the newly configured Cabinet and parliamentary leadership can translate their reform agendas into sustained public trust, robust health security, and equitable economic growth will remain the defining test for the administration as it embarks on its new governance term.
