Mine-clearing operations have also focused on strategic facilities, including Khartoum International Airport, where hundreds of explosive hazards were removed to allow humanitarian flights to resume earlier this year. Additional clearance efforts have targeted homes, schools, hospitals, and farmland in affected communities.
The European Union recently announced additional funding to support mine-action activities in Sudan, as humanitarian organizations continue surveying hazardous areas and expanding explosive-risk awareness programmes for civilians and relief personnel.

*UN Warns of Catastrophic Protection Collapse as Drone Warfare Intensifies Across Sudan
Khartoum, May 17, 2026 - The protection environment across Sudan has deteriorated to what humanitarian agencies describe as a catastrophic level, amid escalating violence against civilians, tightening movement restrictions, and the near-collapse of protective systems in conflict-affected regions.
A joint review of humanitarian assessments indicates that the most severe breakdowns are concentrated in Darfur, Kordofan, and Khartoum, where civilian populations continue to face repeated attacks, displacement, and limited access to basic services.
The Rapid Support Forces (RSF) have been accused of sustaining indiscriminate attacks on civilian areas in northern and central Sudan, while imposing increasingly restrictive controls over populations in territories under their influence in Darfur and West Kordofan. Markets, displacement camps, residential neighborhoods, and critical infrastructure such as water systems have repeatedly come under fire.
Humanitarian observers warn that the rapid expansion of drone warfare has significantly transformed the conflict, with both sides increasingly relying on unmanned aerial systems to target logistics hubs, storage facilities, and training sites. The shift away from conventional front-line combat has coincided with a sharp rise in civilian casualties.
According to recent UN human rights reporting, drone strikes alone accounted for an estimated 880 civilian deaths between January and April 2026, representing the vast majority of recorded civilian fatalities during that period.
The escalation has further crippled already weakened public services. Health facilities, schools, and humanitarian access routes have been severely disrupted, contributing to worsening food insecurity and mass displacement. Aid workers also report that communication blackouts and insecurity continue to obscure the full scale of casualties and abuses on the ground.
Gender-based violence remains widespread, with repeated documentation of mass rape, gang rape, and sexual slavery occurring along transit routes, checkpoints, and within overcrowded displacement camps. Survivors face significant barriers in accessing medical and psychosocial care due to the collapse of health infrastructure.
Humanitarian agencies also highlight growing patterns of discrimination in aid delivery, with displaced populations often excluded from assistance based on ethnic or political affiliations, further deepening vulnerability among affected communities.
Economic exploitation has accompanied the violence, including widespread looting, extortion, and forced displacement. Armed actors are reported to confiscate livestock, property, and food supplies, pushing some households toward extreme survival strategies such as child labour and forced recruitment.
In several frontline areas, siege-like conditions have trapped civilians without access to food, water, or safe evacuation routes. In El Obeid, intensified fighting and drone strikes have forced residents onto hazardous escape routes, with reports of deaths linked to dehydration and exhaustion.
Children remain among the most affected groups. Armed groups continue to recruit minors for combat roles, checkpoint duties, and intelligence operations. The UN Refugee Agency has registered around 42,000 unaccompanied and separated children, with thousands inside Sudan and many more in neighboring countries, exposed to risks of trafficking, abduction, and violence.
The long-term danger is further compounded by widespread explosive ordnance contamination. Landmines and unexploded devices are reported across urban and rural areas, including newly mined zones in parts of Khartoum.
The UN Mine Action Service reports that explosive remnants have killed at least 25 people and injured 52 others so far this year, despite large-scale clearance operations that have removed thousands of devices and rehabilitated millions of square meters of land since the outbreak of war.

The commander, Major General Al-Nour Ahmed Adam, known as “Al-Nour Al-Qubba,” is said to have positioned his fighters on the outskirts of Jebel Aulia and near the southern approaches to Al-Salha in Omdurman, with the Khartoum State Security Committee stating that their presence is part of arrangements approved by the regular army leadership.
Sources clarified that recent drone strike reports in southern Khartoum and in Rabak in White Nile State were not directed at Al-Qubba’s forces, noting that his troops remain confined within Khartoum. They also did not confirm whether the unit-estimated at around 180 lightly armed fighters-would be redeployed to conflict zones in Kordofan or Darfur, saying such decisions rest with the army command.
Meanwhile, social media reports have circulated images and claims about the deployment across residential areas south of Khartoum and in Omdurman, raising local concerns over the presence of armed groups in civilian neighbourhoods.
*Civilian casualties reported in renewed RSF drone attacks around Khartoum
Omdurman, 2 May 2026 - At least five civilians were killed when a drone operated by the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) struck a civilian vehicle in the southern countryside of Omdurman, according to a Sudanese rights group. The vehicle was reportedly travelling along the Al-Jamu’iya road from White Nile State when it was hit, killing all five passengers.
The incident comes amid a renewed surge in RSF drone activity across the capital region, following a period of relative lull. Over the past three days, similar drone strikes have also been reported in areas of Jebel Aulia, south of the capital.
Khartoum authorities have so far remained silent on the latest wave of attacks, including those affecting both Jebel Aulia and Omdurman, with no official statement issued regarding civilian casualties or defensive responses.
Human rights observers have described the strike as part of a broader pattern of attacks on civilian movement routes and populated areas, warning that the escalation further undermines efforts to stabilize the capital after years of conflict that has devastated infrastructure and displaced millions across Sudan.
*Drone strike kills members of Sudan Shield Forces commander’s family in central Sudan
Al Jazira State, 3 May 2026 - A drone strike, suspected to have been carried out by the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), hit the family home of Sudan Shield Forces commander Abu Agla Keikal in the village of Al-Kahli Zaidan in eastern Al-Jazira, killing six family members, including his brother.
Local sources reported that a drone circled the area before launching missiles at multiple homes belonging to the Keikal family. The attack left several dead and others injured, including prominent figures within the Sudan Shield Forces, a group aligned with the Sudanese army since Keikal’s defection from the RSF in October 2024.
Among the victims was Major Azzam Keikal, the commander’s brother, alongside other relatives, in what marks one of the deadliest targeted strikes against the group’s leadership network. Supporters of the Sudan Shield Forces condemned the attack, vowing continued resistance despite the losses.
The incident is the latest in a series of drone strikes against Sudan Shield Forces positions in their strongholds across the Butana region, which spans parts of Al Jazira and Gedaref states. Previous attacks have included a deadly strike on a military camp in May 2025 that killed 13 personnel, also attributed to the RSF.
The Sudan Shield Forces have played a key role alongside the army in recent territorial gains, including operations that helped secure Al Jazira and Khartoum states before expanding into Kordofan and Blue Nile regions.
*Drone strikes hit Khartoum airport and strategic sites amid renewed aerial escalation
Khartoum, 4 May 2026 - Drone strikes targeted several strategic locations across Khartoum State, including Khartoum International Airport, as aerial attacks linked to the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) intensified across multiple regions of Sudan.
Military sources said a long-range drone launched four missiles toward the airport area, with two landing near the eastern section of the facility close to Obeid Khatim Street. The strikes caused limited damage to an administrative building attached to the airport, prompting authorities to evacuate personnel and suspend at least one incoming flight from Port Sudan.
Additional drone attacks were reported near the Signal Corps area in Khartoum Bahri and around the Al-Markhiyat camp in northern Omdurman, though no immediate information was available regarding casualties or material losses.
The latest strikes form part of a broader escalation in drone warfare that has recently affected several Sudanese states, including White Nile, Al Jazira, North Kordofan, Gedaref, and Khartoum. The renewed attacks come months after Khartoum International Airport resumed operations following extensive repairs to damage sustained during the war.
Recent weeks had seen the gradual return of international flights connecting Khartoum with regional destinations including Cairo, Jeddah, and Kuwait City before the renewed drone campaign disrupted activity once again.
*RSF drone strike hits fuel stations in White Nile State
Kosti, 5 May 2026 - At least five people were killed and nine others injured after a drone strike attributed to the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) hit two fuel stations in Kosti, intensifying a recent wave of aerial attacks across White Nile State.
Medical groups described the strike as an attack on civilian infrastructure and warned that repeated assaults on essential services are worsening humanitarian conditions in the region.
The latest incident follows a series of drone operations targeting towns including Rabak and Kenana over the past week. Previous attacks reportedly damaged public service facilities and industrial infrastructure, including a sugar factory.
At the same time, Sudanese military officials renewed accusations that external actors were supporting the RSF’s drone campaign, claiming that aircraft involved in strikes across White Nile, Kordofan, and Khartoum had originated from facilities in Bahir Dar. Sudan also repeated allegations against the United Arab Emirates over alleged backing for the paramilitary group.
Humanitarian and medical organizations have called for greater international pressure to protect civilians and prevent further attacks on infrastructure critical to daily life.
*Sudan reinforces border with Ethiopia amid rising regional tensions
Gedaref State, 7 May 2026 - The Sudanese army has reinforced its military presence along border areas with Ethiopia, deploying additional troops and equipment to East Gallabat, Basunda, and the Al-Fashaga region as tensions escalate following accusations over recent drone strikes in Khartoum.
Military sources said the deployment began earlier this week in response to growing security concerns along the eastern frontier. Sudan recently accused Ethiopia of facilitating drone operations targeting strategic sites, including Khartoum International Airport, alleging that the aircraft originated from facilities in Bahir Dar. Addis Ababa has denied the accusations.
The reinforcements reportedly include additional combat units and military hardware positioned at key locations across Greater and Lesser Al-Fashaga, areas that have long been disputed between the two countries.
Senior Sudanese military officials also carried out inspections of frontline positions in the Basunda sector to assess troop readiness and strengthen morale amid heightened alert conditions along the border.
The latest tensions revive longstanding disputes between Sudan and Ethiopia over the fertile Al-Fashaga borderlands, where clashes and cross-border incidents have periodically erupted in recent years. Sudan regained control of large parts of the territory in 2020 after confrontations with Ethiopian forces and armed groups locally referred to as “Shifta,” who have frequently been accused of attacking farmers and looting agricultural resources during cultivation seasons.
*SPLM-N denies civilian abuse allegations in Kauda amid internal clashes
Kauda, 8 May 2026 - The Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N), led by Abdel Aziz al-Hilu, has rejected allegations of abuses against civilians in its stronghold of Kauda in South Kordofan, attributing recent violence in the area to an internal mutiny within its ranks.
In a statement, the group said the unrest stemmed from a localized breakdown triggered by land disputes and boundary demarcation tensions between communities in the Debi area. It explained that clashes erupted after the removal of boundary markers during a dispute between local tribes, which later escalated into armed confrontation.
The SPLM-N said it intervened militarily to separate the parties involved but claimed that some officers refused orders to report for disciplinary review, leading to what it described as a rebellion against its command structure. The movement insisted that its response was carried out under internal military law and denied that any specific ethnic or tribal group was being targeted.
However, civil society and diaspora-linked organizations, including the Nuba Mountains Media Executive Office, have raised serious concerns over reported violence against civilians in Kauda. Their statements cite accounts of burned homes and schools, looted property, and forced displacement, warning that the incidents could amount to war crimes and crimes against humanity under international law.
The same organizations have called for an independent international investigation, alleging that high-level commanders may have been involved and that documentation of the events has been submitted to international accountability bodies, including the International Criminal Court and human rights organizations.
*Sudanese army retakes strategic Blue Nile town amid continued clashes
Ed Damazin, 9 May 2026 - The Sudanese army announced on Saturday that it has regained control of the town of Al-Kaily in the southern Blue Nile State following intense fighting with a coalition of the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N) faction led by Abdel Aziz al-Hilu.
Military sources said the town, located near the border with Ethiopia and considered strategically significant within the Kurmuk axis, was seized by the RSF-allied forces on 25 April before being retaken after days of heavy clashes. The army’s 4th Infantry Division stated that its units, supported by allied formations, conducted coordinated operations that resulted in the “liberation” of the area and the withdrawal of opposing fighters.
According to the military command, the recapture of Al-Kaily inflicted significant losses in personnel and equipment on RSF-aligned forces, including units linked to commander Joseph Touka, with remaining fighters reportedly retreating from the battlefield.
In Ed Damazin, thousands of residents gathered to celebrate the army’s return to the town, as the military pledged to continue operations aimed at securing the wider region and border zones amid ongoing instability.
The fighting in Blue Nile has intensified since February, with RSF and SPLM-N allied forces previously capturing several towns, including Kurmuk, and reports of looting and destruction of civilian infrastructure contributing to mass displacement. Local officials estimate that tens of thousands of civilians have fled recent violence.
Sudanese authorities have also continued to accuse Ethiopia of providing logistical support to the RSF-SPLM-N alliance operating in the border region, allegations that Ethiopia has repeatedly denied.

At least three explosions were heard following the attacks, which also targeted an RSF training centre used for military instruction.

In response, RSF forces imposed a complete telecommunications blackout in the city, according to local accounts, amid heightened security measures following the bombardment. The RSF has previously converted several civilian facilities in Nyala into training and coordination centres for combat operations, including programs involving drone warfare and air defence systems.
The airport has remained a strategic focal point in the conflict, with both sides repeatedly targeting the facility as part of efforts to disrupt supply routes and military logistics in South Darfur, where fighting continues to intensify alongside broader nationwide battles.

Local emergency committees reported that the attack struck a market area where civilian vehicles travelling between Tina and Kutum were gathered, resulting in multiple deaths and the destruction of transport trucks. The strikes also damaged surrounding infrastructure, worsening already fragile conditions in the border region.
According to local sources, the bombardment extended to the “Am Saleh” well, a key water source for surrounding communities, rendering it inoperable and killing livestock that residents rely on for their livelihoods.
The emergency room in Karnoi condemned the attack, describing it as a deliberate targeting of civilians and essential infrastructure. It said repeated strikes on markets, water facilities, and transport routes constitute violations of international humanitarian law.
Humanitarian conditions in the area are deteriorating rapidly, with local authorities calling for urgent medical evacuations of the wounded to facilities in Tina and the delivery of emergency supplies, including medicine, shelter, and water support.
Officials and community responders have appealed for international intervention to ensure civilian protection and establish safe humanitarian corridors amid continuing fighting between the Sudanese army and Rapid Support Forces (RSF)-aligned forces in the wider North Darfur region.

Local responders called on all parties to spare civilians from the fighting and urged humanitarian and human rights organizations to document alleged violations and strengthen civilian protection mechanisms.

Nyala has increasingly become a central RSF stronghold and serves as the administrative centre of the group’s parallel governing structure led by Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo.

Local sources also reported arrests carried out by RSF intelligence units targeting traders and civilians in central markets and transport hubs.
Pro-RSF platforms claimed that air defence units shot down a military drone over the city, circulating videos of wreckage allegedly linked to the incident, though independent verification remains unavailable.
Nyala has become increasingly strategic in the conflict due to its role as a logistics and supply hub for RSF operations in Darfur, with routes linking the city to neighbouring Chad, Libya, and Somalia.

*ICRC Launches Humanitarian Flights from Jeddah to Accelerate Aid Access to Sudan
Jeddah, May 14, 2026 - The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) has begun operating humanitarian flights from King Abdulaziz International Airport in Jeddah, in a move aimed at strengthening relief operations and improving access to conflict-affected populations in Sudan.
The new air bridge is intended to speed up the deployment of humanitarian staff and supplies as needs continue to rise across multiple regions of the country, where ongoing conflict has severely constrained aid delivery and movement.
According to the organization, the decision to operate from Jeddah-closer to Sudan than its previous staging point in Djibouti-will significantly reduce travel time and improve logistical flexibility. The location allows flights to reach Port Sudan in approximately one hour, enabling faster rotations of personnel and more responsive emergency operations.
Humanitarian officials say aid agencies continue to face major obstacles in accessing vulnerable communities, as insecurity, damaged infrastructure, and administrative constraints hinder field operations across several states.
ICRC representatives emphasized that the new arrangement will enhance operational efficiency and ensure more consistent support to affected populations. They noted that maintaining a rapid and sustainable presence near crisis zones is critical as humanitarian needs continue to escalate.
The organization also expressed appreciation to both Saudi Arabian authorities and Sudanese officials for facilitating the logistics required to establish the new flight corridor.
The ICRC has been active in Sudan since 1978, working in coordination with the Sudanese Red Crescent Society. Its activities include healthcare support, rehabilitation of water infrastructure, family reunification services, and promotion of international humanitarian law in conflict-affected areas.
Conclusion
The situation in Sudan between May 1 and 16, 2026, reflects a country caught in a precarious balancing act between severe conflict-driven devastation and fragile pockets of infrastructural and economic recovery.
On the political and military front, the conflict has entered a deeply complex phase. While the national army has managed strategic recaptures-such as retaking the town of Al-Kaily in Blue Nile State -it faces an increasingly massive paramilitary adversary, with the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) expanding their ranks to an estimated 450,000 fighters. This swelling military footprint, combined with shifting alliances like the RSF’s joint operations with the SPLM-N , threatens to further entrench what political observers warn is a dangerous drift toward parallel "militia rule" and the fracturing of state institutions. Furthermore, the conflict is increasingly taking on a volatile geopolitical dimension. High-level accusations by Khartoum against external actors like the UAE and Ethiopia regarding cross-border drone logistics have triggered ambassador recalls and a military reinforcement of international borders , risking a broader regional escalation.
Humanitarily, the civilian population continues to bear a catastrophic burden. The rapid proliferation of advanced drone warfare has emerged as the leading driver of civilian deaths, rendering markets, neighborhoods, and vital infrastructure highly unsafe. This technological escalation is compounded by widespread ground hazards; despite continuous clearance operations by organizations like UNMAS, anti-personnel and anti-vehicle landmines heavily contaminate urban and rural landscapes. This creates an active threat for the nearly 1.8 million displaced civilians attempting to return to the capital region. Concurrently, widespread documentation of gender-based violence , ethnic exclusion in aid delivery , and the forced recruitment of children have triggered intense global scrutiny. While international bodies push for war crimes investigations through frameworks like the "Banjul Declaration" and the U.S. House Committee recognizes genocidal acts in Darfur , the transitional government’s rejection of external legal mandates underscores the deep impasse surrounding international accountability and justice.
