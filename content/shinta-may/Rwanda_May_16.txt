*Rwanda Launches Insurance Scheme for Greenhouse Farmers
Kigali, May 7, 2026 - The Rwanda Agriculture and Livestock Board (RAB), through the Government-backed Tekana Urishingiwe Muhinzi Mworozi insurance program, has introduced a new insurance scheme designed specifically for greenhouse crop farmers in a move aimed at strengthening agricultural resilience and boosting productivity.
The initiative, officially launched in Kigali on May 7, 2026, brought together more than 120 greenhouse farmers and stakeholders from across the agricultural sector.
The new insurance package expands the existing crop and livestock insurance program introduced in 2019, under which farmers contribute 60% of insurance costs while the Government subsidizes the remaining 40%.
Previously, the insurance program covered selected crops such as rice, maize, potatoes, cassava, soybeans, beans, millet and pepper, along with livestock including chickens, pigs and fish. However, greenhouse farming had remained outside the scheme despite its growing importance in Rwanda’s agricultural transformation agenda.
Greenhouse farming is widely recognized for its high productivity and efficient land use, allowing farmers to harvest large quantities from relatively small areas while reducing exposure to pests and diseases. However, it also requires substantial investment in infrastructure, irrigation systems and specialized equipment.
Joseph Museruka, Director of the Tekana Urishingiwe Muhinzi Mworozi Program at RAB, said the new insurance product was introduced in response to long-standing requests from greenhouse farmers who face significant financial risks from natural disasters such as storms and floods.
“We introduced this insurance because greenhouse farming involves heavy investments. Some farmers invest between 30 and 40 million francs, and weather-related disasters can cause devastating losses,” he said.
According to RAB, the new insurance scheme aligns with the Government’s agricultural transformation strategy for 2024-2029, which aims to increase land dedicated to export-oriented crops from 79,000 hectares to 97,000 hectares.
Farmers welcomed the initiative, describing it as a major step toward protecting investments and ensuring sustainability in modern agriculture.
Cécile Nyiransabimana, a greenhouse farmer from Nyanza District who grows tomatoes, cucumbers and peppers, recalled how strong winds once destroyed protective coverings on her greenhouse, damaging crops that were nearly ready for harvest.
“The peppers quickly dried up after the greenhouse was damaged, and we suffered major losses,” she said.
Mukandayisenga Donathille, production and marketing officer at the Rwanda Greenhouse Farmers Cooperative in Rwamagana, said several greenhouses owned by the cooperative had previously been destroyed by wind, forcing some farmers to abandon the business due to lack of insurance coverage.
Patrice Rusangwanwa, director of the Muyanza Green Fresh Cooperative in Rulindo District, noted that greenhouse farming remains highly profitable despite the high capital required to establish the infrastructure.
He explained that a greenhouse measuring 8 by 30 meters costs approximately 11 million Rwandan francs, while larger structures can cost nearly double that amount.
The new insurance package will allow farmers to insure not only crops but also greenhouse structures, irrigation systems and transportation of produce, depending on individual needs and investment capacity.
Under the Tekana Urishingiwe program, insured farmers who experience losses are compensated within 30 days after reporting claims through participating insurance companies including BK Insurance, Radiant, SONARWA and Old Mutual.
Since its establishment in 2019, the Government-backed insurance initiative has supported more than 200,000 farmers and livestock keepers annually.
To date, over 9.3 billion Rwandan francs have been paid in compensation, including more than 5.2 billion francs to crop farmers and 4.1 billion francs to livestock keepers. The Government has also contributed over 7 billion francs in subsidies to support the program.

*Rwanda’s State Assets and Finances Surpass Rwf22 Trillion
Kigali, May 8, 2026 - Rwanda’s total state assets and finances have exceeded Rwf22 trillion, according to the 2024/2025 report presented by the Office of the Auditor General of State Finances.
The audit, conducted using international accounting standards, assessed the value of government-owned assets and financial resources to determine the country’s overall financial position.

Despite the progress, the Auditor General called for greater attention to project implementation, service delivery, proper use of government equipment, and enforcement of audit recommendations.
The report further disclosed that Rwanda currently has 436 unused government assets valued at Rwf8.2 billion, an increase from Rwf7.9 billion recorded in 2024.
Unused assets include medical equipment, markets, land, buildings, technological devices, animal weighing scales and primary healthcare infrastructure.

*Rwanda Plans to Establish Fifth National Park in Urugezi Wetland

Conservation efforts in the wetland have reportedly led to significant ecological recovery, with the number of sedge species increasing from 71 in 2017 to 351 recorded by August 2025.

The withdrawal, which took place on May 10, affected areas including Sange, Lubirizi and Kabunambo. Shortly after AFC/M23 fighters pulled back, forces loyal to the Democratic Republic of Congo and allied Wazalendo militias reportedly moved into the vacated zones.
AFC/M23 had previously withdrawn from the city of Uvira and surrounding areas earlier this year, relocating toward northern localities such as Sange, Lubirizi, Bwegera and Luvungi.
The rebel alliance said it had requested the deployment of neutral international forces to secure the areas before its departure, arguing that civilians could face renewed insecurity if Congolese government troops, Wazalendo militias, Burundian forces and FDLR fighters returned.
According to AFC/M23, international monitors were never deployed despite diplomatic discussions surrounding the issue. The group also claimed that the United States had continued to pressure it to retreat further toward Kamanyola in Walungu Territory, returning to positions held before fighting escalated in the Ruzizi Valley in December 2025.
The movement alleged that Washington had promised the deployment of neutral patrol forces if AFC/M23 withdrew to positions located at least 75 kilometers from the contested zones, though skepticism remained due to previous experiences in areas vacated by the group.
Residents in parts of the Ruzizi Valley reportedly fled alongside withdrawing AFC/M23 fighters, fearing possible reprisals or renewed clashes following the arrival of rival armed groups.
Local reports indicated that by the morning of May 11, AFC/M23 fighters were still visible in parts of Luvungi, including around health facilities, public roads and sports grounds.
Ongoing troop movements and fighting along the strategic RN5 road have severely disrupted civilian travel between the cities of Uvira and Bukavu in recent days.
Diplomatic efforts aimed at stabilizing eastern Congo continue despite persistent violence. One proposed mechanism involves the deployment of monitors under the Expanded Joint Verification Mechanism Plus (EJVM+), expected to operate alongside UN peacekeeping forces.
The EJVM+ initiative was agreed upon during peace talks held in Montreux, Switzerland, in April 2026 between the Congolese government and AFC/M23. However, clashes have continued across eastern DRC, with no effective monitoring mechanism yet in place to oversee ceasefire violations.
Earlier this month, AFC/M23 leader Corneille Nangaa wrote to US Secretary of State Marco Rubio accusing the United States of favoring the government of President Félix Tshisekedi in mediation efforts.
Nangaa argued that the Congolese government had failed to fulfill commitments made during the peace negotiations, including the release of 317 AFC/M23 detainees and the cessation of hostilities, while AFC/M23 claimed it had respected agreed measures such as prisoner releases and troop withdrawals from Uvira and Walikale.
The rebel alliance also criticized a mining agreement signed between the United States and the DRC in December 2025, claiming the deal had strengthened Kinshasa’s political position while military operations continued in eastern Congo.
*RDF Chief Visits Rwandan Peacekeepers in Central African Republic
Bangui, May 12, 2026 - The Commander of the Rwanda Defence Force, Maj Gen Vincent Nyakarundi, has visited Rwandan troops deployed in the United Nations Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization Mission in the Central African Republic (MINUSCA).
During his visit to Bangui, Maj Gen Nyakarundi met members of the First Battalion (RWABATT-1) stationed at the Socatel M’Poko facility, where he commended the soldiers for their professionalism, discipline and commitment to peacekeeping operations.
He conveyed a message of appreciation from the RDF high command, praising the troops for their role in maintaining stability under challenging operational conditions.
The army chief also briefed the deployed forces on Rwanda’s current security situation and broader regional developments, highlighting ongoing security dynamics affecting the Great Lakes region.
According to the RDF, the visit is part of efforts to strengthen military cooperation between Rwanda and the Central African Republic, particularly in the context of peacekeeping and bilateral security partnerships.
Maj Gen Nyakarundi is expected to meet other contingents of Rwandan peacekeepers serving under UN missions as well as those deployed through bilateral arrangements between Kigali and Bangui.
*RDF Officer Recalls UN Rejection of Joint Force Proposal During 1994 Genocide
Kigali, May 12, 2026 - A senior officer in the Rwanda Defence Force has criticized the United Nations for failing to act during the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi, recalling that proposals aimed at saving civilians were rejected despite clear warnings about the massacres.
Speaking during a commemoration event marking the 32nd anniversary of the Genocide against the Tutsi, Colonel Désiré Migambi Mungamba said the UN ignored calls from then-RPA commander Paul Kagame to allow a joint force involving the Rwanda Patriotic Army (RPA), the former Rwandan Armed Forces (FAR) and the United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda (UNAMIR) to protect civilians.
The event was organized by the Rwanda Demobilization and Reintegration Commission (RDRC) at the Kigali Genocide Memorial in Gisozi and attended by former combatants from armed groups operating in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, including former members of the FDLR.
According to Col Migambi, UN peacekeeping commander Roméo Dallaire had requested reinforcements to prevent the genocide, but the international community failed to respond. He added that President Kagame later proposed the creation of a coordinated force to rescue civilians, but the proposal was also rejected.
Migambi said the international community’s failure in Rwanda later contributed to the development of the “Kigali Principles on the Protection of Civilians,” a set of 18 guidelines adopted in Kigali in 2015 to strengthen UN peacekeeping operations and improve civilian protection during conflicts.
The principles were established to address shortcomings observed during the genocide, when peacekeepers failed to prevent mass killings despite being present in the country.
RDRC Chairperson Valerie Nyirahabineza said the Kigali Genocide Memorial serves as a reminder of the dangers of poor leadership and genocide ideology.
She addressed former fighters currently undergoing reintegration programs, urging them to reject extremist ideologies and contribute to rebuilding national unity.
The ceremony included members of the 77th repatriation group, composed of 245 former combatants returning from armed groups based in the DRC. They are currently receiving civic and reintegration training at the Mutobo Demobilization Centre in Musanze District.
One of the returnees, former FDLR Sergeant Major Prosper Ndayisenga, said visiting the memorial helped correct narratives they had been taught while in exile.
He stated that learning about the planning and execution of the genocide encouraged former fighters to support reconciliation and national unity, while urging others still in armed groups in eastern Congo to return to Rwanda.
*AFC/M23 Warns Ceasefire Could Collapse Amid Renewed Fighting in Eastern DRC
Minembwe, May 14, 2026 - The Alliance Fleuve Congo/March 23 Movement (AFC/M23) has warned that it may no longer respect the ongoing ceasefire process, accusing the government of the Democratic Republic of Congo of continuing military offensives while mediators remain silent.
The warning was issued by AFC/M23 deputy leader Bertrand Bisimwa following renewed clashes and air operations in the Minembwe area of South Kivu Province.
According to the rebel coalition, Congolese government forces launched attacks beginning early on May 14 using drones, KT-6 aircraft, Sukhoi-25 fighter jets and ground troops in several densely populated areas, including Kalingi, Gakenke, Bidegu and Rugezi.
AFC/M23 spokesperson Lawrence Kanyuka also reported drone activity over the territories of Masisi, Rutshuru and Kalehe, describing the operations as part of a broader military escalation.
Bisimwa accused Congolese forces of targeting civilian-populated zones and said the renewed attacks were forcing residents to flee amid growing insecurity.
He argued that the military operations undermine recent decisions by AFC/M23 to continue participating in peace negotiations and criticized mediators involved in the process for failing to react to what the group described as repeated ceasefire violations by Kinshasa.
“It is incomprehensible that mediators and partners supporting the talks remain silent while provocations continue,” Bisimwa said, warning that AFC/M23 would respond if the attacks persist.
The rebel leader further accused international mediators of favoring the government of President Félix Tshisekedi, claiming this imbalance has encouraged continued military pressure against the coalition.
Earlier this month, AFC/M23 leader Corneille Nangaa wrote to US Secretary of State Marco Rubio expressing concern over what he described as Washington’s partiality toward Kinshasa.
Nangaa also criticized a mining cooperation agreement signed between the United States and the DRC in December 2025, arguing that the Congolese government was using the partnership to strengthen its position while continuing military operations in eastern Congo.
*DRC Government Forces Prepare Major Offensive in Minembwe, AFC/M23 Says
Minembwe, May 15, 2026 - The Alliance Fleuve Congo/March 23 Movement (AFC/M23) has accused the government of the Democratic Republic of Congo of preparing a major military offensive targeting Minembwe and surrounding areas in South Kivu Province.
According to AFC/M23 spokesperson Lawrence Kanyuka, Congolese authorities ordered the deployment of heavily armed combat helicopters from Kalemie in Tanganyika Province to South Kivu on the morning of May 15 to support ongoing military operations.
Kanyuka said the helicopters were being deployed alongside other military equipment already used in recent attacks in the Minembwe area, including drones, heavy artillery and Sukhoi-25 fighter jets.
The rebel coalition alleged that government forces intend to intensify operations in Minembwe and accused unnamed foreign partners of politically supporting Kinshasa’s military campaign.
AFC/M23 stated that it would continue fighting to protect civilians, arguing that the international community has remained silent over recent attacks in the region.
The latest escalation follows military operations carried out on May 14 in areas including Kalingi, Gakenke, Bidegu and Rugezi, where AFC/M23 reported airstrikes and ground assaults involving drones and combat aircraft.
AFC/M23 deputy leader Bertrand Bisimwa warned that the group may no longer observe the ceasefire if mediators fail to respond to what it describes as repeated provocations by the Congolese government.
Bisimwa said it was unacceptable for mediators and facilitators involved in peace negotiations to remain silent while military operations continue, adding that the rebel coalition could not be expected to uphold agreements unilaterally.
Earlier this month, AFC/M23 leader Corneille Nangaa sent a letter to US Secretary of State Marco Rubio accusing Washington of favoring the government of President Félix Tshisekedi.
Nangaa argued that a mining cooperation agreement signed between the United States and the DRC in December 2025 has strengthened Kinshasa’s position while military operations continue in eastern Congo.
