*Guelleh Overhauls Government in Major Cabinet Reshuffle
Djibouti, May 17, 2026 - President Ismail Omar Guelleh carried out a significant cabinet reshuffle on Sunday, introducing new ministers, reallocating key portfolios, and dismissing several senior officials in one of the most extensive government reorganizations in recent years.
The changes include the departure of several high-profile figures, notably the Minister of Health Ahmed Robleh Abdilleh, the Minister of Youth and Culture Hibo Moumin Assoweh, and the Minister of Trade and Tourism Mohamed Warsama Dirieh. They have been replaced respectively by Mouna Osman Aden, Fardoussa Moussa Egueh, and Orbis Helaf Orbis, with the latter two joining the government for the first time.
Further exits include Minister Delegate for Decentralization Kassim Haroun and Secretary of State for Sports Hassan Mohamed Kamil. Their positions are now held by Omar Houssein Omar and Fatouma Ali Abdallah.
The reshuffle also triggered a broader reallocation of ministerial responsibilities. Mohamed Abdoulkader Moussa moves from the Environment portfolio to head the Ministry of Communication, responsible for Posts and Telecommunications, replacing Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon. Bahdon, in turn, takes over the expanded Ministry of Agriculture, Water, Fisheries, Livestock, and Marine Resources, while Mohamed Ahmed Awaleh assumes leadership of the Environment Ministry.
At the Interior Ministry, Omar Abdi Said replaces Said Nouh Hassan, who has been reassigned to the Ministry of Infrastructure and Equipment.
In the energy sector, Djama Mohamed Hassan joins the cabinet as Minister of Energy and Natural Resources, replacing Yonis Ali Guedi, who is moved to the Ministry of Labor, responsible for Formalization and Social Protection.
The government also welcomes Hana Farah Assoweh as Minister of Women and Family Affairs, succeeding Mouna Osman Aden, who has been appointed Minister of Health.
Meanwhile, Safia Mohamed Ali Guadileh becomes Minister Delegate for Digital Economy and Innovation, replacing Mariam Hamadou Ali. The latter is reassigned to the Ministry of Investment and Private Sector Development, previously held by Guadileh.
Despite the extensive changes, the reshuffle preserves the core balance within the executive branch, reinforcing President Guelleh’s strategic control over key ministries.

*Djibouti Pushes Ambitious Aquaculture Drive to Boost Food Security and Investment
Djibouti, May 12, 2026 - Djibouti has launched an ambitious push to develop its aquaculture sector, with authorities and international partners unveiling plans aimed at transforming fish production, reducing reliance on seafood imports, and creating new economic opportunities.
The initiative was presented during a major workshop held Tuesday at the Sheraton Hotel, bringing together government officials, international experts, financial institutions, training centers, and private sector operators. The event was organized by the Ministry of Agriculture, Water, Fisheries, Livestock, and Marine Resources in partnership with the World Bank as part of the country’s broader economic transformation strategy under the national development agenda for 2030.
Opening the meeting, Minister Mohamed Ahmed Awaleh stressed that the objective extends beyond attracting investors, focusing instead on building a complete aquaculture value chain covering production, processing, transport, and commercialization.
The project envisions the establishment of a modern marine aquaculture system inspired by the Norwegian model, including floating fish cages across three strategic coastal zones, notably in Obock and Tadjourah. Authorities estimate that the initiative could eventually generate up to 18,000 tons of fish annually, significantly strengthening the country’s food self-sufficiency.
Plans also include the construction of specialized infrastructure such as live-fish transport vessels, automated feeding systems, a hatchery in the Red Sea region, and a fish processing plant within a free trade zone.
Officials say Djibouti possesses strong natural advantages for the sector, including an extensive coastline, favorable climatic conditions, and positive technical feasibility studies that position the country among Africa’s emerging aquaculture destinations.
However, authorities acknowledged that the sector’s success will depend on major investments in maritime infrastructure, cold-chain logistics, processing facilities, and workforce training. Participants at the workshop highlighted the importance of developing local expertise to ensure the long-term sustainability and competitiveness of the industry.
The initiative builds on discussions launched during the Agribusiness Forum held in June 2025 with support from international partners including the World Bank, the Food and Agriculture Organization, the African Development Bank, and the International Fund for Agricultural Development.
Concluding the workshop, the minister reaffirmed the government’s commitment to implementing the aquaculture transformation under the ADEEG 2025-2030 National Development Plan. He also called on the media to help raise public awareness and promote the project’s economic potential as Djibouti seeks to diversify its economy and strengthen food security.

With temperatures continuing to rise and demand for cooling rapidly increasing, officials say the project is designed to protect public health while reducing pressure on the national electricity grid.
The strategy promotes modern technologies and environmentally sustainable solutions, including bioclimatic construction methods, improved thermal insulation, high-efficiency cooling equipment, and greater use of renewable energy sources, particularly solar energy.
Authorities expect the program to deliver significant economic and social benefits. In the energy sector, it is intended to lower electricity consumption and reduce the risk of grid overloads. In healthcare, the plan seeks to improve the storage and preservation of vaccines, medicines, and other sensitive medical supplies.
The initiative is also expected to support the expansion of a modern cold chain system, considered essential for agriculture, fisheries, food distribution, and export activities.
Officials further highlighted the social impact of the project, noting that improved cooling systems could help populations cope with increasingly severe heat waves while providing better protection for vulnerable communities.
Economically, the program is expected to create new employment opportunities in areas such as energy efficiency, industrial refrigeration, sustainable construction, and renewable energy technologies.
By launching the initiative, Djibouti joins a growing number of countries promoting sustainable cooling as a strategic infrastructure priority increasingly recognized at the international level alongside water and electricity services.
The government says the plan reflects its commitment to addressing future climate challenges while reinforcing the country’s long-term energy, economic, and social resilience.

Participants examined a broad range of potential threats facing the country, including epidemics, climate-related disasters, humanitarian crises, and other public health emergencies.
Using the “One Health” approach, which emphasizes the interconnection between human, animal, and environmental health, experts assessed national vulnerabilities and existing response mechanisms.
Officials stated that the conclusions and recommendations developed during the workshop will contribute to the creation of a national multi-hazard risk profile intended to guide future public policy and emergency preparedness strategies.
The document is expected to support improvements in health surveillance systems, prevention measures, and national response capacities for future crises.
In closing remarks, the Secretary General of the Ministry of Health praised the level of participation and the spirit of intersectoral cooperation that characterized the discussions, stressing that modern health threats require coordinated action across multiple institutions.
The WHO Representative reaffirmed the organization’s long-term commitment to supporting Djibouti in strengthening its public health preparedness and emergency response systems.
Through the initiative, Djibouti aims to build a more resilient healthcare system capable of anticipating risks and protecting the population from future health crises and disasters.

*Djibouti Security Forces Arrest 125 in Major Anti-Crime Operation
Djibouti, May 17, 2026 - Djiboutian security forces arrested 125 people during a large-scale anti-crime operation carried out across several neighborhoods of the capital, authorities announced on Saturday.
According to security officials, the operation was conducted by units of the National Guard with support from mobile police forces as part of ongoing efforts to combat rising criminal activity in the city.
The raids targeted several areas considered sensitive by authorities, including West Salines, Arhiba, Maka Al Moukaram, the Riyadh market district, and zones surrounding Siesta Beach.
Officials said the operation was launched on the basis of intelligence gathering and judicial investigations focused on individuals suspected of involvement in various criminal activities.
Among those arrested were individuals accused of participating in organized vehicle theft operations targeting parked cars in the capital.
Authorities stated that suspects connected to criminal investigations were taken into custody for further questioning and legal procedures.
Security officials also reported that undocumented foreign nationals detained during the operation were repatriated to their respective countries of origin.
The operation comes amid intensified security measures in Djibouti City following concerns over increasing petty and mid-level crime in recent months.
Authorities indicated that security patrols and anti-crime operations will continue as part of broader efforts to strengthen public safety and combat criminal networks across the capital.

Conclusion
The period from May 10 to May 30, 2026, marks a pivotal phase of political renewal, strategic economic planning, and institutional modernization for Djibouti.
Politically, the country underwent a major governance refresh. President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh’s extensive cabinet reshuffle replaced several long-standing ministers with new executive figures. This transition was seamlessly executed through a series of structured ministerial handovers overseen by the Prime Minister, emphasizing institutional continuity and an accelerated push toward national development and digital transformation. On the diplomatic front, Djibouti successfully advanced its external relations. This was demonstrated by the reinforcement of strategic ties with Somalia to promote security in the Horn of Africa, a high-level parliamentary delegation exchange with France, and deep developmental dialogues with the UNDP.

The launch of an ambitious, Norwegian-inspired aquaculture drive aims to dramatically boost fish production and reduce import reliance. Internally, public security remained a top priority, as evidenced by a large-scale National Guard anti-crime operation that dismantled urban criminal networks and enforced regional stability.
