The report concludes that the region is experiencing a sustained deterioration in living conditions, driven by insecurity, destruction of economic infrastructure, and a climate of impunity affecting civilian protection.
Kal Akal calls for urgent action by national and international stakeholders, including independent investigations into alleged violations and strict adherence to international humanitarian law by all parties involved in the conflict.

Reports indicate that several convoys have been targeted along key supply routes, including commercial vehicles believed to be operating from neighboring countries. On May 5, a Mauritanian national was reportedly injured after a convoy came under fire on the Nioro du Sahel-Bamako axis.
The disruption has raised concerns over a potential supply crisis in the capital if access roads continue to be blocked or attacked. Key transport routes linking Bamako to Senegal via the Kayes-Sandaré corridor, as well as to Mauritania via the Nioro du Sahel axis, are also reported to be affected.
In a parallel political reaction, opposition figure Étienne Fakaba Sissoko, associated with the CFR Front led by Imam Mahmoud Dicko, blamed the military authorities for the worsening security and political situation.
He accused the ruling authorities of mismanagement, violations of political agreements, and escalating instability, while calling for an inclusive national dialogue involving all actors, including armed groups. He also advocated institutional reforms aimed at decentralization, while maintaining national unity, and suggested that a political transition away from military rule is necessary to restore stability.
*Wave of Arrests Targets Political Figures in Bamako
BAMAKO, May 4, 2026 - A new series of arrests has been reported in the Malian capital amid rising political and security tensions following recent violent incidents in the country.
On May 4, Youssouf Daba Diawara, described as a close associate of Imam Mahmoud Dicko and a leading figure in the “Convergence of Action for Mali” movement, was reportedly taken away by unidentified armed individuals in what witnesses described as a kidnapping-style arrest.
Additional reports indicate that other individuals linked to political and civil movements have also been detained, while some persons reportedly sought by authorities are believed to have left the country to avoid arrest.
These developments come in the aftermath of the April 25 attacks, which included an assault on the residence of the Minister of Defense in Kati and resulted in the death of senior officials, further deepening instability and political uncertainty in Bamako.
*Assimi Goïta Assumes Defense Portfolio Alongside Presidency
BAMAKO, May 4, 2026 - Malian transitional authorities have announced the appointment of President Assimi Goïta as Minister of Defense, in addition to his role as Head of State.
The decision follows the death of Defense Minister Sadio Camara during the April 25 attack on his residence in Kati, which was attributed to JNIM in earlier statements by authorities.
According to the announcement, the reshuffle is intended to ensure continuity in the management of defense and security affairs at a critical moment for the country’s stability.
The development underscores the centralization of security responsibilities within the transitional leadership as Mali continues to face intensified armed threats and ongoing military operations across several regions.
*Mass Graves Reported in Tessalit After Military Withdrawal
TESSALIT, May 2, 2026 - Multiple mass graves have been discovered at the Amchach camp in the Tessalit area following the withdrawal of Malian armed forces and personnel associated with the Russian Africa Corps, according to local reports.
The site was reportedly taken over by the Azawad Liberation Front (FLA) shortly after the departure of the previous forces.
Preliminary accounts suggest that several bodies were found in the graves, with initial information indicating that victims may have been brought to the location before being executed.
The circumstances surrounding the discoveries remain unclear, and no independent verification has yet been established. Authorities and relevant actors have not issued detailed official statements on the findings at this stage.
*African Corps Convoy Moves from Anefif Toward Gao Amid Uncertainty
KIDAL, May 8, 2026 - A convoy of approximately 50 vehicles and six motorcycles belonging to the “African Corps” was observed departing the Anefif area in northern Mali, heading toward Gao, according to local reports.
The movement reportedly took place without accompanying Malian military personnel, and the exact purpose of the transfer has not been clarified at this stage.
The development comes in a context described as a fragile and evolving security situation in parts of northern Mali, where troop movements and shifting control dynamics continue to be reported.
No official statement has yet been issued regarding the convoy’s destination or operational objective.
*Air and Drone Strikes Reported in Aguelhok Area Amid Ongoing Clashes
KIDAL, May 8, 2026 - Multiple aerial and drone operations were reported in the Aguelhok area, in northern Mali, as tensions continue to escalate between armed actors operating in the region.
According to available accounts, four airstrikes attributed to the Malian army and African Union Corps, using a Sukhoi-24 aircraft, targeted the El Youdj area near Aguelhok. Initial reports indicate that the strikes did not result in casualties or material damage.
In a separate development, the Azawad Liberation Front (FLA) reportedly carried out drone strikes against the Aguelhok military camp. The attack triggered defensive fire from forces stationed inside the camp, according to local sources.
The situation reflects continued volatility in the area, where aerial operations and counterattacks have become increasingly frequent amid shifting control dynamics in northern Mali.
*JNIM Attack Targets Supply Convoy Near Ségou Amid Growing Blockade
SÉGOU, May 9, 2026 - Around twenty buses and supply trucks en route to Bamako were set on fire on Saturday in Zambougou, a locality located approximately 30 kilometers from Ségou, according to local reports.
The vehicles, reportedly belonging to multiple transport operators including Air Niono, Diarra Transport, Somatra, and one unidentified company, were part of a convoy heading toward the capital when they were attacked.
Several additional trucks were also burned during the incident, which has been attributed to fighters linked to the Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM), according to available accounts.
The attack comes as JNIM continues to enforce a blockade on key road networks leading to Bamako, a situation that has persisted since the end of April and is increasingly affecting the movement of goods and fuel supplies to the capital.
The incident adds to growing concerns over supply chain disruptions and the security of major transport corridors in central Mali.
*Airstrike Reported in Timbuktu and JNIM Claims Multiple Attacks in Northern and Central Mali
TIMBUKTU, May 10, 2026 - Local sources report that a drone strike attributed to the Malian army targeted a group of migrants in the Inagozmi area, north of Timbuktu, resulting in two deaths and several injuries.
According to these accounts, the strike hit a vehicle carrying travelers in the village of Ingouzami, raising concerns over civilian casualties in ongoing military operations in northern Mali.
In a separate statement, the Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM) condemned the incident, claiming it was carried out using a Turkish-made drone operated by the Malian army. The group described the strike as part of what it called repeated attacks on civilians, though these claims could not be independently verified.
JNIM reported that the attack resulted in the death of two individuals and injuries to another person.
Meanwhile, in the Mopti region, JNIM announced that it had carried out an attack on May 9, targeting a convoy reportedly composed of Malian army and Russian personnel between Douentza and Bambara using improvised explosive devices.
The series of incidents highlights continued insecurity across northern and central Mali, where both aerial operations and ground attacks remain frequent amid ongoing conflict dynamics.
*JNIM Claims Ambush on Malian Army Near Niono
NIONO, May 8, 2026 - The Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM) has claimed responsibility for an ambush targeting a Malian army patrol south of the town of Niono in the Ségou region.
In a statement, the group said at least three soldiers were killed during the operation and that it also seized weapons, military equipment, and other materials from the scene.
The incident is part of a series of reported attacks in central Mali, where armed groups have intensified operations against military positions and patrols in recent weeks.
Authorities have not yet issued an official statement confirming the details or toll of the reported ambush.
*Malian Military Reorganizes Forces in Northern Sector Under Operation DOUGOUKOLOKO
ANEFIS, May 3, 2026 - The commander of Military Region 7 under Operation DOUGOUKOLOKO, Colonel Attaher Maïga, held a coordination meeting on Sunday with Malian Armed Forces units deployed at the Anefis camp.
The meeting focused on updating troops on the evolving security situation, reviewing operational conditions on the ground, and discussing recent measures introduced by the military command structure.
According to the commander, the army’s high command has decided to reorganize deployed forces within Military Region 7 as part of ongoing operational adjustments in the north.
He indicated that this restructuring will be centered around two key strategic locations, Anefis and Aguelhok, which remain focal points in the current security framework.
The initiative reflects continued efforts by military authorities to adapt deployments in response to shifting dynamics in northern Mali.
*Mali and Slovakia Discuss Security Cooperation and Institutional Engagement
BAMAKO, May 7, 2026 - The Minister of Security and Civil Protection, Major General Daoud Aly Mohammedine, received the new Ambassador of the Slovak Republic to Mali, Martin Podstavek, during an audience held on Friday at midday in the capital.
The Slovak diplomat, resident in Abuja and accredited to multiple countries in the region, was accompanied by Yasmine Cissé, Honorary Consul of Slovakia in Bamako.
Ambassador Podstavek had presented his credentials to the President of the Transition, General Assimi Goïta, on May 7, 2026, as part of his official accreditation process.
The meeting with the Minister of Security provided an opportunity for initial exchanges aimed at establishing diplomatic contact and discussing Mali’s security needs and potential areas of cooperation.
Authorities described the engagement as part of broader efforts to strengthen international partnerships in support of national security and civil protection priorities.
*Authorities Report Ongoing Fuel Deliveries to Stabilize National Supply
BAMAKO, May 10, 2026 - Hundreds of tanker trucks carrying fuel were received on Sunday at depots in the Malian capital, according to official information.
Authorities say the deliveries form part of ongoing efforts to ensure a continuous national fuel supply amid recent logistical and security challenges affecting transport routes.
The government highlighted the involvement of defense and security forces, transport operators, and oil companies in maintaining supply chains, particularly through escorting convoys and facilitating safe passage to storage facilities.
Several technical services, including Customs, the General Directorate of Commerce and Competition (DGCC), CECOGEC, and security units, remain mobilized to oversee unloading operations and accelerate distribution to meet domestic demand.
Officials stated that the newly received volumes will strengthen existing reserves and help cover current consumption needs across the country.
Conclusion
The period under review (April 28 to May 10, 2026) reflects a Mali at a critical and highly volatile juncture, marked by intensified insecurity, institutional centralization, and mounting humanitarian and economic pressures.
Security and governance consolidation: A key political development is the further concentration of executive and defense authority in the hands of General Assimi Goïta, following his appointment as Minister of Defense after the death of Lieutenant General Sadio Camara during the April 25 attacks. This institutional reshuffling unfolds alongside a sensitive judicial and military investigation alleging possible internal complicity in the coordinated assaults, further deepening tensions within the security apparatus.
Rapid territorial shifts: The security landscape in northern Mali is undergoing significant and fluid changes. Reports of withdrawals by Malian Armed Forces and allied foreign elements from strategic positions such as Tessalit (Amachach) and Anefis have been followed by the consolidation of control by armed coalitions, including the FLA-JNIM alliance in several localities. The reported discovery of mass graves in recently vacated zones has further intensified concerns over civilian protection and the broader human rights situation in affected regions.
Economic and logistical pressure on the capital: Bamako is increasingly affected by a tightening blockade attributed to JNIM, with repeated attacks on transport convoys and the destruction of fuel and supply vehicles in Koulikoro, Ségou, and surrounding corridors. These disruptions have placed significant strain on national supply chains, raising the risk of shortages. In response, authorities continue to mobilize security forces and logistics structures to escort convoys and stabilize fuel distribution, though persistent threats to key routes remain a major vulnerability.
Diplomatic continuity amid internal strain: Despite domestic instability, Mali maintains active diplomatic engagement, including the reception of new ambassadors and continued international cooperation in sectors such as agriculture, migration, and education. However, this outward diplomatic activity contrasts with rising internal tensions, including reported arrests of political figures and heightened surveillance of public discourse through cybercrime enforcement measures.
In synthesis, while the transitional authorities are attempting to reinforce military coordination through operational restructuring and sustain international partnerships, the simultaneous pressures of territorial instability in the north and an increasingly constricted capital underscore a deepening national crisis with complex and interlinked security, political, and economic dimensions.
