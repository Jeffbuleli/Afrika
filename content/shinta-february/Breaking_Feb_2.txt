Breaking News for Feb. 2, 2026
*Kamikaze Drone Attack on Kisangani Airport Thwarted
Kisangani DR Congo, February 2, 2026 — The civilian Bangboka International Airport in Kisangani, northern Democratic Republic of Congo, was targeted by kamikaze drones overnight between Saturday and Sunday, according to the Tshopo provincial government.
Local authorities confirmed that eight enemy drones were intercepted and neutralized before reaching their target. The runway, which is also used by FARDC attack drones and fighter jets conducting strikes against AFC/M23 positions more than 400 kilometers away, was not damaged.
In a televised address, provincial officials stated that the drones were “equipped with unconventional munitions and submunitions” and failed to reach their objectives. No group has claimed responsibility, but authorities have accused the AFC/M23 and Rwanda of orchestrating the attack, alleging it was intended to undermine the Washington peace agreement.
The provincial government has called for calm and urged residents to resume normal activities, insisting there is “no reason to travel” at this time.
*Journalists Forced Out of UNPC Offices
Goma DRC February 2, 2026 — The National Union of Congolese Press (UNPC) in North Kivu has been ordered to vacate its offices in Goma by RTNC provincial authorities. The building, donated by MONUSCO, will cease operations as of today.
Journalists say the move is linked to AFC/M23 control of RTNC and denounce it as pressure on press freedom. They vow to continue working despite the risks.
*Passenger Convoy Attacked, Several Injured
Rutshuru DRC February 2, 2026 — A passenger convoy traveling from Rutshuru to Butembo was ambushed on Sunday afternoon on the Rwindi–Kanyabayonga road, an area under AFC/M23 control.
Armed assailants opened fire and used bladed weapons, injuring several passengers. No deaths were reported. All valuables were looted, and victims were taken to medical facilities in Kanyabayonga.
Civil society groups warn that such attacks are becoming increasingly frequent and are calling for urgent measures to protect civilians and secure the road.
*FARDC Soldier Kills Wazalendo Member
Walikale DRC February 2, 2026 — A FARDC soldier shot and killed a member of the Wazalendo self-defense group in Mubi, Walikale territory, North Kivu, on Sunday.
The victim died shortly after being taken to hospital. The suspected shooter has been arrested, and an investigation is underway. Civil society is calling for urgent measures to prevent further incidents amid rising tensions between regular forces and local militias.
*Heavy Fighting Erupts in Minembwe
Minembwe, South Kivu February 2, 2026 — Violent clashes have erupted in Minembwe, South Kivu, between the FARDC, backed by Wazalendo, and a coalition of armed groups including AFC/M23, RED Tabara, Twirwaneho, and Gumino.
According to the army, fighting has continued for five days as forces attempt to retake the town, considered a stronghold of Twirwaneho and its allies. FARDC claims to have inflicted heavy losses on the rebels and says civilian protection remains a priority.
Local leaders have issued an urgent appeal to youth to distance themselves from the rebellion and support peace efforts.
*DRC & UAE Sign Major Economic Deal
February 2, 2026 — President Félix Tshisekedi has arrived in Abu Dhabi for the signing of a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement between the DRC and the United Arab Emirates.
The agreement, finalized after two years of negotiations, will be signed at Qasr Al Watan in the presence of UAE President Mohammed bin Zayed and President Tshisekedi. It aims to expand trade across key sectors and strengthen bilateral economic ties.
Niger: ISIS-Sahel Claims Attack on Niamey Air Base
February 1, 2026 — ISIS-Sahel has released images claiming the destruction of at least one drone and one helicopter at Air Base 101 in Niamey.
The group says several aircraft were destroyed and that fighters breached the base, contradicting official statements. Local sources report 27 killed, including three Russian soldiers and 24 Nigerien troops, with 18 wounded evacuated to military and civilian hospitals.
*Kidal, Mali: Azawad Forces Attack Malian Army Patrol
January 30, 2026 — A unit of the Azawad army carried out an attack on a Malian army patrol and Russian mercenaries from the African Corps in In Bogitan, Anfif area, Kidal region.
An armored vehicle was destroyed, and several Malian soldiers were killed, according to military sources.
*Mali: JNIM Claims IED Attack in Ségou
February 1, 2026 — JNIM has claimed responsibility for an IED attack on a Malian army vehicle between Markala and Ké-Macina in the Ségou region.
No official casualty figures have been released.
*Timbuktu: JNIM Targets Malian Army Vehicle
February 1, 2026 — JNIM claims to have attacked a vehicle of the Malian army and Russian forces with an explosive device between Goundam and Diré in the Timbuktu region.
*Two Tuareg Marabouts Executed After Arrest
Niamey, January 29, 2026 — Two Tuareg marabouts were summarily executed by Nigerien security forces following their arrest in the Niamey 2000 neighborhood. The victims, including Ahmad Ag Almouner Ag Madda, a respected elder from Tessit, Gao, were taken by military personnel in the morning and found dead by late afternoon.
No official charges were filed, and the fate of a third man arrested with them remains unknown. Their burial is scheduled for January 30 at Yantala cemetery.
Calls are growing for an independent investigation into these extrajudicial killings amid rising communal tensions.
*Niger: Islamic State Claims Assault on Niamey Airport
January 29, 2026 — The Islamic State has claimed its fighters stormed Niamey International Airport, targeting the main Nigerien army airbase.
According to the group, the attack destroyed the air command center, several drones, military aircraft, vehicles, and ammunition depots. The militants vowed continued attacks against state forces.
*Niger: ISGS Attacks Ayorou National Guard Camp
Tillabéri, February 2, 2026 — The Islamic State in the Greater Sahara (ISGS) attacked the Ayorou National Guard camp, killing at least 5 security personnel and wounding 3.
The assailants reportedly seized armed vehicles, weapons, and ammunition during the assault.
Conclusion
The security situation across the Democratic Republic of Congo, Niger, and Mali remains highly volatile, marked by advanced drone attacks, targeted insurgent strikes on military installations, and escalating communal tensions. In the DRC, persistent instability in the east continues to threaten civilian safety and press freedom, even as the government pursues international engagement and economic diversification, exemplified by the new partnership agreement with the UAE. Meanwhile, the sustained operations of armed groups such as AFC/M23, ISIS-Sahel, and affiliated militias highlight the fragility of regional peace efforts and the ongoing vulnerability of civilian populations. The convergence of sophisticated military threats and humanitarian crises underscores the urgent need for coordinated national and international responses to stabilize the region.
