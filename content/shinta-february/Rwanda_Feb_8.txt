*Rwanda’s Foreign Minister Says UN Has Failed to Learn from Past Genocides
Kigali, January 27, 2026 - Rwanda’s Minister of Foreign Affairs and Cooperation, Amb. Olivier Nduhungirehe, has accused the international community, particularly the United Nations, of failing to draw meaningful lessons from past genocides, citing the continued targeting of vulnerable communities decades after the Holocaust and the Genocide against the Tutsi.
Speaking at the 81st commemoration of the Genocide against Jews at the Kigali Genocide Memorial, Nduhungirehe said that despite global vows of “never again,” mass atrocities have continued. “In 1945, the whole world said it would never happen again, but it happened again 49 years later in Rwanda,” he said, referring to the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi. “Even today, 32 years later, Tutsi and Mulenge communities in eastern DRC are still being targeted because of their identity.”
He questioned whether the UN has truly learned from the genocides in Europe and Rwanda, arguing that hate speech and violence continue to be ignored in parts of the region.
The ceremony brought together international organizations, members of the Jewish community in Rwanda, diplomats, and senior Rwandan officials. It marked the annual remembrance of the Holocaust, in which more than six million Jews were killed under Nazi rule.
Israel’s Ambassador to Rwanda, Einat Weiss, reflected on the shared histories of Israel and Rwanda, describing humanity as a mix of great good and profound evil. He acknowledged the persistence of Holocaust denial and distortion but praised Rwanda for its approach to remembrance, justice, reconciliation, and reconstruction.
The UN Resident Coordinator in Rwanda, Ozonnia Ojielo, called for stronger international cooperation to combat hate speech and punish those who trivialize genocide, stressing that the principle of ntibizongere (“never again”) must be implemented, not merely repeated.
German Ambassador Heike Uta Dettmann noted that while Jews rebuilt their lives in Germany after the Holocaust, antisemitism continues to surface worldwide, calling it shameful and urging stronger action against hatred.

*Rwanda Urges UN to Enforce International Law Equally for All Countries
New York, January 28, 2026 - Rwanda’s Ambassador to the United Nations, Martin Ngoga, has called on the UN Security Council not to selectively target developing countries while ignoring violations committed by powerful nations. The remarks were made during a debate organized by Somalia on international law enforcement.
Ambassador Ngoga stressed that international law exists, but its enforcement is inconsistent. “The collapse of respect for the law did not start yesterday… principles were respected only when necessary,” he said, highlighting that serious violations are often ignored when committed by influential states, while developing nations are punished disproportionately.
He criticized the council for delaying action even when civilians are dying and for failing to prevent ethnic persecution, stating, “Violations of international humanitarian law are discussed rather than condemned. Silence and discrimination destroy trust faster than any weapon.”
Ngoga emphasized that security concerns of all states must be taken seriously, regardless of size or power. “Selective enforcement… undermines the credibility of this institution. The question is not whether the rule of law is necessary, but whether we want to enforce it equally for all countries, large and small,” he said.
UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres acknowledged that powerful countries often dictate what is considered acceptable, particularly during conflicts and divisions, creating a “wall” that influences international law selectively.
Ambassador Ngoga concluded that for international law to be effective, regional and international organizations must cooperate, and UN agencies need stronger coordination to ensure equal enforcement across all countries.

*RRA Gives Vehicle Owners One Month to Complete ‘Mutation’ or Face Penalties
Kigali, January 27, 2026 - The Rwanda Revenue Authority (RRA) announced that vehicle owners who have not yet completed the transfer (‘mutation’) of their vehicles have been given one month to do so, warning that failure to comply may result in penalties, including the confiscation of vehicles. The announcement was made during a joint press conference with the Rwanda National Police, which is responsible for road safety.
The campaign runs from January 26 to February 28, 2026, and aims to assist owners who acquired vehicles through purchase, inheritance, or gifts but have not transferred them to their names, as required by law within eight days of ownership change.
Improved Services for Vehicle Transfers
Deputy Commissioner for Taxation and Communications at RRA, Uwitonze Jean Paulin, said the agency has expanded services to facilitate transfers:
In Kigali, transfers are now available in Gikondo, Canal Olympia in Rebero, and Kigali Pelé Stadium in Nyamirambo, in addition to the Masaka office.
Working days have been increased from Tuesdays and Thursdays to every day except Sundays, and staff handling transfers have been increased tenfold.
Provincial offices will also provide services daily except Sundays.
So far, more than 16,000 people have applied for vehicle transfers: 3,000 with no obstacles will receive services immediately, while 10,000 must first resolve specific issues. Uwitonze emphasized that all applications will be processed and no applicant will be denied service.
Common obstacles include: the previous owner being deceased or abroad, or unresolved tax arrears, which must be cleared before the transfer. Fees for transfers are 30,000 Rwf for motorcycles and 60,000 Rwf for cars, payable by one or both parties involved.
Safety and Enforcement Concerns
National Police Spokesperson ACP Rutikanga noted that untransferred vehicles complicate traffic enforcement. Vehicles may be driven by someone other than the registered owner, making it difficult to assign responsibility for violations or fines.
He added that some drivers intentionally use unregistered vehicles, which may contribute to accidents and hinder police investigations, as drivers often flee the scene.
The RRA and National Police urged all vehicle owners to complete the mutation process promptly to avoid penalties and improve road safety.
