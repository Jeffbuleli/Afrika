*Maxime Prévot Sounds the Alarm: “A Woman Is Raped Every 4 Minutes, a Child Every 30 Minutes in Eastern DRC”
The Belgian Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs, Maxime Prévot, raised alarm on Friday, January 31, following the European Union Foreign Affairs Council meeting, over the security and humanitarian crisis in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.
Describing the situation as a “forgotten conflict,” the Belgian minister highlighted the scale of sexual violence, stating that “a woman is raped every four minutes and a child every 30 minutes in eastern DRC.”
Faced with this emergency, Maxime Prévot called for stronger and more immediate EU action.
“On the ground, the situation is worse than ever, extremely worrying from a humanitarian perspective. This is one of the reasons why, along with other colleagues, we insisted that this issue be placed on the agenda. This cannot be a forgotten conflict: the European Union has a role to play,” he said.

However, he emphasized that the humanitarian emergency remains the top priority.
“A woman is raped every four minutes, a child every thirty minutes.” He therefore urged a rapid and effective European response, particularly through the reactivation of Goma airport to enable the large-scale delivery of humanitarian aid, insisting that “there is absolutely no question of military use of this airport.”

*Washington Hosts Inaugural Ministerial Meeting on Critical Minerals, Spotlight on DRC
Washington, D.C., February 4, 2026 - The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) is at the center of global attention as the inaugural Ministerial Meeting on Critical Minerals convenes this Wednesday under the chairmanship of U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio. The high-level gathering aims to strengthen and diversify global supply chains to reduce China's dominance in this strategically vital sector.
The meeting forms part of “Project Vault,” an initiative launched under the Trump administration to establish a strategic U.S. reserve of critical minerals including rare earth elements, cobalt, and lithium, backed by $12 billion in funding.
Global Participation Organizers expect delegations from over 50 countries, spanning Europe, Asia, and Africa. The DRC will be represented at the highest level, with President Félix Antoine Tshisekedi officially attending.
A Central Player The Democratic Republic of Congo, with its vast reserves of cobalt and coltan, is considered a key actor in the global energy transition. Analysts say the Washington meeting could redefine the geopolitical balance around critical minerals, positioning Kinshasa as a strategic partner of the United States.
Recent bilateral engagements, particularly with Minister of Mines Louis Watum Kabamba, underscore a new phase in the DRC-U.S. strategic partnership. Washington has signaled interest in a “minerals for security” agreement, potentially offering preferential access to Congolese copper, cobalt, and coltan in return for American investments in infrastructure.
The outcomes of this ministerial meeting are expected to have far-reaching implications for both the global supply of critical minerals and the economic and strategic positioning of the DRC on the international stage.

*Julien Paluku Accuses Rwanda of Targeting Congolese Minerals Behind the M23 Conflict
Julien Paluku: “The real FDLR that Kigali is after are Congolese minerals.” During the memorial day organized on Tuesday, January 27, at the Academy of Fine Arts in Kinshasa, the former governor of North Kivu and current Minister of Foreign Trade, Julien Paluku Kahongya, reiterated a strong position of the Congolese government: “The occupation of certain urban areas by the AFC/M23 rebels, supported by Rwanda, has not only a military dimension, but above all an economic one.”
A Strategy of Destruction and Seizure
Julien Paluku denounced what he calls Rwanda’s strategy of destroying the human capital and economic fabric of the DRC. According to him, behind the military offensives lies a logic of systematically plundering Congolese natural resources.
“The volume of mineral exports to Rwanda has increased from $70 million to $2 billion in one year, even though Rwanda does not possess any mineral deposits. The real FDLR he is pursuing in the DRC is Congolese minerals,” he stated.
This declaration highlights one of the central issues of the conflict: the transformation of Congolese minerals into foreign wealth, fueling a parallel economy that weakens the Congolese state and deprives local populations of the benefits of their own resources.
At both the UN and the African Union, President Félix Tshisekedi and the government repeatedly emphasize that the war waged in eastern DRC by “Rwanda via the AFC/M23” has economic aims through the plundering of mineral resources.
On February 18, 2025, during a press conference, the Minister of Mines, Kizito Pakabomba, accused Rwanda of costing the DRC more than $1 billion annually. He also warned of Rwanda’s goal of reaching over $2 billion by 2026 through the illegal exploitation of Congolese mines.
One Year After the Fall of Goma
Paluku's remarks were made during the commemoration of the first anniversary of the M23's capture of Goma. The Suminwa government sought to mark this date by paying tribute to the resilience and unity of the Congolese people in the face of occupation.
The Minister of Communication and Media, Patrick Muyaya, emphasized the refusal to forget or trivialize the war in the East, recalling that remembrance is "an act of governmental responsibility."
The Themes of the Commemoration
The day of remembrance was structured around four themes:
The humanitarian crisis: thousands of displaced persons and victims of violence.
The economic stakes: the plundering of minerals and the weakening of the productive sector.
Justice for the victims: the need to hold perpetrators accountable.
The restoration of territorial integrity: a central objective of the Congolese government.
A Battle for the Future
By emphasizing the economic dimension of the conflict, Julien Paluku underscores that the war in the East is not only an armed struggle, but also a battle for the economic sovereignty of the DRC. His remarks resonate as a warning: without a clear strategy to protect national resources, the resilience of the Congolese people risks being undermined by a perpetual cycle of exploitation, some observers believe.

*Congolese Activists Call for Wider Awareness of the DRC’s Digital Law and Code
Congolese human rights activists, influencers, and bloggers are urging the popularization of the DRC’s Digital Law and Code, following a conference on gendered disinformation and the fight against cyberbullying, which concluded on Wednesday, January 28, in Kinshasa.
Speaking at the event, Ghislain Kabeya Mottomoke from the Ministry of Digital Economy stressed that the main goal is to strengthen public awareness of the Digital Code.
“We have the Digital Code, particularly Article 356, which defines everyone’s responsibilities. Anyone who spreads tribalist, racist, or xenophobic content faces penalties ranging from one month to two years in prison and fines of 500,000 to 10,000,000 Congolese francs,” he explained. “We then move to Article 359, which condemns harassment.”
He further noted that Article 360 explicitly prohibits the spread of rumors and false information:
“When such content is disseminated, it incurs criminal liability. Penalties range from one to six months in prison and a fine of 500,000 to 1,000,000 Congolese francs. These penalties may be combined or applied separately, at the discretion of the judge.”
For General Jean-Romain Kalemba Makengo, representing the Directorate of Telecommunications and New Technologies of the Congolese National Police, public collaboration remains essential in combating cybercrime.
“The Congolese National Police is working hand in hand with the public to stop cyberbullies in their harmful activities,” he said.

*Trial of General Philémon Yav: Informant Confirms Alleged Demoralizing Remarks in Goma
Goma, January 28, 2026 - The trial of Lieutenant General Philémon Yav Irung continued on Tuesday, January 27, with the hearing of four senior military officers as witnesses in the case brought by the Auditor General of the Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC).
Those heard by the court were Lieutenant General Ndima Kongba Constant, former military governor of North Kivu; Major General Sylvain Ekenge Bomusa Efomi, former communications adviser to the same governor; Brigadier General Yangba Tene Dany, former operations adviser; and Brigadier General Mwehu Lumbu Evariste, former commander of the Sukola 2 South operational sector in South Kivu.
The judges questioned the witnesses about two messages that, according to the prosecution, form the basis of the charges and the arrest of General Yav. General Mwehu was specifically asked about a conversation with the defendant during a meeting in Goma, during which General Yav allegedly told him: “You, General Mwehu, are always at the front. The victory will benefit these Bangala. We won’t see you.” The prosecution argues that this statement was likely to demoralize the troops.
In his testimony, General Mwehu confirmed the exchange, stating that such remarks, coming from a superior officer, could indeed discourage soldiers. According to the prosecution, this constitutes the offense of inciting military personnel to act contrary to duty or discipline, while another message reportedly attributed to the late General Peter Cirimwami is being treated as treason, comparable to participation in an insurrection.
“Your Honor, it’s true,” General Mwehu told the court. “The Commander of the Third Defense Zone did not have an office in Goma; his office was in Kisangani. He often came for hearings or operational meetings and usually stayed at the Serena Hotel. I was returning to the field when I learned he was in Goma. I went to see him to give operational reports. As we were leaving the hotel courtyard, he made that statement. I did not react, and we moved on to another topic.”
General Yav Irung is suspected of having links to the inner circle of James Kabarebe. Reassigned to eastern DRC in 2020, he was appointed commander of the Third Defense Zone, a strategic military area covering North Kivu, South Kivu, Ituri, Maniema, and Tshopo. His trial comes nearly three years after his arrest in September 2022.
Nicknamed “The Tiger,” General Yav is among officers from the Greater Katanga region whose arrests have been cited by some political figures, including former President Joseph Kabila, as evidence of marginalization under President Félix Tshisekedi. The Congolese army has consistently rejected these claims, insisting that military justice operates strictly within the framework of the law and without ethnic considerations.

*New Ceasefire Monitoring and Verification Mechanism Explained
DRC-AFC/M23 February 3, 2026 - The Congolese government and AFC/M23 have adopted the terms of reference for a joint mechanism to monitor and verify the ceasefire agreement, reached on Monday in Doha.
The mechanism consists of two complementary components:
Ceasefire Monitoring and Verification Mechanism (CMVM) - the supervisory and decision-making body.
Extended Joint Verification Mechanism Plus (EJVM+) - responsible for technical activities and on-the-ground verifications.
The CMVM is composed equally of government and rebel representatives, with observers from the African Union, Qatar, the United States, and the ICGLR. MONUSCO also participates. Decisions are made by consensus, requiring both parties to establish a quorum. Meetings are conducted primarily virtually, on a monthly or ad hoc basis.
The Joint Monitoring and Verification Committee+ (JMVC+) serves as the operational arm, tasked with:
Monitoring, verifying, and reporting on the implementation of the permanent ceasefire under the Doha Framework Agreement.
Investigating alleged violations and reporting observed breaches.
Monitoring the security and humanitarian situation in affected areas.
Promoting compliance with agreed security measures and building confidence between parties.
Joint verification teams, appointed by the JMVC Commander, include an equal number of representatives from both sides, with observers where possible. They operate in areas of reported violations, with unrestricted front-line access. MONUSCO facilitates logistics, including transportation and communications.
The reporting framework ensures weekly incident summaries, monthly analytical reports, and, where appropriate, public statements on major developments. All information is shared among mechanism members according to impartiality, transparency, and confidentiality principles.
The mechanism operates within the framework of commitments to a permanent ceasefire and a comprehensive peace process, remaining active until its mandate is fulfilled or the parties decide to terminate it jointly.

Discussions focused on Angola’s role in advancing lasting peace in the Great Lakes region and its commitment to an inclusive political dialogue in the DRC.
The European Union reiterated its call for immediate de-escalation in eastern DRC, an effective and lasting ceasefire, and an end to all support for armed groups. Brussels also stressed the need to implement commitments under UN Security Council Resolution 2773, the Washington Agreements, and the Doha Framework, including:
Respect for the territorial integrity of the DRC,
Withdrawal of Rwandan troops from Congolese territory,
An end to support for the M23, and
The neutralization of the FDLR.
During the mission, Mr. Borgstam also chaired the EU delegation at the inaugural coordination meeting for the Lobito Corridor, held on February 5 in Luanda at the request of the governments of Angola, the DRC, and Zambia. The meeting brought together political leaders and international partners to strengthen coordination around this strategic infrastructure project.
Considered a flagship project of the EU’s “Global Gateway” strategy, the Lobito Corridor aims to connect landlocked regions of Angola, the DRC, and Zambia through an open-access transcontinental railway anchored at the Port of Lobito. The EU says investments mobilized under the “Team Europe” initiative for this corridor exceed €2 billion.
Following the meeting, Johan Borgstam welcomed progress in regional and international coordination, highlighting the corridor’s economic and social potential, including its capacity to attract private investment, create jobs, and boost regional trade.
*AFC/M23 Drone Attack on Kisangani Airport: Investigations Continue and Measures Taken in Response to New Threats (Government)
February 8, 2026 - The drone attack carried out by the Rwandan-backed AFC/M23 rebellion against Kisangani International Airport and other military installations in the capital of Tshopo Province was discussed during the Council of Ministers meeting held on Friday, February 6, at the African Union City complex, chaired by Prime Minister Judith Suminwa.
In his security briefing, the Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of National Defense and Veterans Affairs, Guy Kabombo, stated that the Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC) are maintaining their positions and ensuring the protection of civilians and property against attacks by the RDF-M23-AFC coalition, which have been systematically thwarted.
“This is particularly true of the aerial attacks using kamikaze drones, primarily targeting Bangoka International Airport and other military installations in Kisangani. All the drones launched were destroyed before reaching their targets. Initial findings from the investigation, based on the analysis of recovered debris, indicate the likely country of manufacture, the type of drones used, and the explosive payload carried by each device. However, the investigation is ongoing and appropriate measures are being taken to address any new threats,” the meeting report states.
Regarding South Kivu, the Deputy Prime Minister reported that loyalist forces repelled attacks by the RDF-M23-AFC coalition, regaining control of several villages. According to Guy Kabombo, the FARDC are continuing operations to defend national territory in the mid- and high-altitude plateaus of Fizi, Uvira, and Mwenga.
The drone attack on Kisangani airport, claimed by the AFC/M23, also prompted a strong response from the African Union. Through the Chairperson of the African Union Commission, Mahmoud Ali Youssouf, the organization firmly condemned the attack on airport infrastructure in a major urban center.
In a statement released on Friday, February 6, the African Union Commission emphasized that such an act, which seriously endangers civilians, constitutes a flagrant violation of international humanitarian law, particularly the principles of distinction, proportionality, and precaution, and warned against the spread of hostilities to cities far from the front lines.
These incidents come months after the ratification of the Washington Accords between Kinshasa and Kigali, yet the situation on the ground remains alarming, marked by ongoing fighting between government forces and the Rwandan-backed AFC/M23 in North Kivu and South Kivu. Both sides continue to accuse each other of violating these agreements.
The same applies to the Doha talks, held under the auspices of the Emir of Qatar, which have struggled to produce tangible results. These negotiations, aimed at addressing the root causes of the conflict, including the restoration of state authority and the reintegration of armed groups, recently resumed after a prolonged deadlock and resulted in the signing of the terms of reference for the ceasefire verification mechanism between the two parties.
Conclusion
In summary, the period from January 24 to February 8, 2026, reflects a Democratic Republic of Congo at a critical crossroads, defined by a complex interplay of diplomatic hope, persistent conflict, and strategic economic repositioning.
While the government has made significant strides in securing international support-notably through a strategic realignment with Washington regarding critical minerals and the renewal of AGOA trade benefits-the security situation in the East remains dire. The humanitarian crisis continues to escalate, highlighted by the staggering rates of sexual violence and the displacement of thousands due to ongoing clashes with the Rwandan-backed AFC/M23 and ADF rebels.
Moving forward, the restoration of peace and territorial integrity will depend on:
Effective implementation of peace accords, such as the Washington and Doha agreements, which currently face significant stagnation.
Strengthening national cohesion through inclusive dialogue and the consolidation of state authority in volatile regions like North Kivu, South Kivu, and Ituri.
Securing economic sovereignty by ending the illegal plundering of mineral resources and finalizing key infrastructure like the Lobito Corridor and the Banana port.
Ultimately, while the DRC is positioning itself as a central player in the global energy transition, the success of this vision remains tethered to its ability to resolve the persistent cycle of violence and exploitation in its eastern provinces.
