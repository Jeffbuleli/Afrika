*Massive Human Rights Violations Reported in Azawad and Central Mali – 2025
Bamako, February 5, 2026 – The 2025 annual report of a leading human rights monitoring organization reveals a disturbing surge in human rights violations in Azawad (northern Mali) and parts of central Mali, documenting 374 serious incidents committed amid a non-international armed conflict.
The report attributes most violations to the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) operating alongside foreign armed actors, including Wagner Group and later Africa Corps. Documented abuses include:
Extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances, and arbitrary arrests.
Torture and the destruction and looting of civilian property.
Drone strikes causing hundreds of casualties, including women and children, contributing to a reported 553 deaths.
The economic, social, and environmental impact is equally alarming: over 2,600 civilian properties have been destroyed or looted, and deliberate forest fires have further endangered vulnerable communities. While sexual violence remains under-documented due to security challenges, the report classifies it as a war crime, highlighting the gravity of the abuses.
In response to these systematic violations of international humanitarian law, the organization calls for:
Independent investigations.
Immediate cessation of attacks on civilians.
A strong fight against impunity.
The report urges both Malian authorities and the international community to take urgent action to protect civilians, ensure justice, and restore prospects for lasting peace in Mali.
*Alarming Human Rights Violations Reported in Azawad – January 2026
Bamako, February 6, 2026 –The security and humanitarian situation in Azawad has sharply deteriorated, with a surge in human rights violations against civilians in January 2026, according to a new report by a local human rights organization.
The report documents multiple forms of abuse, highlighting a troubling trend of violence and lawlessness:
10 cases of abduction, enforced disappearance, and torture, primarily targeting civilians.
8 cases of summary executions and airstrikes, resulting in loss of life.
11 cases of looting, destruction, and ransacking of civilian property, affecting homes, businesses, and livelihoods.
Over 10 humanitarian and environmental consequences, including forced displacement, food insecurity, and deteriorating living conditions.
8 cases of environmental damage, linked to bombings, fires, and infrastructure destruction.
Notably, no cases of rape or sexual violence were officially recorded this month, though the report cautions this may reflect limitations in documentation rather than absence of the crime.
The findings underscore a systematic violation of international humanitarian law and a pressing need for action. The organization calls for the urgent protection of civilians, the initiation of independent investigations, and the prosecution of perpetrators.
The situation in Azawad remains critical, with both human and environmental consequences intensifying as the month progresses.

Conclusion
The period from February 2 to 15, 2026, underscores a Mali defined by a stark tension between diplomatic assertiveness and internal fragility. While the transitional government under General Assimi Goïta continues to promote a vision of sovereign diplomacy and national ownership, the realities on the ground reveal an escalating security crisis and mounting humanitarian challenges.
Key Takeaways
Diplomatic Recalibration: Mali is actively diversifying its international partnerships, consolidating relations with Saudi Arabia, Iran, China, and Japan. At the same time, ties with traditional Western partners, notably the United States, remain contingent on compliance with constitutional benchmarks and clarity regarding foreign military presence.
Security & Humanitarian Crisis: Despite ongoing efforts to neutralize terrorist cells, insurgent groups such as JNIM and ISIS-Sahel continue to display high operational mobility, launching deadly ambushes across the north, center, and even previously stable western and southern regions. The abduction of senior officials and reports of severe food shortages within the military highlight systemic vulnerabilities in national defense structures.
Economic Sovereignty vs. Fragility: The government has undertaken strategic initiatives to secure economic independence, including the launch of a Tier III Data Center and the renewal of gold mining permits. However, these efforts are undermined by a persistent fuel crisis and the ongoing extortion of rural populations through "zakat" and ransom demands by armed groups.
Human Rights Challenges: Reports from 2025 and January 2026 depict a grim situation of systematic violations, including extrajudicial killings and drone strikes. While judicial reforms have been pursued, the detention of journalists and allegations of military-involved massacres reveal a complex road toward accountability and rule of law.
Ultimately, Mali stands at a crossroads. The government’s pursuit of absolute sovereignty is being rigorously tested by a resilient insurgency and a worsening humanitarian landscape. The success of the “Mali Kura” vision will hinge on the state’s ability to translate diplomatic ambition into concrete security and economic stability for its citizens.
