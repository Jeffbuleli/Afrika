*Malian Foreign Ministry Strengthens Staff Awareness
Bamako, January 22, 2026 — On the occasion of World AIDS Day, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, in partnership with UNAIDS, organized an awareness and information session for its staff to deepen understanding of HIV/AIDS, promote responsible behavior, and help reduce stigma.

*Head of State Issues Directives to Mali’s Two New Ambassadors
Koulouba, January 29, 2026 – The President of the Transition and Head of State, His Excellency General Assimi Goïta, received Mali’s two newly appointed ambassadors on Thursday, January 29, 2026, as they formally assumed their duties. They are Mr. Seydou Coulibaly, Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary to the Republic of Angola, and Brigadier General Boubacar Diallo, the new Ambassador of Mali to the Republic of Rwanda.
Following the audience, Mr. Seydou Coulibaly expressed his deep gratitude to the Head of State for the confidence placed in him. The discussions reviewed the national situation and explored prospects for strengthening bilateral relations between Mali and Angola, a country with which Mali maintains excellent political ties. The President of the Transition reaffirmed Angola’s strategic importance, recalling Mali’s long-standing commitment to peace, notably illustrated by the sacrifice of Alioune Blondin Bèye, former UN Special Representative.
The Head of State issued clear directives aimed at enhancing bilateral cooperation, while stressing the importance of protecting Malian nationals residing in Angola. He underscored that this diplomatic action must align with the guiding principles of Malian public policy: national sovereignty, respect for strategic choices, and defense of the population’s best interests. The Ambassador also stated that his mission would include informing Angolan authorities about Mali’s ongoing restructuring, the reforms underway, and the role of the Malian diaspora in nation-building.
For his part, Brigadier General Boubacar Diallo, Ambassador to the Republic of Rwanda, welcomed the renewed confidence of the Head of State and received specific instructions on his roadmap, particularly in the areas of security, economy, and diplomacy. The President emphasized Rwanda’s strategic importance on the regional and international stage, especially in security, innovation, and economic development.
Ambassador Diallo highlighted the need to strengthen bilateral cooperation, still in a consolidation phase, notably through the effective implementation of agreements signed under the Joint Cooperation Commission. He also stressed the importance of providing accurate and balanced information on Mali in the face of disinformation, and of promoting economic opportunities for Malian entrepreneurs.
These two meetings underscore the firm commitment of the highest authorities to revitalizing Mali’s diplomacy in the service of peace, development, and the defense of national interests.

*Opening of the 2026 CIMERAP Meeting
Bamako, January 28, 2026 – Under the chairmanship of His Excellency Mr. Moustapha Traoré, Ambassador and Secretary General of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, the Interministerial Committee for Support to the Preparation of Initial and Periodic Reports on the Implementation of Conventions Ratified by Mali (CIMERAP) opened its 2026 session on Wednesday, January 28, 2026, at the headquarters of the Ministry, in the presence of Ms. Fosca Giulidori, Deputy Representative of UNICEF in Mali.
This first session of 2026 marks a key milestone in the process of monitoring and evaluating Mali’s international commitments, particularly regarding the implementation of the Optional Protocols to the Convention on the Rights of the Child and the follow-up to the recommendations of the Committee on the Rights of the Child, issued during its May 2024 session.
In her address, the UNICEF Deputy Representative praised Mali’s steadfast commitment to children’s rights, noting that it is deeply rooted in the country’s ancestral values and millennia-old civilizations. She cited the Charter of Kurukan Fuga, proclaimed in 1236 by the Mali Empire, as one of the earliest international references to fundamental human rights.
Speaking on behalf of His Excellency Mr. Abdoulaye Diop, Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, the Secretary General reaffirmed Mali’s strong commitment to child protection, describing it as a legal, moral, and strategic imperative for the country’s sustainable development and stability.
He urged members of the Interministerial Committee to carry out a thorough review of the relevant legal instruments and to prepare a draft report on the implementation of the Optional Protocol on the involvement of children in armed conflict, as well as the Optional Protocol on the sale of children, child prostitution, and child pornography.

Conclusion
The period from January 18 to February 1, 2026, underscores a Mali navigating a complex duality of proactive diplomacy and intensifying internal security threats. While the Malian government has successfully strengthened strategic international partnerships—notably with China to advance infrastructure and energy projects, and with the WTO to advocate for the national cotton sector—the domestic landscape remains highly volatile.
The security situation is marked by a significant escalation in insurgent activity and mounting human rights concerns:
Insurgent Expansion: The Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM) and Islamic State in the Sahel (IS-Sahel) have demonstrated increased operational capacity, seizing military posts near key hubs such as Sikasso and Koulikoro. A recent ambush on the Diboli-Kayes axis resulted in the destruction of approximately 50 fuel tankers, exacerbating the national energy crisis.
Military Response: The Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) are increasingly pivoting toward air power and AI-powered weaponry to counter these threats, supported by the operationalization of the 6,000-person AES Unified Force, aimed at stabilizing the broader Sahel region.
Civilian Impact: Amidst ongoing fighting, troubling reports have surfaced of civilian abuses by joint patrols of the army and Russian Africa Corps in Kidal and Mopti. Concurrently, political tensions persist, exemplified by the detention of figures like Moussa Mara and Choguel Maïga.
Ultimately, Mali stands at a critical juncture. The successful realization of the “Mali 2063” strategic vision and the tangible benefits of its new diplomatic alliances hinge on the government’s ability to restore security along vital trade routes and foster national reconciliation, all while confronting persistent extremist violence and growing domestic opposition.
