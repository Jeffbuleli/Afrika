Kidal, February 27, 2026 – Reports from the Kidal region indicate a growing humanitarian crisis linked to the actions of the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa), supported by Russian mercenaries.

Conclusion
The second half of February 2026 reflects a Mali navigating a dual reality: a government actively asserting its sovereignty and regional leadership, while simultaneously facing a volatile security environment and worsening humanitarian concerns.
Strategic Sovereignty and Diplomacy
Mali has reinforced its “Mali-first” approach through the formalization of the AES Confederation Year II Roadmap, which prioritizes defense, diplomacy, and development independent of traditional Western frameworks. While the government maintains engagement with the European Union and the United Kingdom, it is pivoting decisively toward strategic partnerships with Saudi Arabia, China, and Iran.
Domestically, this orientation is reflected in new economic directives favoring local products and national companies in public procurement, signaling a push for economic self-reliance and support for Malian enterprises. Initiatives like Operation Sunkalo Solidarity 2026 further underscore the government’s emphasis on social cohesion, national unity, and community support.
Persistent Security and Humanitarian Friction
Despite these political and diplomatic strides, security challenges remain severe. The Mopti and Kidal regions continue to be flashpoints for asymmetric warfare, with JNIM and Islamic State – Sahel Province conducting frequent ambushes, IED attacks, and targeted operations against Malian forces and Russian mercenaries.
Compounding this, reports of water point destruction, abductions, and other human rights violations in northern Mali signal a deepening humanitarian crisis. These developments threaten the impact of national initiatives like Sunkalo Solidarity and underscore the fragile state of civilian protection in conflict-affected regions.
Looking Ahead
The trajectory of Mali’s transitional government will depend on whether the institutional consolidation of the AES Confederation can translate into tangible stability and security for populations in the northern and central regions, while maintaining social cohesion and advancing economic self-reliance.
