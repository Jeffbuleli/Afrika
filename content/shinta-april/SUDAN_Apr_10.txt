*RSF Leader Urges UN to Open Aid Offices in Territories Under His Control
Sudan April 7, 2026; The leader of the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, known as “Hemetti,” has called on the United Nations to establish humanitarian offices in areas controlled by his forces to facilitate aid delivery amid Sudan’s ongoing conflict.
Speaking following a meeting in Nairobi with the UN Secretary-General’s Personal Envoy for Sudan, Pekka Haavisto, Hemetti said he had formally requested expanded UN presence in RSF-held territories to improve humanitarian coordination.
The United Nations has previously rejected the RSF’s attempt to establish a parallel government under its “Founding Alliance,” but continues to engage with the group’s leadership in a limited capacity for humanitarian access and civilian protection discussions.
The meeting between Hemetti and Haavisto took place on Monday in the Kenyan capital, following the envoy’s broader regional consultations that included visits to Sudan and Egypt.
According to statements from the alliance aligned with the RSF, Hemetti urged the UN to accelerate the establishment of offices in areas under its control, arguing that such a step would improve aid distribution to affected civilians.
He also called for an independent international investigation into the origins of the conflict, including allegations of chemical weapons use, and insisted that accountability for the outbreak of war is essential for any political settlement.
Hemetti blamed elements linked to Sudan’s Islamist movement and allied army factions for triggering the conflict, while also criticizing what he described as oversimplified international narratives that fail to account for Sudan’s long-standing political crises.
During his regional mission, Haavisto also met with Abdel Fattah al-Burhan in Port Sudan, as well as Egyptian officials, Arab League representatives, and various Sudanese political actors in Cairo.
The UN envoy reiterated the organization’s commitment to ending the conflict and said consultations are ongoing with all parties to identify pathways toward a political and humanitarian resolution.
*UN Envoy Meets RSF Leader in Nairobi to Discuss De-escalation in Sudan
Nairobi April 6, 2026; The United Nations Personal Envoy for Sudan, Pekka Haavisto, held talks on Monday in Nairobi with Rapid Support Forces commander Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, known as “Hemetti,” as part of ongoing efforts to explore pathways toward de-escalation in Sudan’s conflict.
The meeting in the Kenyan capital forms part of Haavisto’s first regional diplomatic tour aimed at advancing dialogue between Sudan’s warring parties. It follows earlier discussions in Khartoum with the head of Sudan’s Transitional Sovereign Council, Abdel Fattah al-Burhan.
According to the United Nations, the talks focused on practical steps to reduce violence and improve the protection of civilians. UN spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric described the engagement as a “constructive opportunity” to exchange views, adding that all parties consulted have expressed willingness to cooperate with the UN process.
He stressed that such commitments must quickly translate into tangible progress toward ending civilian suffering.
Prior to arriving in Kenya, Haavisto held consultations in Cairo with Egyptian officials, the Arab League, and representatives of the Sudanese diaspora. The mission comes amid a broader expansion of UN humanitarian operations in Sudan, including the return of Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator Denise Brown to Khartoum with a core UN team, marking a partial re-establishment of UN presence in the city.
The United Nations estimates that more than 1.6 million people have returned to Khartoum in recent months, despite ongoing risks from unexploded ordnance and damaged infrastructure.
Meanwhile, the humanitarian situation remains critical. A recent drone strike on Al Jabalayn Teaching Hospital in White Nile State killed 10 health workers and injured 22 others, according to UN reporting.
The UN’s 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan for Sudan seeks $2.9 billion to assist 20 million people but remains severely underfunded, with only 16% of required funding received to date.
*Burhan Pledges Commitment to Democratic Transition on Anniversary of 2019 Uprising
Sudan April 6, 2026; Sudan’s army chief and head of the Sovereign Council, Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, has reaffirmed the military’s commitment to a democratic transition and eventual transfer of power to civilians.
In a recorded speech marking the seventh anniversary of the 2019 mass sit-in outside the army headquarters in Khartoum, Burhan said the armed forces remain determined to complete the political transition process initiated after the ousting of former president Omar al-Bashir.
The address, filmed at multiple military locations, highlighted the army’s historical role in Sudan’s uprisings, referencing the 1985 revolution that removed Jaafar Nimeiri and the 2019 protests that ended Bashir’s three-decade rule.
Burhan described the military as a partner of the Sudanese people in what he termed the “battle of dignity,” referring to the ongoing conflict against the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), as well as the broader political transition process.
He reiterated that the armed forces are committed to enabling a democratic process in which Sudanese citizens can freely choose their system of governance.
“This is an occasion to renew our pledge to the Sudanese people that we are their sons and partners,” he said, adding that the military would continue working toward completing the transition begun in 2019.
Burhan stated that the Sudanese population has historically stood with the military during pivotal moments, including the 1985 and 2019 uprisings, and has again aligned with it during the ongoing war that began in April 2023.
He further said Sudanese citizens would ultimately prevail and reject humiliation or tyranny.
The speech also referenced the 2019 sit-in at the General Command, which ended on April 11 with Bashir’s removal, and the subsequent violent crackdown on June 3, 2019, in which dozens of protesters were killed or injured.
A judicial investigation into the crackdown, led by Nabil Adib under the transitional government of former Prime Minister Abdalla Hamdok, did not publish its findings before the 2021 military takeover led by Burhan.

The ceremony was attended by senior Sudanese officials, including Foreign Minister Mohieddin Salem and Khartoum Governor Ahmed Osman Hamza, alongside representatives from the United Nations and its affiliated agencies.
Government officials welcomed the move, describing it as an important signal of improving conditions and renewed cooperation between Sudan and international partners.
The foreign minister said the return of UN operations to Khartoum reflects a shared commitment to stabilizing the country and advancing peace efforts. He emphasized the government’s intention to work closely with UN agencies under national initiatives aimed at achieving long-term stability.
He also reiterated the government’s position on the conflict, stating that authorities remain committed to restoring order and rejecting external interference in Sudan’s internal affairs.
The minister called on regional organizations, including the African Union and IGAD, to take more active roles in supporting stability and reassessing Sudan’s standing within regional frameworks.
UN officials highlighted the gradual resumption of activities in the capital as part of broader efforts to expand humanitarian operations and coordination across the country.
The return to Khartoum also coincides with ongoing work by UN agencies and humanitarian leadership, including Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator Denise Brown, to address the country’s deepening crisis.
While the reopening signals a shift toward re-engagement on the ground, the broader security and humanitarian situation in Sudan remains fragile as conflict continues in multiple regions.

While reaffirming the importance of dismantling remnants of the former regime, the SPLM-RDF said the ongoing war has shifted national priorities toward ending hostilities and addressing immediate humanitarian needs.
The group also called for broader measures aimed at de-escalation, including facilitating access to education by harmonizing national examinations across conflict zones, ensuring the delivery of humanitarian aid, and improving coordination in critical sectors such as health and currency management.
It further urged warring parties to lift sieges on cities, allow freedom of movement, and commit to halting attacks on civilians and infrastructure, including the use of drones.
The statement concluded with a call for the United Nations Security Council to adopt a binding resolution requiring both sides to implement humanitarian measures and work toward reducing the impact of the conflict on civilians.
*New Political Alliance Launched in Khartoum to Advocate Civilian Rule
Khartoum February 18, 2026; A new political coalition has been formed in Khartoum, marking the first such initiative in the capital since the outbreak of war in April 2023.
The alliance, named the Revolutionary Forces for National Issues, brings together several political groups and movements, including factions of established parties and youth organizations. Its founding platform calls for national reconciliation, the establishment of a civilian-led government, and the disarmament of all militias.
Among its key objectives is the removal of the military from political life and the restoration of a constitutional system, alongside the creation of a legislative council to guide the transition.
A senior figure within the coalition said the initiative aims to present an inclusive vision that addresses the needs of all segments of Sudanese society. The alliance is also advocating for a national constitutional conference to oversee the transition process and safeguard sovereignty from external interference.
The coalition expressed strong opposition to armed groups, including the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and other militias operating alongside government forces. It called for their dissolution and disarmament, emphasizing the need for a unified national army with exclusive control over the use of force.
The group also highlighted the importance of justice and accountability, proposing transitional justice mechanisms that include compensation for victims, reparations, and institutional reforms to address past violations.
Some political parties involved in the drafting process have yet to formally endorse the alliance, pending internal consultations.
Structurally, the coalition will operate through a leadership body, a general secretariat, and a supreme coordination council, which is expected to include representatives from civil society, youth and women’s groups, professional sectors, and religious organizations.
The alliance underscored that regional and international actors should play a limited role, focusing on facilitating dialogue rather than directing outcomes.
It also confirmed plans to engage with the “Quintuple Mechanism,” a diplomatic framework involving the African Union, Intergovernmental Authority on Development, the United Nations, the Arab League, and the European Union, which is working to support political dialogue and help resolve Sudan’s ongoing crisis.

*Sudan Approves Economic Measures to Stabilize Currency as Pound Hits Record Low
Sudan April 2, 2026 ; Sudan’s Higher Economic Committee has approved a set of emergency measures aimed at stabilizing the national currency and addressing worsening economic instability.
The decisions were taken during a meeting chaired by Prime Minister Kamil Idris and attended by senior ministers responsible for economic planning, finance, and trade.
The Sudanese pound has continued its steep decline since the outbreak of war in April 2023, falling from around 600 per US dollar to approximately 3,665 per US dollar on the parallel market, reflecting severe macroeconomic pressures.
Authorities attribute the collapse to disrupted production, declining exports, and the prolonged impact of the conflict on national economic activity.
The newly approved measures include tighter regulation of luxury imports, policies to expand and diversify exports, and incentives aimed at encouraging Sudanese expatriates to channel remittances through formal banking systems.
Officials also reviewed the country’s foreign exchange reserves, the availability of essential commodities, and broader fiscal performance during the meeting.
The committee stressed the need to regulate export revenues more strictly, reduce government spending, and eliminate informal or illegal fees affecting economic activity.
In addition, state institutions have been instructed to adopt electronic revenue collection systems and complete foreign trade procedures through a centralized digital platform designed to improve transparency and reduce corruption.
Economic authorities warn that Sudan continues to face severe structural challenges, including high inflation, foreign currency shortages, and a widening trade deficit driven by weak exports and heavy import dependence.

*UN Warns of Life-Threatening Aid Cuts for Sudanese Refugees in Chad
Sudan April 9, 2026; More than one million Sudanese refugees in Chad are facing severe reductions in essential humanitarian services as funding shortages threaten to disrupt food, water, shelter, and healthcare assistance, United Nations agencies warned on Thursday.
The UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) and the World Food Programme (World Food Programme) said they are confronting a combined funding gap of $428 million, which could force further cuts if not urgently addressed.
The crisis comes as the conflict in neighbouring Sudan approaches its third year, driving mass displacement across borders and placing heavy pressure on host communities in eastern Chad.
According to UNHCR, current resources are sufficient to support only a fraction of the refugee population, leaving large numbers without adequate shelter and basic services. In several camps, water supplies fall far below minimum humanitarian standards, while overcrowding has severely strained education and healthcare systems.
The WFP has already been forced to reduce food rations, with women and children among the most affected. Aid agencies also report that tens of thousands of refugees remain stranded near border areas due to insufficient funding for relocation to safer settlements inland.
Humanitarian officials warn that continued shortfalls could push vulnerable families into increasingly dangerous survival strategies, including reduced meals, unsafe living conditions, and heightened exposure to disease and insecurity.
Chad is now hosting one of the largest refugee populations in the region relative to its population size, with pressure especially acute in eastern provinces where refugees make up a significant share of local communities.
The UN agencies are calling for urgent international support to prevent further deterioration of conditions and to sustain life-saving assistance in the coming months.

On February 19, 2026, a United Nations fact-finding mission concluded that the RSF had committed acts amounting to genocide during its takeover of El Fasher. Earlier findings by the UN Human Rights Office documented more than 6,000 deaths during the first three days of the assault, with thousands more killed during the subsequent occupation or while attempting to flee.
Assessments by the International Criminal Court and the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights indicate that war crimes and crimes against humanity may have been committed in the North Darfur capital.
The Sudan Doctors Network warned that the continued targeting of civilians based on identity reflects an escalating pattern of systematic violence across the Darfur region. It called on the international community and human rights organizations to take urgent action to halt ongoing violations, which have displaced thousands of women and children since October.
The statement also held RSF leadership responsible and urged global powers to take more decisive action to address the humanitarian crisis. *MSF Reports Over 3,300 Sexual Violence Cases in Darfur
Darfur, March 31, 2026 - A new report by Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) has revealed that more than 3,300 survivors of sexual violence were treated in Sudan’s Darfur region over a period of less than two years, underscoring the scale of abuses in the conflict-affected areas.
The findings, released Tuesday, show that 3,396 survivors received care at MSF-supported facilities in North and South Darfur between January 2024 and November 2025. The report draws on medical data, survivor testimonies, and interviews with local actors working closely with affected communities.
Women and girls accounted for 97% of the victims, with MSF warning that the actual number of cases is likely far higher due to limited access to medical services and the risks associated with seeking care.
According to the report, fighters from the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and allied militias were responsible for widespread and systematic sexual violence. Patterns of abuse indicate that in North Darfur, armed men were involved in more than 95% of reported cases, while in South Darfur, around 60% of assaults involved multiple perpetrators.
The report, titled “I Have Something to Tell You: Surviving the Sexual Violence Crisis in Darfur,” highlights a sharp escalation in violence following the capture of El Fasher in October 2025. In the aftermath, MSF treated more than 140 survivors who had fled to Tawila, with 94% reporting attacks by armed men.
Many survivors said they were assaulted while fleeing violence, often in front of family members and frequently by multiple perpetrators. The report also notes that non-Arab communities were deliberately targeted as part of a broader pattern of humiliation and intimidation.
Between December 2025 and January 2026, MSF recorded 732 additional cases in displacement camps around Tawila. Women reported facing attacks both during their journeys and within the camps themselves.
At the Daba Naira camp in Tawila, at least 206 survivors received consultations specifically related to rape. Most victims were only able to access medical care approximately three months after the assault, reflecting both the dangers of travel and the lack of immediate services.
The report highlights that overcrowding, poor security, and unsafe living conditions-including distant water points and unprotected sanitation facilities-have significantly increased the vulnerability of displaced women.
Tawila, now under the control of the Sudan Liberation Movement led by Abdul Wahid al-Nur, has become a refuge for more than 715,000 displaced people living in dire humanitarian conditions.
MSF also documented testimonies from 150 survivors in the weeks following the RSF takeover of Zamzam camp, located southwest of El Fasher, further illustrating the widespread nature of the abuses.
The organization warned that the continued lack of protection and access to essential services is exacerbating the crisis, calling for urgent international action to safeguard civilians and support survivors.

*Sudanese Forces Accused of Torture and Arbitrary Detention, Rights Group Says

In response, the Attorney General’s office rejected allegations of widespread arbitrary detention and deaths in custody. The report also indicates that individuals from Darfur have been disproportionately targeted, with some accused of collaborating with the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), which has its origins in the region. Witnesses described cases in which detainees were beaten and accused of bringing the conflict to the capital.
Humanitarian workers have also reportedly been affected. Local volunteers providing aid services, including community kitchens, were subjected to interrogation and detention. In one case, an aid worker in East Khartoum was held for more than two weeks and questioned about funding sources following the army’s recapture of the area.
Despite a January 2026 directive by military leader Abdel Fattah al-Burhan ordering the release of hundreds of women from a prison in Omdurman, rights monitors indicated that many remain in detention without due process. The report further notes that at least 25 women have been sentenced to death on charges of collaboration.
HRW documented cases of deaths resulting from alleged torture in custody and reported that several individuals remain forcibly disappeared.
The organization called on Sudanese authorities to grant full access to international investigators, including United Nations and African Union fact-finding missions. However, the government has maintained that international mechanisms are unnecessary, stating that domestic investigations are ongoing.

Humanitarian conditions have worsened due to continued attacks on infrastructure. However, humanitarian agencies report that 65% of people in need are unable to access healthcare due to cost barriers and limited service availability.
The United Nations has reiterated its call for an immediate cessation of hostilities and unimpeded humanitarian access. The Secretary-General’s Personal Envoy for Sudan, Pekka Haavisto, is currently in Nairobi holding consultations aimed at addressing the worsening conflict and humanitarian crisis.

Conclusion
The situation in Sudan as of April 10, 2026 is defined by a stark contradiction: cautious diplomatic re-engagement on one hand, and escalating, increasingly technologized warfare alongside a deepening humanitarian collapse on the other.
While the reopening of the United Nations headquarters in Khartoum signals a symbolic return of international presence and renewed engagement, and while regional consultations and emerging political coalitions suggest attempts to shape a post-conflict trajectory, these developments remain largely disconnected from conditions on the ground.
Diplomatic re-engagement amid political fragmentation
The UN’s return to Khartoum marks a notable shift toward in-country coordination after nearly three years of remote operations from Port Sudan. At the political level, new civilian-oriented alliances continue to emerge, including coalitions advocating for transitional governance and civilian rule. However, these efforts are fragmented, often competing, and shaped by internal divisions-such as disputes over institutional reforms and the reactivation of legacy anti-corruption bodies.
At the same time, Sudan’s rival power centers remain entrenched. The military leadership publicly reiterates commitments to a democratic transition, while the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) seek parallel legitimacy, including proposals for independent UN humanitarian offices in territories under their control-an approach that highlights the ongoing contest over sovereignty and recognition.
Escalation of drone warfare and foreign involvement
The conflict has increasingly shifted toward high-intensity, technology-driven warfare. Drone strikes and loitering munitions have become central instruments of combat, with hundreds of civilian deaths reported in the first months of 2026 alone.
Civilian infrastructure has been repeatedly targeted, including hospitals, energy facilities, markets, and residential areas. Notable incidents include strikes on medical facilities in White Nile State and mass casualty attacks in civilian gatherings, underscoring a pattern of attacks that continues to erode basic protections for non-combatants.
This escalation is further complicated by the growing internationalization of the conflict. Military sources and investigations indicate the presence of hundreds of foreign fighters supporting the RSF in roles involving drone operations, artillery systems, logistics, and technical command functions. These transnational networks have expanded the operational capacity of armed groups and widened the geographic footprint of the war.
Humanitarian and economic collapse
At the humanitarian level, Sudan faces a rapidly deteriorating emergency. The 2026 response plan remains severely underfunded despite tens of millions in need, while neighboring countries such as Chad are hosting over a million Sudanese refugees under increasingly unsustainable conditions.
Reports from humanitarian and human rights organizations document widespread abuses, including mass displacement, sexual violence, attacks on healthcare systems, and arbitrary detention. In parallel, the economic situation continues to deteriorate sharply, with the national currency collapsing and authorities introducing emergency stabilization measures aimed at containing inflation and restoring basic fiscal control.
Overall trajectory
Sudan is increasingly trapped in a cycle where diplomatic initiatives and political restructuring efforts are outpaced by the intensity and sophistication of ongoing conflict. The expansion of drone warfare, the involvement of foreign fighters, and the systematic targeting of civilian infrastructure point to a conflict that is becoming both more complex and more difficult to contain.
Without a sustained and enforceable international framework to protect humanitarian corridors, restrict attacks on civilian infrastructure, and support a credible political settlement, the risk of long-term state fragmentation and social collapse continues to rise.
