*Study in Uganda Documents Rare Intergroup Violence Among Chimpanzees in Kibale National Park
Uganda April 10, 2026 - Primatologists studying long-term chimpanzee behavior in Kibale National Park have reported a rare and prolonged episode of lethal conflict between two chimpanzee communities that had previously coexisted peacefully for years.
The findings, published by researchers including University of Michigan anthropologist John Mitani and University of Texas researcher Aaron Sandel, document a breakdown in social relations that escalated into sustained intergroup violence beginning around 2015.
According to the study, the two chimpanzee communities gradually shifted from cooperative coexistence to hostility, with repeated violent encounters leading to a significant number of deaths over several years. Researchers estimate that dozens of individuals were killed during the conflict, including juveniles.
Mitani, who has studied the groups for more than two decades, described the transformation as unexpected, noting that long-standing social tolerance between the communities deteriorated rapidly. Sandel similarly observed that the groups had effectively separated by 2018 following years of escalating aggression.
The researchers report that the conflict involved coordinated group attacks and highly aggressive behavior, including assaults on vulnerable individuals, which contributed to a marked population decline in one of the communities and growth in the other.
They emphasize that such intergroup violence, while rare, has been documented in chimpanzees and may reflect deep evolutionary roots of territoriality and competition among social primates.
The study is being cited in broader scientific discussions about primate behavior, cooperation, and conflict, with researchers noting that it may offer insight into the evolutionary origins of organized group violence.
While drawing comparisons to human conflict is scientifically sensitive, the authors stress that the findings highlight both the complexity of chimpanzee societies and the risks of intergroup competition in species with structured social organization.

*Uganda Maintains Lower Fuel Prices Than Regional Neighbours Despite Global Oil Shock
Kampala April 15, 2026 - Uganda continues to record comparatively lower fuel prices than several East African countries, even as a global surge in crude oil prices and geopolitical tensions drive up pump costs across the region.
International oil markets have remained under pressure in recent weeks, with crude prices exceeding $100 per barrel amid supply disruptions and heightened instability in the Middle East since late February 2026. The situation has been further aggravated by rising freight and insurance costs affecting global fuel shipments.
Despite these challenges, Uganda remains slightly more affordable at the pump compared to its regional peers, including Kenya, Rwanda, and Burundi, though it remains dependent on fuel imports routed through the Kenyan corridor.
Regional price comparison
In Kenya, petrol averages about Shs5,871 per litre, with diesel at similar levels. In contrast, Uganda’s petrol prices stand at approximately Shs5,244 per litre, while diesel averages around Shs5,085, although prices in parts of Kampala have reportedly reached about Shs5,600.
In Rwanda, petrol is priced at around Shs5,814 and diesel at Shs5,558, while Tanzania records averages of Shs5,415 for petrol and Shs5,387 for diesel. Ethiopia remains significantly lower, with petrol at roughly Shs3,363 and diesel at Shs3,848. Burundi reports average prices of about Shs4,959 for petrol and Shs4,874 for diesel.
Structural reforms and supply chain changes
Uganda’s relatively lower prices have been partly attributed to reforms in its fuel importation system. In 2024, authorities implemented the Petroleum Supply (Amendment) Act, 2023, which assigned the Uganda National Oil Company (UNOC) sole responsibility for fuel imports.
The reform reduced reliance on intermediaries in Kenya, a system previously criticised for adding costs through middlemen margins. Uganda now directly sources fuel from international suppliers under long-term contracts, while still relying on regional infrastructure for transportation.
Taxation differences across East Africa also continue to shape retail prices. Kenya applies relatively higher fuel taxes, including a 16% Value Added Tax alongside excise duties and other levies, contributing significantly to higher pump prices. Uganda’s tax regime remains comparatively lower, helping cushion consumers against global shocks.
In Rwanda, fuel pricing is tightly regulated, with the government periodically setting maximum retail prices and occasionally subsidising costs to stabilise the market during global price spikes.
Supply outlook and government assurances
Ugandan authorities have sought to reassure the public over fuel availability, noting that additional shipments are expected to strengthen national reserves.
The Uganda National Oil Company confirmed that about 119 million litres of fuel were expected to arrive through Mombasa on Wednesday to boost national stock levels.
“This shipment will further strengthen national fuel reserves and ensure continued availability across the country,” UNOC said.
The developments underscore how global market volatility, national procurement systems, taxation policies, and regional logistics continue to shape fuel price dynamics across East Africa.

*Three Killed, One Injured in Night Attack in Adjumani as Police Launch Investigation
Adjumani April 11, 2026 - Police in Adjumani District are investigating a deadly overnight attack that left three people dead and one seriously injured in Ukusijoni Sub-county, as security forces intensify efforts to track down the assailants and calm rising tensions in the area.
According to a police incident report registered at Adjumani Central Police Station under CRB 191/2026, the attack occurred at around 2:30 a.m. on April 10, 2026, in Ofua East, Rubangabini Village.
The victims have been identified as Annah Abiria (48) and Susan Unzia (56), both residents of Acimari East in Ewafa, Obongi District, and Karulino Mamawi (64) from Onyire Afoji in Moyo District. Police said one of the bodies had not yet been recovered from nearby bushland at the time of reporting.
A fourth victim, Pastore Moroga (50), a farmer from Iyiri Village in Ukusijoni Sub-county, survived the attack but sustained injuries and is receiving treatment at Adjumani General Hospital.
North West Nile regional police spokesperson SP Collins Asea confirmed the incident, saying preliminary investigations point to an armed group that attacked residents using bows, arrows, and machetes before fleeing through the Zoka Forest.
“We strongly condemn this act of violence. Our teams responded promptly, but the suspects had already fled the scene. However, five individuals armed with bows and arrows have been apprehended to assist with ongoing investigations,” Asea said.
He added that joint security forces, including the army, assisted in rescuing the injured victim and have launched a manhunt for other suspects believed to be at large.
Police and local investigators have visited the scene, while the bodies of the deceased have been taken to Adjumani General Hospital for postmortem examinations. A search operation is ongoing to recover the missing body despite difficult terrain conditions.
Authorities have urged residents to remain calm and avoid retaliatory actions, calling instead for peaceful coexistence and cooperation with ongoing investigations.
“We urge both communities to remain calm and avoid retaliatory actions. Matters of this nature must be handled through lawful means as investigations continue,” Asea added.
The incident has renewed concerns over recurring inter-community tensions in parts of the West Nile sub-region, with leaders calling for stronger dialogue and preventive security measures.
Conclusion
The period between April 6 and 19, 2026, portrays Uganda as a country pursuing an ambitious economic transformation agenda while navigating mounting political and social scrutiny. Authorities have intensified efforts to position the country as a regional investment hub, actively courting foreign capital-particularly from China and European partners-and strengthening bilateral engagement with countries such as Rwanda.
These initiatives are anchored in Uganda’s long-term “tenfold growth strategy,” which aims to expand the economy to approximately US$500 billion by 2040 through the ATMS framework-Agriculture, Tourism, Manufacturing, and Science & Technology. Recent milestones, including the Domestic Gold Purchase Programme and the rollout of the National IP Peering Exchange, signal a dual focus on reinforcing financial resilience and accelerating digital infrastructure development.
However, this economic momentum is unfolding alongside notable governance and policy challenges.
The incoming 12th Parliament has generated cautious optimism around potential opposition cohesion but also drawn criticism over its unprecedented size-556 members-and the capacity of a legislature with a high proportion of first-time MPs. At the same time, fiscal policy decisions have triggered public debate, particularly the allocation of 100 million Ugandan shillings to legislators, which critics argue contrasts sharply with funding gaps in critical sectors such as education.
Legislative developments have also raised concern. The proposed sovereignty bill has sparked debate over potential state overreach into private life and its possible implications for diaspora remittances, a key source of external financing.
Externally, while Uganda continues to benefit from relatively lower fuel prices compared to regional peers-largely due to procurement reforms-the country remains exposed to global oil market volatility, particularly amid geopolitical tensions affecting supply chains.
Ultimately, Uganda’s progress toward its 2040 transformation agenda will depend on its ability to convert macroeconomic stability and rising foreign investment into tangible outcomes, notably job creation and poverty reduction. Sustained success will require a careful balance between asserting national policy priorities and maintaining an open civic space, alongside strengthening transparency and efficiency in public investment management.
