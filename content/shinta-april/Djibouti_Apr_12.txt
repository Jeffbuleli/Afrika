*President Guelleh Pays Tribute to FRUD Leader Ali Mohamed Daoud
Djibouti, April 2, 2026 - The President of the Republic, Ismail Omar Guelleh, has paid tribute to Ali Mohamed Daoud, widely known as Jean-Marie, a figure in Djibouti’s political landscape and president of the Front for the Restoration of Unity and Democracy (FRUD), who died overnight from Wednesday to Thursday following a prolonged illness.
In a statement released on Thursday, President Guelleh praised the late politician’s enduring contributions to both political and social life, describing him as a figure who consistently placed “the general interest above personal interests.”
He also recalled Daoud’s role in efforts to restore peace and national cohesion during the 1990s, a period marked by episodes of unrest in the country. “Our fellow citizens will long remember the invaluable contribution he made to restoring peace and cohesion,” the president said.
According to the head of state, Daoud’s popularity stemmed from his “social intelligence and open-mindedness,” qualities that earned him broad respect across Djiboutian society.
President Guelleh extended his condolences to the family of the deceased, as well as to members and supporters of the FRUD and the broader Union for the Presidential Majority (UMP).
In response to the death, the president announced the suspension of his electoral campaign for three days, cancelling a planned tour of the regions of Obock and Tadjourah.
A former minister and a widely respected national figure, Ali Mohamed Daoud leaves behind a large family and a significant political legacy.
The head of state, accompanied by several government officials including Prime Minister Abdoulkader Kamil Mohamed, as well as senior members of the UMP and a large cross-section of the public, attended the funeral ceremony held in his honor.

*Djibouti, World Bank Sign $36.5 Million Deal to Boost Water Access
Djibouti, March 31, 2026 (ADI) - The Republic of Djibouti has signed a partnership agreement with the World Bank aimed at improving access to drinking water and strengthening resilience to drought, officials said Tuesday.
The agreement, signed in Djibouti City, was attended by Minister of Economy and Finance Ilyas Moussa Dawaleh and the World Bank’s representative in the country, Fatou Fall. It marks a new phase in cooperation between Djibouti and the institution on water security challenges.
Under the deal, funding of approximately 6.5 billion Djiboutian francs (around $36.5 million) will be allocated to priority projects, with the goal of increasing national drinking water coverage to 98% by 2030. Djibouti, a country frequently affected by water scarcity, has identified access to water as a key development priority.
“This agreement reflects the commitment of the highest authorities to act swiftly in the face of water-related challenges, which constitute a national priority,” Dawaleh said, noting that the initiative aligns with the strategic vision of President Ismail Omar Guelleh.
He also praised the World Bank’s continued support for Djibouti’s development, particularly in infrastructure and essential public services.
For her part, Fatou Fall reaffirmed the institution’s commitment to supporting national efforts to expand access to water. “This partnership aims to sustainably improve living conditions and strengthen resilience to the effects of drought,” she said.
The agreement is part of the broader Djibouti ICI National Development Program, which prioritizes improvements in water services with the backing of international technical and financial partners.
*Djibouti Inaugurates €107 Million Shipyard to Boost Maritime Industry
Djibouti, April 4, 2026 - The President of the Republic, Ismail Omar Guelleh, on Thursday presided over the inauguration of the Djibouti Ship Repair Yard (DSRY), a major maritime infrastructure project aimed at strengthening the country’s position as a regional shipping hub.
Developed through a partnership with Damen Shipyards Group and financed by Invest International, the project represents an investment of approximately €107.5 million.
The facility, described as the largest of its kind in the Red Sea and East Africa, includes a floating dock measuring 217 meters in length and 43 meters in width, with a lifting capacity of up to 20,100 tons. It is designed to serve vessels transiting the strategic Bab el-Mandeb Strait, one of the busiest shipping routes in the world.
Speaking at the ceremony, President Guelleh подчеркнул the strategic importance of the new infrastructure, highlighting Djibouti’s geographic position at the entrance to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. He noted that the country has a responsibility to provide maintenance and repair services to passing vessels, positioning the project as part of a broader national strategy for maritime and industrial development.
Beyond infrastructure, the project is expected to generate around 350 direct jobs and up to 1,400 indirect jobs, while also contributing to skills development in technical fields.
Minister of Infrastructure Hassan Houmed described the shipyard as a “strategic national lever” to enhance port competitiveness and support the growth of the blue economy. Similarly, Aboubaker Omar Hadi, head of the Djibouti Ports and Free Zones Authority, подчеркнул its role in reinforcing the country’s ambition to become a leading maritime hub under its Vision 2035 framework.
For his part, Arnout Damen, Chairman of Damen Shipyards, reaffirmed the company’s commitment to ensuring the long-term success and operational excellence of the facility.
The inauguration of the DSRY marks a significant milestone in Djibouti’s efforts to expand its maritime capabilities and strengthen its role in regional and global trade.

*Djibouti and Somalia Launch Joint Initiative to Advance AI in the Somali Language
Mogadishu, April 6, 2026 - A new milestone in regional scientific cooperation was marked with the launch of an artificial intelligence initiative aimed at strengthening digital tools for the Somali language, through collaboration between Djibouti and Somalia.
The initiative was presented during a ceremony held at the Jazeera Hotel in Mogadishu, bringing together researchers, policymakers, and academic institutions. Two major developments were unveiled: a research project focused on detecting fake news in Somali-language social media content, and the launch of the Somali-Language AI & Innovation Lab (SAIL).
The research project, titled “Somali-Written Fake News on Social Media using NLP & Deep Learning,” applies natural language processing and deep learning techniques to identify misinformation targeting Somali-speaking users online.
The program is the result of collaboration between the University of Djibouti, through its Center for Research in Mathematics and Digital Technology (CRMN) and CEALT, and Jamhuuriya University of Science and Technology in Mogadishu, via its Center for Graduate Studies.
Djiboutian researchers, including Houssein Ahmed Assoweh, Director of CRMN, and Souleiman Omar Hoch, Director of CEALT, played key roles in the development and implementation of the project.
Officials described the initiative as part of a broader strategy to position the Horn of Africa as an emerging hub for artificial intelligence applied to African languages. Beyond institutional cooperation, the project aims to expand the use of Somali in modern digital technologies.
The research has already received international recognition, including the Best Paper Award at the AfricaNLP workshop, held within the ACI international conference, a major event in the field of artificial intelligence and natural language processing.
According to researchers involved in the project, the long-term objective is to strengthen the presence of Somali in digital ecosystems, enabling applications such as machine translation, voice assistants, education technologies, and misinformation detection tools.
“This collaboration is set to strengthen in order to put artificial intelligence at the service of the linguistic and cultural preservation of the Somali language,” said Assoweh. “Our objective is to transform Somali from a language with limited resources to one fully equipped and integrated into modern technologies.”
The initiative reflects a growing emphasis on regional cooperation in innovation and digital sovereignty, with both countries seeking to attract international funding and build sustainable AI capacity in the region.

*Djibouti Showcases Water Infrastructure and Training Ambitions During French Delegation Visit
Djibouti, April 8, 2026 - The National Water and Sanitation Office of Djibouti hosted a French delegation on Tuesday, including the ambassador of France and the Director General of the French Development Agency, for a visit focused on key water infrastructure and capacity-building projects.
The visit highlighted two flagship initiatives: the Douda wastewater treatment plant and ONEAD’s newly established training center, both central to Djibouti’s strategy for sustainable water management.
Commissioned in 2014, the Douda plant has become a cornerstone of the country’s wastewater treatment system. Initially designed to process 2,000 cubic meters per day-serving approximately 40,000 residents-the facility was expanded in 2025 to handle up to 3,300 cubic meters daily, effectively doubling its coverage to around 80,000 inhabitants. The plant integrates flows from eleven pumping stations, reflecting significant investment in network infrastructure.
Officials described the expansion as a tangible demonstration of ONEAD’s long-term planning and operational capacity in addressing growing urban water needs.
Alongside infrastructure development, ONEAD is investing in human capital through its new training center, aimed at strengthening technical expertise in the water sector. The facility is equipped with modern classrooms, digital tools, and collaborative workspaces designed to support both theoretical and practical instruction.
According to the Office, an ambitious training program has been set for the year, with 58 sessions planned and more than 1,000 employees expected to benefit. A certification process will assess both academic knowledge and field competencies.
The presence of the French delegation underscored the importance of bilateral cooperation in advancing these projects. Officials highlighted the role of international partnerships in supporting Djibouti’s efforts to modernize essential public services and build long-term technical autonomy in water management.

*EU Launches €962,000 Civil Society Grants Programme in Djibouti
Djibouti, April 1, 2026 - The European Commission has launched the “Djibouti Civil Society Grants 2026” initiative, aimed at strengthening democratic governance, inclusive development, and the role of civil society organizations (CSOs) in the country.
Implemented under the Commission’s thematic programme for civil society, the initiative seeks to empower local and international CSOs as independent actors in governance and public policy processes. The programme emphasizes institutional capacity-building, inclusive participation, and socio-economic resilience.
With a total budget of €962,000, the grants will support projects designed to enhance civil society engagement in decision-making and regional development planning. Priority areas include policy advocacy, institutional strengthening, and economic empowerment, with a particular focus on women, youth, and vulnerable groups.
According to programme guidelines, selected projects must demonstrate sustainability, measurable impact, and strong collaboration with regional councils and local stakeholders. The initiative also aims to foster partnerships that can improve long-term development outcomes.
Eligible applicants include legally registered non-profit organizations, NGOs, and civil society groups operating in Djibouti. Both national and international organizations may apply, provided they are officially registered in the country and can demonstrate solid operational and financial capacity, as well as relevant experience in governance, development, or social inclusion.
Applicants are required to submit detailed project proposals outlining objectives, target beneficiaries, implementation strategies, and expected results. Emphasis is placed on inclusivity, collaboration, and long-term sustainability.
The application deadline for the Djibouti Civil Society Grants 2026 is April 16, 2026. Officials encourage interested organizations to submit proposals ahead of the deadline to avoid potential technical issues during the application process.

*Djibouti Coast Guard Intercepts Migrant Smuggling Vessel Near Obock
Djibouti, April 12, 2026 - The Djiboutian Coast Guard has intercepted a vessel carrying undocumented migrants and armed smugglers off the coast of Obock, near the strategic Bab el-Mandeb maritime corridor.
According to security authorities, the operation was carried out during reinforced maritime surveillance activities around the Seven Brothers Islands, a key transit zone at the entrance to the Red Sea.
A patrol unit based in Khor Angar detected and intercepted the vessel as it attempted to enter Djiboutian territorial waters near Assa Fod, after reportedly departing from the Yemeni coast.
Nine undocumented migrants were taken into custody during the operation. Authorities stated that the vessel also carried armed smugglers in possession of an AK-47 assault rifle and two loaded magazines, allegedly intended to facilitate an illegal landing along the Obock coastline.

The individuals apprehended are expected to be referred to judicial authorities in the coming days as investigations continue.
