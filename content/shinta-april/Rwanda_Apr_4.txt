*Cameroon restores Vice President role through constitutional amendment
Yaoundé, April 4, 2026 - Cameroon’s Parliament has approved a constitutional amendment reinstating the position of Vice President, a post that had been abolished in 1972.
The amendment was passed by an overwhelming majority of lawmakers in the National Assembly and Senate, according to official parliamentary figures.
The new provisions establish that the Vice President would assume the presidency in the event of death or incapacitation of the Head of State, while certain constitutional powers would remain reserved exclusively for the President.
The revised framework also allows the President to appoint and dismiss the Vice President, who would serve alongside the Head of State for a defined term.
The move is widely seen as part of efforts to formalize succession arrangements in Cameroon’s political system, amid ongoing discussions about long-term governance stability.
President Paul Biya has remained in office since 1982, making him one of the world’s longest-serving heads of state. The last time Cameroon had a Vice President was in the early 1970s before the position was abolished.

Conclusion
The period from March 23 to April 4, 2026 reflects a region shaped by a combination of persistent insecurity, active diplomacy, and ongoing modernization, with Rwanda playing a notable role in several developments across East Africa.
Eastern DR Congo remained highly unstable, marked by deadly ADF attacks and continued tensions between the DRC government and the AFC/M23 coalition. Although the DRC announced plans to disarm the FDLR, mistrust persists, as M23 continues to allege collaboration between the DRC forces and armed groups. Uganda’s military leadership has also suggested a possible conditional withdrawal from the region, adding further uncertainty to an already fragile security environment.
At the diplomatic and governance level, Rwanda expanded its international engagement through new ambassadorial appointments and strengthened cooperation with regional partners, including the export of digital tax systems to other African countries. Across the continent, broader political developments such as African Union procedural disputes and constitutional adjustments in Cameroon reflect ongoing institutional and governance transitions.
Economically, the region continues to attract investment, particularly in Rwanda’s industrial and infrastructure sectors, supported in part by international partners including China. The growth of digital financial services, including mobile lending platforms, highlights a wider regional shift toward financial inclusion, innovation, and modernization. Rwanda is also increasingly positioning itself in emerging discussions on digital governance and technologies such as artificial intelligence.
At the same time, Rwanda continues institutional reforms aimed at improving governance and efficiency, including updates to electoral frameworks and the expansion of plea bargaining within the justice system. Social policy efforts are also focused on issues such as student mental health and addressing long-standing challenges related to genocide memory and ideology.
Overall, while the region shows clear progress in economic development, governance reform, and digital transformation, long-term stability remains dependent on resolving armed conflicts in eastern DR Congo and strengthening trust and cooperation among regional actors.
