*Security Deteriorates in Azawad as Rights Group Reports Civilian Abuses Amid Ongoing Conflict
Azawad, March 2026 - The security and humanitarian situation in northern Mali’s Azawad region has sharply deteriorated, with civilians increasingly caught in escalating violence, according to the monthly report No. 03/AKA/2026 published by the Kal Akal Association.
The report documents a rise in abuses against civilians during March 2026, including executions, massacres, arbitrary arrests, and forced disappearances. It attributes some incidents to armed elements linked to the Islamic State group, while also citing operations conducted by joint patrols involving the Malian Armed Forces and Russian personnel from the Afrika Korps.
According to the findings, nine cases of executions and massacres were recorded, alongside three cases of arrests and disappearances, including detentions in the Timbuktu region and the disappearance of a shepherd near Kidal. One serious injury was also reported during a military operation involving the Malian Armed Forces, supported by Russian forces.
The report further highlights seven incidents of destruction and looting, including the burning of homes, destruction of water wells, confiscation of livestock, and damage to civilian property. These incidents, it notes, have severely affected already vulnerable rural and nomadic communities.
The Kal Akal Association strongly condemned the violence and called for an end to impunity, urging the international community to pay closer attention to the situation in Azawad. It also called for independent investigations and urgent measures to protect civilians.

Conclusion
The reporting period from March 29 to April 12, 2026, reflects a Mali navigating a complex landscape of assertive diplomacy, structural economic challenges, and a volatile security environment.
Diplomatic Realignment and Regional Leadership Mali has notably recalibrated its foreign policy, most significantly by withdrawing recognition of the Sahrawi Republic in favor of supporting Moroccan sovereignty, a move that has strengthened ties with Rabat while drawing criticism from Algiers. Simultaneously, Bamako has demonstrated leadership within the African Group at the UN and reaffirmed its commitment to the Confederation of Sahel States (AES), advocating for a multilateralism that prioritizes national sovereignty and regional solidarity.
Economic Resilience Amidst Utility Crises On the domestic front, the government reported strong agricultural performance for 2025, with cereal production exceeding 11.4 million tons, and set ambitious targets for 2026. However, these successes are contrasted by an acute utility crisis in Bamako, characterized by prolonged power and water outages and persistent fuel shortages. Despite the arrival of over 600 fuel tankers in early April, supply remains insufficient to stabilize the market against global oil price volatility and local distribution bottlenecks.
Security Volatility and Humanitarian Concerns The security situation remains the most critical challenge, marked by persistent IED attacks and abductions by groups such as JNIM and IS-Sahel across the northern, central, and southern corridors. While the Malian Armed Forces reported the neutralization of over 50 militants in the Ségou region, northern areas like Ménaka and Kidal continue to see escalating violence and reports of civilian abuses. Furthermore, border tensions with Mauritania have emerged following fatal incidents involving Mauritanian nationals, necessitating careful diplomatic engagement to maintain regional stability.
In summary, while Mali is making strategic strides in international diplomacy and agricultural planning, the immediate path forward is heavily pressured by the need for enhanced internal security and the stabilization of essential public services.
