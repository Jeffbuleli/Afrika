DRC, Jul. 31, 2026.
REPORT ON THE SITUATION IN DRC FROM July 16 to 31, 2026
Politics and Diplomacy
Tshisekedi Opens Path to Inclusive National Dialogue With Religious Leaders
Kinshasa, July 17, 2026 — President Félix Tshisekedi agreed to launch an inclusive national dialogue after meeting leaders of the country’s main religious denominations, saying the country needed greater unity amid political tension and the conflict in the east.
Cardinal Fridolin Ambongo Besungu said details of the process would be clarified “along the way,” while church leaders thanked Burundi’s Évariste Ndayishimiye and Congo-Brazzaville’s Denis Sassou N’Guesso for earlier regional contacts. The meeting followed months of dispute over who should organise talks, where they should be held, and whether armed actors in the east could take part.
Ambongo said the exchanges focused on national cohesion at a time when the country faces aggression linked to the eastern crisis. The government had previously insisted that dialogue be organised in Kinshasa by state institutions and exclude AFC/M23 rebels—conditions opposed by parts of the opposition.
*Religious Leaders Urge Majority and Opposition to Back Dialogue for Peace
Kinshasa, July 17, 2026 — After Tshisekedi’s decision to pursue national dialogue, leaders of CENCO, the Church of Christ in Congo (ECC), Revival Churches and the Muslim community called on both the ruling majority and the opposition to support the initiative.
They said they would accompany the process and that a roadmap would be published soon. The appeal came as opposition coalition C64 prepared a July 22 march in Kinshasa against constitutional change and related political disputes.
Church facilitators described the talks as a national process rather than a church-owned project, while signalling that practical modalities still had to be negotiated with political actors.
*Opposition Suspends Kinshasa March and Sets August 15 Dialogue Deadline
Kinshasa, July 20, 2026 — The C64 opposition coalition postponed its planned July 22 demonstration in Kinshasa after appeals from CENCO and the ECC, while demanding that President Tshisekedi formally convene the national dialogue before August 15.
Failing that deadline, the coalition said it would resume street action. Religious mediators told opposition interlocutors that no subject should be excluded in advance and that participants should help define the agenda.
Government messaging continued to refer to a presidential ordinance setting organisational rules, while church representatives emphasised a hybrid format mixing elements of the presidency’s earlier proposal and CENCO’s approach. ECC spokesperson Éric Nsenga said no actor should be placed in a posture of exclusion, though contacts with some platforms, including Sauvons la RDC, had not yet taken place.
*Constitutional Court Clears Referendum Law With Multiple Reservations
Kinshasa, July 28, 2026 — The Constitutional Court ruled that the bill setting conditions for organising a referendum is constitutional, subject to reservations on articles including 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 19, 21, 23 and 43.
The decision allows President Tshisekedi to promulgate the text after its adoption by the National Assembly and Senate, provided the court’s reservations are annexed when the law is published in the Official Journal. Tshisekedi had referred the bill to the court under Article 160 of the Constitution after defending Parliament’s right to legislate during Independence Day remarks on June 30.
Opposition groups accuse the majority of using the referendum framework to prepare constitutional change that could loosen presidential term limits—an allegation rejected by the presidential camp, which says the reform is about institutional functioning. The ruling landed as C64 and other opponents had suspended field actions in favour of the announced dialogue.
*Article 5 Coalition Defends Referendum Path and Court Ruling
Kinshasa, July 30, 2026 — The Article 5 Coalition (C5) defended the referendum process and called for respect of the Constitutional Court’s July 28 decision clearing the organisation law with reservations.
The statement came amid polarised debate over whether referendum legislation is a legitimate institutional step or a vehicle for contested constitutional revision. Supporters present the court review as evidence of inter-institutional cooperation; critics maintain that the political intent behind the reform remains the core dispute.
*Kinshasa Rejects AFC/M23 Role in National Dialogue
Kinshasa, July 30, 2026 — Congolese authorities again rejected participation by the AFC/M23 coalition in the proposed national dialogue, framing inclusion of the armed movement as equivalent to bringing Rwanda into talks among Congolese.
The position restates a longstanding government red line: dialogue among Congolese political and social actors must not legitimise an armed group Kinshasa links to foreign aggression in the east. The stance contrasts with some church and opposition voices that have argued armed Congolese actors with grievances should not be excluded from conflict-resolution talks.
*Katumbi Moves to Restore Cohesion Inside Ensemble pour la République
Kinshasa, July 30, 2026 — Moïse Katumbi intervened to calm an internal dispute inside Ensemble pour la République after public speculation over differences between cabinet director and spokesperson Olivier Kamitatu and senator Salomon Idi Kalonda.
In a joint statement dated July 29, Kamitatu and Kalonda said they had spoken at length at Katumbi’s request and that misunderstandings between them had been resolved. The episode came as Ensemble, a C64 member, navigates positioning around the national dialogue and the coming political calendar.
Party militants had worried that the reported rift could weaken opposition cohesion at a sensitive moment. The joint communiqué sought to project restored unity around Katumbi’s leadership.
*Kinshasa Announces FDLR Demilitarisation Protocol Under Washington Framework
Kinshasa, July 29, 2026 — Regional Integration Minister Floribert Anzuluni said the FDLR had signed a protocol on demilitarisation, demobilisation and cantoning, describing it as a major step in implementing the CONOPS attached to the Washington peace agreement with Rwanda.
Anzuluni said Kinshasa’s obligations include neutralising the FDLR, while Rwanda committed to withdraw forces it presents as defensive measures. He argued Kigali has long used the FDLR issue as a pretext for presence and resource predation in eastern Congo, while insisting the DRC would still fulfil its commitments through sensitisation and, if needed, force.
He said community outreach with MONUSCO support began in October 2025, followed by deployment of two battalions around Walikale after limited results by March, after which FDLR political and military leaders contacted the government on surrender modalities.
*Kigali Dismisses Congolese FDLR Protocol as a “Non-Event”
Kigali, July 29, 2026 — Rwandan Foreign Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe rejected Kinshasa’s announced FDLR disarmament protocol, saying it has no basis in the Washington accords and calling the move a non-event.
Nduhungirehe said the FDLR are the object of the agreement, not a party to it, and accused Kinshasa of trying to circumvent security obligations under CONOPS and related operational orders, including measures expected within 90 days. The exchange deepened the gap between Congolese claims of progress and Rwandan insistence that neutralisation commitments remain unmet.
*Anzuluni Says Rwanda Is Surprised by Congolese Demobilisation Work
Kinshasa, July 30, 2026 — Responding to Kigali’s criticism, Anzuluni said Rwanda appeared surprised by the “methodical work” undertaken by the DRC and was now contesting results Kinshasa considers real.
He said the state’s technical demobilisation structure, PDDRCS, had prepared an operational plan with a timeline and budget that still needed formalisation with stakeholders. Kinshasa presented the protocol as proof it is advancing Washington commitments even as hostilities, ceasefire accusations and divergent interpretations continue on the ground.
*Government Says Five of Six Identified FDLR Zones Are Outside Army Control
Kinshasa, July 30, 2026 — Anzuluni said the parties to the Washington follow-up mechanism identified six zones hosting FDLR fighters in North and South Kivu, and that five of them are under Rwanda/AFC-M23 control rather than FARDC authority.
Only the Walikale area, he said, remains under Congolese control among the identified zones, explaining why military deployment focused there. Four of the contested zones are in North Kivu and one in the South Kivu high plateaus, according to the minister.
The claim is intended to undercut arguments that Kinshasa alone can immediately eliminate the FDLR threat everywhere it has been mapped, while keeping pressure on Kigali over territorial control in the east.
*DRC Uses Security Council Presidency to Link Natural Resources and Peace
New York, July 22, 2026 — During its July presidency of the UN Security Council, the DRC convened a high-level debate on natural-resource governance as a foundation for peace, security and prosperity, following earlier sessions on conflict-related sexual violence and an Arria-formula meeting on resources and conflict.
Congolese officials argued that permanent sovereignty over natural resources must be exercised so that wealth serves populations rather than armed groups and illicit networks. Kinshasa said existing tools on mineral traceability, corporate due diligence and conflict financing remain fragmented and insufficiently coordinated.
The diplomatic push reflects the country’s dual position as a major source of critical minerals and a theatre where illegal extraction continues to feed insecurity, especially in the east.
*United States Reaffirms Strategic Importance of Lobito Corridor
Washington, July 30, 2026 — U.S. officials reaffirmed the strategic character of the Lobito Corridor for American investment in Africa, highlighting the transport link connecting mining regions of the DRC and neighbouring countries to Atlantic export routes.
The statement fits a broader competition over critical-mineral supply chains and infrastructure corridors. For Kinshasa, corridor politics intersect with domestic debates on resource governance, industrialisation and the security of mining zones.
*C64 Calls Constitutional Court Ruling a “Judicial Rebellion”
Kinshasa, July 29, 2026 — The C64 coalition rejected the Constitutional Court’s clearance of the referendum organisation law as null and void, accusing the court of “judicial rebellion” and complicity in what it called a plot against constitutional order.
Ensemble secretary-general Dieudonné Bolengetenge read a joint declaration saying the ruling opens the way to Balkanisation and exposes those involved to high-treason sanctions under Congolese law. C64 listed four red lines it said Congolese should not accept: circumvention of the Constitution, personalisation of institutions, constitutional change, and a third presidential term.
The statement hardened opposition messaging even as the same coalition had suspended street protests to give dialogue until August 15 a chance.
*France Says Inclusive Dialogue Can Help Restore National Concord
Kinshasa, July 28, 2026 — France said holding inclusive national dialogue in the DRC would help restore national concord, adding international political backing to the process announced after Tshisekedi’s meeting with religious leaders.
The comment came as Congolese actors still disputed format, participation and timing. External endorsements have become part of the political atmosphere around the talks without settling the outstanding disagreements.
*Goma March Against UN Sanctions on AFC/M23 Paralyses Daily Life
Goma, July 28, 2026 — A march in Goma against United Nations sanctions targeting AFC/M23 disrupted socioeconomic activity in the city, reflecting organised public mobilisation in an area under the coalition’s influence.
Demonstrators framed the sanctions as unjust, while the protest illustrated how political messaging and street action continue in the east alongside military and diplomatic tracks. Businesses and transport were reported disrupted during the demonstration.
*Muyaya Says Court Ruling Validates Referendum Framework Without Launching a Vote
Kinshasa, July 31, 2026 — Government spokesperson Patrick Muyaya said the Constitutional Court’s clearance of the referendum organisation law validates a legal framework but does not itself mean a referendum has been organised or launched.
The clarification sought to cool speculation that the July 28 ruling automatically triggers a constitutional vote. Opposition critics still treat the law as a pathway to contested change; the presidential camp insists procedure and politics remain separate steps.
*African Union Adopts Six-Month Roadmap to Relaunch Eastern Peace Process
Addis Ababa/Kinshasa, July 31, 2026 — The African Union adopted a six-month roadmap intended to relaunch the peace process in eastern DRC, adding a continental timeline to stalled Washington and Doha tracks.
Details on sequencing, monitoring and parties’ obligations were still being unpacked by diplomats. Kinshasa presents AU engagement as complementary pressure for troop withdrawal and armed-group neutralisation; implementation will depend on buy-in from regional actors and the warring coalitions.
*DRC Prepares to Hand UN Security Council Presidency to Denmark
New York, July 31, 2026 — After chairing the Security Council through July, the DRC prepared to pass the rotating presidency to Denmark.
Kinshasa used the month to spotlight conflict-related sexual violence and natural-resource governance as peace issues. Officials cast the hand-over as closing a high-visibility diplomatic window rather than an end to Congolese advocacy on those themes.
*Kinshasa Spells Out FDLR Protocol Steps Through Dissolution
Kinshasa, July 31, 2026 — Authorities detailed what the FDLR demilitarisation protocol envisages beyond initial signature: disarmament, demobilisation, cantoning, a follow-up mechanism, relocation of fighters and a path through to dissolution of the group.
The presentation aims to show operational content after Kigali dismissed the deal as a non-event. Congolese officials say a technical operational plan with timeline and budget still needs formal stakeholder endorsement, while arguing many mapped FDLR zones remain outside FARDC control.

Social and Health
*Suminwa Visits Rwampara and Mongbwalu as Ebola Epicentre Response Continues
Bunia, July 24, 2026 — Prime Minister Judith Suminwa visited Ebola treatment and response sites in Rwampara and Mongbwalu, the epicentre of the country’s 17th Ebola outbreak, to show state support for patients, communities and front-line teams.
At Rwampara, she observed biosecurity procedures and patient care; authorities highlighted recoveries including a 15-month-old infant and mother, described as the first baby officially cured in the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak. In Mongbwalu, responders listed shortages of electricity and drinking water, insecurity, continuing mining activity, community resistance and weak support for community relays.
Suminwa handed over 10 off-road vehicles to provincial response coordinator Prof. Steve Ahuka and said partner financing now allows a focus on logistics, ambulances, oxygen and safe burial equipment. Five provinces—Ituri, North Kivu, South Kivu, Tshopo and Haut-Uele—are affected.
*WHO Chief Warns Ebola Fight Needs Ceasefire and International Solidarity
Geneva/Kinshasa, July 25, 2026 — WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus warned that insecurity and conflict are obstructing the Ebola response and called for stronger community engagement plus political action toward a ceasefire to improve humanitarian access.
Priorities remain cutting transmission in Ituri through surveillance, clinical care, safe burials and community involvement. Tedros said the Congolese people cannot confront the epidemic alone and need sustained international support and peace.
*Government Begins Paying Risk Bonuses to Ebola Response Workers
Kinshasa, July 28, 2026 — Authorities said they had begun paying risk bonuses owed to front-line workers fighting the 17th Ebola epidemic across five affected provinces, after strikes over arrears disrupted parts of the response, especially in Ituri.
The Incident Manager’s office said President Tshisekedi is closely following the response and noted Suminwa’s recent field visits. More than 12,000 providers in Ituri alone—about 80% of responders—are concerned, spanning clinicians, epidemiologists, hygienists, laboratory staff, logisticians and community relays.
Payments started with teams at the Elikya treatment centre in Bunia, with file cleaning continuing to resolve disputes. Officials urged teams to maintain operations until the outbreak is controlled, while insecurity and even attacks on some treatment centres continue to complicate the fight.
*Cholera Kills 34 in Three Weeks in Shabunda

Cholera has already caused thousands of deaths nationwide in 16 provinces according to recent tallies, driven by poor water and sanitation access and displacement. Local outbreaks in remote territories strain already limited provincial health systems.

*Ituri Military Governor Orders Dismantling of Illegal Roadblocks

*First Human Dose Given in Global Bundibugyo Ebola Vaccine Trial
Oxford/Kinshasa, July 28, 2026 — Oxford Vaccine Group announced the first volunteer had received ChAdOx1 BDBV in the BD-Ebov Phase 1 trial, the first clinical study of a vaccine designed for Bundibugyo Ebola.
WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called the launch an important step and said data would guide next development stages. The candidate uses the same viral-vector platform as the Oxford-AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine; CEPI is funding an $8.6 million programme with Oxford and the Serum Institute of India, which produced a stockpile of about 620,000 doses and supplied trial vials. Further studies are being prepared with partners in Uganda if early results are favourable.
Unlike Zaire-strain Ebola, Bundibugyo still lacks a licensed vaccine or specific treatment, leaving the DRC’s 17th outbreak dependent on classic containment tools.

Conclusion
The events from July 16 to 31, 2026, demonstrate Rwanda’s continued momentum toward long-term socio-economic modernization, technological adoption, and proactive global diplomacy, even while navigating complex regional security dynamics.
Diplomatic & Global Reach: Through diaspora-led Liberation Day events in Turkey and Indonesia, alongside new diplomatic, trade, and economic agreements with partners like Poland, Pakistan, and Belarus, Rwanda continues to expand its international footprint and build resilient global partnerships.
Economic & Infrastructure Progress: Key achievements—such as launching the first phase of the Kivu Belt Water Supply System, optimizing costs for the Bugesera Nuclear Research Centre, and establishing a dedicated national agency for biodiversity conservation—highlight a strong commitment to sustainable development, environmental management, and strategic public investment.
Health & Technological Integration: High-profile engagements with global health leaders showcased Rwanda’s rapid adoption of AI-enabled health systems, paperless clinical workflows, and localized telemedicine solutions designed to significantly improve healthcare delivery and lower maternal mortality.
Security & Humanitarian Efforts: Domestically and regionally, Rwanda remains a central actor. While enhancing security capabilities through international training collaborations, authorities have maintained dedicated humanitarian efforts by receiving, screening, and reintegrating hundreds of returnees from the Democratic Republic of Congo.
Overall, this period underscores a resilient nation focused on strengthening its institutional framework, empowering its citizens through technology and infrastructure, and promoting regional stability.
