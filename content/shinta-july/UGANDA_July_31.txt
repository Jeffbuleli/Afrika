Politics and Diplomacy
*Uganda Nominates Olara Otunnu for United Nations Secretary-General
Kampala, Uganda, July 26, 2026 — The government formally seconded Ambassador Olara Otunnu as its candidate for United Nations Secretary-General, urging member states to support a diplomat with more than four decades in international service.
A Foreign Affairs statement signed by Permanent Secretary Vincent Waiswa Bagiire described Otunnu as a distinguished statesman and former Under-Secretary-General for Children and Armed Conflict who also served as Uganda’s UN permanent representative in the 1980s. Officials said Kampala would campaign for him among Security Council members, while Otunnu noted travel complications linked to regional Ebola formalities had slowed his early New York outreach.
Other declared contenders include Michelle Bachelet, María Fernanda Espinosa, Rafael Grossi, Rebeca Grynspan, Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett and Macky Sall. The next secretary-general is expected to take office on January 1, 2027.

Kampala, Uganda, July 28, 2026 — Health Minister Chris Baryomunsi declared Uganda officially Ebola-free after completing the mandatory 42-day monitoring period that began when the last Ugandan national patient was discharged on June 16.

Conclusion
Between July 16 and July 31, 2026, Uganda navigated a complex interplay of international diplomacy, high-stakes political friction, economic initiatives, and critical public health milestones.
Diplomacy & Security: Uganda positioned itself prominently on the world stage by formally nominating Olara Otunnu for United Nations Secretary-General and Rosette Muzigo-Morrison for the International Criminal Court. Concurrently, Kampala hosted the AUSSOM Extraordinary Summit, taking a lead role in urging alternative, sustainable financing models for African-led peacekeeping as UN support in Somalia winds down.
Domestic Politics & Legal Controversies: Tensions surrounding opposition trials reached a flashpoint with the courtroom collapse and subsequent intensive care admission of Dr. Kizza Besigye, sparking severe criticism from rights advocates regarding trial conditions and legal representation. In contrast, a press freedom milestone was achieved with President Museveni authorizing the reopening of the Nation Media Group.
Public Health Successes & Challenges: Uganda marked a major victory by officially declaring an end to its domestic Ebola outbreak while exporting medical expertise to assist the neighboring Democratic Republic of Congo. However, local public health infrastructure faced criticism over medical waste management in Bidibidi refugee settlement and post-Cleaning Day waste disposal struggles.
Economic Momentum & Innovation: Economic developments demonstrated strong forward momentum through expanded export earnings, Tyms’ landmark partnership with OpenAI, and long-term infrastructure and green-energy projects like the Karuma green hydrogen initiative. Nevertheless, contractor delays on vital road projects and financial losses at Dfcu Bank highlighted ongoing operational and economic hurdles.
Overall, this fortnight highlights Uganda’s growing influence in regional and global governance alongside persistent internal pressures surrounding rule of law, political governance, and infrastructure execution.
