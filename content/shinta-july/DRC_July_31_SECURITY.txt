DRC, Jul. 31,  2026.
REPORT ON THE SECURITY SITUATION IN THE DRC FROM July 16 to 31, 2026
Security and Humanitarian Affairs
*Kinshasa Presents FDLR Disarmament Protocol Under Washington Framework
Kinshasa, DRC, July 31, 2026 — The Congolese government said Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda (FDLR) representatives had signed a protocol on disarmament, demobilisation, cantonment, monitoring and relocation through to dissolution, presenting the step as implementation of the U.S.-brokered Washington accord with Rwanda.
Regional Integration Minister Floribert Anzuluni said the document resulted from talks aimed at answering Kigali’s long-standing security concerns. Kinshasa argued five of six identified FDLR zones now lie under Rwanda/AFC-M23 rather than FARDC control, complicating unilateral Congolese enforcement.
*Kigali Dismisses FDLR Protocol as a “Non-Event”
Kigali, Rwanda, July 30, 2026 — Rwanda’s foreign minister rejected Kinshasa’s FDLR disarmament announcement as contrary to Washington commitments and labelled it a “non-event,” saying the initiative did not meet agreed verification standards.
The clash hardened mutual distrust just as African Union facilitators adopted a six-month eastern Congo roadmap. Congolese officials countered that Kigali appeared surprised by methodical Congolese work and was now contesting progress already achieved.
*Rwanda Returns 39 People Once Sent Through MONUSCO DDRRR Channel
Goma, DRC, July 29, 2026 — Thirty-nine people were sent back into the DRC via the Grande Barrière crossing from Gisenyi after time at Rwanda’s Mutobo centre; returnees said they had earlier been transferred to Rwanda under MONUSCO’s DDRRR programme as alleged FDLR members—an affiliation they disputed.
MONUSCO said the next day it played no role in the July 29 transfer and was not present when the group re-entered Congo. Rwanda has said some returnees did not meet criteria for its rehabilitation programme. The episode feeds mutual propaganda among Kinshasa, Kigali and AFC/M23 over who manipulates demobilisation caseloads.
*African Union Adopts Six-Month Eastern Congo Peace Roadmap
Gaborone, Botswana, July 30, 2026 — AU facilitators adopted a six-month roadmap and operational action plan covering July–December 2026 after high-level talks in Gaborone on July 25–26 with SADC and Botswana hosting.
Priorities include political analysis, military-security tracks, humanitarian access, armed-group dialogue, regional economic and natural-resource issues, and reconciliation. Former Nigerian president Olusegun Obasanjo, chairing the AU facilitators’ group, pressed for tighter coordination among African mediation architectures.
*UN Sanctions Corneille Nangaa and Five Other Armed-Group Leaders
New York/Kinshasa, July 16, 2026 — The United Nations imposed fresh sanctions on Corneille Nangaa and five other armed-group leaders linked to eastern Congo violence.
Kinshasa welcomed pressure tools while still arguing that only Rwandan withdrawal and enforceable ceasefire verification would change facts on the ground. U.S. officials separately said they remained ready to use all available instruments on conflict-linked natural resources.
*Precarious Calm Returns to Southern Masisi After Week of Heavy Fighting
Masisi, North Kivu, July 30, 2026 — A precarious calm settled over parts of southern Masisi after nearly a week of fighting between AFC/M23 rebels and wazalendo fighters that began on July 24.
Clashes with heavy and light weapons concentrated in Kibabi I, Kibabi II and Ufamandu I. Civilians fled Kasoko, Mushebere and surrounding villages, with many seeking refuge in Ngungu. Residents remain fearful of renewed hostilities despite the relative lull.
*Masisi Fighting Earlier Drove Fresh Displacement Toward Ngungu
Ngungu, Masisi, July 19, 2026 — Clashes between AFC/M23 and FARDC triggered another displacement wave toward Ngungu, while thousands of displaced at Kibabi/Buporo appealed for absent humanitarian aid.
Fragile calm between Walikale and Masisi on July 21 was accompanied by force reorganisation on both sides. Traders were also robbed in a Kafunsi ambush on the Masisi–Walikale axis on July 16.
*FARDC Drone Strikes and Explosion Rock AFC/M23 Positions at Nyabiondo
Nyabiondo, Masisi, July 28–29, 2026 — On July 28 a military drone dropped two bombs on an AFC/M23 position near the local Catholic church around 08:00, triggering panic and suspending daytime activity for hours in Osso Banyungu sector.
A strong explosion on an AFC/M23 position the following day again sowed panic; precise casualties and the weapon used in the second blast were not clearly established in initial reports. The incidents fit a pattern of aerial and blast pressure alongside ground contests across Masisi–Walikale axes.
*Walikale Wazalendo Faction Fights Paralyse Traffic and Force Flight
Walikale, North Kivu, July 28–29, 2026 — Intra-wazalendo clashes between Uhuru RDC factions at Kilambo in Bafuna grouping paralysed traffic between Ntoto and Ngora, while tensions between commanders Shukuru Bulenda and Boaz over Malembe headquarters drove residents of Malembe and Mumba to flee on July 28.
The fragmentation of supposedly allied self-defence groups continued to generate civilian displacement even where AFC/M23 was not the immediate opponent. Road closures deepen isolation of communities already caught among FARDC, AFC/M23 and local armed groups.
*AFC/M23 Internal Fighting at Kashesha Leaves Displaced Still Sheltering
Walikale, North Kivu, July 25, 2026 — Internal AFC/M23 factional fighting at Kashesha kept displaced families from returning, illustrating command fractures inside the rebellion.
Analysts separately described AFC/M23 as a dual-headed movement whose cohesion would shape the war’s next phase. Reinforcements reported at Ihula and Malemo earlier raised fears of a push toward Mpety.
*M23 Again Ranked Deadliest Armed Group in the East
Goma, DRC, July 18, 2026 — Conflict trackers said the M23 had again become the deadliest armed group in eastern Congo, reversing earlier periods when other actors led civilian-harm tallies.
UN and civil-society voices warned the rebellion threatened civilians and violated Congolese sovereignty and territorial integrity. Washington-accord rhetoric on accountability continued without an immediate halt to front-line violence.
*Night Attacks Kill Two in Nyiragongo Territory
Nyiragongo, North Kivu, July 30, 2026 — At least two people were killed in overnight armed raids in Nyiragongo Territory north of Goma, with looting reported and attackers still unidentified.
Around 11 p.m. on July 29, gunmen hit a shop in Bugamba 1 (Mudja grouping) and shot dead the young owner after stealing goods. About 1 a.m., assailants stormed a home in Kiziba 1 (“9 Portes”), fatally wounding the woman owner; other injuries were reported. Some residents said attackers wore military-style clothing.
The area, under AFC/M23 control, had seen relative calm for months. Families decried weak judicial mechanisms and called for stronger protection as banditry multiplies.
*Thousands Flee to Bush in Fizi and Mwenga After Reported Drone Strikes
Fizi/Mwenga, South Kivu, July 29, 2026 — National deputy Nyamangyoku Ishibwela raised an SOS for thousands of civilians from Mulima, Point Zéro and Rugezi in Fizi, and Kipupu, Mikenge and Kiseke in Mwenga, who fled into the bush without shelter or aid after July 24.
He attributed the flight to kamikaze-drone bombardments he said originated from Rwanda and were continuing, leaving wounded people without care and children exposed to hunger and disease. He urged the FARDC to protect civilians and the government to deliver urgent assistance. Attribution of responsibility remains contested in the wider regional information war.
*Mwenga: Kipupu Retaken as Kamituga Records Sexual Killing
Mwenga, South Kivu, July 16, 2026 — Army and wazalendo forces retook Kipupu, though populations remained displaced after earlier fighting; in Kamituga, a woman was raped and killed in a wazalendo-influenced zone.
South Kivu’s patchwork of army, rebels and self-defence groups continued to produce both territorial claims and protection crises. Verification of front-line “liberations” rarely matched civilian returns.
*ADF Violence Keeps Beni at Centre of Civilian Toll
Beni, North Kivu, July 28, 2026 — Local organisation Badilika said nearly 200 civilians were killed by armed groups between April and June across Beni, Rutshuru, Masisi and Walikale, with Beni the epicentre despite continuing joint Congolese-Ugandan Shujaa operations.
Rights monitors say military pressure has not yet translated into reliable village and road protection. Separate July incidents included at least 10 civilians killed in a Mangina ADF attack on July 16.
*ADF Killings Surge in Mambasa and Babila Babombi
Mambasa, Ituri, July 28, 2026 — Allied Democratic Forces fighters intensified attacks in Mambasa, abducting six civilians including two children at Nakoa on the Makumo–Mantumbi axis a day after a deadly Makumo raid.
Civil society said more than 190 civilians had been killed since January in Babila Babombi alone. On July 26, more than 25 civilians were reported killed in under 48 hours across Mambasa, while ADF expansion toward Haut-Uélé remained a stated concern.
*Komanda Marks One Year Since ADF Parish Massacre of 43 Civilians
Komanda, Ituri, July 27, 2026 — Residents commemorated 43 civilians killed in the July 26–27, 2025 ADF attack on Bienheureuse Anuarite parish in Irumu territory, holding a memorial Mass at the same church.
The anniversary fell amid fresh ADF raids in Irumu and Mambasa that revived fears of another mass-casualty cycle. Faith communities remain both targets and organisers of solidarity for survivors.
*Researcher Says Congo “Lost Time” Grasping ADF’s Jihadist Mutation
Kinshasa/Beni, July 31, 2026 — Researcher Claude Sengenya argued authorities and partners long misunderstood the ADF, which mutated after 2017 from a Uganda-focused politico-military refuge force into a jihadist threat documented by UN and independent reporting.
He said delayed recognition cost lives as the group expanded across Ituri and North Kivu. Security debate increasingly focuses on ideology, recruitment and cross-border networks rather than treating the ADF only as a residual rebellion.
*Deminers Neutralise Seven Explosive Devices at Mutwanga
Mutwanga, Beni, July 27, 2026 — UNMAS, AFRILAM and Civil Protection destroyed seven explosive devices at Mutwanga, including one inside Saints-Pierre-et-Paul Catholic parish and six at the SICOVIR concession in Nzenga.
Loud detonations alarmed residents even as officials said the clearance reduced civilian risk. Explosives had earlier been found buried in a Mutwanga concession on July 22. Clearance remains essential for safe return of displaced people and restoration of farmland and roads.
*Radio Technician Found Dead After ADF Abduction at Mangina
Mangina, Beni, July 23, 2026 — A radio technician abducted by ADF fighters was found dead, adding to a string of civilian targeting cases along the Beni–Lubero belt.
Civil society also warned of ADF return to Lubero’s Bapere sector. A mining-square attack on the Beni–Lubero limit killed about ten people the same week.
*MONUSCO Hands Over Modern Building to Ruwenzori Sector in Beni
Beni, North Kivu, July 31, 2026 — MONUSCO provided a modern building to the Ruwenzori sector administration in Beni Territory, presented as support to local governance and stability efforts in an ADF-affected zone.
Infrastructure hand-overs form part of the mission’s stabilisation footprint alongside military and civilian protection tasks. Residents will judge impact by whether services actually operate from the new premises.
*Rutshuru Ambush Kills Four, Including Local Chief
Kashalira, Rutshuru, July 28, 2026 — Unidentified gunmen ambushed motorcyclists in Mutanda grouping, killing four people including the chief of Kirima locality.
The daytime attack in Bwito chiefdom deepened insecurity on rural tracks. Earlier in the period, two fish-transporter motorcyclists were abducted on the Ishasha–Nyakakoma axis.
*Kabare Armed Raid Kills Woman and Wounds About Ten at Artisanal Camp
Luhihi/Lomera, Kabare, July 28, 2026 — Armed men attacked the Kajabwe artisanal miners’ camp overnight, killing one woman and wounding at least ten others while looting valuables.
Hours of gunfire emptied the site. South Kivu’s civilian economy around small-scale mining remained exposed to night raids.
*Haut-Lomami: FARDC Repel Mai-Mai Incursion at Bukama Before Presidential Visit
Bukama, Haut-Lomami, July 28, 2026 — FARDC units repelled Mai-Mai fighters who emerged from Upemba National Park armed with arrows and bladed weapons and fought for hours in Bukama centre.
Panic sent residents into hiding on the eve of a planned presidential visit to the province. The army said the attackers were quickly contained.
*Mai-Ndombe: FARDC Announce Arrest of Mobondo Leader “Odon” and Seven Guards
Mai-Ndombe, DRC, July 27, 2026 — The army said it arrested Mobondo militia chief “Odon” and seven bodyguards, a claimed blow to a western Congo armed network.
Security was separately reinforced at Yumbi after communal tensions. Kinshasa continues to fight multi-front insecurity far beyond the Kivu theatre.
*Kwilu: Army Patrols RN1 Against Highway Robbers at Kimbinga
Kimbinga, Bulungu, July 28, 2026 — The 11th military region launched regular patrols on National Road 1 after robbers used night-time tree barricades to stop vehicles and strip passengers.
The campaign aims to reopen safe travel on a strategic western artery. Similar banditry patterns have repeatedly forced military road presence outside the east.
*CRP Militia Enters Lenga Village in Djugu Without Fighting
Djugu, Ituri, July 29, 2026 — The CRP militia took control of Lenga village in Djugu Territory without combat, illustrating continued fluid armed-group movements in Ituri even where large battles are absent.
Civilian populations in such areas often face sudden shifts in who controls taxation, movement and local security with little warning.
*Ituri Civil Society Demands Real Enforcement Against Illegal Road Barriers
Bunia, Ituri, July 31, 2026 — The provincial civil-society coordination Forces Vives d’Ituri urged authorities to enforce the military governor’s order dismantling illegal barriers, saying soldiers still collect money from travellers on some roads.
Coordinator Dieudonné Lossa said illegal checkpoints remain a top public grievance and fuel both harassment and insecurity. The appeal follows field reports that rackets persist on axes including Bunia–Bogoro despite a July 25 province-wide instruction.
*Insecurity Continues to Obstruct Ebola Treatment and Aid Access
Ituri, July 26, 2026 — Ebola responders reported that insecurity blocks access to some localities and that armed groups have attacked treatment centres, compounding an epidemic spanning multiple provinces.
WHO and Congolese officials alike link epidemic control to improved humanitarian access and reduced fighting. Displacement camps with overcrowding are seen as high-risk environments for further spread.
*MSF Warns Humanitarian Crisis Remains Largely Forgotten Despite Rising Needs
Kinshasa, DRC, July 25, 2026 — Médecins Sans Frontières warned that the DRC’s protracted humanitarian crisis remains largely forgotten even as needs grow, calling for stronger international engagement.
Aid groups cite funding shortfalls, access constraints and overlapping emergencies—conflict displacement, Ebola, cholera and sexual violence—as reasons civilian suffering persists despite diplomatic processes in Washington and Doha.
*ICRC Urges Parties to Respect IHL Amid Explosive Weapons Use
Geneva/Goma, July 24, 2026 — The International Committee of the Red Cross called on all parties to respect international humanitarian law in light of explosive weapons affecting civilians in eastern Congo.
The appeal followed drone strikes, ambushes and urban shootings that blurred front lines and civilian spaces. Protection actors warn that without restraint, displacement and trauma will outrun mediation calendars.
*Tracep Cargo Plane Crashes in Walikale; MONUSCO Helps Recover Survivors
Walikale, North Kivu, July 26–31, 2026 — A Tracep Congo Aviation LET L-410 cargo flight from Beni toward Shabunda territory crashed near Buruko/Kamaninge in Walikale after an engine failure and lost radio contact while trying to divert.
The aircraft carried two crew and about two tonnes of cargo. Search teams found the wreckage days later; one crew member survived and the captain died. By July 31, MONUSCO peacekeepers were reported assisting recovery of crash survivors as investigations continued.
*Walikale Mine Collapse at Angoa Renews Artisanal-Site Safety Alarms
Angoa, Walikale, July 20, 2026 — A deadly landslide at the Angoa mining site renewed warnings about unsafe artisanal extraction under conflict conditions.
Security vacuums and armed taxation often push diggers into unstable pits. Humanitarian actors link mine disasters to broader protection failures in Walikale.
*Kinshasa Police Round Up 578 People in Gombe Night Sweep
Kinshasa, DRC, July 30, 2026 — Provincial police said 578 people were detained during a reinforced overnight patrol in Gombe targeting suspected troublemakers and illicit activity in the city centre.
The sweep followed weeks of urban gun violence complaints in Makala and Selembao, where a deputy demanded stronger protection after multiple shooting deaths. A young man was also shot dead at home in Makala on July 23.
*Butembo Student Killed by Gunmen on Eve of Thesis Defence
Butembo, North Kivu, July 27, 2026 — Law student Muhindo Mutokambali Victoire was shot at his Mukuna home by unidentified armed men and later died of wounds, a day before defending his dissertation at the Official University of Ruwenzori.
Classmates said the killing underscored urban armed banditry in Butembo beyond classic ADF/M23 front lines. Families demanded rapid investigation.
*Lake Kivu Boat Shooting Leaves Armed Man Dead and Three Civilians Wounded
Lake Kivu, July 25, 2026 — A shooting aboard a boat killed an armed man and seriously wounded three civilians, extending insecurity from land routes onto lake transport.
Maritime and lacustrine corridors remain vital for trade between North and South Kivu shores. Travellers reported heightened fear of armed boarding.
*Central Bank Data Show Security Spending More Than Doubled in 2025
Kinshasa, DRC, July 28, 2026 — The Central Bank of Congo’s monetary-policy report said security spending more than doubled in 2025 to about 8,599 billion Congolese francs (roughly $3.9 billion), up 119% from 2024.
The surge, tied to eastern military operations including against the M23, widened the state’s cash deficit. Economists note that higher budgets have not yet translated into durable civilian safety in Beni, Masisi or Ituri.
*AFC/M23 Reinforcements at Ihula and Malemo Raise Fear of Push on Mpety
Walikale/Masisi, July 24, 2026 — AFC/M23 rebels reinforced positions with men and ammunition at Ihula in Walikale and Malemo in Masisi, according to local security sources, in what was described as preparation for a renewed offensive toward Mpety and Mindjendje in Banakindi/Kisimba.
The redeployment revived fears among civilians along contested Walikale–Masisi lines even as other sectors reported fragile calm. Traders and displaced families watched for whether the buildup would translate into a new ground push.
*France Says M23 Rebellion Threatens Civilians and Congo’s Territorial Integrity
New York, July 26, 2026 — France’s deputy permanent representative to the United Nations, Jay Dharmadhikari, said the M23 rebellion threatens civilian security and violates the DRC’s sovereignty and territorial integrity, urging de-escalation and respect for Resolution 2773.
He spoke during an open Security Council debate on peaceful dispute settlement while Kinshasa held the July Council presidency. Paris also denounced illicit natural-resource exploitation as a driver of eastern conflict.
*UN Experts Urge End to M23 “Reign of Terror” Through Torture
Geneva/Goma, July 23, 2026 — United Nations human-rights experts called for an end to what they described as a reign of terror imposed by the M23 through torture, including sexual violence, as civilian victim testimonies continue to multiply.
They urged states and the UN to help revive the December 2025 ceasefire understanding between Kinshasa and the M23. The appeal added international pressure alongside battlefield reports from Masisi, Walikale and Nyiragongo.
*North Kivu Civil Society Warns of ADF Surges and AFC/M23 Entrenchment
Mangina, Beni, July 21, 2026 — Forces Vives du Nord-Kivu, meeting in Mangina, warned that ADF attacks continue to hit Mangina, Mbau and Oicha with massacres, house burnings and abductions, while AFC/M23 strengthens control in occupied zones.
The provincial civil-society coordination said the dual pressure leaves civilians trapped between jihadist raids and rebel administration. It called for more effective protection and clearer political-military responses.
*Partners Push Ceasefire Verification Mission Deployment in South Kivu
Kinshasa, July 21, 2026 — Diplomatic partners pressed for rapid deployment of the first joint expanded ceasefire-verification mission in South Kivu, even as questions remain over how to operate where MONUSCO lacks a provincial mandate.
The debate intersects Doha and Washington tracks and Kinshasa’s inclusive-dialogue announcement. Without field monitors, mediators warn paper ceasefires will not restrain drones, ambushes or taxation rackets.
*UN Rights Office Records More Than 20 Percent Jump in Violations in May
Kinshasa, July 25, 2026 — The UN Joint Human Rights Office said rights violations and abuses rose sharply in May, with cases up about 26% and victims about 20% compared with April’s 382 recorded incidents.
The surge was linked to intensified armed-group attacks on civilians, especially in the east. The same reporting cycle noted a drop in conflict-related sexual violence in May—at least 40 incidents affecting 59 victims—showing uneven trends across protection indicators.
*Conflict-Related Sexual Violence Falls in May, UN Says
Kinshasa, July 26, 2026 — UN human-rights monitoring documented a sharp May decline in conflict-related sexual violence in eastern Congo, recording at least 40 incidents affecting 59 victims, including 33 women, 25 girls and one man.
Officials cautioned that under-reporting remains chronic and that a monthly drop does not cancel the wider protection crisis. Advocates tied lasting reduction to access, accountability and an end to sieges and forced displacement.
*Rubaya Coltan Trade Accused of Financing M23; UN Pressed to Act
New York, July 26, 2026 — During a Security Council high-level debate on natural resources and peace, France and other speakers highlighted illicit mineral exploitation—including coltan from Rubaya—as financing for the M23 and related networks.
Kinshasa, holding the Council presidency, framed resource governance as a peace-and-security issue rather than a purely commercial dossier. Calls mounted for stronger UN and state action against smuggling corridors.
*United States Says It Will Use All Tools to Back Peace Around Resources
New York, July 26, 2026 — The United States told the Security Council it remained ready to use all available tools to help the DRC, Rwanda and Sudan pursue peace and prosperity tied to responsible natural-resource management.
The statement aligned with Washington-accord accountability messaging that “we will not fear to demand accounts from those who threaten peace.” Kinshasa used the presidency platform to argue prevention must serve peoples, not criminal networks.
*Military Dispute Turns Into Fatal Shooting at Logu, Killing Two Civilians
Logu, Walikale, July 25, 2026 — A dispute among soldiers inside a house in Logu (Wassa grouping) around 20:00 on July 24 degenerated into a firefight that killed one civilian on the spot and left a second critically wounded; both civilian victims later counted among the dead in local tallies.
The incident deepened distrust of armed actors even when they are nominally state forces. Residents described panic across the locality after the night shooting.
*Makala and Selembao Shootings Prompt Deputy to Demand Ministerial Hearings
Kinshasa, July 27, 2026 — National Deputy Eric Tshikuma told the National Assembly that weekly gun deaths in Makala and Selembao require urgent independent investigations, arrests and prosecution of suspects, and hearings of relevant security ministers.
The plenary intervention on July 25 framed capital-city banditry as a governance failure alongside eastern war. It preceded the large Gombe police sweep that detained 578 people nights later.
*Bandundu Troops Briefed Against Leaking Operations on Social Media
Bandundu region, July 20, 2026 — The 11th military region held a sensitisation session for soldiers on phone and social-media use, warning against filming operations or posting material that reveals troop movements.
Captain Anthony Mualushayi of the region’s communications service said online posts can endanger units engaged on multiple fronts. The briefing reflects command concern that OPSEC failures amplify vulnerability to ambushes and information warfare.
*Masisi Centre Briefly Resumes Market Life Between Battle Waves
Masisi, North Kivu, July 24–25, 2026 — After violent AFC/M23–wazalendo clashes sowed panic in Masisi centre, socio-economic activity progressively and then more normally resumed before later fighting again forced displacement southward.
The stop-start pattern—markets open, then empty—defined civilian life across the period. By July 30, only a precarious calm held in southern grouping villages.
