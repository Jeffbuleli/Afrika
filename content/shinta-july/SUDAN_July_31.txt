REPORT ON THE SITUATION IN SUDAN FROM July 15 to 31, 2026
Politics and Diplomacy
*RSF Accepts Humanitarian Truce but Rejects Withdrawal Terms
Khartoum, Sudan, July 15, 2026 — The Rapid Support Forces submitted their reply to U.S. peace proposals, accepting an unconditional three-month humanitarian truce under United Nations supervision in Darfur, Kordofan, Ed Damazin and the Nuba Mountains while rejecting demands to withdraw from captured territories.
An RSF official said the package also covered security arrangements, restructuring of state institutions, transitional justice and political dialogue toward a transitional government. Sudan’s Security and Defence Council, chaired by Gen. Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, had separately submitted its own response days earlier.
U.S. Senior Advisor Massad Boulos cautioned against claims of a breakthrough, saying multiple substantive issues remained unresolved or rejected and that only a formal announcement would confirm any deal.

Economy and Tech.
*EU Gold Ban Sparks Debate Over Feasibility and War Funding
Khartoum, Sudan, July 15, 2026 — The European Council banned the purchase, import or transfer of Sudanese gold and restricted mercury and cyanide exports to Sudan, aiming to cut conflict financing.
Gold has been Sudan’s main hard-currency earner since South Sudan’s secession removed most oil revenues. Mining specialists and economists debated whether the ban can be enforced given informal networks, neighbouring transit routes and domestic dependence on gold to finance fuel, wheat and medicines.

The measures added a London track to EU commodity restrictions and U.S. financial pressure. Smuggling and artisanal chains still complicate enforcement on the ground.

Conclusion
The period from July 15 to July 31, 2026, marks a critical and volatile juncture in the Sudanese conflict, characterized by shifting battlefield dynamics, deepening humanitarian paralysis, and persistent diplomatic fragmentation. While the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and allied movements secured key operational breakthroughs by reclaiming critical transit corridors in North Kordofan, the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) responded with asymmetric escalation, decentralized commander autonomy, and retaliatory strikes on strategic rear infrastructure. Consequently, key urban centers—most notably El Obeid—remain trapped under severe siege-like conditions, exposing hundreds of thousands of civilians to dual threats of militarized violence and weaponized resource scarcity.
On the economic and diplomatic fronts, structural divides continue to impede a viable path toward peace. International regulatory measures—such as targeted UK sanctions and EU commodity bans on gold—face severe enforcement bottlenecks within a hyper-informal conflict economy reliant on illegal transit routes and parallel extortion networks. Concurrently, political dialogue remains stalled: warring factions prioritize battlefield leverage over ceasefire compliance, while civilian political coalitions remain deeply fragmented over regional mediation formats.
As rainy-season logistics compound aid delivery blockades, systemic destruction of essential water, power, and healthcare networks is driving a severe escalation in cholera outbreaks, mass displacement, and famine-level food insecurity affecting over 20 million people. Moving forward, the trajectory of the crisis hinges entirely on whether international facilitators can establish enforceable, politically independent civilian protection mechanisms and secure unhindered humanitarian access across Darfur, Kordofan, and central Sudan. Without these structural safeguards, tactical military shifts will continue to yield only widespread destruction and escalating human suffering.
