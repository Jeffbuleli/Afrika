*Uganda Electoral Commission Launches 2026 Election Review, Plans Reforms Ahead of Future Polls
Masaka, Uganda, July 15, 2026 – Uganda’s Electoral Commission has begun a nationwide assessment of the 2026 General Elections, engaging stakeholders to identify challenges and develop reforms aimed at improving future electoral processes.
During a consultation meeting in Masaka, Electoral Commission officials said the review seeks to examine what worked well during the elections, identify shortcomings, and collect recommendations on strengthening election management and public confidence.
One of the key concerns raised during the consultations was inadequate voter education. The Commission said it would review its voter education programmes and improve efforts to ensure citizens receive sufficient information before future elections.
The Commission also defended the role of security agencies during elections, saying their involvement is intended to maintain law and order and protect citizens rather than interfere with the electoral process.
Officials said the electoral roadmap is guided by legal requirements and acknowledged concerns raised by stakeholders over issues such as election timelines and the management of results at tally centres. These concerns will be reviewed as part of preparations for future polls.
The Commission also addressed criticism regarding the independence of its members, stating that commissioners undergo parliamentary vetting and approval before assuming office.
According to the Commission, some election-related challenges, including violence, voter bribery, and disputes among candidates, are linked to the conduct of political actors rather than the electoral body itself.
Ahead of the upcoming Local Council I (LC1) elections, authorities urged candidates and voters to maintain peace, emphasizing that grassroots elections involve communities where competitors and voters continue to live together after polling.
The Commission said approximately Shs53 billion has been allocated to support LC1 election activities, including payments for officials involved in voter register updates, nominations, and polling operations across more than 70,000 villages.
The post-election consultations are part of a broader assessment aimed at improving electoral transparency, strengthening voter education, and addressing concerns raised by stakeholders before Uganda’s next electoral cycle.

KISOZI, July 16, 2026 — President Yoweri Museveni has said guaranteed markets are essential for expanding production and accelerating Uganda’s industrial growth, arguing that farmers can supply agro-processing industries if they are assured of stable demand.
Speaking during the commissioning of the National Enterprise Corporation (NEC) Meat and Beans Processing Plant in Kisozi, Gomba District, Museveni described the facility as a major step in Uganda’s industrialisation agenda, promoting value addition and strategic self-reliance.
The plant, established through a partnership between NEC and businessman Francis Ragwa, will process canned beef and beans for domestic consumption and export markets.

He pointed to the growth of the coffee and dairy sectors as examples of how market-driven production can transform industries and improve economic opportunities.
“Once I am sure about the market, I can mobilise Ugandans to produce because the economics are correct. The stimulus for production is the market,” Museveni said.

Museveni also praised NEC and its partners for completing the project despite challenges, including delays linked to the Covid-19 pandemic.
Minister of Defence and Veteran Affairs Kiryowa Kiwanuka described the facility as an important milestone in Uganda’s pursuit of industrialisation and self-sufficiency. NEC Managing Director Lt Gen James Mugira said the project was initiated in 2019 with the goal of establishing a beef and beans processing plant alongside a tin can manufacturing facility.
He said the factory would support import substitution by producing canned food products locally, reducing government spending on imported rations for the Uganda People’s Defence Forces (UPDF), Uganda Police Force, and Uganda Prisons Service.
Mugira added that local production would strengthen national food security and reduce risks associated with global supply chain disruptions.
Francis Ragwa, Managing Director of NEC Meat and Beans Processing Plant, said extensive research and international benchmarking guided the development of the facility. He called for Uganda’s Ankole cattle to be positioned as a premium global brand by moving beyond selling beef as a basic commodity.

*Uganda Suspends Land Deal Roles for Campaigning LC I Chairpersons
KAMPALA, July 16, 2026 — The Ministry of Lands, Housing and Urban Development has temporarily restricted Local Council I (LC I) chairpersons seeking re-election from participating in land transactions until the conclusion of the upcoming village elections.
The directive aims to prevent possible fraud, disputes, and irregularities during the campaign period ahead of the July 28 LC I chairperson elections.
In a public notice issued on Thursday, the ministry instructed campaigning LC I chairpersons to stop witnessing, endorsing, recommending, or overseeing land-related transactions until the electoral process is completed.
“The advisory has been issued as a precautionary measure to safeguard the integrity of land transactions during this transition period and to minimise the risk of disputes, fraud, or other irregularities that may arise,” the ministry stated.
The ministry advised members of the public against seeking land transaction services from LC I chairpersons who are actively campaigning, including verification of ownership, witnessing land sale agreements, resolving boundary disputes, or handling other administrative land matters.
Those with urgent land issues were encouraged to seek assistance from qualified legal practitioners or follow alternative lawful procedures until the elections are concluded.
The ministry said the temporary measure was intended to protect the interests of all parties involved and prevent costly mistakes during the transition period.
Land disputes remain a major source of conflict in Uganda, with local leaders often playing an important role in verifying ownership, confirming identities of sellers, identifying boundaries, and witnessing agreements at community level.
Although LC I chairpersons do not have the authority to transfer land ownership or issue official land titles, they are frequently relied upon because of their knowledge of local communities and land histories.
Legal experts have previously warned that LC I endorsements only provide community-level verification and do not replace formal legal procedures required under Uganda’s land laws. Buyers are still expected to conduct proper due diligence before completing land purchases.
According to the Electoral Commission roadmap, elections for Village (LC I) chairpersons will take place on July 28 across Uganda’s 71,214 villages, followed by Parish (LC II) chairperson elections on August 10.
The Ministry of Lands said LC I chairpersons will resume their normal role in land-related matters once the electoral process is concluded.

“He will be deeply missed not only by those who knew him, worked with him and were led by him, but also by generations of Ugandans who drew inspiration from his exemplary leadership and political career,” Babalanda said.
A hybrid National Organising Committee chaired by Babalanda, with representation from Ngirisi’s family, has been established to oversee preparations for the burial.
The official burial is scheduled for Friday, July 17, at Ngirisi’s ancestral home in Hakitengya, Tokwe Sub County, Bundibugyo District, following a funeral service at Hakitengya Technical School.

Speaking after the tour, Gen. Kante expressed appreciation for the scale and ambition of Dei BioPharma’s operations, describing the facility as an inspiring example of Africa’s capacity to develop advanced pharmaceutical industries.
“The visit this evening allowed us to see beautiful things. I would like to congratulate Dr. Magoola for everything he is doing in the field of biotechnology,” Gen. Kante said.
He added that the experience would be shared with the participating officers’ home countries as an example of Africa’s potential in pharmaceutical manufacturing.
“I hope that each of these trainees, upon returning home, will be able to bear witness to what we have seen here at Dei BioPharma,” he said.
Gen. Kante also invited Dr. Matthias Magoola, founder and Managing Director of Dei BioPharma, to explore opportunities to expand the company’s work across Africa.
Responding to the delegation’s remarks, Dr. Magoola said the visit demonstrated the growing importance of Uganda’s investment in biotechnology and pharmaceutical production.
He said many of the visiting officers had never encountered a facility of such scale on the continent, highlighting the strategic importance of developing local manufacturing capacity.
“This is not surprising, as only a few countries, such as Morocco and Egypt, have attempted to move into manufacturing. Some of the students are from those regions, yet you heard them say they have not seen anything like this elsewhere,” Magoola said.
Dr. Magoola also linked Uganda’s willingness to share pharmaceutical technology with other African countries to President Yoweri Museveni’s Pan-African vision.
He said African countries should work together to reduce dependence on imported medicines and vaccines.
“Africa does not need to depend on others for vaccine and drug manufacturing. I am operating in that spirit of Pan-Africanism that our president consistently emphasizes,” he said.
Addressing Africa’s continued reliance on imported medicines, Magoola identified limited technological capacity as the main challenge preventing local production.
“The problem is a lack of technology. Africa currently imports 85 percent of its medicine,” he said, adding that Dei BioPharma had developed its own innovations, secured patents in the United States, and built the capacity to manufacture vaccines and medicines locally.
He argued that expanding pharmaceutical production could transform Africa’s economy by improving access to affordable healthcare products while creating industrial opportunities.
“Our ultimate vision is to make these biological drugs, which 90 percent of our population currently cannot afford, accessible and affordable,” Magoola said.
Dei BioPharma Chief Executive Officer Dr. Chetna Jain said the visit reflected growing continental recognition of the company’s achievements.
“It shows that what we are doing here is being recognized across Africa. People are aware of Dei BioPharma, and they are willing to come here and collaborate with us after seeing what we have developed,” she said.

Located on more than 250 acres in Matugga, the campus includes multiple manufacturing units focused on biotechnology, injectables, generic medicines, nutraceuticals, oncology products, and vaccines.
The facility is designed to produce treatments for diseases including malaria, HIV, and cancer, as well as insulin, monoclonal antibodies, and mRNA-based therapeutics.
The National Drug Authority licensed Dei BioPharma in May 2024 to manufacture medicines, including non-beta-lactam tablets and hard gelatin capsules.
The company holds more than 100 patents filed with the United States Patent and Trademark Office and is preparing for large-scale mRNA vaccine production in line with international standards.
The facility is projected to create more than 40,000 professional jobs and has been identified by Ugandan authorities as a potential driver of industrial growth and reduced dependence on imported medicines.
The pharmaceutical tour formed part of wider military engagements between Uganda and Mali. During the visit, Ugandan defence officials highlighted ongoing cooperation between the two countries under a military training agreement, which has so far supported the training of 58 Malian officer cadets.
Gen. Kante also announced that Mali had offered Uganda 23 training opportunities, including a slot at the Mali War College, further strengthening defence cooperation between the two nations.
*Tayebwa Calls on OACPS States to Prioritise Value Addition and End Raw Mineral Exports
BRUSSELS, July 17, 2026 — Uganda’s Deputy Speaker of Parliament and President of the Organisation of African, Caribbean and Pacific States (OACPS) Parliamentary Assembly, Thomas Tayebwa, has urged member states to place trade, industrialisation, and value addition at the centre of their development strategies.
Addressing the 121st OACPS Council of Ministers at the Egmont Palace in Brussels, Tayebwa said the organisation’s future relevance would depend on its ability to advance practical economic policies that improve citizens’ livelihoods, create jobs, strengthen industries, and help countries benefit more from their natural resources.
He identified the continued export of unprocessed minerals as one of the biggest challenges facing Africa and other developing regions, arguing that countries must move away from exporting raw materials while importing finished products.
Tayebwa said the issue, which has also been repeatedly highlighted by President Yoweri Museveni, requires urgent action to ensure African countries capture more value from their resources.
“I think at the level of the Council of Ministers, we need to start tackling the hard questions relevant to our people. One of them is... the issue of Africa and the developing world exporting unprocessed minerals as raw materials,” Tayebwa said.
He noted that Africa possesses an estimated US$29.5 trillion in mineral wealth, representing about 20 percent of global mineral resources, yet continues to lose significant economic opportunities because much of its minerals are exported in raw or minimally processed form.
According to Tayebwa, the current model allows external economies to benefit more from Africa’s resources by processing raw materials into higher-value products, while African countries miss out on industrial growth, employment opportunities, and increased revenues.
