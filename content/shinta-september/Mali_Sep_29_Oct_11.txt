Mali, 11 October 2025.
REPORT ON THE SITUATION IN MALI FROM Sept. 29 to 11 October 2025
Politics and Diplomacy
*President Tiani Visits Mali to Strengthen Sahel Security Cooperation
BAMAKO, Mali - Niger’s President, General Abdourahamane Tiani, conducted a friendship and working visit to Bamako on September 30, marking his second official trip to Mali since assuming power in July 2023.
The visit underscored the growing military and political coordination within the Alliance of Sahel States (AES). Following a one-on-one meeting with Malian President Assimi Goïta, the two leaders held an extended working session attended by senior officials, including the defense ministers of both nations.
Discussions centered on the fight against terrorism and the accelerating security deterioration across the Sahel. The two presidents agreed to fast-track the operationalization of the AES joint military force, a unified command structure designed to enhance coordinated counterterrorism operations.
President Tiani emphasized that the creation of joint battalions composed of Nigerien, Malian, and Burkinabè soldiers would represent a decisive step toward collective security and sovereignty in the region. The move reflects the alliance’s broader vision of self-reliant defense and regional stability free from external dependency.
*High Court Dismisses Case Challenging Dissolution of Political Parties
BAMAKO, Mali - The High Court of Bamako’s 6th district this week rejected a complaint filed by political parties challenging the transitional government’s decision to dissolve them. The court ruled the petition unfounded, effectively upholding the government’s actions.
The political parties have indicated they may appeal the decision. Other proceedings related to the case are ongoing in both lower courts and the Constitutional Court.
Mountaga Tall, one of the parties’ lawyers, explained that the main objective was to have the repeal of the Political Parties Charter - the legal basis for the dissolution decree - reviewed by the Constitutional Court. The High Court route was pursued with the support of members of the National Transitional Council (CNT), but this approach proved difficult under current political conditions. Legal experts note that, alternatively, parties could raise an exception of unconstitutionality before a court, which would then refer the matter to the Constitutional Court via the Supreme Court.
*Former MP Mansour Haidara Assassinated Near Bamako
BAMAKO, Mali - Abdoul Djalil Mansour Haidara, former member of Mali’s National Assembly and prominent religious and media figure, was killed by unidentified gunmen on the road to Ségou, approximately 150 kilometers from Bamako.
According to sources close to his family, Haidara was targeted while attempting to pass a stationary vehicle and was struck by heavy gunfire, dying instantly. The attack occurred in a region that has seen repeated assaults by the Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims in recent weeks.
Haïdara, a former member of the Rally for Mali party, served in parliament from 2013 to 2020 and was also a member of Mali’s High Islamic Council. His death has drawn widespread mourning from Mali’s political community, Muslim leaders, and the press.
*President of Ségou Regional Council Abducted Alongside Driver
SÉGOU, Mali - Mr. Siaka Dembélé, president of the Ségou Regional Council, and his driver were kidnapped on Friday afternoon, October 3, 2025, while traveling on the road to Bamako. Authorities have not reported any updates on their whereabouts since the abduction.
The council’s secretary general and first vice president have called for public prayers for the safe and swift release of both individuals. This incident marks the first reported hostage-taking along the Ségou-Bamako corridor since a series of attacks began in the area three weeks ago.
*Mauritanian Foreign Minister Visits Bamako to Strengthen Bilateral Ties
BAMAKO, Mali - On October 8, 2025, Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug, Mauritania’s Minister of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation, and Mauritanians Abroad, visited Bamako and met with Mali’s transitional President, Army General Assimi Goïta. He delivered a message from Mauritanian President Mohamed Cheikh El Ghazouani, emphasizing fraternity, cooperation, and mutual solidarity between the two countries.
The visit comes amid heightened tensions over migration and trade. Mauritania has regularly arrested and expelled Malian nationals, despite agreements on free movement, while Mali recently closed thousands of Mauritanian-owned shops in response. Mauritanian media report that the minister advocated for reopening these businesses.
The Malian presidency highlighted the need for dialogue and urged the reactivation of the Mali-Mauritania high commission to provide a structured framework for ongoing consultations.
*US Introduces Visa Deposit Requirement for Malian Nationals
BAMAKO, Mali - The US Embassy in Mali announced on October 10, 2025, that Malian nationals applying for B-1/B-2 visas (business or tourism) will be required to pay a deposit of $5,000 to $10,000 starting October 23.
The pilot program, implemented by the US State Department in coordination with the Treasury Department and the Department of Homeland Security, mandates that consular officers consider the applicant’s circumstances when determining the deposit amount. Applicants must also submit Department of Homeland Security Form I-352 via the Treasury Department’s Pay.gov platform, agreeing to the bond’s terms.
The deposit requirement applies to all Malian passport holders, regardless of the location where the visa application is submitted.
*Mali Harmonizes Passport Prices with AES Confederation
BAMAKO, Mali - The Malian Council of Ministers adopted a draft decree on Wednesday standardizing passport prices in line with the Confederation of Sahel States (AES) travel document framework. The move aims to facilitate free movement of people, goods, and services amid ongoing regional security challenges.
The AES e-passport, introduced on April 18, 2025, meets International Civil Aviation Organization standards and protects personal data. Under the new decree, the standard passport service is set at 45,000 CFA francs, while the premium service costs 90,000 CFA francs - reductions of 10,000 and 20,000 CFA francs respectively from previous rates.
Following Mali’s withdrawal from the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS), Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger launched the common passport earlier this year, with prices now harmonized across the three countries.
Economy
*Fuel Crisis Deepens as Terrorists Block Supply Routes
MALI - Economic activity across Mali has slowed sharply following a terrorist-imposed blockade on hydrocarbon imports that began in mid-September. The disruption has triggered severe fuel shortages in regional capitals including Koutiala, Nioro du Sahel, and Ségou, as well as in hundreds of rural sub-prefectures.
Vehicles of all types have been immobilized, and prolonged power outages have plunged many towns into darkness - some for an entire week. According to authorities, more than 100 tanker trucks have been burned by armed groups since the blockade began.
Although the army is providing escorts for convoys, attacks persist, discouraging transporters from resuming deliveries. The capital and border cities remain relatively stable, but national energy output has fallen drastically - from 18 hours of power per day to barely six.
*AES and Togo Customs Chiefs Meet in Bamako
BAMAKO - The directors general of customs from Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali, and Togo met in Bamako on October 2 to assess progress in interconnecting the customs IT systems of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) and Togo.
The high-level meeting followed technical discussions by customs experts and aimed to strengthen regional trade facilitation and economic integration. Participants reviewed the experts’ work, identified challenges hindering system interconnection, and issued recommendations to accelerate implementation in line with the AES Confederation’s economic and social development goals.
*Mali to Host First National Electronic Banking Exhibition
BAMAKO - Mali is set to host its first National Electronic Banking Exhibition on October 9 and 10, 2025, in Bamako. The announcement was made by the Professional Association of Banks and Financial Institutions of Mali, in collaboration with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO), and the West African Monetary Union (UEMOA).
The biennial event will highlight innovation and modernization in the banking and digital finance sector across Mali and the UEMOA region. The program includes exhibitions, technical workshops, and high-level panels focusing on electronic payment security and partnerships between banks and fintech companies.
*Fuel Shortages Cause Widespread Disruption Across Mali
BAMAKO, Mali - Mali is grappling with a severe fuel crisis, as long queues form daily at gas stations, with motorists often waiting three to four hours for a few liters of gasoline. Since October 7, only about 10% of gas stations have been operational, with regions like Mopti, Koutiala, and Ségou experiencing outages for over ten days.
The shortages are attributed to a blockade imposed by terrorists, forcing fuel convoys to travel under army escort.
On October 8, 300 tanker trucks arrived in Bamako under military protection, temporarily easing supply for the capital. However, by October 11, refueling challenges resurfaced, and many regions remain without access to fuel.
The crisis has caused urban transport costs to surge, with taxi and motorcycle fares doubling, and bicycles becoming the primary mode of transportation in some areas. In response, the Customs Directorate General has announced 24-hour operations at the petroleum products office to facilitate timely declaration processing and support importers.
*Fuel Shortages Trigger Widespread Electricity Blackouts in Mali
BAMAKO, Mali - The ongoing fuel crisis in Mali is severely disrupting electricity supply, with more than 80% of the country’s energy generated from thermal sources. Mopti, the nation’s fifth-largest city, has been completely without electricity for the past ten days, causing hospitals and essential services to operate at a reduced capacity.
In the capital, Bamako, power availability has dropped to just six hours per day, affecting public and private sector operations. Prior to the fuel blockade imposed by terrorist groups, cities already faced energy challenges, but electricity supply was significantly higher - Bamako previously had 19 hours of daily power.
Experts warn that unless a resolution is reached with the armed groups, Mali could face a nationwide standstill within two weeks, risking public unrest and increased terrorist proximity to urban centers.
*Mali Awards Mining Rights to U.S. Firm Flagship, Signaling Strategic Sector Revival
BAMAKO, Mali - On October 8, the Malian government took a decisive step to revitalize its gold mining sector, signing an agreement with U.S.-based company Flagship to resume production at the Morila gold mine, one of the country’s most prominent mineral assets. The deal was formalized by the Société de Recherche et d’Exploitation des Ressources Minérales du Mali (SOREM), which owns full capital of the mine in southern Mali.
Minister of Mines, Prof. Amadou Keita, highlighted the significance of the agreement as part of SOREM’s operationalization, a state-owned entity established under the directive of President Assimi Goïta to ensure that Mali’s mineral wealth directly contributes to national development. “The reforms undertaken aim to bring the mining sector back under state control within a framework of win-win partnerships with investors,” said Keita, urging SOREM to execute its mandate with diligence.
Economy and Finance Minister Alousséni Sanou echoed the optimism, noting that the Morila mine, which holds estimated reserves of 2.5 million ounces of gold, has the potential to catalyze economic growth following a nearly two-year shutdown.
Analysts view the agreement as part of Mali’s broader geopolitical and economic strategy. In recent months, senior U.S. officials, including White House Deputy Assistant Director for Counterterrorism Rudolph Atallah and Assistant Secretary of State for West Africa William B. Stevens, visited Bamako to propose American solutions for counterterrorism and private investment initiatives in the Sahel. The Morila deal may therefore reflect Mali’s willingness to engage U.S. investors alongside its existing collaboration with Russia, balancing strategic partnerships while addressing domestic economic and security challenges.
The restart of Morila represents both a symbolic and practical milestone, signaling the government’s commitment to restoring state influence over strategic resources and attracting international investment to bolster economic resilience.
Social and Health
*Teacher Strikes Disrupt School Reopening in Mali
BAMAKO, Mali - Classes resumed across Mali on October 1, but strikes by teachers in several regions have disrupted the school year. The protest stems from unpaid September salaries, with affected employees reportedly listed as “fictitious civil servants” following a government audit.
The audit initially flagged more than 35,000 names, but subsequent verification revealed errors, with many legitimate civil servants mistakenly categorized and denied pay. In response, the Minister of Territorial Administration ordered the release of salaries for teachers who could provide registration receipts.
Despite these measures, union committees in multiple localities have continued their strike until all affected educators receive full payment.
*Insecurity Leaves Over 500,000 Children Out of School in Mali
BAMAKO, Mali - Children’s rights organizations report that more than 500,000 schoolchildren are unable to attend classes due to the closure of at least 1,700 schools nationwide, according to UNICEF Mali and the March 2025 Education Cluster report.
The school closures are largely a result of insecurity, with areas under the control of terrorist groups deemed unsafe for educational activities. To mitigate the impact, local authorities and social services have implemented alternative solutions for displaced children.
In Goundam, northern Mali, a primary school opened on October 1, 2025, for students displaced from the M’Béra camp in Mauritania. Classes are conducted in temporary structures to ensure continuity, under the guidance of local authorities and the special delegation of the municipality of Gargando.
*Teaching of the French Revolution Suspended in Malian Schools
On October 9, 2025, Mali’s Ministry of Education issued an official directive to all directors of the Centre d’Animation, instructing them to suspend the teaching of the French Revolution of 1789 in 9th-grade history classes. This lesson, traditionally the first in the 9th-grade history curriculum, has been a cornerstone of civic education in Mali.
The decision comes amid heightened diplomatic tensions between Mali and France, reflecting a broader push by the transitional government to assert cultural sovereignty. Proponents argue that the measure strengthens national identity by prioritizing Malian perspectives in the school curriculum.
However, critics warn that removing this foundational topic could weaken students’ understanding of democratic principles, civic responsibility, and broader global history. The full implications for educational outcomes remain uncertain, and the Ministry has yet to clarify whether alternative content will replace the French Revolution in the syllabus.
Observers note that this move could have ripple effects on history education nationwide, potentially influencing how Malian students understand both their own national heritage and international historical contexts.
Justice and Rights
*Trial of Former Prime Minister Moussa Mara Continues in Bamako
BAMAKO, Mali - On Monday, September 29, former Prime Minister Moussa Mara, honorary president of the Yelema Party, appeared before the National Cybercrime Center (PNLC) for his ongoing trial. The proceedings were conducted under heightened security and remained tense but orderly between the public prosecutor and Mara’s legal team.
Despite assurances of legal representation, Mara’s request for release was denied. The case has been taken under advisement, with judgment scheduled for October 27. The public prosecutor is seeking a 24-month prison sentence, while the defense has requested full acquittal, arguing there is no legal basis for pretrial detention.
Mara was arrested following a tweet in which he criticized the detention of politicians and civil society leaders, asserting that they were prisoners of conscience. He has remained in preventive detention for nearly three months pending trial.
*Trial Continues for Two Members of Mali’s National Transitional Council
BAMAKO, Mali - Legal proceedings against two members of Mali’s National Transitional Council (CNT) continued on October 2, 2025, before the Criminal Court of the National Cybercrime Unit. The court adjourned the case until November 27 and set a deposit of 5,500,000 CFA francs. Lawyers representing the political parties confirmed that the sum will be paid.
The hearing was attended by CNT member Moulaye Keita, while Biton Mamari Coulbaly was absent. Authorities confirmed that his absence would not affect the scheduled trial date. Both council members face charges of verbal abuse against political party leaders and incitement to violence.
Security and Humanitarian Affairs
*Terrorist Attack Near Bamako Leaves Civilians and Soldiers Dead
BAMAKO, Mali - The security situation in central Mali deteriorated further this week following a deadly ambush less than 200 kilometers from the capital. On Wednesday, October 1, armed extremist groups attacked public transport vehicles on the Bamako-Ségou highway that were under military escort.
According to local sources, all members of the escort were killed in the assault, along with several civilians. Among the victims were the wife and two children of the president of the Mopti Administrative Court, who died at the scene. The court’s judge, also among the passengers, sustained gunshot wounds but is reported to be in stable condition.
In total, at least six civilians were confirmed dead, though the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) have not released an official casualty count for their personnel. The assailants later published images online displaying weapons allegedly seized during the attack - a move consistent with their propaganda tactics aimed at demonstrating battlefield dominance.
The incident underscores the persistent threat posed by militant groups operating across central Mali, even within proximity of Bamako, and highlights the ongoing challenge of securing major transportation routes linking the capital to regional centers.
*Wave of Explosive Attacks Targets Malian Armed Forces
SÉGOU / KIDAL, Mali - Armed extremist groups have intensified their campaign against Malian defense and security forces, employing improvised explosive devices (IEDs) to deadly effect across multiple regions.
On October 2, an army vehicle struck a roadside bomb between Bla and San in the Ségou region, killing all soldiers on board. The following day, a second explosion occurred on the Aguelhoc-Alkit road in the Kidal region, where another Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) vehicle hit a mine. According to militant sources, all nine occupants were killed.
The Malian army has not publicly commented on either incident. However, security analysts note that the back-to-back attacks illustrate the insurgents’ continued ability to disrupt military operations despite recent government offensives.
In response, the army’s general staff reportedly intensified counterterrorism operations across central and northern Mali. While officials acknowledged sustaining significant losses, they emphasized ongoing efforts to maintain pressure on armed religious groups and restore state control in contested zones.
*Two Drones Shot Down Over Timbuktu Airport
TIMBUKTU, Mali - Residents of Timbuktu were jolted awake early Monday, September 29, by a series of explosions that the military later confirmed were caused by hostile drones targeting the city’s airport.
In an official statement, the Chief of Staff of the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) announced that two “enemy drones” attempting to strike the airport had been intercepted and destroyed in flight. The statement praised the “exemplary responsiveness and professionalism” of the defense units involved in the operation.
The incident marks a continued escalation in the use of unmanned aerial attacks by armed groups in northern Mali, underscoring the evolving nature of the conflict and the growing importance of air defense capabilities in the country’s counterterrorism strategy.
*Timbuktu Military Base Welcomes 300 New Recruits
TIMBUKTU, Mali - On September 30, the Timbuktu military base officially integrated 300 newly trained soldiers - including 10 women - into the Malian Defense and Security Forces. The recruits took their oath of allegiance under the national flag during a ceremony held at Camp Fort Cheick Sidi Bekaye, attended by the representative of the Governor of Timbuktu and the regional military commander.
The new soldiers successfully completed a six-month comprehensive training program - the first of its kind in the region - covering weapons handling, tactical maneuvers, and civic education. During the ceremony, the recruits showcased their skills in assembling and disassembling weapons, performed educational skits on drug prevention, and demonstrated combat techniques.
To recognize outstanding performance, the top three trainees received awards for excellence and bravery. The deployment marks a significant reinforcement of security personnel in northern Mali, amid ongoing efforts to stabilize the region and strengthen state authority.
*Two Terrorists Killed While Extorting Farmers in Central Mali
MALI - On October 1, Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) troops intercepted two armed extremists who were collecting illegal “rakat” payments - a form of extortion disguised as religious taxation - from local farmers.
According to military sources, the encounter escalated into an exchange of fire when the suspects attempted to flee. Both were swiftly neutralized by the patrol unit. Soldiers recovered two AK-47 rifles and a motorcycle at the scene.
In a statement, the General Staff commended the professionalism and vigilance of the troops involved, describing the operation as part of broader efforts to dismantle terrorist financing networks that exploit rural communities under the guise of religious authority.
*Malian Army Kills Ten Terrorists and Recovers Stolen Livestock in Markala
MARKALA, Mali - On October 2, the Malian Armed Forces (FAMa) conducted a successful counterterrorism operation in Markala, Ségou region, killing around ten militants and recovering livestock stolen from local communities.
According to an official statement from the army’s General Staff, the operation was launched following intelligence reports indicating the presence of armed groups in possession of animals taken from residents of three nearby villages.
The military released footage showing weapons, motorcycles, and other equipment seized from the neutralized assailants. Officials said the operation disrupted one of several networks financing terrorist activities through cattle theft - a practice increasingly used by armed groups to sustain their logistics and operations in central Mali.
*Malian Forces Neutralize Key Daesh Leader Boubacar Demougui in Joint Air Operation
MÉNAKA, Mali - In a significant counterterrorism operation, the Malian army, in coordination with security partners from Burkina Faso and Niger, successfully carried out targeted air strikes in the Inarabane area, southwest of Ménaka. Drones were deployed to precision-target several senior leaders of the Daesh organization, resulting in the elimination of key operatives, including Boubacar Demougui, a high-ranking Daesh commander responsible for northern Ménaka.
Security sources describe Demougui as the operational head of multiple active cells in the region, overseeing logistics, tactical deployments, and management of terrorist detachments. He was reportedly directly involved in the execution of Sidi Barka, president of the Ménaka civil society, an act that sent shockwaves through local communities. Intelligence further confirms that members of Demougui’s family were implicated in the kidnapping of a Spanish hostage in Algeria, highlighting the cross-border scope of his network.
Officials note that the death of Demougui represents a significant blow to Daesh, which maintains influence across northern Mali and the tri-border area encompassing parts of Mali, Niger, and Burkina Faso. However, experts caution that the group’s entrenched presence and decentralized structure may allow for rapid leadership replacement, potentially sustaining operational continuity in the region.
The operation underscores the importance of multinational collaboration in counterterrorism efforts and signals a continued commitment by Sahelian states to degrade extremist networks threatening regional stability.
*Moroccan Military Delegation Visits Bamako to Strengthen Security Cooperation
BAMAKO, Mali - A Moroccan military delegation visited Mali on Tuesday, October 7, 2025, as part of ongoing efforts to enhance defense collaboration between the Kingdom of Morocco and the Republic of Mali. The delegation was received at the Directorate of Army Communications, Telecommunications, and Information Technology (DTTIA), where officials discussed the implementation of an electronic border security system, a priority for both nations.
Malian military authorities emphasized the strategic importance of advanced communications and technology for coordinating operations on the ground and ensuring the safety of civilians and property. During the discussions, the Malian side presented the missions, technical capacities, and future ambitions of the DTTIA.
Moroccan intelligence has historically played a role in negotiations for the release of hostages in Mali. Analysts suggest that this new partnership, centered on cutting-edge electronic security systems, will bolster Mali’s counterterrorism capabilities and improve operational efficiency along its borders.
*Eleven Malian Officers Removed Following Coup Attempt Allegations
BAMAKO, Mali - On October 8, 2025, eleven officers, including two generals, were formally removed from Mali’s armed and security forces. The decision, signed by transitional President Assimi Goïta, follows the findings of a Council of Inquiry investigating suspicions of a coup d’état.
All eleven officers have been in custody since mid-August for allegedly attempting to destabilize the institutions of the republic. They remain detained at a secret location, with neither families nor legal representatives informed of their conditions. Observers indicate that the case may soon proceed to the courts.
*Army base destroyed in Timbuktu
On Thursday, October 9, terrorist groups linked to al-Qaeda attacked the Kelhoussa military base in Timbuktu. Videos released by the extremists show thick black smoke rising from the camp, claiming total destruction. The Malian army has not provided details on casualties or material losses.
*Security post at Bamako entrance attacked
On the night of Tuesday, October 8, armed men assaulted the Malian Armed Forces checkpoint in Zantiguila, 50 km northeast of Bamako. Soldiers repelled the attack, neutralizing several assailants and seizing motorcycles and weapons.
*Tanker convoy attacked in Kayes
On October 8, a Malian army-escorted hydrocarbon convoy of 10 tanker trucks was ambushed by terrorists in the Kayes region. The attackers set the tankers on fire, resulting in soldier casualties and the convoy failing to reach its destination.
Conclusion: Mali at a Crossroads Between Strategic Resilience and the Threat of Collapse
The period from September 29 to October 11, 2025, reveals a Mali facing a multidimensional crisis, where growing insecurity is catalyzing profound economic and social destabilization. The country is navigating between determined efforts to assert its sovereignty and a reality on the ground that threatens the very foundations of the state.
On the security front, the situation has deteriorated considerably. Extremist groups are intensifying their attacks, employing various tactics (ambushes, explosive devices, drones) and extending their influence to the gates of Bamako. The assassinations of prominent figures and kidnappings demonstrate the audacity of the attackers, while the Malian armed forces, despite targeted successes such as the neutralization of terrorist leaders, are suffering significant losses.
This insecurity has devastating economic consequences, materialized by a terrorist blockade on hydrocarbons. The country is plunged into a severe fuel and electricity crisis, paralyzing transportation, the economy, and essential services, raising fears of a shutdown. At the same time, the government is striving to lay the foundations for long-term economic resilience, as evidenced by the strategic revival of the Morila gold mine with an American partner and regional integration efforts within the ESA.
Politically, the transitional government is pursuing a dual strategy: consolidating its authority internally, marked by the dissolution of political parties and firmness in the face of any opposition, and building a new regional security architecture with its ESA partners. On the international stage, Bamako is navigating diplomatic tensions, particularly with France, and pragmatic diversification of its partnerships (United States, Morocco, Mauritania).
Finally, the human cost of this instability is immense. The education system is in ruins, with more than half a million children out of school and teachers on strike. The daily lives of the population are directly affected by shortages and rising costs.
In summary, this report paints a picture of a state in the midst of a struggle for its sovereignty and survival, engaged on several fronts: military, economic, and diplomatic. While strategic initiatives are being launched, security pressures and their immediate repercussions on the population and the economy pose an existential threat to Mali's short-term stability.
