Bukavu, 4 October 2025.
REPORT ON THE SITUATION IN THE DRC FROM 29 September to 4 October 2025
Politics and Diplomacy
*DRC-Rwanda: Joint Monitoring Committee acknowledges obstacles to implementation of peace agreement.
Meeting on 2 October 2025 in Washington in the presence of representatives from the DRC, Rwanda, the United States, Qatar, Togo and the African Union, the joint monitoring committee for the peace agreement signed on 27 June acknowledged the persistence of obstacles to its implementation, notably the neutralisation of the FDLR (Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda) and the withdrawal of Rwandan forces. While welcoming the agreement on the exchange of prisoners reached in Doha and the upcoming resumption of talks with the AFC/M23 scheduled for 6 October, the committee insisted on the need for effective compliance with the agreement, access for the ICRC (International Committee of the Red Cross) to detainees and the abandonment of all hostile rhetoric. It also called for restraint in the face of tensions in Uvira and Walikale, and announced a new meeting after the joint security coordination mechanism meeting scheduled for the end of October 2025.
*DRC-Rwanda: Kinshasa blocks signing of regional economic agreement at the last minute
The Democratic Republic of Congo refused to sign the regional economic integration framework (CIER) negotiated with Rwanda under US mediation at the last minute, even though both delegations were ready to initial the text on Friday 3 October during discussions in Washington, after several days of negotiations. According to Rwandan Foreign Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe, President Felix Tshisekedi instructed his delegation not to sign, citing fears of a negative reaction from Congolese public opinion. The CIER, the economic component of the peace process launched on 27 June 2025 in Washington, aimed to strengthen cooperation in the mining, industrial, energy, agri-food, tourism and health sectors, while building on the security commitments discussed within the framework of the Joint Security Coordination Mechanism (MCCS). Kigali rejects the argument that Kinshasa made the signing conditional on the prior withdrawal of its troops from the east of the country, describing this justification as a "media pretext".
*Kinshasa: Samuel Mbemba calls on Africa to unite against the massacres in eastern DRC
At the close of the African forum on transitional justice, held from 30 September to 2 October 2025 in Kinshasa under the auspices of the African Union and the Congolese Government, the Minister for Human Rights, Samuel Mbemba, issued an urgent call for mobilisation in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Reaffirming the Congolese state's commitment to strengthening the system of reparations for victims throughout the country, he stressed that this struggle transcends borders and political divisions, calling on African countries to translate their commitments into concrete action.
*Kongo-Central: Provincial deputies summoned to Kinshasa amid institutional crisis
Deputy Prime Minister of the Interior Shabani Lukoo Bihango summoned the provincial deputies of Kongo-Central, members of the bureau and petitioners to Kinshasa for urgent consultations as political tensions intensify. The crisis erupted on 30 September with the filing of five petitions against the provincial assembly's bureau at the start of the parliamentary session, before taking a legal turn on 1 October when MP Mantezolo referred the matter to the Constitutional Court. Between political manoeuvring and legal action, the Kongo-Central case has now taken on national significance.
*Maniema: Kasongo civil society organises "dead city" days and peaceful march to denounce insecurity
Since Wednesday 1 October 2025, civil society in Kasongo, in Maniema province, has been staging "dead city" days, with schools, markets and shops closed in protest against growing insecurity in the region. The main motive for this mobilisation is the resurgence of banditry and armed robbery, which affect local populations on a daily basis, disrupt trade and limit access to basic services. On Friday 3 October 2025, a peaceful march brought together members of civil society around a memorandum submitted to the territorial authority. The demonstrators are demanding, in particular, the immediate departure of certain military, police and administrative officials deemed incapable of protecting the population, and are calling for concrete measures to restore security and confidence in local institutions.
*DRC: Bintou Keita urges the Security Council to translate its commitments into concrete progress.
Speaking before the UN Security Council on Tuesday 30 September 2025, the head of MONSCO, Bintou Keita, denounced the persistent gap between decisions taken on paper and the reality on the ground in the DRC, where violence and massacres continue, with more than 1,000 civilians killed since June in Ituri and North Kivu. Presenting the Secretary-General's quarterly report, she warned of a drastic drop in humanitarian funding, with only 14.8% of the 2025 response plan covered, and called for a permanent ceasefire and a lasting peace agreement. While welcoming the diplomatic efforts of Doha and Washington, she urged the international community to translate its commitments into tangible progress for the Congolese people.
*DRC-Ukraine: Tshisekedi invited to Kyiv by Zelensky
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky invited his Congolese counterpart Félix Tshisekedi to visit Kiev during a telephone conversation on Friday, 3 October 2025. The invitation aims to strengthen bilateral cooperation in areas such as defence, agricultural technologies, energy decentralisation and the digitisation of public services. Zelensky expressed his gratitude to Tshisekedi for the solidarity shown by the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) towards Ukraine in the face of Russian aggression. He also stressed the importance of peace, respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity, and proposed a mutually beneficial partnership between the two nations.
*Kasaï-Oriental: Félix Tshisekedi expected this Sunday, 5 October, in Mbuji-Mayi
The President of the Democratic Republic of Congo, Félix Tshisekedi, is expected on Sunday, 5 October 2025, in Mbuji-Mayi, the capital of Kasaï Oriental, for an official visit focused on the socio-economic development of the province. According to Governor Jean-Paul Mbwebwa Kapo, the Head of State will inaugurate the official university of Mbuji-Mayi, inspect several infrastructure projects and meet with local authorities to discuss the revival of the Bakwanga Mining Company (MIBA), a declining pillar of the economy. This presidential visit is raising high hopes among the population, who see it as an opportunity to revitalise employment and reinvigorate construction projects in this long-marginalised province.
*National dialogue in the DRC: the roadmap drawn up by religious denominations marks a decisive step
On 3 October 2025, the Special Representative of the United Nations Secretary-General in the Democratic Republic of Congo, Bintou Keita, welcomed before the Security Council the initiative of the country's main religious denominations, which have drawn up a roadmap for a national and inclusive dialogue. This initiative, supported by President Félix Tshisekedi, aims to establish a climate of trust conducive to the 2028 general elections. The roadmap consists of four stages: the "Month of Peace", dialogue between experts, political dialogue bringing together the opposition, the majority and civil society, and finally an international conference on peace and good living in the Great Lakes region. Ms Keita stressed that without this initiative, the 2028 elections could take place in a climate of mistrust and insecurity.
*DRC: State of siege extended in North Kivu and Ituri, debates over possible extension
On Thursday 2 October 2025 in Kinshasa, the National Assembly and the Senate adopted a further extension of the state of siege in the provinces of North Kivu and Ituri, despite dissenting voices questioning its effectiveness. MP Daida Moleka, elected representative of Lukunga, argued for its extension to South Kivu, citing the growing activism of armed groups and the M23 rebellion, but this proposal was rejected by several elected representatives, particularly from North Kivu, who questioned the results achieved. The interim president of the National Assembly, Isaac Jean Claude Tshilumbayi, undertook to forward this suggestion to the government while calling on parliamentarians to strengthen their support for the armed forces in the face of the ongoing security crisis.
Economy
*DRC: Digitisation of import-export operations comes into effect on 5 October
From 5 October 2025, all applications and renewals for import-export numbers in the Democratic Republic of Congo will have to go exclusively through the Single Window for Foreign Trade (GUICE), the Ministry of Foreign Trade has announced. This reform, which is part of the modernisation of public services, will enable economic operators to carry out all procedures related to import, export and transit online, without having to travel. The aim is to reduce delays and costs, enhance transparency and traceability, and bring the DRC into line with World Trade Organisation standards. This digitalisation marks a key step in improving the competitiveness of Congolese foreign trade.
*Congolese franc: Tshisekedi calls for speculation to be curbed despite currency appreciation
On Friday 3 October, President Félix Tshisekedi praised the joint efforts of the Government and the Central Bank of Congo (BCC), which have led to a 9.1% appreciation of the Congolese franc against the dollar since 19 September. However, he urged the executive to maintain strict budgetary discipline and take vigorous measures against speculative practices that weaken the market, particularly due to the rate disparities observed in Kinshasa. According to the minutes of the Council of Ministers presented by Patrick Muyaya, this monetary improvement is the result of bank reserves, tax levies and targeted interventions by the BCC. Tshisekedi insisted on the need for a more innovative and coherent approach to sustainably strengthen the credibility and stability of the Congolese franc.
*DRC and Japan sign USD 2.6 million food aid agreement
On 1 October 2025, Minister of State for Foreign Affairs Thérèse Kayikwamba Wagner signed an agreement with the Japanese government for USD 2.6 million in food aid. This assistance, provided in the form of Thai rice, is part of the 2025 fiscal year and aims to improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable Congolese populations. The ceremony was attended by the Japanese Ambassador to the DRC, Ogawa Hidetoshi, and representatives of the Japanese Ministry of Planning and the Japan International Cooperation System (JICS). This programme reflects Japan's commitment to human security and sustainable development in the Democratic Republic of Congo.
Social
*Kongo-Central: Judith Suminwa launches modernisation works on the Kwilu-Ngongo-Kimpangu road
At the end of her tour of Kongo-Central, Prime Minister Judith Suminwa launched construction work on the Kwilu-Ngongo-Kimpangu toll road on Wednesday 1 October 2025, a project entrusted to the China Road and Bridge Corporation and TECNOVIA consortium. With an estimated cost of USD 160.38 million, 20% of which is financed by the government and 80% by a bank loan, this project involves the modernisation of 96 km of dirt track into a two-lane asphalt road, the construction of a modern dry port in Kimpangu and the rehabilitation of urban roads. Considered a strategic corridor linking National Road No. 1 to the Angolan border, this infrastructure is to be completed in 24 months and promises to boost the local economy, improve mobility and generate employment opportunities, a prospect welcomed with enthusiasm by users.
*Kinshasa: the Congolese franc appreciates without easing the burden on the household budget
In Kinshasa, despite the recent appreciation of the Congolese franc against the dollar - from an average of 2,800-2,900 FC to around 2,400-2,550 FC - the prices of basic commodities remain high, causing discontent among households. Traders explain this discrepancy by the sale of stocks purchased at higher rates, market volatility and the lack of liquidity in Congolese francs, while the population denounces the lack of impact of this monetary improvement on the real cost of living.
*North Kivu: start of school disrupted in 12 schools in Masisi due to the security crisis
Since 1 October 2025, twelve schools in the Waloa Yungu group in Masisi territory (North Kivu) have remained closed due to clashes between rebels from the AFC-M23 group, the Armed Forces of the DRC (FARDC) and their Wazalendo allies. Among these establishments, seven primary and five secondary schools located in the localities of Bibatama and Mitwa, close to the borders of the Luberike group, are affected. The escalation of violence has caused parents, pupils and teachers to flee, with some taking refuge in the bush, leading to a mass displacement of the population and the suspension of school activities. The provincial education authorities are calling for security measures to be strengthened to enable the gradual return of the population and the reopening of schools, fearing that the school year will be lost if peace is not quickly restored.
DRC: 53 confirmed cases of Ebola and 31 deaths, with a fatality rate of 65%
The DRC is facing an Ebola epidemic with a total of 53 confirmed cases, including 31 deaths, and 11 probable cases, representing a fatality rate of 65%, according to the report of the 61st Council of Ministers meeting on Friday 3 August 2025. The Minister of Health, Hygiene and Prevention, Dr Roger Kamba, said that 49 suspected cases had been reported recently, including 14 validated cases, but no confirmed cases since the outbreak began.  At the same time, the situation regarding cholera and Buruli ulcer is showing a downward trend, particularly in Kinshasa, while appropriate response measures are continuing to contain these three epidemics.
*Maniema: Kasongo civil society organises "dead city" days and peaceful march to denounce insecurity
Since Wednesday, 1 October 2025, civil society in Kasongo, in Maniema province, has been holding "dead city" days, with schools, markets and shops closed to protest against growing insecurity in the region. The main motive for this mobilisation is the resurgence of banditry and armed robbery, which affect local populations on a daily basis, disrupt trade and limit access to basic services. On Friday 3 October 2025, a peaceful march brought together members of civil society around a memorandum submitted to the territorial authority. The demonstrators are demanding, in particular, the immediate departure of certain military, police and administrative officials deemed incapable of protecting the population, and are calling for concrete measures to restore security and confidence in local institutions.
*Bukavu: five dead and several injured after torrential rains
At least five people were killed and several others injured following torrential rains that fell on Bukavu on the evening of 3 September, causing flooding, landslides and destruction of homes in several neighbourhoods. According to local civil society organisations, the most serious damage was recorded on Avenue de la Montagne, Kanga axis, while other material damage affected Avenue Hewa Bora, Avenue Paysage and Avenue Kibarabara in particular. With the rainy season now underway, the authorities are calling on the population to be vigilant in order to prevent further tragedies.
Justice and Rights
*DRC: Joseph Kabila sentenced to death by military high court for treason and war crimes
On Tuesday, 30 September 2025, the military high court in Kinshasa sentenced former Congolese President Joseph Kabila to death, finding him guilty of treason, participation in an insurrectionary movement and war crimes. Absent from the hearing, the senator for life was also ordered to pay $33 billion in damages, including $29 billion to the Congolese state and $2 billion to each of the provinces of North Kivu. According to the court, Kabila leads the AFC/M23 rebel coalition and, together with Corneille Nangaa and Rwanda, is orchestrating a plan to overthrow the country's institutions by force. While dismissing the seizure of his assets and the question of his nationality, the verdict marks a major judicial and political turning point, causing a national uproar. However, the Common Front for Congo (FCC), his political family, denounced the verdict as an "illegal conviction" and saw it as an exploitation of the justice system to undermine his moral authority. For this platform, it is a frontal attack on the rule of law and a dangerous democratic setback that plunges the DRC into a zone of political and institutional uncertainty.
*DRC: Temporary suspension of the issuance of religious permits, nationality certificates and legal personality documents.
On Sunday 28 September 2025, the Ministry of Justice announced a temporary five-week suspension of the issuance of religious permits, nationality certificates and legal personality documents in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. This decision, motivated by the discovery of corruption, trafficking, sensitive documents and parallel networks, aims to clean up the system and restore public confidence. Minister of State Guillaume Ngefa specified that a thorough investigation and audit will be conducted to identify those responsible and put sustainable mechanisms in place, in a context marked by numerous complaints from civil society and abuses by public authorities.
Security and Humanitarian Affairs
*Clashes in Bafwasende: FARDC and Wazalendo repel armed group 9 km from the city centre
On Friday, 3 October 2025, an armed group, believed to be affiliated with the ADF, was repelled about 9 kilometres from the centre of Bafwasende, in Tshopo province. The Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC) and local Wazalendo militiamen joined forces to counter this advance, after local youth had initially hindered the attackers' progress. The clashes were reported near Buyuli, in the Bangbabi region, on the border of the Opienge territory. The situation caused widespread panic among the population, leading to mass displacement to Nia-Nia and Kisangani. According to witnesses, heavy and light weapons fire could be heard as far away as central Bafwasende. Local authorities and civil society remain vigilant in the face of the persistent threat posed by the ADF in the region.
*Ituri: thirteen dead in clashes between armed groups in Djugu
At least thirteen people, including displaced persons and seven combatants, were killed on Thursday during clashes between the CODECO militia and the Zaïre armed group in Maze, in the territory of Djugu (Ituri), according to MONUSCO, which condemns this resurgence of violence. The following night, further gunfire attributed to unidentified armed men caused the displacement of nearly 2,500 civilians to a UN base in Gina, 30 km from Bunia. The UN mission, which has stepped up its joint patrols with the FARDC, is calling for an impartial investigation and urging armed groups to immediately cease hostilities in accordance with the Aru II Peace Agreement.
*Ituri: ADF intensifies attacks on the Komanda-Mambasa axis
Since Thursday, ADF rebels have stepped up their attacks on civilians along National Road 4 between Komanda and Mambasa, in the territories of Irumu and Mambasa. On Friday 3 September, they ambushed a tanker truck near Takumanza before being repelled by the FARDC, which shelled their positions near Lolwa, where violent clashes took place. This resurgence of activity has caused many residents to flee to safer areas, while road traffic has been temporarily paralysed. Faced with this persistent threat, civil society and local authorities are calling on the Congolese and Ugandan joint forces to intensify their operations in order to neutralise these rebels.
*Ituri: MONUSCO condemns the resurgence of violence in Djugu and Gina
In a statement issued on 3 October 2025, MONUSCO strongly condemned the violence that occurred on 2 October in the territory of Djugu (Ituri), which caused the deaths of more than thirteen people, including internally displaced persons and seven members of armed groups. The clashes involved elements of CODECO and the armed group Zaïre à Maze, while gunfire attributed to unidentified armed men in Gina caused the displacement of approximately 2,500 civilians to the MONUSCO base. The UN mission has strengthened its presence on the ground, conducted patrols to protect civilians and called on the Congolese authorities to conduct impartial investigations to bring the perpetrators to justice, while urging armed groups to respect the Aru II Peace Agreement and immediately cease hostilities against the population.
*Ituri: 4,000 civilians take refuge around the MONUSCO base in Gina amid fighting between the FARDC and CODECO
Since the end of September 2025, around 4,000 civilians, including nearly 1,950 women and 700 children, have taken refuge around the MONUSCO base in Gina, in the territory of Djugu, Ituri, fleeing intensified clashes between the Armed Forces of the DRC (FARDC), CODECO militiamen and the Convention for Popular Revolution (CRP). Faced with gunfire in the town of Tcha-Toba, MONUSCO reinforced its patrols with armoured vehicles to secure access and protect the population, evacuating three wounded people to Bunia for specialised care. The rapid intervention of the peacekeepers made it possible to limit human losses and secure civilians in an area regularly exposed to violence.
*Beni: 11 years of ADF massacres, the population demands better protection
On 2 October 2025, the population of the Beni territory in North Kivu commemorated 11 years since the start of the massacres perpetrated by ADF rebels, which since 2014 have resulted in the deaths of at least 10,000 civilians and the displacement of entire villages. In Oicha, scientists, civil society actors and security officials organised a public forum to analyse the causes of the persistent insecurity and issued an urgent appeal to the government to strengthen its strategy for protecting the population. Participants also recommended support measures, including tax exemptions for affected communities, to alleviate the suffering of a population traumatised by more than a decade of violence.
*Kisangani: army calls on population to be vigilant against M23 infiltrations
On Monday 29 September 2025, Brigadier General Jean Daniel Batambombi, deputy commander in charge of civic and patriotic education at the FARDC General Staff Headquarters, urged the population of Kisangani, in Tshopo province, to actively participate in the fight against infiltrations by M23 rebels. During a meeting at Martyrs' Square attended by mayors, neighbourhood leaders, motorcycle taxi drivers and local officials, he stressed that protecting the city is not solely the responsibility of the authorities, but requires collective vigilance and collaboration with the defence and security forces to prevent the M23 from spreading beyond North and South Kivu. The participants expressed their willingness to cooperate while denouncing certain logistical difficulties and hassles that hinder their work.
*DRC: 96 vehicles handed over to security services to strengthen protection of the population
On 2 October 2025, the Congolese government, through Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of the Interior Jacquemain Shabani, handed over 96 Toyota Land Cruiser 4x4 vehicles to the security services to improve the mobility and effectiveness of the forces in protecting the population and their property. Of these vehicles, 70 were allocated to the Congolese National Police, 12 to the territorial administrators and provincial police stations of the provinces of Lomami and Sankuru, while other beneficiaries included the ANR, the DGM and the National Cyber Defence Council. This donation is part of the security services modernisation programme and was accompanied by a sample of weapons equipment, with the minister calling for responsible use that respects human rights despite budgetary constraints and tensions in the east of the country.
*Luhihi: closure of mining site by M23 leads to surge in crime
Since the closure of the Luhihi artisanal mining site by AFC/M23 rebels on 30 August 2025, the security situation has deteriorated significantly in this area located in the territory of Kabare and part of the city of Bukavu, in South Kivu. The ban on access to the site has led to a mass exodus of artisanal miners from North Kivu, South Kivu and neighbouring countries. Since then, the area has been plagued by a resurgence of burglaries, armed assaults, thefts and murders, particularly in nearby neighbourhoods such as Mulambula, Cikonyi in Bagira, and Irambo in the commune of Ibanda, according to civil society. This citizen group attributes these acts to young people who have become idle since the site was closed. Security agents deployed by M23 leaders have arrested at least four people suspected of committing these acts, but the situation remains difficult to contain, according to witnesses. A new armed incursion in Ishungu has left one person dead and several injured. Mapenzi Manyebwa, a civil society activist, condemns this situation and calls for urgent action to restore security and end the spiral of violence.
*South Kivu: FARDC launches counter-offensive to retake Nzibira
The Armed Forces of the DRC (FARDC), supported by the Wazalendo militias, launched a counter-offensive on Tuesday 29 September 2025 to regain control of Nzibira, a strategic town in the Walungu territory recently occupied by AFC-M23 rebels. Fighting is concentrated around the Mulunga, Chirwa, Nyamarhege, Mulamba and Kaniola mountains, where the army is also using targeted drone strikes. Despite rebel resistance, the AFC-M23's advance towards other mining areas, such as Mwenga-Centre and Kamituga, is being slowed by defensive positions established by the FARDC and Wazalendo in Culwe, Lubimbe and Ngando, located about 20 km from the occupied areas.
*DRC: nearly 540 civilians executed and more than 1,450 people forcibly recruited by the M23
A United Nations report published on 2 October 2025 reveals that the Rwandan-backed M23 armed group is responsible for serious human rights violations in North and South Kivu provinces. Over the past three months, nearly 540 civilians have been summarily executed, including 325 in Rutshuru territory during anti-FDLR operations, including 52 women and 24 children in the Bwisha chiefdom. At the same time, more than 1,450 people have been forcibly recruited into the M23 ranks and sent to training camps. Despite the ceasefire agreed under the Doha peace process, the rebels are continuing their offensives in Walikkale and Masisi, causing tensions to escalate. In response, the Congolese air force has intensified its strikes against M23 positions, particularly in Buleusa, while the international community is calling for the protection of civilians and respect for humanitarian law.
*Maniema: MSF announces early closure of its project in Salamabila due to lack of funding
On 3 October 2025, Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) announced the early closure, scheduled for the end of October, of its project to care for victims of sexual violence in Salamabila, Maniema, which was initially planned to run until 2026. After seven years of activity and more than 16,000 survivors treated since 2019, the NGO justifies this decision by the drastic drop in humanitarian funding, with only 15% of the response plan covered, and by the need to redirect its resources to other critical areas, particularly South Kivu. However, MSF stresses that it has achieved its objectives by developing a replicable community model, while maintaining a minimum response to health emergencies in the province, and calls on the authorities and donors to ensure the continuity of the achievements in order to avoid a breakdown in the protection of vulnerable populations.
Overall comment: This report highlights tensions in the DRC: diplomatic deadlock with Rwanda, internal political crises, persistent violence in the east, and social fragility exacerbated by poverty and epidemics. Despite some economic progress, such as the digitisation of foreign trade and the stabilisation of the Congolese franc, the benefits for the population remain limited.
In short, the country needs inclusive political dialogue, strengthened regional diplomacy, a reformed and better-equipped army, and urgent social measures to restore peace and national confidence.
