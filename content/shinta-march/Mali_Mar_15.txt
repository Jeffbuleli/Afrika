*Bamako Faces Fuel Shortages, Power Cuts, and Water Disruptions Amid Public Discontent
Bamako, March 14, 2026 - The Malian capital is grappling with a severe fuel supply crisis, particularly affecting diesel, which has disrupted electricity and water services and fueled growing public frustration. The shortages, ongoing for approximately two weeks, follow a persistent hydrocarbon supply instability after the Jama'at Nasr al-Islam wal Muslimin (JNIM) imposed a fuel embargo on the country in September 2025.
Residents report queues of up to three kilometers at gas stations, with trucks, buses, cars, and motorcycles waiting hours for diesel. While gasoline remains relatively available, the diesel shortage has worsened, echoing the crisis of last October and November when the armed embargo’s impact peaked.
The energy sector has been heavily affected, as Mali’s power plants rely on diesel. Many neighborhoods are now experiencing power outages exceeding 24 consecutive hours, while other areas receive electricity for only three to seven hours per day-far below the transitional authorities’ promise of roughly 19 hours of electricity daily during Ramadan.
Water supplies are also affected, as pumping stations depend on the national electricity grid operated by Énergie du Mali (EDM). Power outages have led to frequent water cuts in multiple districts, compounding public grievances.
Frustration has grown in the capital, expressed openly on social media and in traditional discussion forums known as “grins.” Citizens have also questioned taxes on mobile phone services and money transfers, introduced a year ago to fund energy sector improvements, citing a lack of transparency in the management of these funds. A widely shared social media slogan captures the mood:

The government has attempted to downplay the crisis. Minister of Industry and Trade Moussa Alassane Diallo described the country’s petroleum supply as “regular,” attributing shortages to speculative practices and hoarding by some operators. Authorities have introduced measures such as limiting truck fuel to 600 liters per vehicle over 72 hours to better manage distribution.
Meanwhile, state media continue highlighting initiatives to distribute solar panels to artisans, promoted by President of the Transition Assimi Goïta as part of efforts to address the energy crisis. Despite these measures, observers warn that the fuel and electricity shortages highlight the fragility of Mali’s supply system, particularly amid ongoing security challenges and logistical difficulties, leaving the capital facing one of its most severe service crises in recent years.
Conclusion
The period from February 1 to March 15, 2026, illustrates a dual reality for Mali: a time of strategic diplomatic expansion alongside acute internal security and infrastructure challenges. While the transitional government has achieved notable international and legal milestones, the daily lives of citizens continue to be shaped by insecurity and disruptions to essential services.
Key Strategic Developments
Diplomatic Realignment: Mali has advanced a multipolar foreign policy, marked by high-level tributes to Iranian leadership, credential presentations in Kazakhstan, and strengthened cooperation with China.
Shifting Western Relations: Relations with the United States show signs of a pragmatic thaw, highlighted by the lifting of military sanctions and ongoing negotiations to resume intelligence-gathering flights.
Legal and Economic Milestones: Mali secured a major legal victory, with regional courts declaring the 2022 ECOWAS/WAEMU sanctions illegal. The government also advanced local development, including the allocation of 18.4 billion CFA francs from the Local Mining Development Fund to municipalities.
Persistent Challenges
Escalating Insecurity: Insurgent groups, including JNIM and the Azawad Army, have intensified attacks, using IEDs and drones against Malian forces and Africa Corps personnel. The seizure of key transport routes, such as Kayes-Bamako, underscores the ongoing struggle to maintain territorial control.
Humanitarian and Service Crisis: Bamako faces a severe fuel and diesel shortage, triggering widespread power and water outages. Public discontent has grown, with traders protesting fires, insecurity, and service disruptions across the capital.
Ultimately, the transitional authorities confront the challenge of transforming diplomatic and legal gains into tangible stability. The success of the National Strategy for Emergence (SNEDD 2024-2033) will hinge on securing strategic corridors, restoring reliable basic services, and addressing the urgent humanitarian and security needs of the population.
