*Rwanda Partners with Japan to Strengthen Disaster Management
Kigali, 13 March 2026 – Rwanda is learning from Japan to improve its ability to prevent and respond to disasters including earthquakes, floods, landslides, and lightning, which have historically caused loss of life, damaged infrastructure, and cost the country heavily in repairs.

Japan, situated near the Pacific Ocean, is among the world’s most disaster-prone nations, regularly affected by tsunamis and earthquakes. The conference also commemorates the over 15,900 people who died and the 2,500 still missing from the 11 March 2011 earthquake and tsunami in Japan.
According to Gen. Maj. (Rtd) Albert Murasira, Rwanda’s Minister of Disaster Management, researchers attending the conference, including those from the University of Rwanda, are studying Japanese approaches to early disaster detection, evacuation planning, and preventive measures. “These two days are for us to meet and learn how they work, but now the advantage is that schools and university researchers have also come, so they can continue research for Rwanda’s needs,” he said.
Japanese Ambassador to Rwanda, Kazuya Nakajo, emphasized the potential impact of Japanese technology on saving lives and supporting Rwanda’s economic growth. “I believe that Japanese technology and the NST2 development plan will play a role in preventing disasters and promoting continued economic growth in Rwanda. Together, we can deal with these disasters effectively,” he said.
Rwanda is already implementing programs such as tree planting to reduce landslides and erosion, alongside initiatives to combat deforestation. Japanese company Jibu Gaz One Ltd. showcased an innovative solution for providing affordable gas to low-income households. Traditionally sold in six-kilo cylinders, Jibu Gaz now offers 1, 2, or 3-kilo cylinders, costing 1,600 Rwandan francs per kilo, helping households avoid returning to charcoal or firewood, a key contributor to deforestation.

*Rwanda Continues Efforts to Finalize Border Demarcation with Neighbors
Kigali, 13 March 2026 – Rwanda is continuing a long-standing process to confirm its official borders with neighboring countries, more than two decades after the initiative was first launched, the Ministry of Environment has said.
Speaking before Parliament, Minister of Environment Dr. Bernadette Arap explained that the process began in 2000, when the Cabinet approved the creation of a national commission to oversee border negotiations and demarcation with neighboring states. The commission officially started work in February 2001, supported by a technical team.
“Significant progress has been made, including demarcation work with the Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda,” the Minister said. However, she noted that the process remains incomplete, as some borders have not yet been formally approved by all parties involved.
As a precaution, during the national land registration program (2009–2013), authorities instructed residents living within 200 meters of international borders not to register their land, in order to avoid potential disputes. As a result, some land in these areas remains unregistered to date.
Negotiations are ongoing. Recent engagements include meetings with Tanzania and a February 2025 session with Uganda, where both sides agreed to resume border demarcation efforts.
Land Boundary Disputes Persist
In addition to international borders, Rwanda is also addressing internal land boundary discrepancies identified during the nationwide land registration process.
Minister Arap revealed that 222,423 land plots have already been corrected, while efforts continue to resolve outstanding cases across 582 villages. Challenges included unclear boundaries due to terrain such as forests, pastures, and farmland, as well as instances where landowners were absent during the initial mapping.
To address this, authorities are now using modern mapping technologies to finalize corrections, with the goal of completing the process within the year.
The government is working with development partners to support boundary corrections in districts such as Kirehe, Nyagatare, and Kayonza, with plans to expand the effort to 10 additional districts.
Officials noted that while some residents can hire private land surveyors, others are being supported through local administrative structures to ensure fair representation and resolution.
The ongoing efforts reflect Rwanda’s commitment to clarifying territorial boundaries, strengthening land governance, and preventing future disputes both domestically and across its borders.

*Denis Sassou Nguesso Re-Elected President of Congo-Brazzaville
Brazzaville, 18 March 2026 – Denis Sassou Nguesso has been re-elected president of the Republic of Congo, securing a fifth term with 94.82% of the vote following elections held on 15 March 2026.
The 82-year-old leader, representing the Congolese Labour Party, defeated six other candidates. His closest challenger, Mabio Mavoungou Zinga, received 1.48% of the vote.
The results, announced by national authorities, extend Sassou Nguesso’s rule, which has spanned nearly four decades since he first came to power in 1979. Although he briefly lost power in 1992, he returned to leadership in 1997 following a civil war.
Opposition candidates have a five-day window to file appeals, which will be reviewed by the Constitutional Court before the final results are confirmed.
A constitutional amendment adopted in 2015 removed presidential term limits and age restrictions, enabling Sassou Nguesso to run for additional terms.
The Republic of Congo, located in Central Africa, borders Gabon, Cameroon, the Central African Republic, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and has a population of over six million people.

Additional steps include the neutralization of the FDLR, a phased withdrawal of Rwanda’s security measures, and the protection of civilians affected by the conflict.
The renewed commitment signals a shared intention to achieve lasting peace and stability in eastern DRC and the wider Great Lakes region, though significant challenges to full implementation remain.

*Rwanda Earns Over Rwf236.8 Billion from Tourism in Three Months
Kigali, March 12, 2026 — Rwanda generated more than Rwf236.8 billion (approximately $161.5 million) in tourism revenue over a three-month period from November 2025 to January 2026, according to data from the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda.
The figures highlight tourism as a key driver of the country’s economy, with significant contributions from both air and land arrivals. Visitors entering Rwanda by air accounted for the largest share of revenue, generating $136.7 million. Leisure travelers, including those visiting for holidays and tourism activities, contributed more than $65 million, with gorilla tourism alone accounting for at least 71.4% of that segment.
Regionally, visitors from North America were the top contributors, generating $40.8 million. This performance is partly attributed to Rwanda’s strategic international marketing efforts, including partnerships with major U.S. sports teams such as the Los Angeles Clippers and the Los Angeles Rams under the “Visit Rwanda” campaign. The initiative builds on earlier collaborations with European football clubs including Arsenal FC, Paris Saint-Germain, and FC Bayern Munich.
These partnerships aim to boost Rwanda’s global visibility and attract high-value tourists and investors, particularly from the United States, a major source market for international travel.
Land border entries also contributed to tourism earnings, generating $24.9 million, largely from visitors traveling for family and social purposes. Most land arrivals came through key border points linking Rwanda with neighboring countries including Uganda, Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Tanzania.
The report further indicates that Rwandans traveling abroad spent over $95.9 million during the same period, with air travelers accounting for the largest share of expenditures. Countries within the East African Community remained the primary destinations.
Education emerged as a leading reason for travel, both for visitors entering Rwanda and Rwandans traveling abroad, with students typically recording the longest stays.
Tourism remains a cornerstone of Rwanda’s economic strategy. In 2024, the country welcomed 1.36 million international visitors, generating $647 million in revenue. Authorities are now targeting $1 billion in annual tourism revenue by 2029, as part of broader efforts to expand the sector and strengthen its contribution to national growth.
*Rwanda and Kenya Sign MoU to Boost Cross-Border Payments
Kigali, 11 March 2026 – The Central Bank of Rwanda (BNR) and the Bank of Kenya have signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to facilitate cross-border payments and strengthen regional financial integration.
The agreement, inked on the sidelines of the Inclusive Fintech Forum in Kigali, establishes a ‘License Passporting Framework for Payment Service Providers (PSPs)’, allowing licensed PSPs to operate seamlessly in either Rwanda or Kenya.
Officials said the MoU is expected to promote competition, encourage innovation, and accelerate technology-driven financial services across East Africa. It aligns with the East African Community (EAC) Cross-Border Payment System Masterplan, which seeks to create a common, efficient, and inclusive regional payment system.
A key objective of the agreement is to address regulatory fragmentation by ensuring that a license issued in one EAC country is recognized across member states, enabling broader access to cross-border financial services.
The BNR described the MoU as a significant step toward market growth, innovation, and digital financial integration, all under strict regulatory oversight. The Bank of Kenya reaffirmed its commitment to continued regional cooperation in the financial sector.
*Rwandan Parliament Calls for Stronger Measures Against Illegal Mining
Kigali, 13 March 2026 – The House of Representatives has urged authorities to address illegal mining in a lasting and comprehensive manner, citing its growing threat to public safety and the environment.
The issue was raised during the presentation of an analytical report by the Committee on Foreign Affairs and Security, which highlighted theft and illegal mining as key security challenges.
Deputy Tumukunde Hope Gasatura, Chairperson of the Committee, revealed that between 2020 and 2025, 1,335 cases of illegal mining were reported. Miners have been found operating in people’s fields, rivers, state forests, abandoned mines, and old mine sites, causing environmental damage and loss of property.
He identified several causes of illegal mining, including:
Growth of the mineral market and fraudulent traders
Residents with traditional knowledge of mineral locations
Mining in licensed areas without authorization
Unprotected abandoned or old mines
Youth dropping out of school and joining illegal mining due to financial incentives
Insufficient security personnel by mining companies
Minerals found in private fields and homes
Unauthorized local leaders or former workers exploiting mines
Lack of compliance with labor management and monitoring systems
Gasatura also noted that there are currently 994 unregulated mines across the country, posing risks of accidents, injuries, and deaths.
Deputy Niyorurema Jean Rene recommended the expedited cancellation of mining permits to curb illegal operations. Deputy Uwamahoro raised concerns about markets buying illegally mined minerals, calling for proper monitoring of the chain of custody.
MP Mukabunani Christine proposed the creation of a mining sector unit within the Private Sector Union to strengthen oversight, while MP Kanyandekwe Christine emphasized balancing worker health and safety in legally operating mines.
The Parliament has requested the Ministry of Local Government to present strategies to improve the Mining Task Force, and asked the Rwanda Development Board (RDB) to:
Expedite issuance of mining licenses
Improve monitoring of RMB contracts with companies
Prevent use of unethical subcontractors
Leverage the Inkomane system to track mining activities
The parliamentary push reflects a growing effort to curb illegal mining, protect communities, and ensure the responsible development of Rwanda’s mineral resources.
