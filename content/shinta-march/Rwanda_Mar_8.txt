*Catholic Church Says Condemning Rwanda Will Not Resolve DRC Crisis
KINSHASA, February 24, 2026 — The Catholic Church in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) has stated that repeatedly condemning Rwanda is not a solution to the country’s ongoing security and political crisis, emphasizing instead the need for inclusive national dialogue among Congolese stakeholders.
Church leaders say their current priority is to promote peace talks and national reconciliation, rather than focusing on accusations against Rwanda, which has frequently been blamed by authorities for its alleged support to the AFC/M23 rebellion operating in North Kivu and South Kivu provinces.

According to Church officials, the new approach focuses on creating space for peace negotiations involving all Congolese actors, seen as a more effective path toward resolving the crisis.
Peace Dialogue Initiative
Since early 2025, the Catholic Church and the Anglican Church have been working on a joint initiative aimed at promoting inclusive peace talks between Congolese parties.
Church leaders argue that broad national dialogue, without excluding any stakeholders, offers the best chance for lasting stability in the country.
Government Opposition
The DRC government has expressed reservations about the initiative, maintaining that armed groups should not be included in political discussions.
Authorities insist that individuals who have taken up arms against the state must first lay down their weapons before being considered for participation in political negotiations.
The debate highlights continuing differences between the Catholic Church and the government over the best approach to resolving the ongoing conflict in eastern DRC, where insecurity linked to armed groups continues to affect civilian populations.

In international diplomatic practice, a government first appoints an individual to serve as ambassador to another country, after which the receiving state must approve the appointment before the diplomat assumes official duties.
Once approved, the envoy travels to the host country and presents copies of the credentials to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or an authorized official, confirming their designation.
Within a specified period, the ambassador then presents the original letters of credence to the Head of State, formally completing the process and officially beginning their diplomatic mission in the host country.

*Rwanda Registers Over 1.8 Million Citizens for Digital e-ID System
Kigali, February 25, 2026 – More than 1.8 million citizens have been photographed and registered under Rwanda’s national electronic identity card (e-ID) program, a major digital transformation initiative designed to modernize public services and improve identity verification across the country.
The e-ID system will serve as a single permanent identification document, issued to every Rwandan citizen from birth, securely linking interactions between individuals and both government institutions and private services.
Authorities reported that 1,828,763 people have already been registered across nine districts, including the pilot districts of Huye, Nyanza, and Gisagara, as well as Kamonyi, Muhanga, Ruhango, Nyarugenge, Gasabo, and Kicukiro. The project aims to eventually cover at least 15 million people, including citizens living abroad as well as refugees and migrants.
The digital ID project, which has already received more than $50 million in investment, is expected to complete the validation phase by the end of 2026, with the first electronic ID cards scheduled to be issued starting in August 2026.
Integrated Digital Identity System
The e-ID card will integrate multiple services throughout a citizen’s lifetime. For children, the system will be linked to birth registration, school enrollment, vaccinations, and medical care.
For young people, it will support identity verification for education, examinations, driver’s licenses, and employment records.
Among adults, the digital identity will connect services such as taxation, banking, healthcare, land ownership, marriage registration, business activities, and voting systems. It will also support pensions, social services, and asset management.
In the health sector, the system will link insurance coverage, medical records, medications, and vaccination histories. In education, it will provide a continuous identity record from early childhood to adulthood. The system is also expected to improve social protection programs by preventing identity fraud.
Privacy and Data Protection Measures
Officials emphasized that the digital ID system is designed to protect personal data and will not be used for daily surveillance. Access to personal information will require the explicit consent of the citizen.
Only limited information will be shared when verifying identity. For example, a financial institution may confirm that a person is over 18 years old without accessing the full date of birth. Citizens will be able to revoke access rights at any time.
Under data protection regulations, any misuse of information must be reported within 72 hours, and citizens retain the right to correct data and seek compensation if necessary.
Multiple Forms of Digital Identification
The electronic identity system will be available in three formats to ensure accessibility:
A mobile ID card with a QR code containing essential identity data
A secure electronic ID number for official verification
A Token system, allowing users to conduct electronic transactions without revealing their full ID number
These options are intended to ensure that every citizen can verify their identity even if one method is unavailable.
Inclusive Access to Digital Services
Authorities indicated that the system will remain accessible to citizens without smartphones, using mobile SIM cards and USSD services. Local officials will assist residents who lack access to digital technology, ensuring that no one is excluded from essential services.
Officials and lawmakers believe the national e-ID program will accelerate service delivery, simplify administrative procedures, and reduce the need to carry multiple documents.

*Chamber of Deputies Urges MINAGRI to Reduce Agricultural Spoilage
The Chamber of Deputies has requested the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MINAGRI) to develop strategies to monitor and reduce post-harvest spoilage, particularly for rice and maize, by March 3, 2026.
The decision was taken during the Plenary Session of March 3, 2026, following a study to be conducted by the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda (NISR). MINAGRI has been given three months to implement the measures.
The move comes after an audit by the Auditor General of Government Finance revealed post-harvest losses of 12.4% for rice and 13.8% for maize, exceeding the national target of reducing crop losses to 5% by 2029.
The audit highlighted insufficient and damaged infrastructure, with rice and maize drying facilities operating at 9% and 18% capacity, and storage facilities accommodating only 33% of production, of which 33% were damaged. In some districts, storage facilities were being misused; for example, Nyagatare’s Rwangingo rice cooperative stored hay instead of rice, while in Rusizi, some storage sheds remained unused.
Muzana Alice, Chairperson of the Commission on Land, Agriculture, Livestock and Environment, emphasized challenges such as limited market access, seasonal variations in production, and highly perishable crops, which affect both farmers and consumers. The audit found that of 14 maize cooperatives visited across Nyagatare, Gatsibo, Gisagara, and Rusizi, 12 lacked active markets for stored produce.
The Chamber urged MINAGRI to:
Establish quarterly and annual monitoring mechanisms for perishable crops, supported by NISR research every three years.
Accelerate the implementation of AMIS technology, which will improve the management of agricultural produce and connect farmers to markets.
Develop methods to better link rice and maize production with markets, ensuring farmers can cultivate with confidence even before AMIS technology is fully operational.
AMIS is a forthcoming agricultural information system designed to provide timely and comprehensive data to support farming decisions and market access across Rwanda.
*IAEA Conducts Assessment to Support Rwanda’s Nuclear Power Ambitions
Rwanda is undergoing an International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) assessment to evaluate its readiness to build a nuclear power plant, the agency announced on March 4, 2026. The inspection, conducted by IAEA experts, began on March 2 and will run until March 9, 2026.
The assessment focuses on 19 key areas, including Rwanda’s nuclear energy policy, funding sources, safety measures, and technical capacity to manage potential risks associated with nuclear plants.
Dr. Fidele Ndahayo, Director General of the Rwanda Atomic Energy Agency (RAEB), said the evaluation will identify areas where Rwanda needs to strengthen efforts. “This assessment will help us gain confidence in the international community and attract support to build these plants,” he explained.
The head of the IAEA Expert Group, Aline des Cloizeaux, clarified that the mission is advisory, not a decision-making process. “We are here to provide guidance on what needs improvement so Rwanda can continue its nuclear development plans,” she said.
Rwanda aims to have a nuclear power plant operational by the 2030s and has already invested in training programs, medical nuclear applications, and collaborations with international partners. In 2023, Rwanda signed an agreement with Dual Fluid Energy Inc. (Germany and Canada) to test a nuclear reactor, followed by projects with partners in Russia and the United States focusing on Small Modular Reactors (SMRs).
SMRs are compact plants capable of generating 100 megawatts of electricity, occupying 15–50 hectares, employing up to 230 workers, and being relocatable to remote areas. They are considered environmentally friendly, reducing up to 29 tons of CO₂ per gigawatt-hour (GWh) according to the World Nuclear Association.
The IAEA assessment is a critical step for Rwanda to demonstrate its commitment to safe, sustainable, and internationally compliant nuclear energy development.

*Rwanda Moves Toward Legalization of Virtual Assets
