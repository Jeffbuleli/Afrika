Kigali, August 27, 2026 - The National Bank of Rwanda raised its Central Bank Rate by 50 basis points to 8.75 percent, the highest level since 2009, as policymakers moved to contain persistent inflationary pressure.

The decision marked the third consecutive rate increase and brought cumulative tightening to 175 basis points since November 2025. Inflation rose from 9.1 percent in the first quarter to 13.2 percent in the second quarter, then reached 14.5 percent in July. Energy inflation hit 45.7 percent in the second quarter, while core inflation stood at 12.3 percent.

The central bank expects inflation to remain above the 2 percent to 8 percent target band through much of 2026 before easing toward 7.9 percent in 2027. The economy remained strong, expanding 10 percent year on year in the first quarter, but tighter monetary conditions may raise financing costs for businesses and households.

External trade data showed merchandise exports up 51 percent in the second quarter, driven by minerals, non-traditional exports and re-exports. Imports rose 28 percent, widening the trade deficit to $821.9 million.

Rwanda's minerals sector remains strategically important. The Nyakabingo tungsten mine supplies a significant share of United States primary tungsten concentrate, while China remains a major destination after Rwandan mineral ores received duty-free access from May 1, 2026.

Conclusion

Between August 16 and 31, 2026, Rwanda combined high-visibility continental convening with tighter monetary policy, industrial-land enforcement and continued Great Lakes security diplomacy. The period underscores Kigali's dual track: projecting governance leadership abroad while managing inflation, critical-mineral export momentum and cross-border risks tied to eastern Congo and Uganda.

Continental Leadership: Hosting the Africa Water and Sanitation Systems Leadership Symposium and the Africa Mindset Reset Forum reinforced Rwanda's role as a venue for pan-African policy dialogue, complemented by bilateral outreach spanning Zimbabwe, China accreditation processes, Mozambique defence cooperation and Rwanda-Uganda border command coordination.

Economy And Monetary Tightening: The National Bank of Rwanda raised the Central Bank Rate to 8.75 percent as inflation reached 14.5 percent in July, even as merchandise exports surged 51 percent in the second quarter on minerals and re-exports. Parallel measures - reclaiming undeveloped industrial plots, job-creation financing, irrigation investment, RwandAir fleet expansion and yuan banking outreach - signal an execution focus on productive investment and external competitiveness amid a still-wide trade deficit.

Security And Regional Politics: President Kagame restated that the FDLR threat keeps Rwanda engaged on eastern Congo security, while Doha ceasefire verification advanced in South Kivu. Kigali also rejected ethnic framing in remarks attributed to Uganda's military chief, highlighting the sensitivity of Rwanda-Uganda relations even as border commanders met in Mbarara.

Social And Institutional Integrity: Authorities ordered thousands of primary learners to retake national exams after mass cheating investigations, reinforcing accountability in public systems alongside health, water-access and faith-sector governance stories.

Looking ahead, investors should watch whether rate hikes cool inflation without choking the 10 percent growth narrative, whether industrial-park reallocation lifts occupancy and manufacturing, and whether Great Lakes peace tracks reduce border and compliance risk around minerals and logistics.
