DRC, Aug. 31, 2026.
REPORT ON THE SITUATION IN DRC FROM August 16 to 31, 2026

	1.	Politics and Diplomacy

*Tanzania Backs DRC Sovereignty As Tshisekedi And Samia Deepen Security Cooperation

Kinshasa, August 18, 2026 - President Felix Tshisekedi and Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu Hassan reaffirmed support for the DRC's territorial integrity during talks on August 17-18, strengthening Kinshasa's diplomatic push for the withdrawal of foreign forces and an end to hostilities in the east.

The leaders backed the Washington and Doha peace processes and called for implementation of UN Security Council resolutions. Their joint communique also committed the two countries to deepen defense cooperation and step up action against illicit trafficking in natural resources.

Tshisekedi and Samia also welcomed efforts to neutralize armed groups, especially the FDLR, and noted commitments on disarmament, demobilization, repatriation or reintegration and transitional justice. The position followed a Geneva meeting on August 12-13 where Kinshasa presented a DDR plan, while Kigali has contested the protocol and the Washington framework links FDLR neutralization to Rwandan defensive measures.

The DRC also endorsed Tanzania's bid for a 2029-30 UN Security Council seat, underscoring Kinshasa's effort to anchor its regional diplomacy in African backing as it seeks security guarantees and pressure against external support for armed groups.

*AFC/M23 Tightens Media Controls In Rebel-Held South Kivu

Nyamoma, August 19, 2026 - AFC/M23 authorities in parts of South Kivu banned political programming and the relay of information from Kinshasa, tightening information control in rebel-held territory.

Patrick Busu Bwa Ngwi Shombo, presented as the de facto governor of South Kivu in zones under AFC/M23 control, issued the directive during a meeting in Nyamoma. Media outlets were told to prioritize content on peace, the environment, education and health, with programming subject to prior approval.

The measure followed controversy over a political program on Radio Bukavu FM and signals an effort by the rebel administration to regulate public debate and limit the central government's messaging.

For investors and diplomats, the move underscores the emergence of parallel governance structures in the east, complicating state authority, information flows and any future stabilization framework.

*Tshisekedi Says DRC Sovereignty Is Non-Negotiable As Peace Tracks Advance

Kinshasa, August 20, 2026 - President Felix Tshisekedi said the DRC's sovereignty, territorial integrity and unity are non-negotiable, framing the eastern conflict as a test of national authority and regional diplomacy.

Speaking at the Palais du Peuple during the 10th anniversary of the Higher College of Strategy and Defense, Tshisekedi said Kinshasa seeks a just and durable peace that is not imposed by force. Burundian President Evariste Ndayishimiye, identified as African Union chair, attended the event.

Tshisekedi called for the withdrawal of uninvited foreign forces, an end to support for armed groups and the restoration of state authority across the country. He cited ongoing African Union, Washington and Doha tracks as part of the diplomatic effort.

The message reinforces Kinshasa's insistence that any settlement must preserve central state authority, a key issue for security risk, mining operations and infrastructure planning in the east.

*Ruberwa Challenges Kinshasa Security Strategy In Minembwe

Kinshasa, August 21, 2026 - Ruberwa questioned the government's security priorities in Minembwe, arguing that military pressure on the Banyamulenge area risks deepening tensions while other major localities remain under armed control.

He said the Twirwaneho self-defense group emerged nine years ago amid the expansion of armed groups, framing its creation as a response to local insecurity rather than as the root of the crisis.

Ruberwa argued that the restoration of state authority should be pursued through the Geneva negotiations and not by force alone. The remarks point to continued political contestation over Kinshasa's approach to eastern DRC's armed conflicts and local self-defense movements.

*DRC And Central African Republic Sign Defense Cooperation Deals

Kinshasa, August 21, 2026 - The DRC and the Central African Republic signed memoranda of understanding on military training and intelligence cooperation, strengthening bilateral security ties along a sensitive shared border.

The agreements were signed at the Defense Ministry during CHESD events. CAR Defense Minister Rameaux-Claude Bireau said his country would never serve as a rear base for action against the DRC and emphasized border security and intelligence exchange.

DRC Defense Minister Guy Kabombo Muadiamvita presented the cooperation as part of a collective security response to transnational threats. For Kinshasa, the accords support efforts to prevent armed groups from exploiting cross-border space and to reinforce regional defense coordination.

*United States Welcomes Progress In Doha Framework Talks

Kinshasa, August 22, 2026 - The United States welcomed progress in talks between the DRC government and AFC/M23 under the Doha framework, a diplomatic track aimed at stabilizing one of the country's most consequential security crises.

US adviser Massad Boulos said meetings held in Switzerland from August 17 to 21 advanced the process, with continued support pledged alongside Qatar, Togo as African Union mediator, Switzerland and the African Union Commission.

The parties adopted a roadmap covering ceasefire monitoring and remaining protocols. Sustained progress would be important for reducing conflict risk in eastern DRC, where insecurity continues to weigh on humanitarian conditions, state authority and investor confidence.

*Tshisekedi Opens National Dialogue But Excludes AFC/M23 Rebels

Kinshasa, August 27, 2026 - President Felix Tshisekedi launched an inclusive national dialogue, Abanye-Congo, while ruling out participation by armed groups accused of fighting the constitutional order or serving foreign interests.

In a national address, Tshisekedi said the process would be open to diverse political and civic views, but not to actors illegally occupying territory or operating on behalf of outside powers, naming Rwanda in particular. The exclusion applies to AFC/M23, whose insurgency remains central to the conflict in eastern DRC.

Tshisekedi said the Doha track would remain the channel for ceasefire, disengagement and cantonment arrangements with AFC/M23. He also rejected any general amnesty for war crimes, genocide or serious sexual violence.

The president ordered confidence-building measures while saying judicial independence must be preserved. The approach signals Kinshasa's effort to broaden domestic political consultation without legitimizing armed territorial control in the east.

For investors, the announcement matters because political stabilization and any credible ceasefire process are central to reducing security risk around eastern trade corridors and mineral-rich areas.

*Kabund Rejects Tshisekedi Consultation Plan Amid Reform Debate

Kinshasa, August 29, 2026 - Opposition figure Jean-Marc Kabund challenged President Felix Tshisekedi's proposed popular consultation, arguing that Congolese voters had already expressed their will through elections.

Kabund's intervention adds to the national debate over constitutional reform and political dialogue. The dispute is politically significant because it touches on the legitimacy of any new consultative process and the balance between electoral mandates and institutional change.

The controversy could shape opposition positioning and public confidence as the government weighs its next steps on reform and national consultation.

*African Union Warns Peace Deals Have Not Halted Eastern DRC Fighting

Kinshasa, August 30, 2026 - The African Union chair warned that fighting was continuing in eastern DRC despite recent peace agreements, highlighting the limited impact of diplomacy on conditions in North and South Kivu.

The remarks came in the context of African Union engagement after the Washington and Doha accords, which were intended to support de-escalation and a political pathway out of the conflict. Combat involving FARDC, allied Wazalendo fighters and AFC/M23 forces has nevertheless persisted across key frontlines.

The continued violence raises questions about monitoring, enforcement and the willingness of armed parties to translate diplomatic commitments into battlefield restraint. For the DRC, the credibility of regional and international mediation remains closely tied to whether agreements can produce measurable security gains in the east.

*Tshisekedi Says Humanitarian Workers Are Not Legitimate Targets

Kinshasa, August 30, 2026 - President Felix Tshisekedi said humanitarian personnel must be protected and are never legitimate targets, a national signal of concern over attacks on Ebola responders and aid convoys in Ituri and other eastern provinces.

The statement comes as insecurity continues to complicate emergency health operations and relief access in areas affected by armed violence. Attacks on responders and convoys threaten both epidemic control and the delivery of basic assistance to vulnerable communities.

For investors and partners, the message underscores the government's need to restore minimum security conditions in the east, where violence can disrupt logistics, health systems and broader stabilization efforts.

*Angolan President Warns Eastern DRC Conflict Could Spill Across Great Lakes

Kinshasa, August 31, 2026 - Angolan President Joao Lourenco warned that the long-running conflict in eastern DRC could have effects across the Great Lakes region, adding diplomatic weight to calls for stronger conflict-prevention mechanisms.

Lourenco said the conflict's duration and regional risks make it an issue of uncontested importance. His remarks point to persistent concern among neighboring states that instability in eastern DRC could widen beyond national borders.

The warning underscores the importance of regional diplomacy for DRC's security outlook. For investors, any credible de-escalation framework would be central to lowering political risk in the country's eastern provinces.

	2.	Economy and Tech.

*Kinshasa Orders Faster Disbursement Of External Project Finance

Kinshasa, August 17, 2026 - President Felix Tshisekedi ordered the government to accelerate disbursement of external project finance after an audit found only 24.3 percent of a 10.7 billion U.S. dollar partner-funded portfolio had been spent.

The finding was presented at the August 14 Council of Ministers meeting based on work by the Inspectorate General of Finances. More than 8.3 billion dollars in committed external resources remained undisbursed, limiting the impact of donor and lender support on infrastructure, energy, agriculture, health and education.

Tshisekedi instructed ministers to remove bottlenecks in procurement, project management and coordination with technical and financial partners. For investors, the directive signals official concern that weak absorption capacity is delaying capital deployment and undermining DRC's growth narrative despite large signed financing envelopes.

*Finance Minister Defends Macro Policy And International Market Access

Kinshasa, August 25, 2026 - Finance Minister Doudou Fwamba defended the Suminwa II government's economic record and said reforms were strengthening revenue mobilization, currency stability and access to international capital markets.

In a televised accountability review on August 25, Fwamba said the executive had acted under the 2024-2028 action program to stabilize the macroeconomic framework and improve public financial management after earlier franc pressure that affected purchasing power, including for public-sector wages.

He highlighted DRC's entry into international financial markets as a step to build sovereign credibility, establish a yield curve and secure financing on more favorable terms. Fwamba argued the country now had manageable debt margins to fund priorities.

For investors, the message points to continued reliance on external borrowing and donor programs alongside domestic revenue reforms, with execution speed on financed projects remaining a key indicator of policy credibility.

	3.	Social and Health

*Attack On Ebola Response Team In Ituri Raises Risks To Epidemic Control

Aru, August 18, 2026 - An Ebola response team was attacked in Aru in northern Ituri on August 17, disrupting health operations in an epidemic that has killed more than 2,300 people, including hundreds of children under five.

Ambulances were vandalized and one medic was injured during the incident. The UN Ebola coordination team condemned the attack and urged local authorities, motorcycle taxi associations and residents to support the response and reject violence against health workers.

The incident highlights the operational risks facing epidemic control in eastern DRC, where insecurity and public resistance can delay surveillance, patient transport and vaccination efforts. For the government and donors, protection of medical teams remains central to limiting broader public health and economic disruption.

*Government Updates Ebola Toll And Rules Out Kinshasa Cases

Kinshasa, August 18, 2026 - Health Minister Roger Kamba said the Ebola outbreak had caused 2,370 deaths from more than 5,000 confirmed cases across five provinces, while ruling out any confirmed cases in Kinshasa.

Kamba said the Bundibugyo strain was spreading faster but appeared less lethal than the Zaire strain. The update was delivered at a government briefing with Patrick Muyaya.

Education Minister Raissa Malu said distance learning would be used in 18 high-risk health zones for the 2026-27 school year, reflecting the outbreak's widening effect on public services.

The government's messaging is important for business continuity and public confidence, particularly in Kinshasa, while the school measures show the epidemic is becoming a national policy and service-delivery challenge.

*Butembo Hospital Burned After Confirmed Ebola Case

Butembo, August 19, 2026 - An Ebola treatment response site in North Kivu was burned after a confirmed case, destroying laboratory capacity and raising the risk of wider transmission in an already severe outbreak.

The Bora Maisha hospital in Mukalangirwa, Butembo, was attacked during the night of August 17-18 after a patient with confirmed Ebola had died. Staff and patients fled the facility, and contacts of the deceased patient, including caregivers, remained at large.

The Katwa health zone has recorded 255 confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola cases and 160 deaths. The attack followed earlier July assaults on the Crinovic and Zawadi health posts, underscoring persistent community resistance and security risks around response operations.

The destruction of laboratory assets and disruption of contact tracing threaten to prolong the outbreak in a strategic eastern commercial hub, adding pressure on public health logistics, humanitarian access and local economic activity.

*MSF Says Ebola Recoveries Rise In Ituri As Deaths Remain High

Bunia, August 21, 2026 - MSF said more than 1,100 people had recovered from Ebola in Ituri, but warned that high mortality and delayed treatment continued to threaten outbreak control.

The medical organization cited more than 5,200 confirmed cases and a fatality rate of about 47 percent to 50 percent. It said 62 percent of deaths occurred outside treatment centers and that one person died every 30 minutes during the previous week.

The update came 100 days after the epidemic was declared on May 15, with responders emphasizing early care and community engagement as critical to reducing deaths.

The figures underline the strain on health systems in eastern DRC and the importance of local trust for any sustained containment effort.

*Ebola Crisis Deepens As UN And WHO Seek Access To Conflict Zones

Kinshasa, August 28, 2026 - DRC's Ebola outbreak expanded to 5,945 confirmed cases across six provinces and 60 health zones, underscoring the growing strain on the national health system and humanitarian operations in the conflict-hit east.

By late August, the outbreak had caused 2,862 deaths, a case fatality rate of 48.1 percent. Authorities recorded 1,327 recoveries, 896 patients in isolation and contact tracing coverage of 84.4 percent. Ituri remained the epicenter, though no new health zone was added in the previous 24 hours.

The World Health Organization's Africa regional leadership called on August 25 for a six-month humanitarian truce to allow medical teams to reach populations in northeastern conflict areas. Dr. Mohamed Yakub Janabi said access was decisive to stopping transmission. As of August 20, 158 health workers had been infected and 45 had died.

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres warned on August 27 that Ebola was outpacing response capacity, with about 500 new infections each week and women and children hardest hit. He said the $2.13 billion humanitarian plan was only 48 percent funded and that available resources could last weeks rather than months, while insecurity and displacement continued to hamper response efforts in Ituri and the wider east.

The outbreak has become a macro-relevant risk for DRC, threatening labor availability, cross-border movement, public finances and humanitarian access in areas already affected by conflict and displacement.

	4.	Justice and Rights

*Kinshasa Police Chief Orders Crackdown On Abuse And Corruption

Kinshasa, August 18, 2026 - Kinshasa's provincial police commissioner, Israel Kantu Bakulu, warned officers against abusive weapons use, arbitrary arrests and corruption, signaling a governance push in the capital ahead of the school reopening period.

Speaking to officers in N'djili, Kantu Bakulu cited abusive motorcycle seizures, fabricated infractions and kickbacks among practices undermining public trust. He also linked some police behavior to the persistence of Kuluna gangs in the city.

The commissioner banned the use of scout informers in police stations and ordered that offending officers be sent before military courts. The campaign is intended to support a peaceful reopening of schools and reinforce discipline in the capital's security services.

For investors and operators in Kinshasa, the directive points to official concern over street-level insecurity and police rent-seeking, both of which affect mobility, compliance risk and daily business conditions.

*FARDC Soldier Arrested After Civilian Killed In Kwango

Popokabaka, August 18, 2026 - A Congolese army soldier was arrested in Popokabaka after being accused of killing a civilian with a bayonet, as the 11th military region reiterated a zero-tolerance policy on misconduct against the population.

Second-class soldier Bawile Kipoyo Zedoc, assigned to the 3rd company in Popokabaka, was detained on orders of interim regional commander Brigadier General Moyo Rabbi Richard. He is accused of killing Mukwabonika Jean at Inzofu village in the Yonso sector.

The soldier is to be transferred to the military auditor in Kikwit to face justice. Regional communication officials said the uniform must not shield criminal acts, citing recurring misconduct cases in the 11th military region.

The arrest matters for governance and investor confidence in western DRC, where military abuse cases can fuel local unrest and complicate security for transport and agricultural activity.

*Five Inmates Escape From Crumbling Inongo Prison

Inongo, August 20, 2026 - Five detainees escaped from Inongo central prison in Mai-Ndombe during daylight hours, renewing concern over detention conditions and the capacity of the justice system to hold offenders.

The inmates fled while leaving their cells for basic needs and scaled a low perimeter wall despite warning shots from guards. Interim prison director Puis Ebiaku said the facility, in use since 1928, is severely dilapidated, with cracked walls, missing roofing and inadequate security infrastructure.

Ebiaku called for construction of a modern prison to allow proper operations. The Mai-Ndombe governor said the case had been raised with the central government.

Repeated escapes from the same facility highlight institutional weakness in the interior provinces and raise questions about rule-of-law enforcement beyond major urban centers.

*UN Documents Sharp Rise In June Human Rights Violations

Kinshasa, August 24, 2026 - The UN Joint Human Rights Office documented 657 human rights violations in the DRC in June 2026, a 36 percent increase from May, highlighting the continuing deterioration in conflict-affected provinces.

The violations affected 1,187 victims, with 74 percent recorded in Ituri, North Kivu and South Kivu. The AFC/M23 accounted for 216 cases, while the Congolese armed forces were linked to 94 and the national police to 63.

The findings also pointed to a worsening rights environment linked to the Ebola crisis and to shrinking civic space amid tensions over constitutional reform.

For investors and partners, the figures reinforce the political and operational risks surrounding eastern DRC, including exposure to conflict actors, security-force conduct and constraints on civil society.

*Six Civil Society Activists Freed In South Kivu After Two Weeks

Fizi, August 28, 2026 - Six civil society activists were released after 14 days in detention in Uvira, easing tensions in South Kivu's civic space.

The detentions had drawn concern in Fizi and surrounding areas, where civil society groups play a visible role in monitoring governance, security conditions and humanitarian needs.

The releases reduce an immediate local flashpoint, but the episode underscores the pressure facing civic actors in conflict-affected eastern DRC.

Conclusion

Between August 16 and 31, 2026, the Democratic Republic of Congo remained defined by a sharp contrast between expanding diplomatic and economic agendas in Kinshasa and persistent insecurity, epidemic pressure and humanitarian strain in the east. Peace frameworks advanced on paper, but battlefield conditions, aid-access constraints and governance execution gaps continued to shape the country's near-term risk outlook for investors, lenders and policy partners.

Politics And Diplomacy: President Felix Tshisekedi combined sovereignty messaging with selective dialogue politics, launching a national consultation track while excluding AFC/M23 and drawing criticism from opposition figures such as Seth Kikuni Kabund. Regional diplomacy intensified: Tanzania reaffirmed support for Congolese sovereignty, the DRC and Central African Republic signed defence cooperation deals, Washington welcomed progress under the Doha framework, and Angola warned that eastern fighting could spill across the Great Lakes. The African Union cautioned that existing peace deals had not halted combat, underscoring the credibility gap between negotiation tracks and ground realities. Media controls in AFC/M23-held South Kivu and contested narratives around Minembwe further highlighted how information, identity politics and security strategy remain intertwined.

Economy And Infrastructure Execution: Macroeconomic messaging emphasized reform and market access, with Finance Minister Doudou Fwamba defending revenue mobilization and sovereign borrowing capacity, while the Congolese franc held relatively stable into the school-year spending period. The more material policy signal was absorption capacity: an audit showed only 24.3 percent of a $10.7 billion external project portfolio had been disbursed, prompting presidential orders to unblock procurement and partner coordination. Concurrently, Kinshasa advanced strategically significant files - nearly $500 million in IDA financing, CADECO-linked payroll decentralization, Lobito Corridor concession progress with Mota-Engil under a no-sovereign-guarantee structure, Angola energy and shared offshore-zone cooperation, and renewed debate on Congolese mining equity rules. Local shocks such as the Kalemie fuel strike and customs auctions of warehoused minerals in Lubumbashi underlined how logistics and enforcement frictions still transmit quickly into prices and supply chains.

Social, Health And Institutional Integrity: The Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak remained a major national shock, with rising case and death counts, attacks on response teams in Ituri, a hospital fire in Butembo after a confirmed case, and continued UN/WHO pressure for access in conflict zones. Authorities reported recoveries and ruled out Kinshasa cases, but the epidemic's overlap with insecurity kept containment and mobility risks elevated. On justice and rights, developments ranged from a Kinshasa police crackdown on abuse and corruption to the arrest of a FARDC soldier after a civilian killing in Kwango, prison escapes in Inongo, a UN warning on rising human-rights violations, and the release of civil society activists in South Kivu after detention.

Security And Humanitarian Affairs: Eastern DRC fighting remained the dominant operational risk. Clashes involving FARDC, Wazalendo factions and AFC/M23 continued across Masisi, Walikale, Kalehe, Fizi, Mwenga and Minembwe, producing displacement, emptied villages and contested territorial claims even as a ceasefire verification mission deployed. Drone warfare, attacks affecting aid workers, alleged strikes on civilian or medical logistics, and warnings that aid cuts could hit nearly two million displaced people in Ituri reinforced the humanitarian emergency. A Congo River border clash with Congo-Brazzaville was contained diplomatically, but the broader security picture still points to fragmented authority outside major urban centers.

Looking ahead, DRC's short-term outlook hinges on whether diplomatic tracks can translate into enforceable ceasefires and safer humanitarian corridors, whether external financing envelopes convert into actual project disbursement, and whether energy, corridor and mining-governance initiatives move from announcement to bankable execution. Until those conditions improve, investor exposure should remain selective, structured around secured logistics, critical minerals with enhanced due diligence, concessional and DFI-backed infrastructure, and careful monitoring of eastern security and epidemic spillover risks.
