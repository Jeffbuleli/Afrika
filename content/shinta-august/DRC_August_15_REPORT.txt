*CIA-VAR Clarifies GENOCOST Memory Frame Versus Legal Genocide Recognition  
Kinshasa, DRC, August 4, 2026 - Following the observance of National GENOCOST Day on August 2, which was marked by a memorial event attended by President Félix Tshisekedi, François Kakese, the coordinator of the CIA-VAR initiative, provided critical clarifications regarding the concept of GENOCOST. Kakese emphasized that GENOCOST serves as a national memorial framework aimed at honoring the victims of war and crimes associated with resource predation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo since the onset of the Second Congo War in 1998. He articulated that this framework is distinct from the legal definition of genocide as outlined in the 1948 Convention, which necessitates proof of intent to destroy a particular group.

Kakese's remarks highlighted the complementary nature of GENOCOST and legal genocide recognition, asserting that the two are not mutually exclusive but rather serve different purposes in the context of national memory and justice. He clarified that GENOCOST is not directed against any specific ethnic or social group but is intended to acknowledge the broader impact of conflict and exploitation on Congolese society. This initiative is part of a roadmap being developed under the guidance of the presidency's chief of staff, with the support of the National Fund for the Reparation of Victims (FONAREV), which aims to foster a comprehensive approach to addressing historical grievances.

The legal framework for GENOCOST was established in December 2022, specifically under Article 28 of the relevant law, which provides a basis for recognizing the suffering of victims while navigating the complexities of legal definitions of genocide. Kakese's statements reflect a growing recognition within the Congolese government of the need to address the historical injustices faced by its citizens while adhering to international legal standards.

Although no fatalities were reported, the looting targeted pharmacies, shops, and homes, prompting AFC forces to assert control over the town in a self-defense operation.

On August 6, AFC fighters launched an offensive from Ihula against Wazalendo positions at Rushihe Banamulindwa Kisimba, leading to a brief period of precarious calm on August 7, despite Wazalendo's claims of pushing AFC back to Ihula. The conflict continued to unfold with further skirmishes; on August 8, AFC forces engaged Wazalendo at Misambo Kisimba, where Wazalendo claimed to have maintained control, although casualty figures remain unverified. The fighting intensified again on August 10, when AFC from Ihula confronted CMC Wazalendo forces, prompting civilians to flee into the bush for safety.

The situation took a decisive turn on August 12, when Wazalendo launched an offensive around 16:00 at Kazuba Banansuka Kisimba, successfully dislodging AFC forces and reportedly burning their camps. While the exact number of casualties remains unverified, the ongoing clashes have left the region in a state of precarious calm as of August 13, with both sides preparing for potential further confrontations. The humanitarian implications of this conflict are significant, as civilians continue to bear the brunt of the violence, with many displaced and in need of assistance.

*Partners Note FDLR Protocol and Demand Realistic Neutralisation Plan
Kinshasa, DRC, August 13, 2026 - The International Contact Group for the Great Lakes - the United States, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, the European Union, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden, Switzerland and the United Kingdom - took note on August 13 of Kinshasa’s late-July announcement that an FDLR faction had signed a disarmament, demobilisation and cantonment protocol. The group, chaired by France, demanded a rapid and “realistic” operational plan and calendar for effective FDLR neutralisation, in line with the Washington accords and the 31 October 2024 CONOPS on force disengagement and the lifting of Rwanda’s “defensive measures.” It asked Kinshasa to clarify anti-impunity mechanisms in the text, to work with MONUSCO as a key partner, and warned that any collocation or operational collaboration with FDLR elements is incompatible with Washington commitments. The same statement welcomed the Doha prisoner-exchange step facilitated by the ICRC, urged immediate de-escalation, civilian protection under Security Council resolutions 2773 and 2808, and unimpeded humanitarian access.

The August call sits on a denser late-July record. Regional Integration Minister Floribert Anzuluni said the protocol rested solely on the Washington framework and CONOPS; that FDLR political and military leaders had accepted demilitarisation, demobilisation and cantonment in a first phase if camps were secure and viable and if non-refoulement was guaranteed; and that a second phase foresaw dissolution and voluntary return to Rwanda - or third-country relocation if Rwanda’s security and rights conditions were judged unmet - with an international monitoring mechanism. He argued five of six identified FDLR zones (four in North Kivu, one in South Kivu) were under Rwanda/AFC-M23 control, leaving only Walikale under Congolese authority, and said Rwanda should resume progressive withdrawal so Kinshasa could implement neutralisation. Kigali, through Foreign Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe, dismissed the protocol as a “non-event,” saying génocidaires were the object of Washington, not a party to it, and accusing Kinshasa of dilatory manoeuvres against 90-day CONOPS/OPORD duties. Anzuluni replied that Rwanda appeared surprised by methodical Congolese work and that PDDRCS already held a draft operational plan with timeline and budget awaiting officialisation with stakeholders.

Separately, African Union facilitators meeting in Gaborone on 25-26 July adopted a six-month eastern Congo roadmap and operational action plan for July-December 2026 - covering political analysis, military-security tracks, humanitarian access, armed-group dialogue, regional economy and natural resources, and reconciliation including women and youth - under former Nigerian president Olusegun Obasanjo, with tighter coordination sought with Washington and Doha and a later harmonisation session planned in Arusha. That AU track remains part of the mediation backdrop against which the August 13 Contact Group pressed Kinshasa for concrete FDLR delivery rather than announcement alone.

*Kalehe Fighting Resumes With Civilian Deaths After Brief Lull
Kalehe, South Kivu, August 14, 2026 - Fighting has resumed in the Kalehe territory of South Kivu, resulting in multiple civilian casualties and renewed displacement after a brief period of relative calm. On August 8, three civilians, including a woman and two children, were killed in Bundje Ziralo due to shelling attributed to the RDF/AFC-M23 forces, according to FARDC Lieutenant Reagan Mbuyi. The situation escalated on August 11, when heavy artillery fire was reported from various locations including Chambombo and Lumbishi, leading to further civilian deaths and injuries as clashes intensified between FARDC and AFC forces.

The violence continued to escalate with an offensive launched by AFC on August 12, targeting areas around Katale and Mpanama. FARDC reported holding their positions despite the assault, but the conflict has already claimed the lives of three more civilians, including a woman and two children. The FARDC has cited violations of ceasefire agreements by AFC, particularly in the Kabulungu Kabare region. On August 14, bombardments in Chambombo and Lumbishi resulted in the deaths of five civilians and left four FARDC soldiers wounded, prompting the military to remain on high alert amid a precarious calm in the region. The ongoing hostilities have raised concerns about the humanitarian situation, with many civilians displaced and in urgent need of assistance.

*OCHA: Plaine Savo Camp Triples Past 76,000 Amid Violence and Ebola Risk
Djugu, Ituri, August 14, 2026 - The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) reported a dramatic increase in the population of the Plaine Savo camp, which has now surpassed 76,000 individuals, representing approximately 20% of the internally displaced persons (IDPs) in Djugu. This surge in population, which has tripled since December 5, 2025, is attributed to ongoing violence in the region, where at least 23 IDPs were killed and around 54 seriously wounded between January and June 2026. The situation has been exacerbated by a mid-May ceasefire that has proven ineffective in curbing hostilities, leading to heightened insecurity and displacement.

In addition to the violence, the region is grappling with an Ebola outbreak, with 17 confirmed cases reported in the Bundibugyo Fataki zone as of July 27. The outbreak has further complicated the humanitarian response, as movement restrictions have limited access to fields and markets, which were already closed since the end of March. Women, in particular, face increased risks of gender-based violence (GBV) while attempting to access essential resources. OCHA has called for urgent demands for direct humanitarian intervention, improved civilian site conditions, and enhanced access to medical and basic services, as well as increased funding to address the escalating crisis.

*Armed Men Kill Civilian at Home in Kashira
Masisi, North Kivu, August 14, 2026 - In the night of 13-14 August, around 20:00, armed men shot Jackson Niragire, a father of three, at his home in Kahira/Kashira, Bashali Mokoto grouping. Neighbours tried to evacuate him toward a health facility at Kirumbu, but he died on the way. The motive and identity of the attackers were not established, deepening civilian fear in a territory already marked by contested control.

That killing followed a fragile southern Masisi lull after nearly a week of heavy fighting between AFC/M23 and wazalendo that began on 24 July. Exchanges of light and heavy weapons concentrated in Kibabi I, Kibabi II and Ufamandu I; civilians fled Kasoko, Mushebere and surrounding localities, with many seeking refuge in Ngungu. By about 30 July a precarious calm was reported in several affected villages, but residents remained uncertain and feared renewed hostilities - the same climate of unresolved insecurity into which the 14 August home killing landed.

*Security Mission Visits Kisengo After FARDC-Wazalendo Clashes
Nyunzu, Tanganyika, August 14, 2026 - A security mission led by Colonel Alex Munyalizi Bamaraki of the Usalama forces, alongside Yoane Sululu and Colonel Luntu Kasongo of the Requin battalion, visited the village of Kisengo on August 14 following violent clashes that occurred on August 12. The visit aimed to assess the situation after reports indicated that Wazalendo militia members had opened fire on four soldiers from the Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (FARDC) during a patrol near Kyoko. The incident resulted in the deaths of two Wazalendo fighters, while earlier confrontations had claimed the lives of four Wazalendo and one civilian.

Chief Joseph Lokombe of Kisengo expressed concerns over the ongoing harassment by Wazalendo forces, highlighting the community's distress over the lack of control and security in the area. He commended the efforts of the Requin battalion in maintaining order and providing support to the local population. The security mission's visit underscores the ongoing challenges faced by FARDC in stabilizing the region amid persistent militia activities, as local leaders call for increased military presence and support to ensure the safety of civilians.
