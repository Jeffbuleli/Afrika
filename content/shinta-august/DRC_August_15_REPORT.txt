*Analyst Says Washington Deal Is Political Mineral Gateway, Not Finished Peace

Kinshasa, August 11, 2026 - Analyst Patrick Mbeko criticized the August 7 diplomatic arrangement involving the United States, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Rwanda, describing it as a political framework rather than a settled peace agreement.

He also questioned the handling of Rwanda's role in the diplomatic narrative, saying U.S. sanctions after the Uvira incident amounted to a symbolic gesture rather than a broader condemnation of Rwandan conduct.

Mbeko warned that Congolese negotiators risk weakening Kinshasa's position if language linked to the Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda is used in a way that reinforces Rwanda's arguments. His critique highlights the political and resource stakes surrounding the emerging U.S.-backed track.

*CMOC Rejects Uranium Compliance Allegations At Tenke Fungurume

*Central Bank Governor Defends Franc Strength And Outlines April 2027 Dollar Rules

Kinshasa, August 14, 2026 - Banque Centrale du Congo Governor Andre Wameso defended the recent appreciation of the Congolese franc, saying it was driven mainly by adjustments to reserve requirements rather than large-scale foreign exchange interventions.

Speaking publicly on August 13-14, Wameso said central bank reserves stood at about USD 8 billion, enough to cover around three months of imports. He also pointed to monetary gold accumulation as part of efforts to strengthen financial stability, and said Congolese franc bank deposits rose from CDF 1,450 billion in August 2025 to CDF 2,400 billion at the end of June 2026.

Wameso said the stronger franc had reduced the local-currency cost of dollar-denominated spending, creating a positive net budget effect compared with periods of depreciation. He said the fiscal deficit was largely linked to security spending but had not been monetized by the central bank, instead being financed through Treasury bills and bonds. He declined to disclose the share of war-related spending, calling it a state secret.

The governor also outlined rules due to take effect in April 2027 that would require dollar purchases to be made by card or after conversion into Congolese francs to increase transparency. He said about USD 8.5 billion in cash notes were imported in 2025, while bank deposits increased by USD 1.5 billion, leaving roughly USD 7 billion outside the formal financial system.

The central bank is also working on compliance with Financial Action Task Force standards, including a unique client identifier in cooperation with the African Development Bank. Wameso also backed capitalization of pension funds to support long-term mortgage funding, open recruitment at the central bank, and wider retail access to Treasury securities.

*DRC Bans Copper And Cobalt Concentrate Exports To Push Local Processing

Kinshasa, August 6, 2026 - The Congolese government has enacted a new mining export regime banning shipments of copper and cobalt concentrates, a major policy shift intended to force more local processing and value addition in the sector.

The measure is contained in an interministerial order signed on June 29 by Economy Minister Mukoko Samba, Mines Minister Louis Kabamba Watum and Foreign Trade Minister Julien Paluku Kahongya. It replaces rules issued on August 4, 2023, and introduces a 55% tax on the gross commercial value of "economic-interest mining by-products" - traceable or ultra-trace materials recoverable during the refining of primary minerals.

The government also set out a nine-category nomenclature for these by-products. Only mining title holders, category-B processing entities and artisanal buying houses will be authorized to export marketable products, narrowing the range of actors permitted to participate in exports.

The order provides for a one-year waiver for less-processed products where technical or economic grounds justify an exception. Companies also have a three-month transition period from the signing of the order to prepare for the new regime, although declarations of by-product quantities remain required during that period.

The policy raises compliance and investment implications for copper and cobalt operators in the DRC, where global demand for battery and energy-transition minerals has increased pressure on the state to capture more domestic value from production.

*Kinshasa Opens 60-Day Probe Into Uranium Claims In Cobalt Exports

Conclusion

The period from August 1 to 15, 2026, represents a pivotal juncture for the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). The period is defined by Kinshasa’s aggressive push to leverage diplomatic channels, economic assets, and legal frameworks, juxtaposed against severe security, political, and public health vulnerabilities across the territory.

1. Politics & Diplomacy: Fragile Peace Tracks and Internal Discord

International Frameworks: Kinshasa continues to advance the U.S.-mediated Washington peace track with Rwanda, focusing on the disarmament, demobilization, and cantonment of the FDLR. However, analysts and international observers caution that the arrangement functions more as a political mineral gateway for U.S. corporate interests than a definitive peace deal.

Constitutional & Political Standoff: Domestic politics remain highly volatile over the proposed referendum law. The opposition "Coalition Article 64" (including Martin Fayulu, Jean-Marc Kabund, and Moïse Katumbi) announced a nationwide protest for September 15, opposing any constitutional changes that could alter presidential term limits. President Félix Tshisekedi sent the referendum bill back to Parliament to integrate 13 Constitutional Court reservations.

Regional Influence: The DRC reinforced its regional standing through the election of Mazita Bovy Héritier as Deputy Executive Secretary of the ICGLR and full restoration of its voting rights in SADC after settling long-standing arrears.

2. Eastern Security & Humanitarian Crisis: Multi-Front Instability

Territorial Fragmentation: Analysts warn that the occupation of eastern territories by the AFC/M23 rebellion risks becoming deeply entrenched. The movement continues to establish parallel governance, including appointing university management boards in Goma and Bukavu and naming parallel justice officials.

Active Conflict Zones: Heavy fighting persists across North Kivu, South Kivu, and Ituri. Clashes between AFC/M23, the FARDC, and Wazalendo militias in Walikale, Masisi, and Kalehe have resulted in significant civilian casualties and displacement. Concurrently, Allied Democratic Forces (ADF) raids in Mambasa territory triggered civil society-led mourning strikes and tax noncompliance campaigns.

Diplomatic Operations: A Doha-facilitated prisoner exchange saw 15 individuals handed over to the AFC/M23 via Uganda, providing a rare test of confidence-building measures despite ongoing battlefield hostilities. Meanwhile, the population at the Plaine Savo displacement site in Ituri surpassed 76,000 amid escalating security and health risks.

3. Economy, Mining, and Infrastructure: Value Addition & Fiscal Pressures

Resource Nationalism & Export Banning: Kinshasa enacted a major policy shift banning exports of copper and cobalt concentrates, imposing a 55% tax on gross commercial by-products to force local processing and domestic value addition.

Strategic Logistics & Eurobond Spending: The government approved a $1.258 billion collaboration with Mota-Engil Africa to modernize the Dilolo-Sakania railway line under the Lobito Corridor initiative. Additionally, Central Bank Governor André Wameso defended the $1.25 billion Eurobond issuance as $137.9 million was disbursed toward hydroelectric and airport modernization projects.

Mining Industry Disputes: CMOC rejected allegations that cobalt from its Tenke Fungurume mine exceeded legal uranium thresholds between 2016 and 2020, as the government initiated a 60-day multi-agency investigation.

4. Health & Social Governance: Dual Epidemics and Administrative Reforms

Ebola Outbreak Response: The active Bundibugyo Ebola strain across Ituri, North Kivu, and Haut-Uélé reached over 4,290 confirmed cases and 1,960 deaths. Response efforts faced severe disruptions due to unpaid health worker bonuses in Ituri, though South Kivu successfully cleared its 42-day virus-free milestone. To streamline diagnosis, health authorities launched a mass anti-malaria drug campaign targeting over 1 million people.

Education & Governance Reform: The Ministry of Primary Education regularized 24,296 primary teachers onto the free-schooling bonus system without extra Treasury costs, achieving savings by deactivating duplicate, fictitious, and administrative payroll profiles through new biometric controls.

Ultimately, the DRC’s near-term stability depends on bridging the wide divide between its high-level diplomatic ambitions and the operational realities on the ground. While external economic deals and legal frameworks signal structural progress, true long-term development remains contingent on restoring state authority, enforcing civilian protection, resolving political impasses, and containing active health and security crises in the east.
